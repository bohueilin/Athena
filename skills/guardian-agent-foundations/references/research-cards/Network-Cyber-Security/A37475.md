## Research question
Do conventional Euclidean gradient-based adversarial attacks (FGSM, PGD) remain effective against hyperbolic neural networks, or does a geometry-aware attack that respects hyperbolic curvature produce stronger, more semantically targeted adversarial examples?

## Problem definition
Hyperbolic networks embed hierarchical data (trees, taxonomies, vision-language) on negatively curved manifolds (Poincaré ball, Lorentz model), but their adversarial robustness is under-explored. Conventional attacks are geometry-agnostic: they compute perturbations in zero-curvature space, which may be inefficient or geometrically inconsistent on hyperbolic manifolds. Prior work applied FGM/PGD to synthetic hyperbolic embeddings but did not exploit the radial/angular structure of the output space.

## System or model being studied
Attack target: hyperbolic classification (Poincaré ResNet-20/32) and cross-modal retrieval (HyCoCLIP, Lorentz model). Proposed attack: AGSM (Angular Gradient Sign Method). Key insight — the loss gradient computed in the tangent space of a representation decomposes into a radial (depth/hierarchy-level) component and an angular (fine-grained semantic) component. The paper empirically shows radial shifts barely affect predictions while angular shifts drive most of FGSM's degradation. AGSM computes a tentative FGSM step, measures the feature shift Δh, extracts its angular component (orthogonal to the radial unit vector), back-propagates ⟨h, v_ang⟩ to input space, and applies x_adv = x + ε·sign(∇x⟨h, v_ang⟩). An iterative variant (PAGD) is the PGD analog.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: cause misclassification / retrieval failure by perturbing inputs (untargeted evasion).
- Adversary knowledge: white-box — requires model gradients and access to hyperbolic feature encoder f(·).
- Adversary access: input-space perturbation within an ε budget (ℓ∞-style sign perturbation).
- Adversary capabilities: single-step (AGSM) and iterative (PAGD) perturbations under a fixed ε.
- Attacker budget: perturbation magnitude ε (three levels ε1<ε2<ε3 evaluated).
- Phase: inference (test-time evasion).
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted (fooling rate / accuracy drop).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: standard white-box; not evaluated against adversarially trained / certified defenses.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes white-box access and differentiable exponential/logarithmic maps (Poincaré and Lorentz). Attack operates in the tangent space (T0) via log map, then perturbs input space.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Radial displacement changes hierarchical depth with negligible prediction impact; angular displacement induces semantic misclassification within the same hierarchy level. By isolating and amplifying the angular component, AGSM concentrates the perturbation budget on semantically sensitive directions, yielding stronger fooling than geometry-agnostic FGSM.

## Proposed defense or method
This is an ATTACK paper — it proposes AGSM/PAGD, not a defense. Contributions: (1) argue conventional attacks are suboptimal for hyperbolic nets; (2) radial–angular gradient decomposition to isolate semantically sensitive directions; (3) empirical demonstration of higher fooling rates on classification and cross-modal retrieval.

## Datasets and benchmarks
CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet (TIN) for classification with Poincaré ResNet-20/32; cross-modal retrieval (text-to-image, image-to-text) with HyCoCLIP. Baselines: FGSM, PGD, plus AGSM and its iterative PAGD; clean accuracy reported.

## Evaluation methodology
White-box attacks at three ε budgets; compares post-attack top-1 accuracy (lower = stronger attack) and fooling rates. Includes a decomposition study (clean / radial-shift / angular-shift / FGSM / AGSM) isolating each component's effect.

## Metrics
Top-1 accuracy under attack (Acc@1) and fooling rate. Decomposition on CIFAR-100 / Poincaré ResNet-32 (Table 1): clean 53.44%, radial shift 53.44% (≈no effect), angular shift 25.56%, FGSM 19.67%, AGSM 13.93% (strongest single-step). Main table example (CIFAR-10, PRN-20, ε1): FGSM 56.59 → AGSM 47.63 → PGD 31.83 → PAGD 22.42 (lower accuracy = stronger attack), i.e., AGSM beats single-step FGSM and PAGD beats PGD under equal budget. Trends hold across C-10/C-100/TIN and PRN-20/32.

## Main findings
Author claims: the angular component accounts for most of FGSM's damage while the radial component is nearly inert; AGSM (angular-only) outperforms FGSM and its iterative PAGD outperforms PGD at equal ε on both hyperbolic classification and cross-modal retrieval; geometry-aware attacks reveal that hyperbolic embeddings are especially vulnerable along angular (semantic) directions.

## Negative results
Radial-only perturbation is essentially ineffective (accuracy unchanged), a deliberately reported null result that motivates the angular focus.

## Design implications
Representation geometry changes the attack surface: for any model with structured latent geometry (hyperbolic, spherical), the "sensitive" perturbation direction is not the raw Euclidean gradient. Robustness evaluation for such models must decompose gradients along the manifold's meaningful axes, or it will understate vulnerability.

## Implementation implications
The radial/angular decomposition (log-map to tangent space, project out the radial unit vector, back-propagate the angular component) is a concrete recipe for building geometry-aware attacks — and, inversely, for geometry-aware adversarial training or angular-direction robustification.

## Evaluation implications
Red-teaming hyperbolic / vision-language embedding models with geometry-agnostic FGSM/PGD underestimates true adversarial risk; AGSM/PAGD should be added to the evaluation suite. The decomposition table is a template for attributing which geometric direction drives a model's failure.

## Deployment implications
Hyperbolic embeddings used in retrieval / hierarchical classification (increasingly in multimodal foundation models) carry an under-measured evasion risk along semantic (angular) directions; deployments relying on such embeddings for security-relevant decisions should assume this exposure until robustified.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Angular-shift concentration suggests a possible detection signal (monitoring representation displacement decomposed into radial vs angular) — not developed by the authors but implied. Residual risk: no defense demonstrated.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to white-box adversarial evasion of hyperbolic neural networks (classification + cross-modal retrieval). Adjacent to agent security as transferable adversarial-ML methodology (test-time evasion of embedding models an agent might rely on for retrieval), but NOT directly about prompt injection, tool abuse, or agent-runtime control. Findings are geometry-specific and should not be assumed to transfer to Euclidean models unchanged.
