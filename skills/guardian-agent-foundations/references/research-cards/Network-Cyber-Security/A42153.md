## Research question
How can Time-Series Anomaly Detection (TSAD) be made robust and generalizable for real-world domains — capturing
intra- and inter-variable dependencies, detecting complex anomaly types (sensor-level/regional), reducing dependence
on labeled anomalies, tolerating contaminated training data, and enabling domain-specific foundation models (e.g.,
EEG for epileptogenic-zone identification)?

## Problem definition
Unsupervised TSAD typically assumes anomaly-free training data (rarely true; contamination causes overfitting),
relies on reconstruction-error scores (no generalization guarantee), overlooks inter-variable relations, and is
benchmarked on small/curated datasets with mislabeled or simplistic anomalies. The thesis targets these gaps plus
transfer to biosignals/industrial signals.

## System or model being studied
A program of methods (summarized, published elsewhere), not a single new system: EEG-CGS (contrastive–generative SSL
on EEG subgraphs; AAAI 2023), TSAD-C (graph + diffusion for contaminated data; UAI 2025), MOSAD (open-set TSAD; ECAI
2024), GiCiSAD (graph + diffusion for video/skeleton anomaly; WACV 2025), plus two surveys (SSL-AD in Neural
Networks; G-TSAD in IEEE TPAMI), and a planned EEG foundation model (EZ-TNet, targeting Nature Medicine).

## Threat model

## Attack or failure mechanism
Not applicable. Failure modes are statistical: overfitting to contaminated data, poor transfer of forecasting
foundation models to biosignals, benchmark overfitting to simplistic anomalies.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a security defense. Approaches: self-supervised proxy tasks, graph-based modeling of inter-variable structure,
diffusion models for long-range spatiotemporal dependencies with explicit contaminated-data handling, open-set
adaptation, and domain-specialized foundation models.
