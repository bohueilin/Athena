## Research question
Can a single deep-RL policy unify classification, active learning (AL), and rejection for Android malware detection so that it optimizes the long-term trade-off among detection accuracy, labeling cost, and misclassification risk under realistic concept drift?

## Problem definition
Supervised malware classifiers degrade under concept drift (both goodware and malware evolve, violating IID). Production pipelines bolt on periodic (monthly) AL and rejection separately. Prior DRL work (ICMDP/SINNER) targets class imbalance but (a) ignores concept drift and (b) uses multi-sample episodes that introduce spurious sequential dependencies between independent samples.

## System or model being studied
DRMD: reformulates malware detection as a one-step MDP / contextual bandit (MD-MDP) where each episode is a single sample, avoiding spurious state transitions. Action space {goodware, malware, reject}; rejection routes samples to manual labeling and feeds AL retraining. Trained with PPO modified for one-step episodes; separate actor/critic (4 hidden layers, 512 units, LeakyReLU, dropout); fine-tuned on a sliding window of newest samples to adapt to drift. Reward combines accuracy, class-imbalance upscaling (malware minority ≈10%), a temporal-robustness factor, and a rejection cost/scaling term.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: evade detection over time as malware families evolve (concept drift); the RAMDA feature space is noted as designed for adversarial robustness.
- Adversary knowledge / access: not an explicit adaptive-attacker model; drift is treated as naturally + adversarially evolving distribution, not gradient-crafted evasion.
- Adversary capabilities / budget: not stated as an optimization budget; malware authors "adapt to mimic goodware."
- Phase: deployment / inference over multi-year windows.
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted evasion via evolution.
- Digital / physical: digital (Android apps).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive w.r.t. a problem-space evasion attacker; drift realism is the stressor.

## Trust assumptions
Trusts VirusTotal-threshold labeling (VT ≥ 4) and TESSERACT spatial/temporal constraints for realistic evaluation. Manual labeling capacity is scarce (paper cites ~80 samples/day per company vs ~10,000 daily submissions), so rejection budget is the binding constraint.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Failure = accuracy collapse under temporal distribution shift. Rejection identifies high-misclassification-risk samples for quarantine/manual review; AL periodically retrains on selected samples.

## Proposed defense or method
Unified detection+AL+rejection policy via one-step DRL. Contributions: (1) MD-MDP one-step formulation removing spurious dependencies; (2) integrated rejection action + reward; (3) time-aware evaluation showing drift resilience. On-policy PPO chosen so learning tracks recent samples (vs off-policy replay buffers more susceptible to drift). Deep contextual bandits (NeuralTS/NeuralUCB) argued unsuitable because they assume stationary bounded reward noise, violated under drift.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- Transcendent (Barbero et al. 2022): 259,230 Android apps, 2014–2018.
- Hypercube (Chow et al. 2025): 159,839 apps, 2021–2023.
Both ≈10% malware per TESSERACT spatial constraint; VT threshold = 4. First year train, subsequent years test. Two feature spaces per dataset: Drebin (top 10,000 features, sparse high-dim static) and RAMDA (379-dim, permissions/intents/sensitive APIs, adversarial-robust design).

## Evaluation methodology
Time-aware evaluation obeying constraints C1 (train precedes test), C2 (test from same window), C3 (realistic malware ratio). Baselines compare MD-MDP vs ICMDP using the same agent architecture to isolate the formulation's effect; classification-only (πclf) vs classification-rejection (πcr) policies.

## Metrics
Area Under Time (AUT) with F1 as the underlying performance metric P. Reported: average AUT improvement of 8.66 (classification-only) and 10.90 (classification-rejection) over the best baseline; statistically significant gains over the best baseline in 75% of evaluated settings across feature spaces, datasets, and drift scenarios.

## Main findings
Author claims (first-in-domain): DRL can deliver effective Android malware detection with improved drift resilience; the one-step MD-MDP corrects ICMDP's spurious dependencies; integrating rejection further improves AUT; PPO's on-policy updates suit drift better than off-policy/DCB methods.

## Negative results
DRMD does not beat the best baseline in ~25% of settings (gains "in 75%"). Deep contextual bandit baselines (NeuralTS/NeuralUCB) evaluated in the extended version are reported unsuitable under drift.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Focus on uncertainty-based AL/rejection only; integrating conformal evaluation or explanation-based methods into the reward is left as non-trivial future work. Positioned as a foundational basis, not a complete pipeline replacement.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_ No adaptive problem-space evasion attacker is evaluated, so robustness to gradient/feature-space adversarial malware is untested despite the RAMDA framing. Android-only; Windows PE (the prior DRL setting) not evaluated here. VT-threshold labels are noisy ground truth. Rejection/AL cost is modeled abstractly; real analyst-in-the-loop latency and adversarial poisoning of the AL queue (a known risk the authors cite in their own prior work) are not stress-tested. AUT gains are relative and dataset/feature-space dependent.

## Design implications
Framing a security-detection decision as a one-step contextual bandit with an explicit reject/defer action is a clean pattern for "human_approval as a first-class action" — the model proposes, and rejection routes uncertain cases to a human gate. Directly analogous to an agent that must decide act-vs-escalate under distribution shift.

## Implementation implications
On-policy RL with sliding-window fine-tuning is a practical recipe for keeping a detector current without a growing replay buffer; the rejection-scaling reward operationalizes labeling-budget constraints.

## Evaluation implications
Reinforces that malware/agent-security detectors must be evaluated time-aware (TESSERACT constraints); stationary IID splits inflate performance and hide drift failure — a transferable lesson for any deployed agent-safety classifier.

## Deployment implications
Rejection + monthly AL maps onto a runtime-monitoring + human-in-the-loop containment loop; the temporal reward incentivizes durable policies. Residual risk: adaptive attackers and AL-queue poisoning.

## Monitoring and incident implications
The reject action naturally produces a triage/audit queue; AUT tracks degradation over time as a monitoring signal for retraining triggers.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to drifting binary security classification (Android malware). Transferable in spirit to agent-safety classifiers needing defer/escalate under drift, but NOT a defense against prompt injection, tool abuse, or gradient-crafted evasion — those are out of scope. Numbers do not generalize beyond the two datasets/feature spaces.
