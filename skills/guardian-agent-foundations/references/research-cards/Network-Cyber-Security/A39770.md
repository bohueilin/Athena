## Research question
Can multivariate time-series anomaly detection (MTS AD) catch anomalies that manifest as changes in system DYNAMICS (phase shifts, frequency deviations, morphological distortions) but whose point-wise amplitudes stay in the normal range — cases where reconstruction-error methods produce insufficient anomaly scores?

## Problem definition
Reconstruction-based MTS AD assumes all training points are normal and flags large reconstruction error at test time (mu+3sigma rule). But such methods measure deviation of the time points themselves and miss dynamic anomalies (e.g., a phase shift with valid amplitudes) that generate too-small errors. The paper argues the derivative of the system state (its instantaneous rate of change) carries the missing signal.

## System or model being studied
SDA2D (State-Derivative-Aware NCDE for AD): (1) an NCDE solver (natural cubic spline interpolation to a continuous path, then a controlled differential equation encoder) as a continuous-time sequence encoder handling irregular/incomplete sampling without artificial imputation; (2) a Temporal-Spatial Interaction Module (TSIM) using interacting multi-head self-attention across temporal and spatial branches; (3) a System-State Derivative-Aware Module (SSDAM) computing dz/dt via the chain rule on the NCDE solution and spline derivative; (4) a Joint Reconstruction Module (JRM) that jointly reconstructs the series and the state derivative with a masking strategy.

## Trust assumptions
Training data assumed all-normal (clean); anomalies assumed to appear only at test time. Assumes anomalies induce detectable state-derivative deviations. No trust boundary, authentication, or integrity model.

## Attack or failure mechanism
The failure addressed is a detector blind spot, not an attack: reconstruction error under-scores dynamic-state anomalies whose amplitudes remain nominal, so they evade the mu+3sigma threshold.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a security control. Method: jointly learn to reconstruct both the signal and its NCDE-derived state derivative, so dynamic-evolution anomalies (which perturb the derivative more than the raw value) yield adequate anomaly scores; the derivative visualization also supports anomaly diagnosis / source localization.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Four benchmark datasets referenced: MSL and SMAP (NASA spacecraft telemetry, Hundman et al. 2018), SWaT (Secure Water Treatment ICS testbed, Goh et al. 2017), and TAO (Liu and Paparrizos 2024). Note: SWaT contains real ICS attack scenarios, giving indirect cyber-physical-security relevance. Compared against 15 baselines (CATCH, DADA, FITS, TFMAE, TimesNet, OFA, DCdetector, AnomalyTransformer, CoST, CAE-M, TS-TCC, USAD, OmniAnomaly, MSCRED, DAGMM).

## Evaluation methodology
Point-level classification of each time point as normal/abnormal via reconstruction+derivative error; benchmark-study protocol (Liu and Paparrizos 2024) with searched optimal hyperparameters; ablation and visualization for diagnosis. Full metric tables truncated in extracted text.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- No adversarial evaluation: on SWaT the anomalies ARE cyberattacks, yet the method is not tested against an adaptive attacker who shapes attacks to keep both value AND derivative within normal bounds — a poisoning attacker on the all-normal training assumption would silently degrade detection.
- All-normal-training assumption is a classic evasion surface (training-data contamination) not analyzed.
- Derivative sensitivity may raise false positives under benign regime changes; false-alarm behavior on operational drift not characterized in extracted text.
- Peripheral to agent security; ICS relevance is dataset-incidental, not a modeled threat.

## Design implications
Peripheral for agent security. Transferable insight: for runtime monitoring of agent/host telemetry, point-wise deviation alone under-detects behavioral-dynamics anomalies; modeling rate-of-change (derivatives of state) can surface stealthy shifts that preserve nominal instantaneous values — relevant to EDR/behavioral monitoring (cf. A40815).

## Implementation implications
If adapted to security telemetry, add a derivative/evolution channel to reconstruction-based detectors; use continuous-time encoders to tolerate irregular sampling without imputation that could mask anomalies.

## Evaluation implications
Argues detectors should be tested specifically on dynamic anomalies (phase/frequency/morphology), not only amplitude spikes — an evaluation-coverage lesson for any behavioral anomaly detector.

## Deployment implications
Not a deployable agent guardrail; unsupervised offline/streaming AD with an all-normal-training prerequisite and no adversary model.

## Monitoring and incident implications
