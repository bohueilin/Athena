## Research question
How can the dynamic, fine-grained nature of physical (building/asset) VULNERABILITY be mapped over space and time —
from coarse, static, region-specific labels — using time-series satellite imagery and prior expert belief systems
under weak supervision, for regional disaster-resilience assessment?

## Problem definition
Disaster risk = temporal convolution of exposure, vulnerability, and hazard. Exposure/hazard mapping has advanced,
but physical vulnerability mapping remains static, costly, coarse, region-specific, and poorly calibrated. The task
is weakly-supervised coarse-to-fine spatiotemporal inference with uncertainty quantification.

## System or model being studied
GraphVSSM — a modular Graph Variational State-Space Model integrating (1) graph convolutional networks (GCN) for
spatial/relational structure among buildings, (2) deep state-space modeling for short/irregular time series, and (3)
variational inference (VAE with Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization) for probabilistic latent representation. Four
modules: Observation/Transition Exposure (OE/TE) and Observation/Transition Vulnerability (OV/TV). Building height ~
lognormal, presence ~ Bernoulli, vulnerability ~ Multinomial (extending Pittore et al. 2020 Bayesian updating).

## Trust assumptions
Assumes satellite-derived products and expert/census-derived prior belief systems are trustworthy inputs; the
"weak supervision" concerns label coarseness, not adversarial manipulation.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Not applicable. Failure modes discussed are statistical (overfitting in low-data regimes, noisy/incomplete satellite

## Proposed defense or method
Not applicable (no defense). Method = weakly-supervised variational graph state-space learning with prior-informed
latent states and modular sequential training (OE→TE→OV→TV).

## Datasets and benchmarks
Quezon City (Philippines) city-wide validation; Khurushkul (Bangladesh, 2016–2023, cyclone) and Freetown (Sierra
Leone, 2017, mudslide) case studies; METEOR 2.5D open dataset refining METEOR from 450 m to 90 m and extending to 46
UN-recognized Least Developed Countries at five-year intervals (1975–2030). Inputs include Google Open Buildings 2.5D
Temporal and related satellite products.

## Evaluation methodology
City-wide validation of assumed probability distributions for exposure/vulnerability indicators; high-resolution
demonstration of yearly vulnerability change; release of an open benchmark dataset. Probabilistic (uncertainty-aware)
evaluation rather than adversarial testing.

## Metrics
Probabilistic fit / distributional validation of exposure and vulnerability; spatial resolution improvement (450 m →
90 m). Specific numeric accuracy metrics are in the truncated experimental section and not visible in the excerpt.

## Main findings
Author claims: a unified probabilistic graph state-space approach advances purely analytical Bayesian updating,
enables fine-grained dynamic vulnerability mapping under weak supervision, validates assumed distributions city-wide,
and yields a new open global dataset (METEOR 2.5D).

## Limitations stated by the authors
