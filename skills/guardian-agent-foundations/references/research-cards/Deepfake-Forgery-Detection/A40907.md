## Research question
Can a single countermeasure (CM) generalize across all audio deepfake types (speech, sound, singing voice, music), and does a frequency-domain (wavelet) prompt exist that captures type-invariant authenticity cues?

## Problem definition
All-type audio deepfake detection (ADD): CMs trained on one audio type degrade badly in cross-type detection. The paper establishes an all-type benchmark and seeks a parameter-efficient, type-invariant CM.

## System or model being studied
SSL front-end (XLSR/wav2vec2-xls-r, WavLM, MERT) + AASIST back-end. Two proposed training paradigms: PT-SSL (prompt tuning — learnable prompt tokens prepended per transformer layer, SSL frozen) and WPT-SSL (wavelet prompt tuning — Haar DWT applied to part of the prompt tokens to form LL/LH/HL/HH sub-band tokens). Best model: WPT-XLSR-AASIST, co-trained on all types.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: synthesize deepfake audio (speech, sound, singing, music) that evades detection and undermines multimedia trust.
- Adversary knowledge: not formalized; adversary = audio/speech/music generators (TTS, VC, SVS/SVC, text-to-music). No adaptive attacker modeled.
- Adversary access: produces fake audio distributed downstream; no CM-internal access assumed.
- Adversary capabilities: cross-type generation; the realistic challenge is type diversity and unseen generation methods (e.g., held-out TTM/A-systems in eval splits).
- Attacker budget: not stated in paper.
- Phase: detection at inference/deployment.
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted.
- Digital / physical: digital (clean audio; partially-spoofed and noisy scenarios explicitly excluded).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the primary differences in perceiving audio types lie in the frequency domain, and that a type-invariant discriminative cue exists in a specific wavelet band (authors find the HH/diagonal high-frequency band). Assumes SSL front-ends (designed for speech) under-represent full-frequency information.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Single-type-trained CMs overfit to one audio type's statistics; standard SSL models (wav2vec2) focus on temporal/speech-frequency information and lack full-frequency perception, so they fail to transfer across types (near-chance EER cross-type in Table 2/3).

## Proposed defense or method
PT-SSL learns ADD-specialized prompt tokens with the SSL front-end frozen (author claim: 458x fewer trainable parameters than full fine-tuning). WPT-SSL adds wavelet (Haar DWT) sub-band prompt tokens for full-frequency perception without extra trainable parameters vs PT; all-type co-training yields a universal CM.

## Datasets and benchmarks
New all-type ADD benchmark combining four sources: Speech-19LA (ASVspoof2019 LA), Sound-Codecfake-A3 (AudioGen over AudioCaps), Singing-CtrSVDD, Music-FakeMusicCaps (over MusicCaps). Combined: 199,023 train / 84,438 dev / 189,951 eval. Cross-type protocol with held-out spoofing systems.

## Evaluation methodology
Single-type training tested on all four types (Tables 2–3, frozen and fine-tuned SSL); all-type co-training tested per type; ablations on token count and prompt-placement paradigms (Shallow-PT, After-PT, Del-PT, PT); t-SNE and attention-map interpretability.

## Metrics
Equal Error Rate (EER, %). Headline: co-trained WPT-XLSR-AASIST average EER 3.58% across all evaluation sets (author claim, best reported). Single-type cross-type EERs frequently near 30–50% (chance-like), documenting the generalization gap.

## Main findings
- Author claim: WPT outperforms full fine-tuning under all tested ADD conditions without added trainable parameters.
- A type-invariant deepfake cue concentrates in the wavelet HH band (attention maps focus on the 4th token = HH).
- Prompt tuning reaches near-fine-tuning performance with ~458x fewer trainable parameters.
- Cross-type similarity observed between speech/singing and between sound/music.

## Negative results
Single-type CMs (including strong FT-XLSR-AASIST) fail to generalize cross-type (EER near chance on unseen types) — a documented negative result motivating co-training. Some overfitting (e.g., AASIST on Codecfake-A3) incidentally benefits music, showing brittle, non-robust transfer.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Benchmark deliberately uses clean, non-partially-spoofed audio (noise/partial-spoof interference removed to isolate cross-type study), which bounds real-world applicability.

## Design implications
Parameter-efficient prompt tuning with explicit frequency (wavelet) structure is an effective route to type-invariant audio authenticity detection; frozen SSL + learned prompts reduce training cost and overfitting.

## Implementation implications
Prepend wavelet-derived sub-band tokens (LL/LH/HL/HH) alongside standard prompt tokens per SSL layer; co-train across all target audio types for a single universal CM.

## Evaluation implications
Cross-type EER (train on one type, test on others) is the discriminating metric; per-type averages hide catastrophic transfer failure of single-type CMs.

## Deployment implications
Suited to platforms facing mixed/unknown audio types; validated only on clean audio, so field deployment must add noise/compression/partial-spoof robustness testing.

## Monitoring and incident implications
A single all-type CM simplifies pipeline monitoring; the HH-band interpretability offers a diagnostic signal, though not an authenticity guarantee.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to clean full-audio deepfake detection across speech/sound/singing/music. Should NOT be generalized to noisy/partially-spoofed audio, adaptive evasion, or non-audio modalities without further testing.
