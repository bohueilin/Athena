## Research question
Can a forgery-aware, generalizable AI-generated-image detector be trained using only real images (no synthetic forgeries), while remaining lightweight enough for resource-constrained deployment?

## Problem definition
Cross-model generalization: detectors overfit to model-specific artifacts and entangle with semantic content, failing on unseen generators (GAN -> diffusion -> VAR). Many SOTA methods are also heavy. The goal is a disentangled, model-agnostic, efficient detector that needs no synthetic training data.

## System or model being studied
RealNet, an unsupervised two-stage framework: (1) Real Pattern Extractor (RPE) — a UNet restorer trained in a dual-adversarial denoising framework (restorer + noise generator + discriminator, TripleGAN-style), then frozen; its reconstruction residual on a real image is the Semantic-Agnostic Real Representation (SARR). (2) Feature Transformer applies DFT + amplitude refinement + instance normalization to produce a compact 64-dim real representation (CRR). A Feature Perturbator injects Gaussian noise into CRR to form pseudo-fake representations (PFR, training-only). A lightweight discriminator separates CRR from PFR.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes real-image noise statistics (reconstruction residuals) are compact, semantic-agnostic, and perturbation-sensitive, so that Gaussian-perturbed real features approximate model-agnostic forgery patterns. Assumes any synthetic image will deviate from the learned real manifold. Assumes access to standard real noisy–clean denoising datasets for RPE pretraining.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Prior detectors tie themselves to model-specific spatial/frequency artifacts or to semantic-entangled pretrained (CLIP-ViT) features, so they break under unseen generators. Hard-negative methods with predefined perturbations mainly work for GANs; AEROBLADE-style reconstruction detection is limited to latent-diffusion outputs.

## Proposed defense or method
Learn a disentangled real-only representation (SARR -> CRR) and synthesize pseudo-negatives in feature space (PFR), training a compact discriminator without any synthetic images. RPE objective: dual adversarial GAN loss + L1 reconstruction term + L1 on Gaussian-filtered noise statistics. This yields a positive-compression + pseudo-negative decision boundary.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Evaluated across GAN (ProGAN, BigGAN, CycleGAN, StarGAN), diffusion (ADM, Glide, Midjourney, SD v1.4/v1.5, VQDM, Wukong, DALLE2), and VAR (VAR, CAR, CoDe, FlowAR, Infinity) generators. GAN/diffusion fakes from standard public datasets (Wang et al. 2020). Two newly constructed/released datasets: a VAR-based forgery dataset and a medical synthetic-image dataset (safety-critical distribution shift). RPE pretraining uses public denoising datasets (SIDD, etc.).

## Evaluation methodology
Cross-model generalization and robustness comparison against SOTA detectors; domain-shift evaluation on medical forgeries; efficiency analysis; ablations of RPE / Feature Transformer / Feature Perturbator components.

## Metrics
Accuracy and Average Precision (AP). Author claim: surpasses previous SOTA by 4.51% in accuracy and 3.93% in average precision, at significantly lower computational cost. Exact per-generator numbers per the paper's tables (truncated in extracted text).

## Main findings
- Author claim: SOTA cross-model generalization and robustness across GAN/diffusion/VAR with lower compute, using only real images for training.
- Remains effective on medical forgeries under severe distribution shift.
- Real-only, feature-space pseudo-negative training avoids overfitting to generator-specific artifacts.

## Negative results
Not explicitly framed as negative results; the paper argues prior real-only/training-free methods (predefined-perturbation hard negatives; AEROBLADE) are narrow (GAN-only or latent-diffusion-only), motivating RealNet.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Acknowledges dependence on real-image noise statistics; the method's premise is that synthetic images deviate from learned real patterns — generators that better match real noise statistics could challenge this (implied, not quantified).

## Design implications
A real-only, feature-space pseudo-negative paradigm removes the need to collect/label synthetic forgeries and decouples detection from generator-specific artifacts — attractive for future-proofing against new generators; compact frequency-based embeddings amplify real-vs-perturbed separation.

## Implementation implications
Freeze an adversarially-trained real-noise restorer, take reconstruction residuals as features, compress via DFT + instance norm to low dimension, and train a light discriminator against Gaussian-perturbed positives; drop the perturbator at inference.

## Evaluation implications
Test across architecture families (GAN/diffusion/VAR) and out-of-domain (medical) shifts; report compute cost alongside accuracy/AP to substantiate efficiency claims.

## Deployment implications
Targets edge/resource-constrained and high-stakes (medical) authenticity checks; not validated as robust to adaptive evasion or to post-processing that normalizes noise statistics.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Lightweight real-only detector can be widely deployed as a first-pass authenticity filter; because it depends on noise-residual cues, monitoring should watch for distribution shift and preprocessing that suppresses those cues.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to still-image AI-generation detection where real-image noise residuals are informative. Should NOT be generalized to adaptive/anti-forensic evasion, to modalities beyond images, or to pipelines that aggressively denoise/recompress content without re-validation.
