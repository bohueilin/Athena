## Research question
Can AI-generated image (AIGI) detectors generalize to "in-the-wild" images — those already circulating online or produced by multi-model composite pipelines with post-processing — rather than only to clean single-model outputs, while remaining explainable and efficient?

## Problem definition
Existing AIGI benchmarks confine fakes to controlled single-generator outputs and suffer domain bias (real vs. fake sourced from disparate contexts). Real-world AIGI spans "obviously fake" to highly convincing forgeries, appears mixed with real photos in the same channels (social media, e-commerce, news), and is produced by pipelines of fine-tuned models plus post-processing. The task is defined as detecting naturally-occurring AI-generated images.

## System or model being studied
Two artifacts: (1) MIRAGE, a benchmark built from (a) Internet-sourced, human-verified real/fake images and (b) images synthesized by 64 automated ComfyUI pipelines integrating 53 models (T2I, LoRAs, ControlNets, SAM, GroundingDINO, inpainting) across 8 real-world generation patterns; (2) MIRAGE-R1, a vision-language model trained with a "Heuristic-to-Analytic Reasoning" (HA-R) framework — SFT cold-start then RL with verifiable reward (RLVR/GRPO) — producing an impression → rationale → reflection → answer, with an inference-time adaptive-thinking strategy that engages deep reasoning only when confidence in the fast answer is low.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: spread AI-generated images that evade platform detection or erode trust/authenticity.
- Adversary knowledge: not formalized as an adaptive attacker; "in-the-wild" complexity (unknown post-processing, unseen generators) is treated as distribution shift.
- Adversary access: uploads/circulates images that the detector must classify; may apply composite pipelines and post-processing (face restoration, editing, compositing).
- Adversary capabilities: multi-model generation pipelines, human curation, post-hoc editing; robustness also tested against JPEG compression and Gaussian noise as real-world corruptions.
- Attacker budget: not stated in paper.
- Phase: deployment/inference (content moderation).
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive; no attacker optimizing against MIRAGE-R1.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes trustworthy human annotation (29 trained annotators, three-way plus platform-label unanimity; 11,559 high-confidence images) and that QvQ-Max-generated reasoning traces (given ground-truth labels + in-context example) are adequate supervision. Assumes the constructed OOD split (unseen source B and different T2I models) approximates genuine distribution shift.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Two detector failure modes targeted: (1) binary classifiers over-fit to single-model artifacts and lose generalization plus explainability; (2) CoT VLMs "overthink," making unnecessary errors on obviously-fake easy samples and incurring high inference cost. In-the-wild post-processing (compression, editing) can erase forgery artifacts that laboratory-trained detectors depend on.

## Proposed defense or method
Detection-as-reasoning: fast heuristic verdict plus adaptive reflective re-evaluation to correct the initial impression, dynamically allocating compute by difficulty. Trained via SFT cold-start then RLVR with a confidence-aware/accuracy reward and entropy signal. Intended to jointly deliver generalization, explainability (human-readable rationale/reflection), and efficiency.

## Datasets and benchmarks
MIRAGE (own; ID split 21,685 images with 20,000 train / 1,685 ID test at 1:1 real:fake; OOD split 12,087 images from unseen source B and all 8 generation patterns). External comparisons referenced: GenImage, Chameleon, plus benchmarks compared in Table 1 (CNN-Detection, GenImage, LOKI, Chameleon, AIGI-Holmes). Robustness: ID test set under JPEG compression and Gaussian noise.

## Evaluation methodology
In-distribution and out-of-distribution accuracy on MIRAGE plus a public benchmark; per-generation-pattern OOD subsets for granular robustness; ablation of reasoning structure (G-R-A vs. HA-R with reflection vs. adaptive HA-R); robustness under corruptions without corruption-specific augmentation.

## Metrics
Accuracy (ID and OOD); reported OOD accuracy figures in the excerpt: HA-R-with-reflection 78.13% vs. G-R-A 75.25%; adaptive HA-R 78.46% OOD Acc. (best). Author claim of leading SOTA "by 5% and 10% on MIRAGE and public benchmark, respectively." Robustness accuracy under JPEG/Gaussian reported qualitatively (Tab. 5 truncated).

## Main findings
Author claims: (1) adaptive HA-R achieves best OOD accuracy (78.46%), surpassing always-reflect, by choosing when to think deeply (mitigating overthinking and wrong-impression); (2) reflection/self-correction is critical (+~2.9 pts over G-R-A); (3) MIRAGE-R1 maintains accuracy under JPEG compression and Gaussian noise where baselines drop, without corruption-specific training.

## Negative results
Author-reported: CoT/analytic reasoning "overthinks" easy obviously-fake samples; heuristic (answer-first) reasoning risks post-hoc rationalization. These motivate the adaptive design and are reported as failure modes of prior paradigms.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Concluding text frames the work as "a step toward" practical in-the-wild detection; sources A and B are anonymized proprietary platforms (not named), limiting external reproduction of the human-curated portion. Full related-work and some tables are deferred to appendix/truncated.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Reasoning supervision is distilled from QvQ-Max with ground-truth labels; rationales may be plausible-but-unfaithful, and "explainability" is not independently validated for causal fidelity.
- No adaptive adversary: an attacker aware of MIRAGE-R1 could tune post-processing to defeat it; not evaluated.
- Proprietary/anonymized human-curated sources impede full reproducibility despite released code.
- OOD is constructed from held-out sources/generators available at build time; generalization to future generators is time-bounded and not guaranteed.
- Confidence-based adaptive gating could be manipulated (adversary crafts high-confidence wrong "impressions"); untested.

## Reproducibility

## Design implications
Directly instantiates "models propose, verification decides, adaptive compute": a fast proposer plus a reflective verifier with confidence-gated escalation is a reusable pattern for agent guardrails (cheap check first, deep check on low confidence). Benchmarks must include composite-pipeline and post-processed data to be deployment-representative.

## Implementation implications
Adaptive-thinking inference reduces cost on easy inputs — useful for high-throughput moderation. Requires a calibrated confidence signal; teams should validate calibration and guard against confidence gaming.

## Evaluation implications
Domain-bias-free benchmark construction (real and fake from the same communities) is essential to avoid inflated accuracy. Report per-manipulation and corruption-robustness breakdowns; treat public-benchmark gains cautiously given contamination risk with large pretrained VLMs.

## Deployment implications
Suited to platform content moderation where explanations aid human review. Residual risk: unseen future generators and adaptive evaders; use as a triage/evidence layer feeding human or policy gates, not as final authority.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Log impression-vs-final disagreement and reflection triggers as drift/attack signals; monitor OOD accuracy as new generators emerge; retain rationales as audit records for moderation decisions.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to in-the-wild AIGI image detection with VLM reasoning. Should NOT be generalized to video/audio deepfakes, to adversarially-robust guarantees, or to faithfulness of the generated rationales. Adjacent (not core) to agent security: relevant as a verifier-loop and content-provenance pattern feeding agent evidence.
