## Research question
Why do AI-generated-image detectors fail to generalize across architectural families (GANs vs diffusion models), and can discovering latent architectural signatures within the "fake" class improve cross-generator generalization?

## Problem definition
Cross-generator generalization: a detector trained on some generators must identify images from unseen generators, especially across architecture boundaries (GAN->DM). Surface-level artifacts are fragile to post-processing; the authors instead target latent architectural patterns.

## System or model being studied
TriDetect (Triarchy Detector): a semi-supervised detector using a fine-tuned vision encoder + classifier producing three-way logits (1 real class + 2 fake clusters). It combines binary real/fake classification with unsupervised discovery of GAN-vs-DM sub-structure via Sinkhorn-Knopp balanced clustering and a cross-view (swapped-prediction) consistency mechanism.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: generate synthetic images (via GANs or DMs) that evade detectors, particularly from unseen/newly-emerging generators.
- Adversary knowledge: not formalized; adversary = the space of generative models. No adaptive attacker against TriDetect modeled.
- Adversary access: distributes generated images; no detector-internal access assumed.
- Adversary capabilities: image synthesis across architectures; the realistic threat is architectural novelty (new generator families), not gradient-based evasion.
- Attacker budget: not stated in paper.
- Phase: detection at inference/deployment.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes GAN and DM outputs occupy distinct, separable submanifolds in a well-learned feature space — grounded in a theoretical argument that GANs optimize JS divergence (partial manifold coverage -> boundary artifacts) while DMs minimize KL divergence (complete coverage -> over-smoothing/structured noise residuals). Assumes a pretrained vision encoder provides a suitable feature space.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Detectors overfit to surface artifacts (checkerboard patterns, noise residuals) that are model-specific and removable by simple post-processing, causing the generalization gap. TriDetect reframes the "fake" class as multi-modal (per architecture family) rather than monolithic.

## Proposed defense or method
Joint loss L_total = beta*L_binary + (1-beta)*L_cluster, with L_cluster = w1*L_assignment + w2*L_consistency. Sinkhorn-Knopp enforces balanced cluster assignment (prevents cluster collapse); swapped-prediction supervises one augmented view with the other's cluster assignment; consistency regularization stabilizes assignments across views.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Two standard benchmarks (GenImage; AIGCDetectBenchmark) plus three in-the-wild datasets (5 datasets total), evaluated against 13 baseline detectors (CNNSpot, FreDect, Fusing, LGrad, LNP, CORE, SPSL, UIA-ViT, DIRE, UnivFD, AIDE, NPR, Effort).

## Evaluation methodology
Cross-generator and cross-dataset AUC comparison against 13 SoTA detectors; t-SNE visualizations show unsupervised clusters aligning with GAN vs DM ground-truth generator families.

## Metrics
AUC (per-generator and average). Reported: GenImage average AUC 0.9882 (TriDetect) vs next-best Effort 0.9815; AIGCDetectBenchmark averages of 0.9971 / 0.9869 (best or tied-best per Table 2). Exact numbers per the paper's tables.

## Main findings
- Author claim: TriDetect achieves superior cross-generator and cross-dataset generalization vs 13 baselines on 5 datasets.
- Theoretical claim: distinct optimization objectives (JS vs KL divergence) explain systematically different artifact families; this is presented as the first such theoretical explanation.
- Unsupervised clusters recover architecture families without generator labels (t-SNE evidence).

## Negative results
Not explicitly framed as negative results; baseline detectors (e.g., LGrad ~0.57 avg AUC) show large cross-generator drops, motivating the approach.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Theorems assume generator/score-matching networks have "sufficient capacity" and rely on equality conditions at global optima — idealized assumptions acknowledged in the analysis.

## Design implications
Modeling the "fake" class as multi-modal by generative architecture is a useful inductive bias for authenticity detectors; balanced optimal-transport clustering prevents trivial collapse.

## Implementation implications
Three-way logits (real + two fake clusters) with a binary+clustering joint loss; augmentation-based swapped prediction requires two views per image.

## Evaluation implications
Report per-generator AUC and cross-architecture (GAN->DM) transfer explicitly; average AUC alone hides family-specific failures.

## Deployment implications
Targets open-world detection where new generators appear; effectiveness against a fundamentally new architecture family (beyond GAN/DM) is an open question given K=2.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Cluster assignments could flag which architecture family produced a suspected fake, aiding forensic attribution — but attribution reliability is not independently validated.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to still-image AI-generation detection with a GAN-vs-DM architectural dichotomy. Should NOT be generalized to adaptive evasion, to non-image modalities, or to a world with many co-equal architecture families without revisiting the fixed cluster count.
