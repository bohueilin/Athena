## Research question
Can fine-grained manipulation of a large vision-language model's (LVLM) intermediate activations, rather than only its inputs, improve both the "penetration" (evasion of alignment safeguards) and the "harmfulness" (substantive toxicity) of jailbreak attacks?

## Problem definition
Existing LVLM jailbreaks operate on coarse-grained input manipulation (perturbation-based or structure-based) and ignore internal representations. Consequently they (1) produce anomalous activations that trigger early-stage refusal (limited penetration) and (2) fail to bypass deep alignment, yielding mild/evasive rather than genuinely harmful outputs (lack of harmfulness). The authors seek an attack that manipulates activations at both early and late decoding stages.

## System or model being studied
Generic LVLM decomposition: visual encoder E_V, text encoder E_T, modality connector Θ, LLM-based decoder D. Activations are per-layer intermediate representations H^(l). Six mainstream LVLMs are targeted (text names MiniGPT4, LLaVA, QwenVL, InternVL, GPT-4o; sixth model column truncated in extracted text — "not fully stated in extracted text").

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: induce harmful/restricted output from an aligned LVLM given a malicious instruction (targeted jailbreak).
- Adversary knowledge: white-box — full access to architecture, parameters, intermediate states, and gradients (explicitly stated).
- Adversary access: can craft adversarial visual-textual input pairs; uses gradients (PGD) on the image and last-token activations.
- Adversary capabilities: attention-shifting loss, MMD-based activation alignment, red-teaming model (M_red) to rewrite instructions and select by activation-level harmfulness.
- Attacker budget: T PGD steps on image (ℓ∞ ball radius ε); R generated induction scenarios per instruction. Exact numbers truncated in extracted text.
- Phase: inference-time attack on a deployed/aligned model (no training-data or weight modification).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific harmful behaviors).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: attack is adaptive to model internals; not evaluated against an adaptive defender.

## Trust assumptions
Attacker fully trusts (controls) its own red-teaming model and benign/harmful instruction-response datasets used to build reference activation distributions (H_ben, H_harm). Assumes white-box access to the victim — a strong assumption not available for closed API models (GPT-4o inclusion implies either a transfer/approximation or provided access; extracted text does not resolve how white-box GPT-4o was obtained — reviewer flag).

## Attack or failure mechanism
Two modules. (1) Deceptive Visual Camouflage: an attention-shifting loss L_attn redistributes first-layer decoder attention away from harmful text tokens (W_harm, identified by M_red) toward image tokens (W_img); an abnormal-pattern-concealment loss L_con uses MMD (RBF kernel) to align the last-token activation with a benign activation distribution H_ben; a benign image is perturbed via PGD with L = L_attn + λ·L_con to hide anomalous activation patterns and evade early-stage safety detection. (2) Malicious Semantic Induction: framing-guided induction uses M_red to iteratively generate R distinct scenarios embedding jailbreak steps, then malicious-activation-guided selection picks the candidate whose response last-token activation has minimum MMD to a harmful distribution H_harm, maximizing late-stage harmfulness.

## Proposed defense or method
This is an offensive paper; no defense is proposed. The mechanism above IS the contribution (an attack framework, ActMan).

## Datasets and benchmarks
- Jailbreak instructions: AdvBench (Zou et al. 2023b), MMSafetyBench (Liu et al. 2024).
- Benign initial images X_ben: ImageNet-compatible dataset (Byun et al. 2023).
- I_ben: 2,000 benign instruction-response pairs from GPTeacher.
- I_harm: 2,000 harmful instruction-response pairs crafted following the HADES (Li et al. 2024b) test-data pipeline.

## Design implications
Safety alignment enforced only at the output/token layer is insufficient: an attacker who can shape intermediate activations can suppress both early-stage refusal and late-stage self-correction. Defenses should treat internal representation integrity as an asset, not just I/O filtering.

## Implementation implications
Activation-distribution monitoring (e.g., MMD-style divergence of last-token activations from a benign baseline) is exactly what this attack minimizes — so naive representation-distance detectors can be gamed; detectors must consider the manipulation objective, not a fixed baseline.

## Evaluation implications
Jailbreak robustness for LVLMs should be measured against activation-level (not merely input-level) adversaries, and against multi-stage attacks that separately target early vs late decoding.

## Deployment implications
For any white-box or self-hosted LVLM, exposing gradients/activations (fine-tuning APIs, open weights) materially expands the attack surface. Cross-modal attention redistribution means image inputs are a first-class jailbreak vector.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Runtime monitors that only inspect prompts/outputs would miss this attack; layer-wise activation anomaly detection is implicated but the paper shows it can be concealed. Logging of attention redistribution patterns and last-token activation drift is a candidate signal.

## Applicability boundaries
Findings apply to white-box, self-hosted, gradient-accessible LVLMs. They should NOT be generalized to black-box API-only settings without a transfer study, nor treated as evidence about text-only LLMs (attack exploits the visual modality specifically).
