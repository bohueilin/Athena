## Research question
How can MLLMs maintain safety alignment through *long-chain reasoning* on inputs where each modality is individually benign but their semantic combination is unsafe (the "implicit reasoning risk")?

## Problem definition
MLLMs fail when innocuous unimodal inputs (e.g., an image of "84" disinfectant + text of toilet cleaner + a quantity question) combine into a harmful outcome (toxic chlorine gas). Because resolving such cases needs long reasoning paths, errors introduced at an intermediate step propagate and derail the chain, yielding unsafe answers. Whole-sequence preference optimization (DPO/SimPO/ORPO) is suboptimal because it treats the reasoning path indiscriminately rather than penalizing the specific erroneous step.

## System or model being studied
MLLMs LLaVA-NeXT-LLaMA3 and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (trained with the proposed framework via LoRA). Broad evaluation set of open- and closed-source MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen2-VL, InternVL2, GLM-4V, LLaVA-1.5, DeepSeek-VL, MiniGPT-v2, MiniCPM-v2.6, VILA; GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Gemini).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: induce harmful MLLM output by combining individually-safe image + text inputs (cross-modal implicit-risk elicitation). Note: framed as an emergent safety failure / vulnerability more than a crafted adversarial attack.
- Adversary knowledge: not a classic attacker model; inputs are benign-looking cross-modal pairs. Black-box from the user's side.
- Adversary access: image + text inputs.
- Adversary capabilities: compose cross-modal input pairs whose combination is unsafe.
- Attacker budget: not applicable / not stated.
- Phase: training (SRPO is a training-time alignment framework); failure manifests at inference.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted unsafe outcomes from specific input combinations.
- Digital / physical: digital inputs describing physical-world harms.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: no adaptive adversary; this is an alignment/robustness study.

## Trust assumptions
Deployer/trainer-side white-box (LoRA fine-tuning of the base MLLM). Data generation trusts a multi-agent LLM pipeline (query/reasoning/reflection-check/summary agents) plus manual revision to produce correct safety CoTs and to filter unsafe queries.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Long-chain reasoning branches: at step vi the model may take a wrong continuation (e.g., focus on computing quantities instead of recognizing toxic-gas risk); the error is confined to and propagates through the subsequent branch, ruining the final safety judgment. The large multimodal solution space multiplies such error-prone branches.

## Proposed defense or method
**SRPO (Safety-aware Reasoning Path Optimization) + SSUI dataset + RSBench.**
- **SSUI dataset**: AI-assisted, five-stage multi-agent pipeline over ~25,000 seed safe images (Open Images v7, COCO, EgoShots) → 4,779 finalized samples with interpretable safety reasoning paths; hierarchical taxonomy of 3 primary / 19 secondary / 68 tertiary risk categories; manual revision stage.
- **SRPO stage 1 — Generative Exploration**: from reference CoT paths, temperature-sample multiple continuations at each step to obtain, per step, one correct (positive τ+) and one incorrect (negative τ−) branch, verified by a function F (F(τ)=1 iff final step reaches ground-truth A).
- **SRPO stage 2 — Path Optimization**: jointly (a) LM loss JRef on reference paths and (b) a step-wise log-ratio preference alignment loss JAlign,i = −k·log σ(L(τi+|θ) − L(τi−|θ)) summed over intermediate states, with total objective balancing via weight λ. This penalizes the specific erroneous step rather than the whole sequence.
- Training: LoRA (rank 8, batch 8, lr 5e-5, λ=0.3, sampling temperature 0.5, 8×A100).
- **RSBench**: new benchmark to evaluate effectiveness and safety of CoT reasoning paths.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- SSUI (proposed): 4,779 samples; images from Open Images v7, COCO, EgoShots.
- Evaluation benchmarks: USBBench (esp. harder SIST subset), MSSBench (contextual safety), MLLM-GUARD (5 dimensions), SafeBench (8 categories / 23 sub), VLSBench (6 / 19), plus proposed RSBench.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Dataset is largely AI-generated by a multi-agent LLM pipeline; despite manual revision, label quality and coverage depend on the generating models (potential distributional/annotation bias). Using an LLM multi-agent system to define "unsafe combinations" risks circularity with the models being aligned.
- Only two MLLMs are trained; generalization of SRPO beyond LLaVA-NeXT-LLaMA3 and Qwen2.5-VL-7B is asserted via broad-model *evaluation* but not via broad-model *training*.
- RSBench is self-proposed and used both to tune λ and to report SOTA — risk of evaluation overfitting to the authors' own benchmark.
- No adaptive adversary and no test of over-refusal on genuinely benign cross-modal pairs quantified in the extracted text.
- The correctness verifier F assumes a single ground-truth answer A per case, which may not capture safety nuance in open-ended reasoning.

## Design implications
Safety supervision should be applied at the *reasoning-step* granularity, not the whole response, so alignment survives long multimodal chains. Cross-modal safety requires reasoning about emergent semantics of combined modalities, not per-modality filtering.

## Implementation implications
Step-wise positive/negative branch construction (with a verifier) is a practical recipe for reasoning-aware preference training; LoRA makes it lightweight. Interpretable reasoning-path labels aid auditability of why a refusal was reached.

## Evaluation implications
Cross-modal safety benchmarks should include benign-but-combinable inputs (implicit risk), not just overtly harmful images/prompts, and should evaluate the reasoning path, not only the final label (motivating RSBench). Beware self-benchmark tuning-and-reporting.

## Deployment implications
Applicable to self-hosted MLLM deployments in domains where safe-looking inputs can combine into physical-world harm (chemistry, safety instructions). Adds a training-time cost; inference cost unchanged.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Interpretable safety CoTs provide an evidence trail for post-hoc review of why the model did/didn't flag a combined-input hazard; useful for content-moderation audit.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to image+text MLLM reasoning safety (implicit cross-modal risk). Does NOT address adversarial jailbreak prompts, video, tool-using agents, or text-only models. SOTA claims are conditioned on the evaluated benchmarks (including the authors' RSBench) and two trained models. The multi-agent data pipeline is an annotation tool, not an agent-security artifact.
