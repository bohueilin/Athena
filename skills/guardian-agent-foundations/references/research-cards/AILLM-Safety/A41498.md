## Research question
Do general-purpose content-safety guardrails adequately detect *domain-specific* sensitive financial information (SFI) in GenAI inputs/outputs, and can a taxonomy-grounded, synthetically-trained detector do better for the financial sector?

## Problem definition
LLMs may memorize and inadvertently reveal sensitive information (PII, financial records, credentials); adversaries can extract it via crafted prompts / model inversion / extractable memorization. Sensitive-information disclosure is ranked second in the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, yet most LLM evaluations focus on misinformation/toxicity/representational harm, and existing guardrails largely ignore domain-specific financial risks. A core obstacle is the absence of SFI datasets (organizations will not share confidential data).

## System or model being studied
GARD (Generative Adversarial network Risk Detection): a GAN-based risk-detection model trained on a synthesized, taxonomy-grounded SFI dataset to classify text (LLM inputs and outputs) as risky/non-risky in the financial domain; deployable standalone or alongside existing guardrails. Reference production system: BNY's "Eliza" GenAI interface.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: extract or elicit disclosure of sensitive financial information from a GenAI system.
- Adversary knowledge: black-box prompting (crafted prompt sequences); memorization-extraction attacks referenced.
- Adversary access: input channel to the GenAI system; observation of outputs.
- Adversary capabilities: model inversion, training-data extraction/extractable memorization, prompt injection, jailbreaking to bypass safety and surface SSNs, private communications, account data, etc.
- Attacker budget: not quantified.
- Phase: inference/deployment (input and output moderation).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (retrieve specific SFI).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive (no attacker adapted specifically against GARD is evaluated).

## Trust assumptions
Trusts a curated taxonomy grounded in financial/security/AI-governance standards (GLBA, PCI DSS v4.0.1, FFIEC, ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST SP 800-53/800-122/800-171, OWASP LLM Top 10) plus SME-defined keywords and BNY internal policies; trusts LLMs to generate representative synthetic SFI, and OpenAI text-embedding-ada-002 to encode semantics. SMEs validate a small real-world reference set.

## Attack or failure mechanism
LLMs trained on web-scale data can regurgitate memorized SI even with filtering (cited: clinical-notes leakage; scalable extraction from production models where alignment does not fully prevent memorization). General guardrails with fixed taxonomies miss financial-domain SFI, leaving a disclosure gap (OWASP LLM #2).

## Proposed defense or method
A three-component pipeline: (1) **Synthetic data generation** — build a grounded SFI taxonomy (Appendix 1) spanning PII, authentication/security data, business/proprietary info, customer/account/payment-card data, non-public personal info, regulatory/compliance (SARs, KYC, AML), health-related financial, operational logs, "other"; prompt multiple LLMs (Gemini 1.5-Pro, Mixtral-8x7B, GPT-4o, Llama 3.1) plus real Eliza prompts, guided by SME keywords, to produce risky and non-risky samples. (2) **Embedding** — OpenAI text-embedding-ada-002 → 1,536-dim vectors. (3) **Predictive modelling** — GARD (GAN-based) trained to both detect SFI-bearing text and generate vectors similar to such texts, addressing data scarcity.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Constructed dataset: 17,495 domain-specific samples (synthetic 15,360 + real 2,135; total unique risky 10,098; total unique risky/non-risky 17,495). Validation reference: 115 real-world risky samples validated by three SMEs; synthetic-vs-reference average-embedding cosine similarity = 0.97. Baselines: OpenAI Moderation API, Microsoft Azure Content Safety (ACS).

## Evaluation methodology
Compare GARD against commercial guardrails on SFI detection using precision and recall; assess synthetic-data realism via cosine similarity to the SME-validated reference set.

## Metrics
Precision and Recall. Author headline: GARD recall = 0.98, substantially above commercial baselines whose precision is high but recall is much lower (many risky instances undetected). Synthetic-vs-real cosine similarity = 0.97.

## Main findings
- Author claim: commercial solutions (OpenAI Moderation, Azure ACS) keep high precision but have substantially lower recall, missing many risky financial instances.
- Author claim: GARD achieves recall 0.98, significantly outperforming benchmarks for SFI detection.
- Author claim: synthetic dataset closely matches real risky data (0.97 cosine similarity), supporting its use to overcome data scarcity.
- Contributions: (1) grounded SFI taxonomy for GenAI, (2) comprehensive synthetic SFI dataset, (3) empirically-validated SFI risk-detection model deployable standalone or alongside existing guardrails.

## Negative results
General-purpose guardrails' low recall is itself the motivating negative result (they under-detect domain SFI). GARD's own precision and false-positive behavior are less emphasized in the extracted text, and the precision/recall trade-off vs commercial high-precision tools is not fully characterized.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Data scarcity and organizations' reluctance to share confidential data drive reliance on synthetic generation. Prior trust-framework work identified SI detection as needed but lacked an empirically validated mechanism — a gap this paper targets. (Fuller limitation discussion is in truncated sections.)

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Training is dominated by LLM-synthesized data; real validation set is small (115 SME-validated samples), and cosine similarity to that set does not establish coverage of real-world attack diversity.
- Recall 0.98 on a self-constructed dataset can favor the in-distribution detector; comparison to commercial APIs on the authors' own data may disadvantage baselines built for different taxonomies.
- No adaptive attacker (e.g., obfuscated/paraphrased SFI, encoding, prompt injection to evade the detector) is evaluated.
- Single embedding model (ada-002) and a GAN detector; robustness to distribution shift, new financial products, and non-English content is untested.
- False-positive rate / operational over-blocking is under-reported; high recall alone can harm usability.
- Detector operates on text; it does not prevent underlying memorization or model-inversion, only flags I/O.

## Design implications
Domain-specific guardrails grounded in regulatory taxonomies materially close recall gaps that general-purpose moderation leaves open; treat sensitive-information disclosure as a first-class, separately-instrumented guardrail (input *and* output) rather than assuming a generic safety classifier covers it.

## Implementation implications
Taxonomy-driven synthetic data generation is a viable way to bootstrap detectors where real sensitive data cannot be shared; validate synthetic realism against a small SME-labeled reference set. GARD can be layered alongside existing guardrails as a domain module.

## Evaluation implications
Report precision *and* recall (and false-positive/over-blocking cost); compare against commercial baselines cautiously, since in-distribution self-built test sets and differing taxonomies bias results; include obfuscation/adaptive-evasion tests before claiming robustness.

## Deployment implications
Targeted at regulated financial GenAI deployments (compliance with GLBA/PCI DSS/ISO/NIST/OWASP); as an I/O filter it reduces disclosure risk but does not remove memorized data from the model, so it complements (not replaces) data-sanitization/DP training and access controls.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Flags risky inputs/outputs for logging and blocking; supports compliance auditing of SFI handling. Monitoring should track false negatives (undetected SFI) and false positives (over-blocking legitimate financial queries) over time and across new product terminology.

## Applicability boundaries
Core to LLM I/O guardrails but scoped to **financial sensitive-information disclosure** (a privacy/data-leakage guardrail), not general jailbreak/tool-use agent security. Its taxonomy and detector are finance-specific and English-centric; do not generalize the 0.98 recall to other domains, languages, or adaptive adversaries.
