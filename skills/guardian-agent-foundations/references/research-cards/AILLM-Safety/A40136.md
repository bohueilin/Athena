## Research question
How can deep multi-view clustering better handle view heterogeneity by explicitly decoupling shared vs. view-specific representations and adaptively (rather than via fixed paths/weights) distilling and aligning cross-view knowledge to improve clustering quality?

## Problem definition
View heterogeneity (differing sensing modalities, granularity, sampling) entangles shared semantics with view-specific structure. Prior distillation-based multi-view methods rely on predefined transfer paths or static fusion weights, poorly adapting to diverse view combinations and sample-level variation; distributional shifts across views further limit unified distillation. Goal: a hierarchical, data-driven alignment framework.

## System or model being studied
HOARD, a four-module deep clustering framework: (1) multi-view feature disentanglement into shared z and specific s via view-specific + shared encoders/decoders (reconstruction loss L_rec + orthogonality loss L_ort); (2) shared-feature alignment via granular-ball contrastive learning; (3) cross-view discrepancy mitigation via prototype collaborative transmission (differentiable K-means prototypes + Sinkhorn optimal transport + MMD); (4) an Information Distillation Unit (IDU) modeling inter-view transfer as a learned weighted directed graph, plus attention-based fusion producing the final clustering representation.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a defense. The method: representation decoupling + granular-ball contrastive alignment of shared features + prototype-level optimal-transport alignment of specific features (with momentum prototype smoothing) + dynamic-graph information distillation (IDU: L_dis = ||W⊙E||_1 + ||w̄−a||²) + attention fusion into a unified representation H = MLP([Z‖S]). Overall objective L = L_dec + λ1 L_sha + λ2 L_spe.
