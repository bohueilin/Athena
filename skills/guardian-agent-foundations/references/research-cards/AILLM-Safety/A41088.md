## Research question
How can decision uncertainty in LLMs be measured in morally complex (trolley-problem) scenarios, and what impact does that uncertainty have on human–LLM moral alignment? Can deliberately increasing uncertainty improve alignment with aggregate human moral preferences?

## Problem definition
LLMs increasingly participate in ethical/decision-making settings but tend to be overconfident, producing sharply peaked outputs even under moral ambiguity, which can distort alignment with human preferences. The paper frames "moral uncertainty" as an information-theoretic quantity over binarized moral choices and studies its relationship to human alignment.

## System or model being studied
32 open-source LLMs across 6 families (6 Llama-3 variants; 12 Qwen-2.5/3 variants; 6 Gemma-2/3 variants; 4 Phi-3.5/4 variants; 2 Vicuna; 2 Mistral), evaluated at default weights without fine-tuning. Decisions are elicited on binarized Moral Machine trolley scenarios; the intervention is attention-layer dropout applied at inference time (rates r ∈ {0.05, 0.1}).

## Trust assumptions
- Aggregate human preferences from the Moral Machine experiment (Awad et al. 2018) are taken as the alignment target / ground truth via AMCE conjoint analysis.
- Logit access to open-weight models is available and the first-token "1"/"2" softmax (summing to 0.984 ± 0.028) faithfully represents the binary decision.
- Attention dropout is a legitimate mechanism to inject stochasticity/uncertainty at inference.

## Attack or failure mechanism
The studied failure mode is overconfidence: models produce low-entropy, decisive choices in morally ambiguous scenarios, reducing sensitivity to input variation and to parameter variation (low mutual information I(x;θ|y)), which the authors link to poorer alignment with the spread of human moral judgments. Confidence is quantified as ∆p² = (2p−1)² and uncertainty as binary entropy H(p), decomposed into Total Entropy = Conditional Entropy + Mutual Information.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a defense against an adversary; rather a calibration intervention. Two contributions: (1) an information-theoretic uncertainty metric (binary entropy decomposed into TE/CE/MI over the binarized moral decision), and (2) inference-time "attention dropout" (dropout applied to the softmax-attention output) to deliberately raise uncertainty. Alignment is measured by L2 distance between the human AMCE preference vector δ_h (9 dims) and the model vector δ_m aggregated over 10,000 scenarios; negative ∆L2 = improved alignment.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- AlignmentSet: 10,000 randomly generated Moral Machine scenarios (per Takemoto 2024 setup), evaluated against Awad et al. 2018 human data.
- UncertaintySet: 9 moral dimensions × 1,000 scenarios = 9,000 scenarios (utilitarianism, age, fitness, gender, relation to AV, intervention, law, species, social status).
- Human preference ground truth: Moral Machine experiment survey data (Awad et al. 2018), AMCE via OLS conjoint analysis.

## Evaluation methodology
Binarize each dilemma to a "Case 1/2" choice by appending the assistant token "Case" and reading first-token logits; compute confidence, binary entropy, and TE/CE/MI across models and dimensions. Apply attention dropout at r ∈ {0, 0.05, 0.1} and measure ∆L2 alignment shift relative to r=0 baseline. Correlate MI changes with alignment-score changes.

## Main findings
- Variance in model confidence is greater across models than within moral dimensions — authors conclude moral (un)certainty is predominantly shaped by model architecture/training, not by the specific moral dimension. Gemma family tends to be highly confident; Llama family relatively less confident.
- Attention dropout increases total entropy, mainly by raising mutual information, while conditional entropy stays largely unchanged.
- Under evaluated conditions, dropout generally improved alignment (many negative ∆L2 in Table 1; e.g., Llama-3.1-70B 0.703→0.550 at r=0.10; Qwen3-8B 1.796→1.335); largest improvements at r=0.10. Authors report a correlation between MI increases and alignment improvements.
- Even large, advanced models (Llama3-70B, Gemma3-27B, Qwen3-32B) exhibit substantial uncertainty in morally sensitive scenarios.

## Negative results
- Dropout did not help uniformly: several models showed increased L2 (worse alignment), e.g., Llama-3.2-1B (+0.12 at r=0.10), Qwen3-32B (+0.04), Qwen2.5-14B (+0.11), Vicuna-7b (+0.03). Some Phi/Vicuna models were near-zero change.
- Authors caution that increased uncertainty also increases decision variability, which may be risky in critical scenarios (an explicit trade-off).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Increased uncertainty brings greater variability in decisions, posing risks in critical scenarios; managing this trade-off is left open.
- Open normative question: whether alignment to human consensus should be prioritized over "transcending human limitations" — the paper does not resolve whether mirroring human judgment is the right objective.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Alignment ground truth is aggregate Moral Machine survey data with known cultural/sampling skew; "improved alignment" means closer to that particular aggregate, not to any normative moral standard.
- Binarizing rich moral dilemmas to first-token "1"/"2" logits (0.984 mass, not 1.0) discards reasoning and multi-token behavior; results may not transfer to chat-style or tool-using deployments.
- Only open-weight models with logit access are studied; closed frontier models and RLHF-heavy chat models are excluded, limiting generality.
- Attention dropout is a blunt, global intervention; no per-scenario control, and its effect on general task capability/safety is not measured.
- No adversarial or robustness testing — this is a calibration study, not a security evaluation.

## Design implications
- Uncertainty is a first-class, measurable signal for morally/ethically loaded decisions; systems that treat LLM moral outputs as confident single answers ignore substantial intrinsic uncertainty. For guardian/gating designs: a low-entropy answer to a morally ambiguous prompt is not evidence of correctness.

## Implementation implications
- Binary-entropy / TE-CE-MI decomposition over constrained decision tokens is a cheap, single-forward-pass uncertainty estimate usable as a routing/escalation signal. Attention-dropout can perturb outputs to expose sensitivity, but is model-specific and can degrade determinism.

## Evaluation implications
- Confidence and alignment should be reported together; sampling strategy materially changes final decisions in ambiguous cases. Benchmarks that score only the argmax choice miss the uncertainty structure that predicts (mis)alignment.

## Deployment implications
- Deliberately reducing overconfidence can improve consensus-alignment but increases variability — a risk in high-stakes/critical decisions. Deployers should not use raised-uncertainty behavior where deterministic, auditable decisions are required.

## Monitoring and incident implications
- Track per-decision entropy/MI as a drift and reliability signal; spikes in confidence on ambiguous inputs, or high variability across resamples, are monitorable indicators of miscalibrated moral behavior.

## Applicability boundaries
_(where these findings should and should NOT be generalized)_
- This is an alignment/calibration study on binarized moral dilemmas, NOT an agent-security paper: it does not study prompt injection, tool abuse, jailbreak, poisoning, or any adversary. Its relevance to the Origin/Passport guardian stack is peripheral — chiefly as (a) an uncertainty-as-signal idea for gating and (b) a caution that LLM confidence ≠ correctness in value-laden decisions. - Moderate.
