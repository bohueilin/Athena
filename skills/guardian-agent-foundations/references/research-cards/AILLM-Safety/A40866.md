## Research question
Can jailbreak evaluation be made more accurate by replacing binary or one-size-fits-all multi-dimensional judgments with a scenario-adaptive framework that selects and weights evaluation dimensions per risk scenario, and quantifies harm severity rather than emitting only yes/no?

## Problem definition
Current jailbreak evaluation is fragmented: (1) binary methods (string matching, toxic-text classifiers, LLM judges) give only yes/no and miss severity; (2) emerging multi-dimensional frameworks (StrongREJECT, Security Violation/Informativeness/Relative Truthfulness, AttackEval) apply uniform criteria across scenarios, causing scenario-specific mismatches (e.g., "Relative Truthfulness" irrelevant to "hate speech"). This undermines comparability and harm quantification.

## System or model being studied
SceneJailEval — an agentic evaluation pipeline (a judge, not a victim model) with four modules: (1) Scenario Classifier (AgentSC maps a (query q, response r) pair to one of 14 scenarios); (2) Scenario-Dim Adapter (rule-based dynamic selection of relevant detection/harm dimensions and scenario-specific scoring rules and weights); (3) Jailbreak Detector (AgentJD scores 6 detection dimensions; final J via logical AND); (4) Harmfulness Evaluator (AgentHE scores harm dimensions, AHP-weighted into a harm score H).

## Threat model
- This is an evaluation/measurement framework, not an attack or runtime defense; there is no adversary against the evaluator modeled explicitly.
- Reviewer note: the implicit adversary is the jailbreak attacker whose outputs the framework must correctly classify; adaptive attacks against the judge (e.g., evaluator gaming) are not evaluated in the extracted text.
- Phase: post-hoc evaluation of model responses (offline red-team assessment / benchmarking).
- Digital / physical: digital (text content evaluation).

## Trust assumptions
Assumes trustworthy LLM judge agents (AgentSC, AgentJD, AgentHE) and expert panels for the Delphi/AHP weighting. Assumes an "Uncensored LLM" can be used to synthesize jailbreak responses for dataset construction. Trusts that a rulebase built from literature, regulations (EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF), and MLCommons AILuminate v1.0 captures scenario-appropriate criteria.

## Attack or failure mechanism
N/A — the paper targets a measurement failure mode (evaluation inaccuracy) rather than a security attack. The "failure" addressed is mis-scoring of jailbreak severity when uniform criteria are applied across heterogeneous scenarios (e.g., high false negatives in "Professional Advice" without a Risk-Warning dimension).

## Proposed defense or method
Scenario-adaptive evaluation: 14 scenarios (built on MLCommons AILuminate plus added "Regional Sensitive Issues", "Political Incitement and Elections", "Disinformation") and 10 evaluation dimensions. Six jailbreak-detection dimensions (Rejection, Helpfulness, Regional Compliance, Risk Warning, Political Neutrality, Explicitness) fused by logical AND. Harm dimensions weighted per scenario via Delphi-method ranking (CV/IQR consensus) + Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP eigenvector weights, consistency ratio CR<0.1). Extensible to custom scenarios/dimensions.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- A novel 14-scenario dataset (authors' contribution) with jailbreak-enhanced variants and region-specific cases, annotated via scenario-adaptive explicit rules; constructed using seed prompts, jailbreak enhancement, and an uncensored LLM.
- JBB (JailbreakBench, Chao et al. 2024) open-source dataset used as an external benchmark.

## Evaluation methodology
Compare SceneJailEval's jailbreak-status classification against SOTA evaluators on the authors' full-scenario dataset and on JBB, using F1 as the headline metric. (Detailed per-scenario tables truncated in extracted text.)

## Metrics
F1 score (jailbreak detection). Reported: 0.917 on the full-scenario dataset (+6% over SOTA) and 0.995 on JBB (+3% over SOTA). Harm severity is a separate weighted harm score H(q,r) (continuous), grounded conceptually in DREAD and CVSS threat-modeling analogies.

## Main findings
- Author claim: scenario-adaptive dimension selection/weighting outperforms uniform multi-dimensional and binary evaluators, reaching F1 0.917 (own dataset) and 0.995 (JBB).
- Author claim: the framework fills a benchmark gap for scenario-adaptive evaluation and supports extensibility for organization-specific compliance.
- Decomposing detection into explicit sub-tasks (e.g., "refusal + no valid info", "hidden helpful info") reduces false negatives on hard samples.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Reliance on LLM judge agents (AgentSC/JD/HE) means the evaluator inherits the biases and jailbreakability of its backbone; robustness of the judge to adversarial (evaluator-aware) responses is not shown in extract.
- Delphi/AHP weights are expert- and panel-dependent; reproducibility of weights across different expert pools is uncertain and could encode regional/institutional bias.
- Logical-AND fusion of six detection dimensions may raise false negatives if any single sub-agent errs; the trade-off is not quantified in the extract.
- 14 scenarios are grounded in specific 2024-era regulations (EU AI Act, NIST); scenario taxonomy may drift as policy evolves.
- Headline F1 gains (+6%, +3%) are relative to an unspecified-in-extract SOTA set; comparability depends on annotation ground-truth quality of the authors' own dataset.

## Design implications
Safety evaluation is context-dependent: harm criteria and their weights should be conditioned on the risk scenario. A gate/verifier for agent outputs should not apply a single rubric across all content types — it should route to scenario-appropriate checks (the Models-propose / Environments-verify / Gates-decide lens: this is a richer verifier).

## Implementation implications
Multi-dimensional, sub-task-decomposed judging (refusal vs hidden-help, risk-warning presence, explicitness gradation) can be implemented as composable agent checks fused by policy. Extensibility hooks let organizations add compliance dimensions (e.g., regional taboo lists) without retraining.

## Evaluation implications
Reporting jailbreak "ASR" without a scenario-adaptive, severity-quantifying judge risks both false negatives (hidden help) and false positives (benign professional advice). This framework is a candidate standardized judge for the attack papers in this corpus.

## Deployment implications
As a moderation/eval layer, it could support graded responses (harm score) rather than binary block/allow, enabling policy-tiered handling. Trust in the deployed judge depends on securing the judge agents themselves.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Provides audit-friendly, dimension-level rationales and a continuous harm score suitable for logging and triage severity ranking. Enables trend monitoring per scenario (e.g., rising "Disinformation" or "Regional Sensitive" jailbreaks).

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to text-content jailbreak evaluation/harm quantification for LLMs. Should NOT be treated as a runtime attack defense, a guarantee of correct labeling, or as validated against evaluator-gaming adversaries (untested in extract). Its scenario taxonomy is tuned to specific regulatory frameworks and may not transfer to other jurisdictions unchanged.
