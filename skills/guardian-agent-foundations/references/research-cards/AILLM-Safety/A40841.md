## Research question
Do state-of-the-art Video LLMs reliably surface harmful content that is clearly visible in a video, and if not, which architectural design choices cause them to *omit* it from generated summaries — and can that omission be triggered adversarially?

## Problem definition
Users increasingly "watch-and-read": skim video while trusting the VideoLLM's textual summary to flag anything harmful. If the model omits visible harmful content, viewers get no warning (a "semantic blind spot"), letting harmful videos spread. The paper characterizes this omission vulnerability and roots it in three compounding design flaws.

## System or model being studied
Five representative VideoLLMs: LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2, LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B-DPO, LLaVA-NeXT-Video-32B-Qwen, VideoLLaMA2, ShareGPT4Video. Generic pipeline studied: visual encoder (SigLIP/BLIP-2/EVA-CLIP) → projector (MLP / cross-attention / Q-Former) → pretrained LLM backbone (LLaMA/Vicuna/Qwen). A probing experiment compares LLaVA-Video-7B-Qwen2 vs. its standalone SigLIP encoder.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: embed harmful content (violence, crime, pornography) in a video such that the VideoLLM's summary omits/does not mention it, while it remains clearly visible to humans.
- Adversary knowledge: strict zero-query black-box — no knowledge of architecture, weights, training data, sampling rate, token-compression, or fusion scheme; cannot query the model for optimization. Only prior = the three known architectural flaws.
- Adversary access: ability to edit/insert self-sourced harmful clips into the input video.
- Adversary capabilities: frame replacement, corner picture-in-picture insertion, transparent overlay — constrained so harmful content stays human-visible (no single-frame flashes or imperceptible perturbations).
- Attacker budget: zero-query, training-free, real-time (no per-video adaptation).
- Phase: inference / deployment.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted omission of specific harmful content.
- Digital / physical: digital video manipulation.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive (no query feedback); exploits static structural flaws.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes deployments rely on the VideoLLM summary as a harmfulness gate for casually-skimmed video. Assumes typical efficiency-driven design: sparse uniform frame sampling, aggressive per-frame token compression, and standard modality fusion — all of which the attacks exploit.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Three root-cause flaws (Figure 2): **Flaw 1 Sparse Uniform Sampling** — only ~8/16/32 frames sampled, leaving large temporal gaps; abrupt harmful frames also dampened by frequency aliasing. **Flaw 2 Token Under-Sampling** — per-frame visual tokens compressed (e.g., 14×14 → 7×7, ~25% retained) via bilinear interpolation / average pooling, discarding small peripheral (corner) regions; high-frequency patch signals suppressed as a low-pass effect. **Flaw 3 Modality Fusion Imbalance** — after projection, visual tokens are under-utilized and linguistic priors dominate decoding, so even encoded harmful cues are ignored (validated by probing: standalone SigLIP detects harmful videos far better than the full VideoLLM).
Three matched **zero-query black-box attacks**: **FRA (Frame-Replacement, exploits Flaws 1,3)** overwrite a short random segment with a harmful clip that falls between sampled frames; **PPA (Picture-in-Picture, Flaws 2,3)** embed a visible harmful patch in a frame corner that compression discards; **TOA (Transparent-Overlay, Flaw 3)** overlay a transparent harmful clip on every frame that fusion suppresses.

## Proposed defense or method
None implemented — the paper is a vulnerability/attack study. It calls for denser/adaptive temporal sampling, finer spatial token retention, and more balanced cross-modal fusion that "guarantee semantic coverage rather than speed alone" (recommendations, not evaluated defenses).

## Datasets and benchmarks
- Harmful content categories: violence, crime, pornography (self-sourced clips inserted into benign source videos).
- Probing experiment: 100 benign + 100 harmful examples per category for binary detection accuracy (encoder vs. full model).
- Benign source videos + harmful insert clips; benchmark construction described in truncated section. (Violence-detection references cited.)

## Evaluation methodology
Large-scale evaluation of the three attacks against five VideoLLMs across the three harmful categories, with hyperparameters set so harmful content stays clearly visible/recognizable to humans. Unified metric: Harmfulness Omission Rate (HOR). Plus a visual-probing comparison (SigLIP encoder vs. full VideoLLM) to isolate Flaw 3.

## Metrics
- **Harmfulness Omission Rate (HOR)** — percentage of harmful clips that pass unmentioned (higher = worse safety).
- Reported average HORs: FRA — violence 99%, crime 91%, pornography 100%; PPA — 98% / 87% / 76%; TOA — 93% / 82% / 93%. Author summary: omission exceeds 90% in most cases.
- Probing: full VideoLLM shows a significant detection-accuracy drop vs. standalone SigLIP on all three harmful categories (comparable only on benign videos).

## Main findings
- Author claim: SOTA VideoLLMs almost always omit clearly-visible harmful content; even when harmful content is present in all frames the models consistently fail to identify it.
- Three architectural flaws jointly cause omission; each attack isolates/combines specific flaws with high HOR.
- Modality fusion actively suppresses visual harmful signals that the encoder already captures (probing evidence).
- First systematic safety analysis of VideoLLMs and of this omission vulnerability.

## Negative results
- Pornography under PPA has the lowest omission (76%), i.e., corner-patch attacks are less effective for that category — the only sub-90% average cell, indicating attack efficacy varies by content type/flaw.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Only open-source VideoLLMs evaluated; no commercial/streaming VideoLLM (e.g., GPT-4o video, Gemini) tested, so generalization to production content-moderation systems is unverified.
- No defense is implemented or measured; recommendations (denser sampling, better fusion) are untested for efficacy or utility/latency cost.
- HOR depends on the prompt used to query for harmfulness and on the harmful-clip corpus; robustness across prompts/detectors is not fully shown in the extracted text.
- Ethical/dual-use: publishes reliable evasion techniques for video safety filters; mitigations remain aspirational.
- The zero-query setting is realistic, but the attacks assume the ability to edit the uploaded video, which may not hold where platforms re-encode/re-sample uniformly on their own.

## Design implications
Safety-critical VideoLLMs cannot treat frame sampling and token compression as purely efficiency knobs — they are safety-coverage decisions. Fusion should not let linguistic priors override encoded visual harm signals; the encoder already "knows" more than the fused model reports.

## Implementation implications
Content-moderation pipelines should not rely on a single VideoLLM summary as the harmfulness gate; consider (a) denser/adaptive/keyframe sampling with saliency toward anomalous frames, (b) preserving peripheral spatial tokens, and (c) auxiliary use of the standalone visual encoder's harmful-content signal (which outperformed the fused model).

## Evaluation implications
Video-safety evaluation must test *omission* (visible-but-unreported harm), not just refusal, using a coverage metric like HOR; adversarial insertion (temporal/spatial/overlay) should be standard in red-team suites. Encoder-vs-fused probing is a reusable diagnostic for fusion suppression.

## Deployment implications
Any platform using VideoLLM auto-summaries to flag harmful video is exposed to high-omission-rate evasion; harmful videos can "slip by unchallenged." Defense-in-depth (dedicated video guardrail models, encoder-level detectors, human review) is needed rather than trusting the summary.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Discrepancy between a standalone visual-encoder harmful-content score and the VideoLLM summary is a strong runtime signal that content is being suppressed by fusion — a candidate detector/alarm. Log sampled-frame coverage to reveal exploitable temporal gaps.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to VideoLLM (video+text) summarization/moderation using sparse sampling + token compression. Does NOT directly cover image-only VLMs, text LLMs, or audio; does not address adaptive white-box attacks or physical-camera capture. HOR figures are specific to the five open models, three harmful categories, and human-visible insertion regime tested.
