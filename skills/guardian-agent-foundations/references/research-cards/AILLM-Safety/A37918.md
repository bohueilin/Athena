## Research question
Can non-IID feature-distribution shifts across medical centers in federated learning (FL) be mitigated purely from a data-distribution perspective — by aligning clients' stain distributions — without sharing raw privacy-sensitive histopathology images and without incurring the privacy-leakage risk of training a generative model on raw data?

## Problem definition
In FL over histopathology images from multiple hospitals, differing staining protocols and scanners create inter-client feature distribution shifts (Pi(x|y) != Pj(x|y)), degrading the global model. Prior fixes work via optimization or normalization; distribution-level alignment (e.g., CCST) exists but harms structural detail. FedSDA aligns stain distributions while (a) preserving tissue structure and (b) avoiding raw-data generative training, which prior work shows leaks private data.

## System or model being studied
FedSDA: (1) stain separation (Vahadane) decomposes each image into a stain matrix w (3x2) and density map h; (2) a lightweight single-layer transformer diffusion model G is trained via FedAvg on stain matrices (not raw images) to model the collective target stain distribution, giving each client access to others' stains without sharing images; (3) a stain-alignment step reconstructs images toward the aligned stain distribution to reduce non-IID shift. Classifier fθ is then trained on aligned data.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes FedAvg-style FL where raw images stay local. Server aggregates model updates. The design assumes that sharing/aggregating only stain-matrix-trained diffusion parameters (low-dimensional) leaks less than raw-image generative training; this is asserted from prior work, not proven here. No secure aggregation, DP noise, or TEE is used.

## Attack or failure mechanism
The mechanism motivating the design (from cited literature): training a diffusion model directly on raw data via FedAvg exposes gradients that can be inverted (DataSteal) or used for membership inference (FedMIA), reconstructing sensitive patient tissue images. FedSDA sidesteps this by never training the generative model on raw pixels — only on 3x2 stain matrices.

## Proposed defense or method
Not framed as a formal privacy defense. The privacy-relevant design choice is data minimization: fit the diffusion model on stain matrices rather than raw histopathology images, reducing the sensitive surface exposed to the FL generative-training channel while still enabling cross-client stain alignment for non-IID mitigation.

## Main findings
Author claims: FedSDA improves both optimization-focused baselines and data-distribution baselines on non-IID histopathology classification, while preserving structural detail better than CCST and normalization methods, and requires no extra test-time processing. Training the diffusion model on stain matrices keeps overhead minimal.

## Negative results
CCST fails to preserve structural information (motivating this work); normalization methods collapse stain diversity to a single feature. No self-reported negative results for FedSDA in the extracted text.

## Design implications
Data minimization at the FL generative-training boundary (share/learn a compact, less-identifying representation — here stain matrices — instead of raw sensitive data) is a useful pattern for reducing the leakage surface. Relevant to any FL/agent-memory system that trains shared generative components: prefer low-dimensional, task-sufficient statistics over raw records.

## Implementation implications
If deploying, pair such minimization with an actual privacy mechanism (secure aggregation, DP noise) and validate empirically against gradient-inversion/MIA — minimization alone is not a proof of protection.

## Evaluation implications
A security-grade version of this work would report attack-success curves (MIA AUC, reconstruction PSNR/SSIM under inversion) against the cited attacks, ideally with adaptive attackers aware of the stain-matrix design.

## Deployment implications
For multi-hospital medical FL: reduces (but does not eliminate) raw-data exposure; regulatory contexts (HIPAA/GDPR-style) would still require formal privacy guarantees and DPIA, which this paper does not provide.

## Monitoring and incident implications
No logging/attestation/provenance mechanisms proposed. Any real deployment should monitor the FL communication channel for inversion attempts and version the shared diffusion model for rollback.
