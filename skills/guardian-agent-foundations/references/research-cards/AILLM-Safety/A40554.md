## Research question
Can a black-box adversary jailbreak safety-aligned LLMs more effectively and efficiently by adaptively *sequencing and fusing* multiple disguise strategies (rather than applying a single static strategy or a fixed strategy combination)?

## Problem definition
Existing black-box jailbreaks either use static hand-crafted prompts (brittle, easily detected), single-strategy automated refinement (PAIR, TAP — high query cost, limited adaptivity), or fixed/arbitrary multi-strategy sequences. None dynamically adapt the *order* of strategy application to real-time attack feedback against the target. The paper seeks a diverse strategy pool plus an adaptive selection mechanism.

## System or model being studied
Attack framework MAJIC operating against black-box target LLMs via API only. Auxiliary components: MProxy = LLaMA3-8B-Instruct (simulates aligned behavior for offline matrix initialization), MAttacker = Mistral-7B (helpful-inclined, generates/fuses disguised prompts), MJudge = GPT-4o (harmfulness/success judgment). Target models evaluated: five state-of-the-art models including GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-flash, Gemini, GPT, and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (open- and closed-source).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: elicit harmful/policy-violating content from aligned LLMs (targeted harmful behaviors).
- Adversary knowledge: black-box — API access only, no gradients, parameters, or architecture.
- Adversary access: input prompts and output responses over the API.
- Adversary capabilities: run local auxiliary LLMs (proxy, attacker, judge); iteratively query the target; adapt strategy transition probabilities online.
- Attacker budget: author claim — over 90% ASR with fewer than 15 queries per attempt on average; matrix initialization is a one-time offline cost that does not add to the target query budget.
- Phase: inference / deployment (attacks a deployed, aligned model).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific harmful queries from StrongReject).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: adaptive — Q-learning-inspired online update of the Markov transition matrix.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the adversary can freely operate uncensored/helpful auxiliary models locally (Mistral-7B not safety-aligned) and can obtain reliable success signals from a judge LLM (GPT-4o). Assumes the target exposes an interactive API without aggressive rate limiting or multi-turn anomaly detection.

## Attack or failure mechanism
1. **Disguise Strategy Pool**: refines existing strategies into three categories — Contextual Assumption (philosophical dilemmas, historical analogies, futuristic contexts), Linguistic Obfuscation (leetspeak + euphemism combinations, technical jargon, multilingual, emoji), Role-Playing Framing (security/compliance scenarios) — plus two novel strategies: Semantic Inversion (rewrite query to opposite/positive, answer it, then conceptually reverse) and Literary Disguise (poetry/fable/narrative framing).
2. **Markov formulation**: each state = application of a strategy; strategy selection is a Markov chain. An empirical attack-score matrix A (Ai,j = success rate of strategy j after failure of strategy i) is estimated offline using MProxy over 50 curated StrongReject queries, then Softmax-normalized (temperature T) into transition matrix M.
3. **Dynamic adaptation**: during the live attack, the next strategy is sampled from M and fused by MAttacker; a Q-learning-inspired update Mij_new = Mij + α[r + γ·maxk(Mjk) − Mij] adapts transition probabilities from observed outcomes.

## Proposed defense or method
None — this is an offensive/red-teaming contribution. (Reviewer note: MAJIC functions as an automated red-teaming generator; the authors position it as state-of-the-art attack, not a defense.)

## Datasets and benchmarks
- Local matrix-initialization set: 50 de-duplicated, representative harmful queries curated from StrongREJECT (Souly et al. 2024).
- Evaluation: harmful-query benchmarks against five SOTA LLMs; HarmBench (Mazeika et al. 2024) referenced. Full per-benchmark tables are in the truncated body (not fully in extracted text).

## Evaluation methodology
Black-box attacks on five models; success adjudicated by MJudge = GPT-4o. Comparison against existing black-box baselines (PAIR, TAP, PAP, ReNeLLM, AutoDAN-turbo, etc.). Metrics: attack success rate (ASR) and query count per attempt.

## Metrics
- Attack Success Rate (ASR): author claim over 90% in most cases.
- Query efficiency: fewer than 15 queries per attempt on average.
- Claimed advantage even against Claude-3.5-Sonnet (noted for robust alignment).

## Main findings
- Author claim: MAJIC significantly outperforms existing black-box jailbreak methods on GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-flash, exceeding 90% ASR with <15 queries.
- Adaptive Markov sequencing plus a diverse disguise pool yields higher effectiveness and lower query cost than single-strategy or fixed-combination attacks.
- The refined+novel strategy pool alone already beats most baselines (contribution 1); the Markov chain + Q-learning further improves efficacy/efficiency (contribution 2).

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Success is judged solely by GPT-4o (MJudge); judge bias / false positives are a known confound for jailbreak ASR and are not independently validated in the extracted text.
- No adaptive-defender evaluation: MAJIC is not tested against models augmented with dedicated jailbreak defenses (e.g., self-reminder, decoding-time steering, input filtering); ASR may drop against such deployments.
- Reliance on an uncensored helper (Mistral-7B) and a strong judge (GPT-4o) assumes adversary resources that may not generalize; transferability of the learned Markov matrix across model families is asserted but the supporting tables are truncated here.
- Ethical/dual-use: the paper publishes an operational recipe for high-success jailbreaks; mitigations are not proposed.

## Design implications
Defenses should assume adversaries compose and re-order disguise strategies adaptively; single-pattern detectors and static blocklists are insufficient. Alignment training should cover compositional/multi-strategy disguises (semantic inversion, literary framing) rather than isolated templates.

## Implementation implications
Guardrails at the API boundary should track *multi-attempt* behavior (sequences of near-miss disguised prompts) rather than scoring prompts independently; per-session anomaly and query-budget monitoring would raise the cost of the online Q-learning loop MAJIC depends on.

## Evaluation implications
Red-team suites should include adaptive strategy-sequencing attacks and report query efficiency, not just single-shot ASR. Judge-model choice materially affects reported ASR; use multiple judges.

## Deployment implications
Even strongly aligned models (Claude-3.5-Sonnet cited) are vulnerable under adaptive black-box composition; deployments handling high-risk domains should layer runtime output filtering and rate limiting rather than rely on alignment alone.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Log and correlate multi-turn/multi-attempt disguise patterns; sudden clustering of semantically-inverted or literary-framed prompts against the same intent is a candidate detection signal. The offline-init + online-adapt structure means detection windows exist during the adaptation phase.

## Applicability boundaries
Findings apply to text-only black-box LLM jailbreaking via chat APIs. They do NOT directly address multimodal (vision/video) attack surfaces, white-box optimization, or tool-using agent pipelines. ASR figures are conditioned on the specific target versions and the GPT-4o judge; generalization to other judges/versions is asserted but not fully evidenced here.
