## Research question
Can a modality-aware graph transformer with a global (Gram-based) distribution constraint improve multi-modal entity alignment (MMEA) by capturing within-modality structural context and enforcing cross-modal distribution consistency, reducing interference from shallow look-alike features?

## Problem definition
MMEA identifies equivalent entities across two multi-modal knowledge graphs (MMKGs). Two challenges: (1) intra-modal contrastive methods ignore global distributional differences across modalities; (2) shallow visual/attribute features of different entities can look similar (e.g., two actresses), causing interference; structural context is under-used.

## System or model being studied
MyGram: (a) multi-modal feature extraction (RRGAT for structure; BOW+FC for relations/attributes; VGG/pretrained encoder for images), (b) a modality-aware graph convolutional diffusion module capturing multi-hop neighborhood context plus a Transformer with multi-head cross-modal attention, and (c) a Gram Loss that minimizes the volume (Gramian determinant) of a 4-D parallelotope formed by structural, visual, attribute, and relation embeddings, combined with an InfoNCE contrastive loss.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes benign MMKG inputs; some entities lack images (handled with random-initialized visual vectors). No trust boundary or authorization concept.

## Attack or failure mechanism
No attack. "Interference" is benign feature ambiguity: entities with similar shallow visual/attribute features are hard to distinguish; the method leverages structure to disambiguate.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a security defense. Gram-based distribution alignment (geometric volume regularizer) enforces holistic cross-modal coherence; modality diffusion learning enriches modality-specific features with structural context; top-K candidate selection + sparse contrastive loss over parallelotope volumes for alignment.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Cross-KG: FB15K-DB15K (FBDB15K), FB15K-YAGO15K (FBYG15K). Bilingual: DBP15K. Training seeds: 20%/50%/80% for cross-KG, 30% for bilingual. Five public datasets total (per abstract).

## Evaluation methodology
Standard MMEA retrieval: for each source entity, retrieve the equivalent target entity from the full candidate set; report Hits@k. Ablations and low-alignment-rate / real-world settings referenced (RQ1–RQ5). Numeric ablation tables truncated in this excerpt.

## Metrics
Hits@1 (primary). Author-reported maximum improvements: +4.8% on FBDB15K, +9.9% on FBYG15K, +4.3% on DBP15K over baselines.

## Main findings
Author claim: MyGram outperforms baseline MMEA models on all five datasets, with the Hits@1 gains above; structural context + Gram global constraint improve robustness and alignment accuracy.

## Negative results
