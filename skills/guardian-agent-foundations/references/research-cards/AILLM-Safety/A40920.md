## Research question
Can a comprehensive, fine-grained, human-validated risky-prompt benchmark better expose the safety limits of text-to-image (T2I) models, their internal/external defenses, filters, and jailbreak attacks than existing NSFW-only datasets?

## Problem definition
Existing risky-prompt datasets suffer three deficiencies: (1) limited risk categories (mostly pornography/violence/disturbing), (2) coarse-grained annotation relying on automated moderators without human validation, and (3) low prompt effectiveness (ambiguous prompts that only produce risky images by chance). This undermines reliable safety evaluation of T2I models.

## System or model being studied
T2I generative models (SD1.4, PixArt, SDXL, FLUX, CogView4, SD3, Janus Pro, HiDream — eight open-source models) plus nine internal defenses (concept erasing / fine-tuning / inference-guidance: NP, SLD, Safree, UCE, RECE, SPEED, SafetyDPO, MACE, TRCE), five safety filters, and five jailbreak attack strategies. The authors also build a reason-driven risky-image detector aligning a 3B MLLM to human risk-rationale annotations.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: elicit risky images (pornography, violence, disturbing, illegal, copyright-infringing, politically sensitive) from T2I models; bypass safety filters/defenses.
- Adversary knowledge: mixed; benchmark evaluates black-box jailbreak strategies against deployed filters/defenses.
- Adversary access: prompt-level input to T2I pipeline.
- Adversary capabilities: crafting/paraphrasing risky prompts; five jailbreak methods evaluated (not all enumerated in extracted text; metaphor-based and perception-guided jailbreaks referenced).
- Attacker budget: not quantified per-attack in extracted text.
- Phase: inference / deployment.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific risk categories).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: benchmark is a static evaluation harness; attacks are pre-defined (non-adaptive to the specific defense in the reported protocol).

## Trust assumptions
Assumes GPT-4o + human annotators produce accurate category and risk-reason labels; assumes SD3/FLUX generations are representative for validity filtering; assumes the proposed MLLM detector's judgments approximate human risk perception.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Risky prompts drive T2I models to render risky visual elements. Static classifier/blacklist filters generalize poorly to unseen risk and evolving policies; single filters miss broad risk coverage; internal concept-erasure defenses fail to cover multiple risk types simultaneously and degrade benign fidelity (FID/CLIP-S). Jailbreak strategies bypass existing safety strategies.

## Proposed defense or method
(1) Hierarchical risk taxonomy: 6 primary categories, 14 subcategories, derived from seven platform/company usage policies. (2) Six-stage data pipeline: collection, GPT-4o polishing, diversity filtering (CLIP-score threshold 0.8), GPT-4o+human category annotation, validity filtering (SD3/FLUX generation cross-check), manual risk-reason annotation → 6,432 prompts, 20,792 risky images. (3) Reason-driven risky-image detector: aligns an MLLM (3B) with detailed human risk-rationale annotations.

## Datasets and benchmarks
New: T2I-RiskyPrompt (6,432 prompts / 20,792 images). Prompts partly incorporated from I2P, UnsafeDiffusion, T2VSafetyBench, SafeSora; compared against T2ISafety. Copyright/illegal prompts synthesized via GPT-4o.

## Evaluation methodology
Risk ratio = proportion of prompts producing at least one risky image (2 images/prompt). Defenses grouped into NSFW / copyright / political; FID and CLIP-S measure benign performance degradation. Detector accuracy compared to prior detectors.

## Metrics
Risk ratio; PPL (semantic clarity, 86 for this dataset vs 2,613 for T2ISafety); prompt effectiveness (0.741, highest among compared); FID↓; CLIP-S↑; detector accuracy (91.8% with a 3B MLLM).

## Main findings
As generative capability increases, associated safety risk becomes more pronounced (stronger models generate risky content more readily). Existing internal defenses struggle to defend multiple risk types simultaneously and trade off benign fidelity. Single safety filters fail to cover the breadth of risk. Five jailbreak methods effectively bypass existing safety strategies. Nine key insights reported; authors' detector outperforms existing detectors at 91.8% accuracy using only a 3B MLLM.

## Negative results
No single evaluated defense or filter achieves broad, simultaneous coverage across all 14 risk categories; several concept-erasure defenses cause notable FID/CLIP-S degradation (e.g., MACE) or fail on some risk groups (missing values for copyright/political columns).

## Limitations stated by the authors
Not fully enumerated in the extracted text; authors position the detector as requiring carefully crafted safety policies (MLLM-filter caveat) and note inherent randomness of T2I models (mitigated by 2 images/prompt).

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_ Risk labels depend on GPT-4o + human judgment and a single MLLM detector as ground truth for risk ratio, creating measurement circularity; effectiveness/validity filtering uses only SD3/FLUX, potentially biasing prompts toward those models; only two images per prompt undersamples generation stochasticity; the five attack strategies are not adaptively re-optimized against each defense; copyright/political categories embed jurisdiction-specific and culturally contingent judgments; releasing 6,432 effective risky prompts is itself a dual-use asset.

## Design implications
Safety evaluation for generative models should be reason-grounded and human-validated, not filter-labeled; taxonomies must span copyright and political-sensitivity, not only NSFW. Fine-grained risk-reason annotation enables interpretable detectors — relevant to evidence-logging and auditable safety gates.

## Implementation implications
Deploying a single static filter is insufficient; layered detection (input filter + reason-aware output detector) and multi-category coverage are needed. MLLM-based zero-shot filters need carefully engineered safety policies.

## Evaluation implications
Prompt effectiveness and semantic clarity (PPL) materially affect measured safety; benchmarks with ambiguous prompts overstate randomness-driven risk. Reporting benign-fidelity degradation (FID/CLIP-S) alongside risk ratio is essential to avoid rewarding over-suppression.

## Deployment implications
Stronger T2I models raise safety stakes; providers should assume filters and concept-erasure defenses have coverage gaps and remain bypassable by jailbreaks; content moderation should combine multiple signals.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Reason-annotated detection supports explainable flagging and audit trails; category-level risk ratios provide a monitoring dashboard for regression testing after model/policy updates.

## Applicability boundaries
Findings apply to T2I/multimodal generative safety and content moderation. They do NOT directly transfer to agentic/tool-use or prompt-injection threat models, credential handling, or autonomous-agent security; the paper is about generative-content risk, not agent action authorization. Relevant to Origin/Passport only as a multimodal-safety evaluation and evidence-logging analogue.
