## Research question
*Why* does "shallow alignment" (safety modifications concentrated in early response-token positions) emerge during safety-alignment training, how can incomplete alignment be *detected* at fine granularity, and how can the undertrained later positions be *completed* without full retraining?

## Problem definition
Safety-aligned LLMs remain vulnerable to adversarial attacks, fine-tuning degradation, and context/prefix manipulation. Prior work (Qi et al. 2024) empirically observed that alignment mostly changes early token positions while later positions stay close to the base model, but did not explain the mechanistic cause or provide a detector for *where* learning is incomplete.

## System or model being studied
Open-weight instruction-tuned LLMs and their base counterparts: Llama-3.1-8B / Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Llama-2-7B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Qwen-3-8B-Instruct. Studied at the level of position-wise next-token distributions (aligned vs. base) during autoregressive generation.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: bypass refusal and force harmful generation past safety guardrails (and/or degrade safety via fine-tuning).
- Adversary knowledge: white/gray-box in the analysis (needs logits/hidden states of base+aligned models for the detection/decoding method); the *attacks* modeled include black-box-style prefill/prefix injection.
- Adversary access: ability to supply a harmful instruction x ∈ X_harmful plus a bypass response prefix y_<t ∈ Y_bypass (prefill attack), or to fine-tune the model (harmful fine-tuning degradation).
- Adversary capabilities: adversarial safety context = (x, y_<t) that exploits incomplete later-position alignment; also LoRA/fine-tuning-based safety erosion.
- Attacker budget: validation uses 4-token prefill attacks; main experiments use full targeted-sequence prefills (strongest condition).
- Phase: inference (prefill attacks) and training/post-training (fine-tuning degradation and the defense itself).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (elicit specific harmful content).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: attacks are standard (prefill, LoRA fine-tuning); the defense is evaluated against these but not against an attacker adapting specifically to base-favored-token penalties.

## Trust assumptions
The mechanistic account assumes autoregressive cross-entropy training inherently concentrates gradient signal at early positions (shorter dependency chains, context-dependency asymmetry) and decays it at later positions. The detector assumes access to both base and aligned model logits.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Gradient concentration + signal decay: early tokens receive stronger gradients (contribute to many downstream loss terms and have shorter dependency chains) while later tokens get weaker updates, so safety training *incompletely* transforms later-position distributions, which retain base-model preferences for formatting/punctuation/structure. Error accumulation (Error(t) = Σ_{i<t} ε_i·Influence(i→t)) lets early deviations compound; adversarial prefills push generation into later positions where alignment is weakest, "overwhelming" the residual safety signal.

## Proposed defense or method
- Base-favored tokens (Definition 2): vocabulary positions v where π_base(v|x,y_<t) > π_aligned(v|x,y_<t); a fine-grained indicator of incomplete alignment that aggregate KL cannot localize (Algorithm 1: TopK of logits_base − logits_aligned).
- Inference-time contrastive decoding (functional validation): subtract penalty(v)=α·(logit_base − logit_aligned) from logits at each step to suppress base-favored tokens, sustaining KL separation across positions.
- Targeted Learning Completion (training-time): adaptive L2 penalty on base-favored tokens in harmful contexts (Def 3) + hybrid-teacher distillation (teacher = λ·logits_aligned + (1−λ)·logits_base, λ=1.2) with temperature-scaled KL (τ=2.0). Total loss L_total = L_KL(student,teacher) + α_adaptive·L_completion.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- Attack/harmful: AdvBench (Zou et al. 2023), HEx-PHI (Qi et al. 2024), with adversarial prefixes.
- Benign/utility contrast: Databricks Dolly instructions test set.
- Utility benchmarks: ARC-E, ARC-C, BoolQ, HellaSwag, Winogrande, MMLU, ToxiGen, TriviaQA, TruthfulQA, GSM8K.
- Baselines for recovery: LoRA, Vaccine, SaLoRA, Safe LoRA.

## Evaluation methodology
Position-wise analysis of KL divergence and base-favored-token counts (harmful vs. benign) across generation steps. Functional validation via inference-time contrastive decoding (prefill-attack success + utility). "Deep alignment" evaluation of the training-time completion method across Llama/Qwen families (adversarial robustness + utility preservation). "Deep alignment recovery" after fine-tuning degradation vs. LoRA-based safety baselines (Eval Loss, Harmful Response Rate HRR, utility). A deliberative-reasoning-under-attack analysis (harmful vs. safe, with/without reasoning).

## Metrics
- Prefill attack success rate (%).
- Attack reduction across model families (author states 96–98%).
- HRR (Harmful Response Rate, lower better), Eval Loss.
- Utility: ARC-C, GSM8K, ToxiGen, TruthfulQA, MMLU, etc. (retention within ~1–2 points claimed).
- Position-wise KL divergence profile; base-favored token counts (e.g., 45 vs 30 at early positions, 35 vs 25 at later positions for adversarial vs benign).

## Main findings
- Author claim: shallow alignment is mechanistically caused by gradient concentration/signal decay in autoregressive training — a cause independent of SFT/RLHF/DPO choice.
- Author claim: base-favored tokens (predominantly formatting/structural tokens) localize undertrained regions that aggregate KL masks; adversarial contexts show systematically higher base-favored counts.
- Author claim: inference-time contrastive decoding cuts prefill-attack success from 47.5% → 0.2% on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct while preserving utility (Table 1).
- Author claim: training-time targeted completion yields large attack-rate reductions across Llama/Qwen families (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B 85.9→44.3 and Qwen-3-8B 96.1→16.4 on the reported harmful metric) with utility preserved; best-in-class HRR recovery after fine-tuning degradation vs. Vaccine/SaLoRA/Safe LoRA (Tables 3–4).
- Author claim: completion also increases "safe reasoning" responses under attack (37.8%→60.2%; 9→196 safe-reasoning responses), suggesting a link between alignment completeness and deliberative reasoning.

## Negative results
- Inference-time contrastive decoding, though highly effective, is noted as impractical at deployment scale (concurrent base+aligned loading, extra forward passes per step) — motivating the training-time variant.
- Some baselines (e.g., Vaccine) show utility degradation; the method's own numbers vary by family (Qwen-3-8B residual 16.4% harmful is far above the near-zero prefill result), i.e., not fully eliminated.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Future work should scale the methods to larger models and examine relationships to other safety dimensions; influence-score computation is O(t²). The mechanistic framework is presented as explanatory rather than a formal proof.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- The detector and contrastive decoding require access to a paired base model's logits — not available for closed/API models, limiting applicability to open-weight settings.
- Evaluation is on 7–8B models only; the "96–98%" headline aggregates specific metrics/attacks and should not be read as universal robustness.
- No adaptive adversary that optimizes against base-favored-token penalties is tested; robustness to attacks specifically targeting the completion mechanism is unknown.
- Reliance on "base-favored tokens" that are mostly formatting/structural tokens raises a question (not resolved in extracted text) of whether suppression could harm formatting/utility on longer generations beyond the reported benchmarks.
- ASR/harmful judging protocol details and grader reliability are partly in appendix; some table headers/rows were truncated in the extracted text.

## Design implications
Safety alignment should be evaluated and enforced *across all response positions*, not just the refusal prefix; position-wise distributional coverage is a design target. A base-vs-aligned logit gap is a usable, cheap signal for locating undertrained safety regions.

## Implementation implications
Two reusable levers: (1) inference-time contrastive suppression of base-favored tokens (strong but costly), and (2) training-time targeted L2 penalties + hybrid-teacher distillation to bake in the same effect. For open-weight deployments, the training-time variant avoids per-step overhead. Applicable to hardening against prefill/prefix jailbreaks and to *recovering* safety after (possibly benign) fine-tuning degradation.

## Evaluation implications
Report position-wise KL and base-favored-token profiles, not only aggregate refusal rates; include prefill/prefix attacks with full targeted sequences (strongest condition) and measure utility retention jointly. Fine-tuning-degradation recovery is a distinct axis worth benchmarking (vs. Vaccine/SaLoRA/Safe LoRA).

## Deployment implications
Because inference-time contrastive decoding roughly doubles compute and requires a second model, prefer the training-time completion for production; monitor for residual later-position vulnerability (the method reduces but does not eliminate harmful outputs, e.g., Qwen-3-8B residual). Pair with runtime refusal monitoring.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Prefill/prefix inputs and generations whose later-position distributions revert toward base-model formatting preferences are candidate incident signals; base-favored-token counts can serve as a runtime detector where a base model is available.

## Applicability boundaries
Core to LLM safety. Findings generalize to autoregressive, open-weight instruction-tuned LLMs at 7–8B scale under prefill/fine-tuning threat models. They should NOT be assumed to hold unchanged for much larger models, closed API models (no base logits), multimodal models, or against adaptive attackers targeting the completion mechanism. The mechanistic claim is explanatory, not formally proven.
