## Research question
Can a principled, inference-time, model- and task-agnostic defense reliably suppress adversarial/jailbreak
perturbations across both LLMs and VLMs without retraining or internal model access?

## Problem definition
Training-time defenses (robust fine-tuning, adversarial training, UNIGUARD) need full model access and retraining,
degrade clean performance, and are often infeasible (locked weights, unavailable data, API-only serving).
Inference-time defenses (image transforms, diffusion purification, detection like JailGuard/CIDER, input sanitization,
perplexity filtering, output moderation) are brittle, sample-isolated, and vulnerable to adaptive/OOD attacks. Prior
work suggests jailbreak attacks introduce structured low-rank distortions in embedding spectra — exploitable for
removal.

## System or model being studied
EigenShield, an inference-time defense that uses Random Matrix Theory (RMT) to separate signal-dominant ("causal")
subspaces from adversarial noise in high-dimensional embeddings, then projects inputs onto the causal subspace.
Evaluated on LLMs (GPT-2, LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-7B) and VLMs (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, MiniGPT-4,
InstructBLIP, Qwen2-VL, Florence-2-large).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: jailbreak LLMs/VLMs into emitting harmful/disallowed content via crafted text and/or image
  perturbations.
- Adversary knowledge: spans black-box jailbreaks to gradient/optimization attacks; includes adaptive attacks
  designed against the defense's assumptions.
- Adversary access: input manipulation (text tokens and/or adversarial images); the defender has no need for model
  weights (defense is model-agnostic, embedding-level).
- Adversary capabilities: GCG, TAP, AutoBreach (adaptive), plus multimodal PGD/FGSM/MIM/CW/Square perturbations; OOD
  and distribution-shift inputs.
- Attacker budget: various (optimization- and query-based); specifics per attack per prior work.
- Phase: inference/deployment (both attack and defense operate at inference).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted harmful-output elicitation.
- Digital / physical: digital (text + image embeddings).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: BOTH — explicitly evaluates adaptive attacks (GCG/TAP/AutoBreach) and notes the defense
  may incentivize stronger RMT-evasion attacks.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes clean semantic content aligns with dominant spectral directions (spiked-covariance model) and that adversarial
perturbations are largely orthogonal / spectrally separable from the causal subspace. Guarantees are asymptotic
(p,n→∞) under idealized separability; authors explicitly state practical effectiveness depends on how well real
representations meet these conditions and that under "critical alignment" the assumption can fail.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Jailbreaks manipulate text/image inputs so embeddings acquire adversarial spectral components. The defense's failure
case: if adversarial noise is NOT orthogonal to the causal subspace (spectral overlap / critical alignment),
projection cannot remove it — partial or no filtering. Over-filtering (high coverage γ) can also strip benign OOD
signal.

## Proposed defense or method
Two phases. Phase 1 (threshold selection): compute empirical covariance of clean embeddings; extract outlier
eigenvectors above the Marchenko–Pastur bulk; compute a Robustness-based Nonconformity Score (RbNS) per outlier
(lower = more stable); apply quantile thresholding τ* with coverage parameter γ (γ=0.75 used; swept 0.5–0.9) to
retain "causal" directions. Phase 2 (inference): for each input, eigen-decompose, keep directions above τ*, and
project the embedding onto the causal subspace (e_filtered = P_causal · e_input, P_causal = E_causal E_causalᵀ),
suppressing adversarial components while preserving semantics — no model modification. Theory: Theorems 1–3 and
corollaries give asymptotic subspace identifiability (BBP threshold), asymptotic orthogonality of causal vs
adversarial subspaces, and asymptotic noise annihilation under the stated assumptions.

## Datasets and benchmarks
LLMs: custom jailbreak prompts + RealToxicityPrompts. VLMs: HarmBench (with adversarial PGD) + RealToxicityPrompts
paired with adversarial images. Generalization/composite set: FGSM, PGD, MIM, CW, Square Attack. Adaptive attacks:
GCG, TAP, AutoBreach. OOD-style harmful inputs (Table 3).

## Evaluation methodology
Compare EigenShield vs No Defense and SOTA defenses — LLM: LLaMA-Guard, Erase-and-Check; VLM: Adversarial Training,
UNIGUARD, BlurKernel, Comp-Decomp, DIFFPURE, CIDER. Report ASR and Perspective-API content-harm sub-scores across
standard, adaptive, and OOD settings; sensitivity analysis on coverage γ.

## Metrics
Attack Success Rate (fraction of adversarial inputs eliciting harmful output; VLM ASR via Perspective-API harmful
indicator over the dataset) and five Perspective-API content-harm scores (identity attack, profanity, sexually
explicit, threat, toxicity), each in [0,1]; lower is better.

## Main findings
Author claims, with supporting tables: EigenShield achieves the lowest ASR and content-harm across all tested models.
Examples from Table 1 — GPT-2 ASR 82.34%→5.81% (vs LLaMA-Guard 30.14%, Erase-and-Check 25.53%); LLaVA-v1.5-7B ASR
81.61%→19.20% (best among VLM baselines). Aggregate: reduces ASR by up to 48% over SOTA defenses; lowers LLM jailbreak
ASR by up to 92.9% relative to undefended; VLM ASR by up to 76.5%; adaptive-attack ASR reduction up to 77.7%; OOD ASR
reduction up to 88.4% (LLM) / 80.4% (VLM). Both LLM and VLM embeddings show spiked spectral structure suitable for RMT.

## Negative results
Authors state guarantees hold only under idealized separability; under "critical alignment" (Appendix A.8) filtering
may be partial or fail. Over-filtering at high γ degrades benign OOD performance (γ is an ASR-vs-utility trade-off).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Robustness depends on spectral separability of causal vs adversarial directions; can fail under critical alignment.
- γ tuning trades ASR reduction against utility; over-filtering harms benign OOD inputs.
- The causal subspace is learned from data; spurious correlations/biases in that data can be encoded and preserved,
  potentially disadvantaging underrepresented groups / non-Western, multilingual contexts.
- May incentivize adaptive attacks designed to evade RMT projections; RbNS filtering is non-transparent, so diagnosing
  failures and ensuring auditability are open problems.
- Perspective API (their harm metric) has sociolinguistic/distributional biases, especially on marginalized dialects/
  OOD phrasing.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- Content-harm and VLM ASR both rely on Perspective API as the judge, so reported reductions inherit that classifier's
  blind spots; a single-judge evaluation may over- or under-state real safety.
- Requires a representative set of *clean* embeddings to estimate the covariance/causal subspace; quality and
  representativeness of that reference set is a hidden dependency not stressed in the tables.
- Asymptotic theorems (p,n→∞) are invoked for finite embeddings (p≈768–1024); the authors argue empirical robustness
  but the gap between guarantee and practice is real.
- No latency/throughput cost reported for per-input eigen-decomposition at serving scale.

## Design implications
A defense can operate purely at the embedding/representation layer, independent of model internals — attractive for
black-box/API-served frontier models where retraining is impossible. Spectral/statistical structure of activations is
a usable signal for separating semantics from adversarial noise. Design defenses as plug-in filters with a tunable
utility/robustness knob (γ).

## Implementation implications
For an agent guardrail stack, EigenShield-style projection can sit as an input-embedding sanitizer ahead of the model
across text and image modalities, no weight access needed. Requires a curated clean-embedding reference set to fit the
causal subspace, and a chosen γ. Because RbNS is opaque, pair it with logging for auditability.

## Evaluation implications
Robustness evaluations should span standard + adaptive (GCG/TAP/AutoBreach) + OOD/distribution-shift, across multiple
model families and both LLM and VLM; report per-harm-category scores, not just ASR; and disclose the judge classifier's
bias. Sensitivity to defense hyperparameters (γ) should be swept and reported.

## Deployment implications
Model-agnostic, no-retraining deployment is a strong operational fit for locked/proprietary models. But: assumption
failures (critical alignment), fairness risk from data-learned subspaces, opacity of RbNS, and unreported latency mean
it should be a *layer*, not a sole control. Maps to OWASP LLM jailbreak defense and multimodal input-validation.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Emit which/how-many eigen-directions were filtered per input and the RbNS margins as trace fields; sudden shifts in
spectral mass or inputs sitting near the causal/adversarial boundary are tamper/adaptive-attack indicators. Audit the
causal eigenstructure periodically for demographic bias; retain filtered-vs-unfiltered outputs for incident review.

## Applicability boundaries
**Core** agent-security relevance (inference-time jailbreak/adversarial defense for LLMs and VLMs). Generalizes to
embedding-accessible models across text/image. Should NOT be treated as providing worst-case guarantees: the
protection is asymptotic and conditional on spectral separability, is evaluated with a biased judge (Perspective API),
and does not address tool abuse, agent-to-agent, memory, or supply-chain threats.

## Evidence strength
Moderate (approaching Strong on breadth) — theoretically grounded, evaluated across many LLM and VLM families, against
adaptive (GCG/TAP/AutoBreach) and OOD settings, with visible ASR/harm tables and open code; held back from Strong by
asymptotic/idealized guarantees that can fail (critical alignment), reliance on a single biased judge (Perspective
API), a hidden dependence on a representative clean-embedding reference set, and no reported serving-cost analysis.
