## Research question
Can a single black-box attack bypass both built-in T2I safety filters AND external concept-removal defenses, without any privileged knowledge of the model, by exploiting cross-lingual character-level word recombination ("macaronic" words)?

## Problem definition
T2I models can generate NSFW/banned content. Existing attacks typically defeat only one defense type (either input filters or concept removal); those that defeat both usually need privileged internal knowledge (architecture/weights), limiting real-world black-box use. The gap: a unified, black-box attack against both defense families. Prior black-box attacks (PGJ, SurrogatePrompt) rely on synonym replacement and thus fail against text filters.

## System or model being studied
Target G: Stable Diffusion with safety filters and concept-removal variants (ESD, FMN, SLD variants, SafeGen, DUO, EAP, PromptGuard/ProG). Safety filter F may implement keyword matching, BERT-based NSFW classifiers, and latent-representation filtering (LatentGuard). Attacker constructs macaronic adversarial prompts; feedback via a pretrained NSFW detector / object classifier.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: generate harmful (NSFW / banned-object) images whose semantics match an original harmful prompt while the prompt passes the safety filter.
- Adversary knowledge: black-box — no access to internal parameters or architecture of F or G.
- Adversary access: query access — submit a prompt, observe returned image or a rejection signal.
- Adversary capabilities: identify sensitive words (blacklist + embedding similarity), enumerate cross-lingual translation candidates (up to L≈79 languages via an LLM), character-level slice/recombine into macaronic substitutes, iterate with zeroth-order optimization (ZOO) using NSFW-detector harmfulness score as feedback.
- Attacker budget: query-based iterative optimization; exact query counts truncated in extract.
- Phase: inference/deployment-time attack; no model modification.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific NSFW concept / banned object).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: adaptive to filter feedback (rejection + harmfulness score) but black-box.

## Trust assumptions
Attacker relies on an external LLM for multilingual translation and on a pretrained NSFW detector/object classifier (e.g., LAION CLIP-based NSFW detector) as its oracle. Assumes cross-lingual composed prompts trigger the same visual semantics as the English original in SD and its concept-removal variants (empirically observed, Fig. 1).

## Attack or failure mechanism
Root cause: SD tokenization is non-invertible for low-resource/non-Latin languages (ε(ε⁻¹(ε(v))) ≠ ε(v)), so token-level manipulation is infeasible, but character-level cross-lingual recombination can preserve visual semantics while diverging textual semantics enough to evade filters. Pipeline: (1) Sensitive-word identification (blacklist matching + cosine similarity of word embedding to harmful-concept embeddings above threshold τ). (2) Cross-lingual candidate selection — translate each sensitive word into L languages, insert into templates ("The image of people, <v>, natural." / "The image of <v>."), generate images, score by Harmfulness Score H (NSFW-detector class probability) and Visual Semantic Similarity CLIPSim; keep top-k (≈10). (3) Macaronic substitute construction — character-level slicing/recombination of candidates under parameterized control (β1, β2, α), optimized by ZOO against NSFW-detector feedback. Replace sensitive terms to form the adversarial prompt.

## Proposed defense or method
Offensive paper; no defense proposed. Authors call for cross-lingual adversarial robustness and defenses that generalize beyond monolingual/keyword-matching assumptions.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Harmful prompt categories: sex-related and violence-related NSFW, plus banned-object generation. Uses pretrained NSFW detector (LAION CLIP-based NSFW detector) and object classifiers (Animals-10, Stanford Cars/3D Object references, COCO). Exact prompt-set sizes truncated in extract. Concept-removal defenses evaluated: ESD, FMN, SLD (Max/Strong/Medium variants), SafeGen, DUO, EAP, ProG.

## Evaluation methodology

Measure filter-bypass rate, ASR by harmful category, and semantic similarity to the original harmful prompt. Qualitative masked-image examples across defenses shown. ## Metrics
- Filter bypass rate: up to 100% against major safety filters (author claim).
- Attack Success Rate: up to 92% for sex-related content, 90% for violence (author claim).
- Semantic similarity to original harmful input: up to 0.96 (author claim).

## Main findings
- Author claim: MacPrompt is a unified black-box, cross-lingual attack defeating both built-in filters and concept-removal defenses without model internals.
- Author claim: character-level macaronic recombination preserves visual semantics (CLIPSim up to 0.96) while obfuscating textual embeddings to evade text filters (up to 100% bypass).
- Author claim: strong results even against concept-removal models generally considered harder to attack (ASR 92% sex, 90% violence), outperforming baselines.
- Author claim: exposes cross-lingual adversarial robustness as a new, under-explored T2I safety gap.

## Design implications
T2I safety that assumes monolingual (English) inputs and keyword/embedding matching is fundamentally incomplete: cross-lingual character-level recombination preserves the diffusion model's visual concept while breaking the filter's textual view. Concept removal at the weight level does not remove the concept's cross-lingual triggerability. Defenses must be language-agnostic and operate on generated-image semantics, not just prompt text.

## Implementation implications
Input filters should normalize/transliterate and evaluate cross-lingual and character-recombined forms; latent/image-side moderation (post-generation NSFW detection) is a necessary backstop since prompt-side filtering is evadable. Blacklist and single-classifier text filters are insufficient.

## Evaluation implications
T2I robustness benchmarks must include cross-lingual and macaronic adversarial prompts; evaluating only synonym/obfuscation attacks overstates safety. ASR should be scored by an independent judge, not the attack's own optimization oracle.

## Deployment implications
Deployed T2I services (including those with proprietary black-box filters and concept removal) are exposed to query-based cross-lingual attacks; image-side moderation and rate/query monitoring are implicated as compensating controls.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Signals: prompts mixing multiple scripts/languages or containing non-dictionary character-recombined tokens near known-sensitive concept embeddings; repeated query loops driven by a harmfulness oracle (ZOO). Post-hoc image moderation and provenance logging are candidate detections.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to T2I diffusion models (SD family and, by transfer claim, some commercial systems) with text/image filters and concept removal. Should NOT be generalized to text-only LLM jailbreaks, nor read as evidence that image-side moderation is defeated (the attack targets prompt-side filtering and concept removal; robust output moderation is a proposed backstop, not tested as broken).
