## Research question
Can an LLM be post-trained to respond **safely yet helpfully** to malicious inputs by identifying and removing only the *harmful* semantics (entities/relations) while preserving useful ones — avoiding both unsafe compliance and blanket over-refusal?

## Problem definition
Existing safety methods generalize poorly (prompt engineering / model editing are tailored to specific attacks) or, in RL post-training, tend to **harshly refuse over any possible risk**, sacrificing helpfulness. The authors argue realistic malicious inputs mix harmful and helpful semantics; the model should filter harmful relations and answer the residual safe query, rather than refuse outright.

## Trust assumptions
Trusts the Perspective API risk detector and deepseek-r1 as low-overhead substitutes for human risk annotation/extraction. Assumes harmful intent decomposes into removable entity-relation edges and that reconstructing a query after edge removal yields a genuinely safe-yet-useful request.

## Attack or failure mechanism (that the method targets)
Failure modes of prior defenses: (a) over-refusal when safe relations are misclassified as harmful, and (b) unsafe outputs when harmful relations are treated as safe; and (c) risk that is invisible at the single-triple level but emerges from multi-hop combinations of entities/relations.

## Proposed defense or method
**WALKSAFE** — graph-based risk-aware post-training, three stages:
1. **Entity-Relation Graph (ERG)**: extract (entity, relation, entity) triples (via deepseek-r1) from inputs; entities = vertices, relations = edges; each input = a subgraph of a global ERG.
2. **Risk-aware Random Walk (RRW)** for subgraph risk quantification: score each edge's isolated risk r_eij = σ(wᵀ·emb(tk)+b) using the Perspective model; set walk probability p_eij = 1−r_eij (risky edges walked less); run global and local walks to get expected risk r_global, r_local; **iterative safe-subgraph extraction** removes the highest walk-score edge (w_e = f_local(e)·r_e) until r_local < α·r_global, reconstructing a safe query x_safe.
3. **Bi-GRPO training** (bi-group relative policy optimization): unlike vanilla GRPO (intra-group only), Bi-GRPO adds **inter-group** reward comparison between responses from the original and reconstructed-safe queries, encouraging the model to distinguish harmful vs safe semantics and prefer safe+helpful responses. A multi-stage SFT dataset D_safe = (x_orig, [g_orig, g_safe, x_safe, y_safe]) is distilled (y_safe from deepseek-r1) with an SFT objective, followed by Bi-GRPO with a KL-regularized objective.

## Design implications
Safety filtering can be reframed as **graph surgery**: represent an input as an entity-relation graph, quantify combinatorial (subgraph) risk via risk-weighted random walks, and excise only high-risk edges to preserve helpfulness — a route to reducing over-refusal. Cross-group (original vs sanitized) reward signals can teach a model to internalize the harmful/safe boundary.

## Implementation implications
Bi-GRPO shows a practical way to add **inter-group** contrast to GRPO for safety alignment. Using an external risk classifier (Perspective) and a strong reasoner (deepseek-r1) as low-cost annotators enables SFT+RL alignment without large human refusal datasets — at the cost of inheriting those tools' errors.

## Evaluation implications
Safety evaluation should jointly measure **over-refusal (helpfulness retention) and harm reduction**; combination/multi-hop risk (benign-triple composition) should be an explicit test axis, since single-utterance classifiers miss it.

## Deployment implications
For assistants where blanket refusal harms utility, relation-filtering + reconstruction offers a middle path. But deployment must account for the failure mode of answering a subtly-changed query and for the latency/complexity of graph construction and multi-round walks per input.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Log the extracted ERG, removed edges, and reconstructed query for each flagged input to make safety decisions auditable and to detect drift in the risk scorer. Watch for inputs whose harmful intent survives edge removal (parse-evasion) and for cases where reconstruction changed the user's meaning.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to text LLM post-training for content-safety alignment where inputs admit entity-relation extraction. It should NOT be assumed effective against jailbreaks that defeat triple extraction (obfuscation, code/equation wrapping, ciphered prompts) or against adaptive attackers targeting the RRW threshold, and its SOTA claims are for the (truncated, unverified-here) evaluated conditions only. Not a formal safety guarantee.
