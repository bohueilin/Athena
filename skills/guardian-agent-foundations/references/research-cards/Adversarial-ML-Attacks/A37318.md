## Research question
Can large-scale video diffusion models (>10B params) be distilled into a stable single-step generator that matches
or exceeds the multi-step teacher's quality, including for conditional image-to-video (I2V) tasks?

## Problem definition
Video diffusion attains high fidelity but is prohibitively slow (e.g., Wan2.1-I2V-14B ~15 min for a 5s video, 50
steps, on 8×H20). Existing distillation adapted from image techniques (CD, VSD, AD) fails to single-step-distill
large video models and lacks conditional-task generalization, suffering semantic degradation and "conditional frame
collapse" in I2V. Single-step adversarial distillation is unstable because the large real/generated quality gap makes
discrimination trivial (weak gradients).

## System or model being studied
V-PAE, a two-phase one-step distillation framework: (i) Stability Priming — a VSD-guided warm-up aligning real and
generated distributions via score-gradient differences (Eq. 1–2), using a real model, a fake model with lightweight
LoRA, and a priming generator; (ii) Unified Adversarial Equilibrium — a self-adversarial process reusing the
generator's parameters as the discriminator backbone (self-discriminator with EMA weights θ⁻ + lightweight heads),
Hinge loss (Eq. 3), plus spatiotemporal differential R1 gradient regularization (Eq. 4). For conditional tasks: a
multi-modal Semantic Discriminator Head and a conditional SDS loss (Eq. 5) to preserve video-image subject
consistency.

## Trust assumptions
Not a security setting. Modeling assumptions: the pretrained multi-step video model provides a reliable regularizing
distribution; reusing generator parameters for the discriminator yields a stable Nash equilibrium.

## Attack or failure mechanism
No attack. Failure modes addressed are training-stability failures of single-step adversarial distillation (weak
discriminator gradients from the large real/fake gap) and I2V quality failures (semantic degradation, conditional
frame collapse).

## Proposed defense or method
Not a defense. Method = V-PAE (stability priming + unified adversarial equilibrium + semantic discriminator head +
conditional SDS + spatiotemporal R1 regularization).

## Datasets and benchmarks
VBench-I2V benchmark. Teacher/base model: Wan2.1-I2V-14B. Baselines compared: DMD2, MD (MagicDistillation), LCM, PCM,
DCM, ADD across CD/VSD/AD paradigms.

## Evaluation methodology
Distill all methods from Wan2.1-I2V-14B under equal data/training cost; measure quality on VBench-I2V dimensions
(semantic alignment, temporal coherence, frame quality) and latency for 5s 720×1280 videos on 8×H20 GPUs; qualitative
comparison at 100-NFE vs 1-NFE (Fig. 4).

## Metrics
VBench-I2V sub-scores: I2V score, Subject/Background Consistency (semantic alignment), Motion Smoothness/Dynamic
Degree (temporal coherence), Aesthetic/Image Quality (frame quality), overall Quality Score; and Latency (s).
Reported (author claim): +5.8% average overall quality over acceleration baselines at single step; ~100× latency
reduction (≈15 min → ~10 s) for the large-scale model; competitive/superior to the 50-step teacher in some settings.

## Main findings
- Author claim: first framework to single-step-distill a >10B video diffusion model with quality comparable to (and
  in a few-step/zero-shot manner exceeding) the 50-step teacher.
- Author claim: reusing generator parameters as the discriminator backbone (self-discriminator) gives a stable,
  memory-feasible high-quality equilibrium where frozen/fully-trained discriminators degrade or exceed memory.
- Author claim: semantic head + conditional SDS mitigate I2V semantic degradation and conditional frame collapse.

## Negative results
Author-noted prior-art limitations that V-PAE overcomes (not V-PAE's own): frozen discriminator backbones cause
generator/discriminator asymmetry that degrades single-step quality; full-parameter discriminator training is
memory-prohibitive for 14B video models (Tab. 3 referenced).
