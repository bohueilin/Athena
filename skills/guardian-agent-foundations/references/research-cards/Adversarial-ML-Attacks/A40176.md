## Research question
Can an attacker degrade a cooperative multi-agent (reinforcement-learning) system (MAS) under a *strict black-box* setting — perturbing only observations, without access to internals, value functions, or action-control authority — while remaining both effective (large performance drop) and stealthy (few perturbed agents, low-magnitude, hard to detect)?

## Problem definition
Prior MAS adversarial-attack work assumes unrealistic access: white-box internals/value functions, or direct action-control over target agents, or perturbs *all* agents (poor stealth) or a *fixed* agent (ineffective across diverse states). The authors seek an attack that (a) adaptively selects, per time-step, the most impactful agent to victimize, (b) determines the worst-impact "malicious action" to induce, and (c) realizes it purely by perturbing that agent's observation, all in strict black-box conditions.

## System or model being studied
Trained cooperative multi-agent RL (MARL) policies (joint policy π = {π₁…πₙ}) modeled as a multi-agent MDP. Each agent acts on a local observation; the joint action drives state transitions and a shared reward. Victim policies are frozen at deployment. Application domains cited: UAV swarms, industrial robots, autonomous driving.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: adaptively select one adversary agent per time-step and induce, via observation perturbation, a "malicious action" (the action causing the worst impact) to degrade victim-MAS reward/win-rate.
- Adversary knowledge: strict black-box — no access to victim network architecture, parameters, training algorithm, or value/credit-assignment functions; only observations and corresponding actions are observable.
- Adversary access: can perturb the *observations* delivered to a selected agent (e.g., a physical patch on an agent's camera); can NOT directly set actions at deployment. (During attack-policy *training* the attacker can fully control the adversary's actions in simulation — a training-time convenience, not a deployment capability.)
- Adversary capabilities: trains (1) an Adaptive Selection Policy (SAC-based RL, hierarchical classifiers Cϕ1 for agent, Cϕ2 for action) and (2) proxy agents that imitate the victim via (generative adversarial) imitation learning to obtain white-box gradients for perturbation generation.
- Attacker budget: constrained "perturbation rate" (fraction/number of perturbed agents/steps); evaluated across multiple perturbation-rate settings. Exact budgets are in the truncated results section.
- Phase: attacker-side training of selection policy + proxies; victim attacked at inference/deployment (victim policy frozen).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted in the sense of inducing a specific worst-case malicious action on a selected agent; overall objective is reward minimization (performance degradation).
- Digital / physical: primarily digital (simulated observation perturbation); authors motivate a physical realization via a camera patch.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: the *attack* is adaptive to environment state; it is evaluated against "robust MAS" and existing attack-detection methods, but is not shown against a defender that adapts specifically to AdapAM.

## Trust assumptions
The victim MAS is assumed to trust its raw observation channel (sensor inputs) — there is no observation authentication/integrity check. The proxy agents are trusted to approximate victim behavior well enough that white-box perturbations transfer. Victim policy parameters are static post-deployment.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Two-stage design (AdapAM): (1) **Adaptive Selection Policy** πϕ(iₜ, ãₜ | sₜ) — a hierarchical SAC policy that, from global state, first picks which agent iₜ to attack (Cϕ1) then the malicious action ãₜ to induce (Cϕ2, conditioned on state + one-hot of iₜ); attacker reward is the negated victim reward. (2) **Proxy-based Perturbation to Induce Malicious Action** — proxy agents trained by (generative adversarial) imitation learning map observations→actions to approximate the victim; their white-box gradients solve õᵢ = oᵢ + δ, ‖δ‖≤ε, s.t. πᵢ(oᵢ+δ)=ãₜ (Eq. 3). The perturbed observation is injected so the (frozen) victim agent executes the malicious action. Selecting a single, state-dependent adversary balances effectiveness and stealth.

## Proposed defense or method
None — this is an offensive framework (a robustness/security *evaluation technique* for MAS). It measures stealthiness against existing detection methods and attacks both "normal" and "robust" MAS.

## Evaluation methodology
Attack both normal and robustness-trained victim MAS across the eight environments and multiple perturbation rates; measure attack effectiveness (reward, win rate) and stealthiness via (a) observation distance before/after perturbation and (b) detection rate under existing attack-detection methods. Ablations on adversary-selection and malicious-action-selection are described as included. (Numeric tables truncated in excerpt.)

## Metrics
- Effectiveness: victim reward and win rate (lower = stronger attack), across perturbation rates.
- Stealthiness: perturbation magnitude / distance between pre- and post-perturbation observations (smaller = stealthier); detection rate under existing detectors (lower = stealthier).
- Reported *qualitative* results (abstract/intro): AdapAM achieves "almost optimal" / best attack performance across environments and perturbation rates and produces the "least noisy and hardest to detect" perturbations. Exact numbers are truncated.

## Main findings
Author claims: (1) adaptive per-step selection of a single high-impact adversary plus an induced worst-case action outperforms all four baselines on both normal and robust MAS across eight environments and multiple perturbation rates; (2) proxy-based white-box perturbation transfers well enough to succeed in strict black-box conditions; (3) perturbations are smaller and evade existing attack detectors better than baselines, evidencing superior stealthiness.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_

- "Strict black-box" still requires observing (observation, action) pairs of the victim over many episodes to train the attack — a nontrivial access assumption that blurs the black-box framing.

- Detectors used for the stealth claim are existing/static; an adaptive defender aware of AdapAM's single-agent, state-conditioned pattern is untested.

## Design implications
Multi-agent autonomous systems that trust raw sensor/observation channels are exposed to *selective* observation perturbation that needs neither internal access nor action authority. Treat the observation ingestion path (`physical_sensors`, `model_serving`) of each agent as an attack surface, and design credit-robust coordination that does not collapse when a single high-centrality agent is compromised.

## Implementation implications
Relevant mitigations are observation-integrity/authentication, input sanitization and anomaly detection on per-agent observations, and robustness training that anticipates *adaptive single-agent* adversaries rather than uniform noise. Redundancy/consensus across agents can reduce the leverage of one induced malicious action.

## Evaluation implications
MAS robustness benchmarks should include adaptive, budget-constrained, single-adversary attacks and report both effectiveness (reward/win-rate) and stealth (perturbation size, detection rate), against normal AND robustness-trained victims — matching this paper's protocol.

## Deployment implications
For UAV/robotics/autonomous-driving MAS, a physically realizable observation patch on one adaptively chosen agent could degrade collective performance while evading current detectors. Gate safety-critical collective actions behind cross-agent verification and out-of-band sensor checks; do not assume black-box opacity is protective.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Runtime monitors should watch for anomalous per-agent observation shifts and for one agent's actions repeatedly diverging from consensus/expected policy. Log observation provenance to enable tracing which agent/time-steps were perturbed and to support rollback of compromised episodes.

## Applicability boundaries
_(where these findings should and should NOT be generalized)_
Findings apply to cooperative deep-MARL systems under observation-perturbation, strict-black-box, frozen-victim threat models. They should NOT be read as evidence about LLM/tool/prompt-based agents (different modality and attack surface), nor as breaking any *specific* named defense beyond those evaluated. **Adjacent** to agent security: it concerns multi-agent *system* security and inter-agent dependency, directly relevant to agentic robustness reasoning, but the victims are RL policies, not LLM/tool agents.
