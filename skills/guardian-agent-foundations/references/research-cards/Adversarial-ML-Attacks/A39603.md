## Research question
How can a vision-language foundation model (CLIP) be given zero-shot adversarial robustness — especially against LARGE, unseen perturbations not used during training — WITHOUT sacrificing zero-shot generalization (clean accuracy on unseen classes)?

## Problem definition
CLIP generalizes zero-shot but is highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations (up to ~90% drop). Existing zero-shot-robust methods trade off robustness vs. generalization: image-encoder fine-tuning (TeCoA, PMG) fails against large unseen perturbations; text-embedding expansion (LAAT) helps robustness but disrupts CLIP's intrinsic semantic consistency, hurting generalization. Three empirical insights motivate the work: (1) a stable positive logit-margin offset between small and large perturbations; (2) a strong negative correlation between logit margin and inter-class semantic similarity; (3) enlarging text-embedding distances harms semantic consistency.

## System or model being studied
CLIP (ViT-B/32) as the vision-language foundation model; both image and text encoders co-optimized. Adversarial examples generated with PGD during adversarial fine-tuning. Baselines: TeCoA, PMG, LAAT.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: evade zero-shot classification by adding l∞-bounded perturbations to images, degrading robust accuracy.
- Adversary knowledge: white-box gradient-based attacks at evaluation (PGD, CW, AutoAttack, BIM) against the deployed model.
- Adversary access: input images at inference.
- Adversary capabilities: l∞ perturbations at multiple radii ε ∈ {1,2,4,8}/255 — critically, LARGER radii than the ε = 1/255 used in training (unseen-perturbation robustness).
- Attacker budget: bounded l∞ norm; multi-step attacks (e.g., AutoAttack ensemble).
- Phase: inference/evaluation (evasion); defense operates at adversarial fine-tuning (training) time.
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted robust-accuracy evaluation.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: uses AutoAttack (a strong, parameter-free, partially adaptive ensemble) — stronger than single-attack evaluation.

## Trust assumptions
Standard adversarial-robustness setting: the model is trusted, inputs are untrusted/adversarial. Zero-shot transfer assumes fine-tuning on one dataset (Tiny-ImageNet) generalizes robustly to unseen classes/datasets.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Adversarial perturbations shift image-text alignment away from the true class (captured by the logit margin γ_i^ε). Larger perturbations produce a stable additional margin reduction; semantically similar classes suffer larger margin drops, causing misclassification into confusable classes.

## Proposed defense or method
TIMA = Text-Image Mutual Awareness, two complementary modules:
1. Text-Aware Image (TAI) tuning with Adaptive Semantic-Aware Margin (ASAM): enforces a semantic-aware negative margin s(z^ε,t_k) − s(z^ε,t_ci) = −m·(1 − s(t_k,t_ci)), imposing larger separation for more semantically similar classes; a sample-adaptive gate M_ik activates the margin only for inherently confusable samples (using a frozen clean embedding). Plug-and-play into any adversarial fine-tuning framework; a proposition (under L-Lipschitz + M-Lipschitz-Jacobian assumptions) argues the margin shift is radius-ordered and bounded-variation, provably increasing inter-class distance.
2. Image-Aware Text (IAT) tuning with Semantic-Consistent Minimum Hyperspherical Energy (SC-MHE): uniformly spaces text embeddings on a hypersphere (MHE) to maximize inter-class separation WHILE a semantic-consistency regularizer R aligns tuned text-embedding predictive distributions with the frozen original embeddings on clean images, preserving CLIP's semantic structure and generalization.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Fine-tuned on Tiny-ImageNet. Evaluated zero-shot on 14 datasets: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, Caltech-101, Caltech-256 (generic); OxfordPets, Food-101, Flowers, StanfordCars, FGVC (fine-grained); SUN397 (scene); DTD (texture). Attacks: PGD, CW, AutoAttack, BIM.

## Evaluation methodology
Compare zero-shot robust accuracy and clean accuracy vs. TeCoA/PMG/LAAT under multiple attacks and perturbation radii (incl. unseen ε = 2,4,8/255); ablations on ASAM and SC-MHE; cosine-similarity matrices on CIFAR-10 to visualize semantic-structure preservation; hyperparameter stability (m = 0.10, η = 0.95 default). Implementation: CLIP-B/32, 10 epochs, batch 512, lr 1e-4, SGD momentum 0.9, 2-step PGD (ε = 1/255).

## Metrics
Zero-shot robust accuracy (under PGD/CW/AutoAttack/BIM at various ε) and zero-shot clean accuracy across the 14 datasets. Semantic preservation shown qualitatively via text-text / image-text cosine-similarity matrices. Motivation stats: Pearson r = −0.685, Spearman ρ = −0.651 (p ≪ 0.001) for margin vs. semantic similarity.

## Main findings
- Author claim: TIMA outperforms SOTA (TeCoA, PMG, LAAT) on both robust and clean zero-shot accuracy across multiple datasets and perturbations, especially for large unseen perturbations, by jointly calibrating logit margins (ASAM) and preserving semantic consistency (SC-MHE).
- SC-MHE better preserves CLIP's original semantic structure than LAAT (Fig. 4), addressing the generalization loss LAAT incurs.
- Notably robust to minor perturbation (ε = 1/255) even under clean-data-only training (ablation observation).

## Negative results
LAAT's text-embedding expansion increases robustness but demonstrably damages semantic consistency and clean-data generalization (an analyzed negative result of prior work motivating SC-MHE).

## Design implications
For robust multimodal foundation models, calibrate decision boundaries by class-level semantic similarity and preserve pre-trained semantic geometry rather than blindly expanding embedding distances — robustness and generalization can be jointly improved. ASAM is a drop-in margin component for existing adversarial fine-tuning.

## Implementation implications
Sample-adaptive, semantic-aware margins plus energy-minimization with a semantic-consistency regularizer are practical add-ons to CLIP fine-tuning; a frozen clean encoder is used to gate margins per sample.

## Evaluation implications
Zero-shot robustness should be tested at unseen (larger) perturbation radii and with strong ensembles (AutoAttack), and paired with clean-accuracy/semantic-preservation measurements to expose robustness-generalization trade-offs.

## Deployment implications
Relevant to deploying CLIP-style perception in agentic/robotic or content-understanding systems where adversarial images could mislead classification; improves resilience to unseen-strength attacks while retaining open-vocabulary generalization.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Logit-margin monitoring can flag adversarial drift (margin shift is a measurable signal); semantic-similarity structure can be tracked to detect degradation from robustness interventions.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to zero-shot adversarial robustness of vision-language foundation models (CLIP) under l∞ evasion. It is a DEFENSE. Not about LLM prompt/tool/agent injection; adjacent to agent security as robustness of a perception/foundation-model component that agents may rely on. Does not provide certified guarantees.
