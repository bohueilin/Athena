## Research question
For Adversarial Fully-Observable Non-Deterministic (FOND) planning — where action outcomes are chosen by an adversarial environment and the goal is a strong acyclic policy minimizing worst-case cost to reach the goal — how can domain-independent heuristic search be made efficient via (a) admissible heuristics and (b) dominance-based pruning?

## Problem definition
Adversarial FOND: no fairness assumption; the non-deterministic outcomes are picked by an adversary maximizing cost. The solution is a strong closed acyclic strategy achieving the optimal worst-case value V*(s0) defined by a min-max Bellman equation. The paper fills a gap: no prior domain-independent methods leverage a compact environment description in this worst-case setting.

## System or model being studied
Factored Non-deterministic Planning Tasks (FNDPT) over synchronized products of non-deterministic labelled transition systems (NDLTS). Solved with Bounded LDFS (Learning Depth-First Search, Bonet & Geffner) as the base optimal search algorithm.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: (game-theoretic, not security) an adversarial *environment/player* selects action outcomes to maximize worst-case cost / prevent reaching the goal.
- Adversary knowledge/access/capabilities: full control over which non-deterministic outcome of each action occurs; the planning agent controls action choice only.
- Attacker budget: not a resource-bounded attacker — worst-case over all outcome sequences.
- Phase: planning / policy synthesis (offline), applicable to online planning.
- Targeted / untargeted: n/a (worst-case cost minimization).
- Digital / physical: abstract planning formalism (domain-independent).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: the adversary is fully adaptive within the game semantics (chooses worst outcome each step).

## Trust assumptions
The environment is treated as maximally adversarial (unfair non-determinism); guarantees are worst-case. This is a modeling stance for critical tasks needing bounded worst-case cost, not a trust model over software components.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Not an attack. The "failure" avoided is an unbounded-worst-case (cyclic) policy: because non-determinism is unfair, any cycle can be forced forever, so only acyclic strategies guarantee reaching the goal.

## Proposed defense or method
Two domain-independent techniques (not security defenses): (1) **Single-outcome determinization heuristics** — determinize each action to a single outcome to reuse classical planning heuristics; Theorems 1-2 prove such heuristics remain admissible and consistent for the adversarial task (removing outcomes cannot increase the min-max value). Single-outcome determinization is shown to yield more informative admissible heuristics than the usual all-outcome determinization. (2) **Adversarial dominance pruning** — generalize label-dominance / simulation relations to the non-deterministic adversarial setting (game-simulation relations, Definitions 4-6, Theorems 3-4), computed per-factor and composed into a whole-task dominance relation; used in transition-pruning methods (PruneSource, PruneOutcome, PruneAction, PruneInit), with a cost-0 noop added only for dominance computation.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Planning benchmark domains (a logistics/truck-package example is illustrated). Coverage measured under the blind heuristic and LMCut heuristic with All / First / Last / Random outcome determinizations. Exact domain list and coverage tables were largely truncated in the extracted text.

## Evaluation methodology
Research questions RQ1 (which pruning methods help) and RQ2 (which determinization is best) evaluated by coverage (number of tasks solved) across domains, comparing determinization strategies and pruning combinations.

## Metrics
Coverage (tasks solved) and search-effort reduction across domains.

## Main findings
- Single-outcome determinization heuristics are admissible and consistent (Theorems 1-2) and empirically outperform all-outcome determinization; the best single outcome (First/Last/Random) is domain-dependent and matters a lot (depends on how the "worst" outcome is modeled).
- Adversarial dominance pruning (established correct by Theorems 3-4) significantly reduces search effort in many domains.
- Among pruning variants: PruneSource and PruneOutcome are orthogonal and jointly best ("dominance pruning"); PruneOutcome gives significant coverage gains; PruneInit's overhead is not worth it; PruneAction is sometimes detrimental and often subsumed by PruneSource.

## Negative results
PruneInit does not provide enough reduction to justify overhead; PruneAction is sometimes detrimental; all-outcome determinization is consistently worse than single-outcome determinization for this worst-case objective.

## Limitations stated by the authors
The formalism assumes no zero-cost transitions (Definition 7a); allowing zero-cost cycles would require non-trivial changes. Computing the maximum dominance relation over the full state space is infeasible (exponential), so only factored approximations are used. The best outcome-determinization depends on problem modeling.

## Design implications
For safety-critical control where worst-case (not expected) cost matters, model the environment as adversarial and synthesize strong acyclic policies with guaranteed bounded cost; determinization lets you reuse the mature classical-heuristic toolbox admissibly.

## Implementation implications
Prefer single-outcome (not all-outcome) determinization for admissible worst-case heuristics; combine PruneSource + PruneOutcome dominance pruning; add a compute-only noop to strengthen dominance without violating the no-zero-cost-cycle assumption.

## Evaluation implications
Report coverage across multiple determinization choices, since the "worst outcome" and thus heuristic quality is highly modeling-dependent.

## Deployment implications
Relevant to worst-case-guaranteed planning/control (e.g., critical robotics/logistics tasks against uncontrollable/adversarial dynamics), and to computing a required worst-case budget before improving the non-worst-case policy online.
