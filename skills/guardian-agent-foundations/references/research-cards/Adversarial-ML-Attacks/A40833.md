## Research question
Can an adversary stealthily force Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) to consume excessive server compute — a slowdown/DDoS-style resource-depletion attack — without degrading answer accuracy or relying on easily-detectable decoy tasks?

## Problem definition
LRMs (o1/DeepSeek-R1-style CoT models) spend heavy inference-time compute on reasoning. Prior slowdown attacks (OverThinking) inject a rigid, context-irrelevant decoy task, which capable models (o3) recognize and dismiss, or which derails weaker models into accuracy collapse — making them ineffective or detectable. Goal: amplify reasoning cost (tokens, latency) while preserving the original query meaning and final answer (stealth), economically threatening free-API LRM services.

## System or model being studied
Black-box reasoning LLMs accessed via public API: o3, o3-mini (closed) and QwQ-32B, Qwen3-32B (open). Attack works in direct-prompting and indirect-prompt-injection (poisoned retrieved context) scenarios.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: maximize output token length and latency (server resource occupation) while keeping answer accuracy ≈ original (stealth). Formally L(Y′) ≫ L(Y), Latency(Y′) ≫ Latency(Y), Acc(A′) ≈ Acc(A).
- Adversary knowledge: black-box — public API only; no internal states, parameters, gradients, or architecture.
- Adversary access: submit crafted prompt Q′ and observe output (reasoning content if exposed, plus final answer); or poison external data sources retrieved as context.
- Adversary capabilities: character-level poly-base ASCII obfuscation of a selected fraction of characters (obfuscation ratio ρ), plus an explanatory decoding note.
- Attacker budget: single crafted prompt (no optimization/gradients required).
- Phase: inference/deployment (runtime resource attack).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted at compute cost; answer-preserving.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: static transformation, but randomized per-character base selection resists simple pattern filters; defenses only conceptually analyzed.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the LRM will faithfully attempt to decode the embedded poly-base ASCII (guided by the appended note) rather than refuse or shortcut; assumes reasoning content/latency scale with the injected decoding sub-tasks; in the indirect scenario, assumes an application retrieves attacker-poisoned external text as context.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Four steps: (1) Query Segmentation — split query into characters. (2) Probabilistic Character Selection — pick k = ⌈|S_valid|·ρ⌉ transformable characters at random (randomness defeats fixed-signature filters). (3) Poly-Base ASCII Transformation — for each selected char, ASCII-encode, sample a random base n_j ∈ {2..9,11..36} (excluding base 10 so the decimal ASCII isn't shown directly), convert, and format as <(n_j)val_{n_j}>; per-character random bases prevent a single learnable decode pattern. (4) Adversarial Prompt Reformation — reassemble and append a decoding note N_note (which preserves accuracy by guiding correct interpretation; the authors note it could later be omitted or altered to inject ambiguity and further amplify cost). The model must perform many non-trivial decode+reason sub-tasks before answering.

## Proposed defense or method
This is an attack paper. Two candidate defenses are analyzed (not empirically defeated by a defender in a full study, and argued brittle): pattern-matching input purification (brittle — bypassed by changing delimiters, e.g., [base=n](val)) and perplexity-based filtering (analyzed as a detection strategy). Authors conclude new defenses are needed to secure the integrity of the reasoning process itself.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Four benchmarks: AIME 2024 and AIME 2025 (30 questions each; 60 total math competition problems), HumanEval (164 code problems), and BigCodeBench-Complete (150 sampled from 1,140 tasks).

## Evaluation methodology
Compare ExtendAttack against Direct Answering (DA) and OverThinking across the four benchmarks on the four LRMs; measure response length, latency, and Pass@1 accuracy; evaluation frameworks from Zhang et al. (2025) and the official BigCodeBench harness.

## Metrics
(1) Response Length (output tokens), (2) Latency (seconds to generate), (3) Accuracy (Pass@1) as the stealth indicator. Headline: response length increases >2.7× for o3 on HumanEval; accuracy stays comparable to (sometimes improves over) DA, whereas OverThinking causes large accuracy drops on several models.

## Main findings
Author claims: ExtendAttack significantly amplifies compute overhead (e.g., >2.7× length for o3 on HumanEval; large latency/length increases across models/benchmarks) while preserving — occasionally improving — answer accuracy, unlike OverThinking (ignored by o3; catastrophic accuracy collapse elsewhere, e.g., drops of 23–48 points shown in the comparison table). Works in both direct and indirect (prompt-injection) settings. Per-character random-base obfuscation resists simple rule-based filters.

## Negative results
OverThinking baseline fails on capable models (recognized/ignored) or causes accuracy collapse — the motivating negative result for prior art. No explicit failure mode of ExtendAttack itself reported; defenses (pattern matching) are argued brittle rather than shown to defeat the attack.

## Limitations stated by the authors
The current attack's N_note makes the obfuscation explicit (more detectable); authors frame this as removable/adjustable as models improve. Defenses are analyzed conceptually (pattern matching brittle; perplexity filtering) rather than exhaustively evaluated. No formal limitations section captured in extract otherwise.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- Defense analysis is qualitative; no quantified defender-vs-attacker evaluation (e.g., how much perplexity filtering reduces success at what utility cost).
- Relies on the model attempting the decode; a provider that caps reasoning tokens, truncates, or routes obfuscated inputs could blunt it — not tested.
- Commercial model (o3) behavior/versioning drifts; absolute length/latency numbers are snapshot-dependent.
- Accuracy-preservation depends on the explanatory note; without it, stealth-vs-effectiveness trade-off is under-quantified.
- Evaluated on math/code benchmarks; effect on other task types and on multi-turn agent loops not measured.

## Design implications
Reasoning tokens are a resource an attacker can inflate via the input's semantic structure; LRM serving must treat inference-compute as an attack surface (economic DoS). Token/compute budgets, per-request cost caps, and reasoning-length governors are relevant design controls.

## Implementation implications
Signature-based input filters are insufficient (delimiters/bases are trivially varied); defenses should target the *behavior* (abnormal decode-heavy reasoning, disproportionate reasoning-to-query ratio) and enforce hard reasoning-token ceilings. Indirect-injection variant means retrieved/external context must be sanitized before entering an LRM.

## Evaluation implications
LRM robustness/efficiency evaluations should report length/latency under adversarial, accuracy-preserving inputs — not just accuracy — and include indirect prompt-injection channels; measuring only answer quality misses this threat.

## Deployment implications
Free/low-cost LRM API providers face economic and availability degradation (compute exhaustion for all users) from stealthy inputs that look benign and yield correct answers; billing/quotas and anomaly-based throttling become security-relevant, not just cost controls.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Monitor reasoning-token-per-input ratios, decode-pattern density (poly-base bracket structures), and latency outliers; the indirect channel argues for logging/telemetry on retrieved-context provenance. Perplexity spikes are a candidate (imperfect) detector.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to reasoning LLMs that expend variable inference-time compute and to applications that ingest external context into such models. Should NOT be read as a content jailbreak (it preserves answers and violates no safety policy) nor as evidence about non-reasoning models. Core to agent security: black-box resource-exhaustion + indirect prompt injection.
