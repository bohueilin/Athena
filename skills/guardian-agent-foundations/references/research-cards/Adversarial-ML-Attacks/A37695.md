## Research question
Can adversarial perturbations be crafted that fool downstream video models and Multi-modal LLMs (MLLMs) fine-tuned from an open-source Video Foundation Model (VFM) *without* access to the victim's task, training data, labels, queries, or architecture — using only the public VFM?

## Problem definition
Most transfer-based video attacks assume knowledge of the victim's task/dataset (e.g. Kinetics-400) to train task-aligned surrogates; such assumptions often fail under privacy/data-protection constraints and for large self-supervised VFMs. The paper defines a stricter, more realistic threat: task-agnostic transfer attacks that exploit only the shared, open-source video backbone.

## System or model being studied
Open-source VFMs (e.g. VideoMAE, embedding dim 768) used as frozen feature extractors or fine-tuned backbones for downstream tasks: Temporal Action Detection (feature-based ActionFormer/TriDet/DyFaDet and end-to-end incl. AdaTAD scaled to 1B via PEFT), and video MLLMs evaluated on MVBench and SEEDBench (23-24 video tasks). Attack = Transferable Video Attack (TVA).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: untargeted disruption — maximize downstream task loss / output discrepancy of the victim model derived from a public VFM.
- Adversary knowledge: black-box on victim (no task, data, labels, queries, or full architecture); white-box only on the *public* foundation encoder f_phi used as surrogate.
- Adversary access: perturbs the input video x_tau; optimizes perturbation on the public backbone; no victim queries.
- Adversary capabilities: l-infinity-bounded perturbation, ||delta||_inf <= epsilon; iterative sign-gradient (I-FGSM-style) updates on embedding-space losses.
- Attacker budget: iterative optimization over I steps on the surrogate (epsilon/step details in truncated section).
- Phase: inference-time evasion; leverages the model-distribution/fine-tuning pipeline (public VFM reuse).
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted (maximize discrepancy / induce misclassification/failure).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive to victim defenses.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the victim is derived (fine-tuned or frozen-backbone + head) from a known open-source VFM the attacker can download; assumes shared frozen backbone embeddings retain exploitable vulnerabilities despite downstream adaptation. Central risk: open accessibility of VFM weights.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Fine-tuning/head-attachment introduces gradient mismatch between surrogate f_phi and victim f_phi^tau (Theorem 1: deviation from residual transformations h_{delta phi} of downstream adaptation), degrading naive transfer. TVA attacks the shared embedding space and aligns clean vs adversarial features bidirectionally to reduce this mismatch, and disrupts temporal coherence.

## Proposed defense or method
TVA (Transferable Video Attack), an *attack* with three components:
1. Self-supervised embedding-level attack: maximize L(z, z_adv) where z = f_phi(x); L1 loss promotes sparse embedding deviations, no labels/outputs needed.
2. Bidirectional temporal-aware contrastive loss (Bi-con): both clean->adv and adv->clean contrastive objectives so clean and adversarial features jointly define the space, correcting gradient asymmetry and mitigating surrogate overfitting; applied at temporal and frame levels with negatives across frames.
3. Temporal consistency (TC) loss: disrupts temporal coherence across frames using motion cues, amplifying sequential impact.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Downstream TAD benchmarks (untrimmed video, e.g. THUMOS-style) via ActionFormer/TriDet/DyFaDet/AdaTAD; MLLM evaluation on MVBench and SEEDBench across 23-24 video-related tasks. Pretraining datasets referenced: ImageNet-21k, Kinetics.

## Evaluation methodology
Craft perturbations on the public VFM encoder; transfer to (a) fine-tuned backbones, (b) frozen-backbone + task-head models, and (c) video MLLMs; measure task degradation across 24 video tasks. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 1-2, proofs in appendix) characterizes perturbation-update deviation and contrastive-anchor asymmetry.

## Metrics
Task-specific degradation across the 24 downstream tasks (detection/segmentation accuracy, MLLM benchmark scores). Exact numeric tables are in the truncated body/appendix; extracted text states "extensive experiments ... validate effectiveness" without reproducing all numbers here.

## Main findings
- Author claim: TVA is the first method to attack downstream video tasks and MLLMs using only an open-source VFM, with no victim task/data/query/architecture knowledge.
- Author claim: bidirectional contrastive alignment + temporal consistency substantially improves transfer over single-direction contrastive and prior transfer methods, by mitigating gradient mismatch (formalized in Theorem 1).
- Author claim: effective across 24 video tasks against task-specific models and MLLMs, revealing an underexplored VFM-reuse vulnerability.

## Design implications
Directly relevant to agent/AI-system supply-chain security: open-source foundation-model weights are an attack surface. Any agent that ingests video through an MLLM or task model fine-tuned from a public VFM can be attacked by an adversary who only downloads the same backbone. "Capability derived from a shared public model is also a shared vulnerability."

## Implementation implications
Treat the provenance of foundation-model backbones as security-relevant; consider backbone diversification, secure/robust fine-tuning of encoders, input purification, and randomization to break embedding-space transfer. Do not assume unknown task/architecture provides safety.

## Evaluation implications
Transfer-attack evaluation should include the *task-agnostic, backbone-only* threat model, not just task-aligned surrogates, and should span both task-specific heads and MLLM consumers of the same encoder.

## Deployment implications
Core-adjacent for agentic systems using multimodal LLMs: perception via a public VFM is a cross-task single point of failure. Downstream heads and MLLM reasoning can be disrupted simultaneously by one input perturbation.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Monitor embedding-space deviations and temporal-consistency anomalies at the encoder boundary; log which downstream tasks share a backbone so a single compromise's blast radius is known.

## Applicability boundaries
Generalizes to systems reusing open-source video/image foundation encoders under untargeted transfer. Do NOT generalize to prompt-injection/tool-abuse text-agent threats, to targeted control of outputs, or to victims not derived from a known public backbone. Mechanism is classic feature-space/temporal adversarial perturbation transferred to MLLM/foundation-model settings.
