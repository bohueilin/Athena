## Research question
Can a *sparse* adversarial attack on 3D point-cloud classifiers reach high attack success by selecting a compact subset of points whose *joint* perturbation produces amplified (cooperative) adversarial effects, rather than treating each point's contribution as independent?

## Problem definition
Prior dense point-cloud attacks perturb most/all points, distorting geometry and limiting real-world use; prior sparse attacks perturb few points but assume independent per-point influence, so they need stronger per-point distortion and lose effectiveness under sparsity/distortion constraints. The paper targets the gap: exploiting second-order (cooperative) interactions among a small selected subset.

## System or model being studied
3D deep point-cloud classifiers operating on point sets. Victim models: PointNet, DGCNN, PTv1 (Point Transformer v1), and Mamba3D. The proposed attack is SCP (Sparse and Cooperative Perturbation).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: induce misclassification (framed as untargeted; text notes formulation can be adapted to targeted).
- Adversary knowledge (white/gray/black-box): white-box — requires gradients of the misclassification loss and Hessian-block (second-order) information w.r.t. point coordinates.
- Adversary access: full access to victim model to compute gradients/Hessian blocks and optimize perturbations.
- Adversary capabilities: perturb the 3D coordinates of a compact selected subset of points (l0-sparse); optimize with Adam over binary-search rounds.
- Attacker budget: cooperative subset selection starts from k=256 candidate points; tolerance ϵ=10^-6; 10 binary-search rounds, 500 gradient steps; final attacks modify only ~30–60 points per Table 1.
- Phase: inference-time evasion (crafting adversarial examples against a fixed trained classifier).
- Targeted / untargeted: primarily untargeted (targeted adaptation mentioned, not the main evaluation).
- Digital / physical: primarily digital; a physical transfer demo is reported via a 3D-printed detachable patch that is re-scanned/re-sampled.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive w.r.t. defenses — no defense-aware/adaptive-defense evaluation is presented.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes attacker can query/backprop through the victim classifier (white-box) and can physically fabricate/affix a patch for the physical demo. No assumption of a deployed defense; robustness against point-cloud defenses is not evaluated.

## Attack or failure mechanism
SCP formalizes "cooperation" via a Jensen-type strict inequality: a subset's joint perturbation loss exceeds the weighted average of individual perturbation losses. A sufficient condition is local strict convexity of the misclassification loss w.r.t. the concatenated perturbation vector, i.e., the corresponding Hessian block is positive definite (H≻0). Because computing/searching the full Hessian is infeasible, SCP uses a three-stage greedy pipeline: (i) gradient-magnitude screening to pick top-k influential points; (ii) convexity-preserving expansion using the Schur-complement condition (accept a candidate if the "Schur Surplus" λ_min(C − BᵀA⁻¹B) > 0); (iii) tolerance-based inclusion of marginal points (accept if Schur Surplus > −ϵ). The selected cooperative subset is then jointly optimized to minimize misclassification loss plus a distortion penalty.

## Proposed defense or method
This is an attack paper; no new defense is proposed. The method is SCP itself (cooperative-subset selection + joint perturbation optimization).

## Datasets and benchmarks
ModelNet40 and ScanObjectNN; all point clouds uniformly resampled to 1,024 points.

## Evaluation methodology
Compared against sparse baselines (AdvStick, RandOcc, CritOcc, HighSal, LowSal, MiniPert) and dense baselines (PGD, IFGM, GeoA3, AOF, SI-Adv) across four classifiers and two datasets. Sparse baselines follow original configs; dense methods and SCP are configured to maximum ASR ("maximal adversarialness") before comparing imperceptibility. A physical experiment 3D-prints the perturbed region as a patch and re-scans. Implementation: PyTorch, dual 2.40 GHz CPUs, 8× RTX 3090.

## Metrics
Attack success rate (ASR); imperceptibility via Chamfer distance (CD), Hausdorff distance (HD), number of modified points (#Points), l2-norm, Curvature, Geometric Regularity (GR), and Earth Mover's Distance (EMD).

## Main findings
- Author claim: SCP achieves 100% ASR in all reported cases across the four classifiers and both datasets (Table 1), while other sparse baselines often fall below 80%.
- Author claim: SCP attains lower CD/HD and modifies fewer points than sparse baselines, and imperceptibility is in most cases superior to dense attacks despite far fewer modifications.
- Author claim: a physical patch derived from the localized perturbation region consistently causes misclassification after re-scanning, indicating transfer to the physical domain.

## Design implications
For safety-critical 3D perception (robotics, autonomous driving), robustness testing should include *cooperative* sparse perturbations, not just per-point or dense threat models — independent-point robustness assumptions understate exposure.

## Implementation implications
Defenders profiling input point clouds should consider that very few points (tens out of 1,024) with jointly optimized displacement can flip predictions; anomaly detectors keyed to large geometric change or many modified points may miss such attacks.

## Evaluation implications
Sparse-attack benchmarks that report only #Points and per-point distortion can be misleading; second-order/cooperative structure should be part of the evaluated threat surface. "Maximal adversarialness" comparison protocol is a reasonable practice to normalize attacks before comparing imperceptibility.

## Deployment implications
Physical realizability via a small detachable patch (if the qualitative claim holds) raises supply-chain/physical-tamper concerns for deployed 3D sensing; however, white-box requirement limits direct black-box deployment threat until transfer is demonstrated.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Runtime monitors for 3D classifiers could track statistical outliers in a small subset of high-gradient points and curvature/GR shifts; provenance/tamper checks on physical objects (patches) are relevant given the physical demo.

## Applicability boundaries
Findings apply to white-box, inference-time evasion of 3D point-cloud *classifiers* on object-level benchmarks. They should NOT be generalized to black-box settings, to 3D object detection/segmentation, or to guaranteed physical-world success without further evidence. Peripheral to LLM/agent prompt/tool security; relevant to physical-AI perception robustness.
