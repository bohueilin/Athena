NOTE on true topic: this is specifically a **training-time data-poisoning** attack whose harm is *deferred* to deployment — it does not degrade clean-task accuracy the way availability/unlearnable poisoning does; instead it makes the deployed model abnormally fragile to inference-time evasion perturbations and natural noise. It sits at the intersection of data-poisoning and robustness/evasion, so the "Adversarial-ML-Attacks" label fits but understates the poisoning/supply-chain angle.

## Research question
Can a data-poisoning attack be made stealthy enough to evade the standard defender check — i.e. produce no visible train-vs-validation performance gap — while still causing a serious, *deferred* harm that only manifests after deployment, namely a model that is far more vulnerable than normal to adversarial perturbations and even benign natural noise?

## Problem definition
The authors argue that most prevalent poisoning attacks (unlearnable examples, availability poisons, etc.) share a limitation: their "malicious intention is highly apparent," creating a conspicuous disparity between training-set and validation-set performance that alerts defenders and enables countermeasures (adversarial training, preprocessing, anomaly removal). The paper defines a new attack class, the **Deferred Poisoning Attack (DPA)**, that makes the poisoned model behave normally during *both* training and validation, so the poison is undetectable by the usual train/val consistency check, yet the model is left "extremely vulnerable" at deployment. The stated motivation scenario is Machine-Learning-as-a-Service (MLaaS): a poisoned model is uploaded/served, passes normal evaluation, then fails on subtly perturbed or noisy inputs in the field (e.g. an autonomous-driving sign classifier that reads "Speed Limit 50" correctly in test but misreads a "STOP" sign under fog/rain or a small adversarial perturbation).

## System or model being studied
Image-classification deep networks in a K-class supervised setting. Victim/surrogate architectures evaluated: VGG16, ResNet18, ResNet50, DenseNet121. The attack operates on the training data of such models; effect is measured on the trained ("poisoned") classifier versus a normally trained ("clean") classifier.

## Threat model
- **Adversary objective:** Untargeted degradation of *deployment-time robustness*. Force the model trained on the contaminated dataset to (a) converge to a solution that performs normally on clean train and validation data, while (b) having large local curvature (ill-conditioned / singularized input-Hessian) around each sample, so that small adversarial or natural perturbations cause large loss increase and misclassification. Not a backdoor/trigger and not a clean-accuracy availability attack.
- **Adversary knowledge:** Stated assumption — adversary has access to the entire training dataset but is "unaware of the target model's parameter structure or any output." Perturbations are generated on a **surrogate model** and shown to transfer; in one experiment perturbations are generated on a VGG16 pretrained on full ImageNet and transferred to ResNet18/ResNet50/DenseNet121 (described by the authors as a black-box scenario). So: gray/black-box with respect to the victim architecture and parameters.
- **Adversary access / capability:** Can modify training samples by adding an imperceptible additive perturbation δ, constrained by ‖δ‖_∞ ≤ ε. The adversary does **not** interfere with the victim's training process itself (the victim trains normally on the poisoned data).
- **Budget:** Perturbation budget ε set small for imperceptibility; principal setting ε = 3/255, with additional results at 5/255 and 8/255 (L∞). A key selling point is that DPA is effective at ε = 3/255, where the compared availability poisons (EM, REM) reportedly require larger ε (≈8/255) to take effect.
- **Axes:** White/gray/black-box → gray-to-black-box (surrogate-based, transfers). Phase → **training/data-poisoning-time attack with deployment-time (inference/evasion) manifestation**. Targeted/untargeted → untargeted (general fragility, no fixed target class or trigger). Digital/physical → digital (natural-noise experiments simulate real-world corruption but are digitally injected). Adaptive/non-adaptive → the attack itself is non-adaptive to a specific defense, but the authors also run adaptive-defense experiments (adversarial training, augmentation) against it.

## Trust assumptions
- Assumes the MLaaS / supply-chain trust gap where a consumer trains on or deploys a third-party-provided dataset (or a served model trained on such data) and validates only via clean-accuracy consistency between train and validation.
- Assumes the standard defender detection signal is the train-vs-validation performance gap; DPA is designed specifically to nullify that signal.
- Assumes attacker-perturbed samples pass human inspection (imperceptibility) — supported by the ×5-magnified residual comparison in the paper.
- Implicitly assumes the poison persists through the victim's normal training pipeline (the paper adds robustness-to-augmentation experiments to test this assumption).

## Attack or failure mechanism
DPA is framed as a bi-level / alternating optimization. Overall objective (Eq. 1):
`argmin over θ,δ  [ L(f_θ(x), y) + L(f_θ(x̂), y) − Q(f_θ(x̂), y) ]  s.t. ‖δ‖_∞ ≤ ε`, where x̂ = x + δ.
- First term `L(f_θ(x), y)` (cross-entropy on clean data) keeps the poisoned model performing normally on clean examples — the stealth term.
- Second term `L(f_θ(x̂), y)` keeps loss on poisoned samples similar to a normal model, so no anomalous training behavior.
- Third term is the **Hessian Singularization** regularizer `Q(f_θ(x̂), y) = tr(HᵀH)`, where H is the Hessian of the loss with respect to the *input* x̂. Subtracting Q *maximizes* it, driving up the largest singular value of the input-Hessian and thus the local curvature. The theory (via the standard strongly-convex-neighborhood bound with σ_min(H) and σ_max(H)) is that a large σ_max(H) means small input perturbations produce large loss changes — i.e. a fragile, easily-evaded model. The authors illustrate this as forcing the poisoned model to a "steep local optimum" (Fig. 2): same loss value at the optimum as a clean model, but with a much narrower/steeper basin.
- Optimization is split into two alternating stages (Algorithm 1 / Fig. 3): Stage 1 trains the model on both clean and poisoned minibatches (Eq. 2); Stage 2 fixes θ and updates δ to minimize `L(f_θ(x̂),y) − Q(f_θ(x̂),y)` (Eq. 3), i.e. increase curvature while keeping loss normal. Iterated to convergence.
- **Efficiency relaxation:** Computing σ_max(H) via SVD is expensive and not directly differentiable; computing the full input-Hessian is reported at ≈8 seconds per CIFAR10 image (≈p² backprops for input dim p). They relax to maximizing a lower bound: σ_max(H) ≥ [(1/n) tr(HᵀH)]^(1/2), note tr(HᵀH) = ‖H‖_F², and further bound ‖H‖_F² via Hessian-Vector-Product using `torch.autograd.functional.hvp` (since ‖Hv‖₂ ≤ ‖H‖_F for a unit v). This cuts cost to ≈0.008 seconds while preserving effectiveness.

## Proposed defense or method
The paper's primary contribution is the *attack*, but it also proposes a **defensive training paradigm** as a natural countermeasure: directly **minimizing the curvature** of the loss with respect to the input during training (the inverse of DPA's objective). In Table 6 this "Ours" defense is compared against PGD adversarial training (AT), SAM, randomized-smoothing-style "Smoothing," and TRADES. The authors report that conventional adversarial-training methods are "not entirely effective" at defending against DPA, and that their curvature-minimizing paradigm restores robustness better than the baselines (see Metrics/Findings). This is presented as evidence that standard adversarial training is insufficient and that curvature information "maliciously used" by DPA must be explicitly countered.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- CIFAR10 (Krizhevsky 2009)
- SVHN (Netzer et al. 2011)
- TinyImageNet (subset of ImageNet; Russakovsky et al. 2015)
- Models trained: VGG16, ResNet18, ResNet50 on CIFAR10/SVHN; ResNet18, ResNet50, DenseNet121 on TinyImageNet.
- Baseline poisoning/attack methods compared: EM (unlearnable examples), REM (robust error-minimizing), LSP, AR (autoregressive perturbations); backdoor attacks BadNet, Input-aware, LC, WaNet, SAPA; and (for the availability-poisoning framing) references to AP-Bench-style setups. Evasion attacks used as probes: FGSM, PGD, DeepFool, and CW (in Table 2).
- Natural-noise distributions tested: Gaussian, Poisson, Speckle, Rayleigh.
- Data augmentations / defenses tested: Cutout, Gaussian augmentation, grayscale, JPEG compression, pretrained initialization, SAM, adversarial training, smoothing, TRADES.

## Evaluation methodology
1. **Stealthiness of function:** compare clean-validation accuracy (ACC) of the clean vs poisoned model — the closer, the stealthier.
2. **Deferred vulnerability:** measure robustness of clean vs poisoned models against FGSM, PGD, DeepFool using the minimal-perturbation robustness metric ρ̂ (Eq. 6, after Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. 2016); and measure post-attack classification accuracy under FGSM/PGD/CW at ε = 3/5/8 /255 (Table 2).
3. **Transferability:** generate poison on a surrogate (VGG16/ImageNet-pretrained) and test transfer to other architectures (black-box).
4. **Natural-noise generalization:** measure clean-vs-poisoned accuracy under Gaussian/Poisson/Speckle/Rayleigh noise (Table 3).
5. **Visual stealth:** show poisoned samples and ×5-magnified residuals vs EM/REM/LSP (Fig. 5).
6. **Comparison with backdoors:** show that high backdoor ASR does not make a model generally more vulnerable, whereas DPA does (Table 4).
7. **Robustness to defenses:** data augmentation (Table 5), adversarial-training variants and the proposed curvature-minimizing defense (Table 6). The Experiments intro also states they "challenge our method with … limited poisoning percentages," though detailed limited-percentage numbers are not clearly tabulated in the read pages.
- Hardware: NVIDIA 3090 Ti / 3090 (used for the Hessian-cost measurements).

## Metrics
- **ACC** — clean-validation classification accuracy (stealth indicator; closer clean↔poisoned = stealthier).
- **ρ̂(f) = (1/|D|) Σ_{x∈D} ‖r̂(x)‖_p / ‖x‖_p** (Eq. 6) — mean minimal relative perturbation for a successful evasion; **smaller ρ̂ = more vulnerable**. Reported per probe attack: ρ̂_F (FGSM), ρ̂_P (PGD), ρ̂_D (DeepFool). p=2 for DeepFool, p=∞ for FGSM/PGD.
- **Post-attack classification accuracy** under FGSM/PGD/CW at fixed ε (Table 2; lower = more vulnerable).
- **ASR** (attack success rate) — used only in the backdoor comparison (Table 4).

## Main findings
(Direct paper findings; numbers as reported in Tables 1–6.)
- **Deferred vulnerability confirmed under the evaluated threat model.** In Table 1, poisoned models retain near-clean validation accuracy in several settings while their robustness metrics ρ̂ drop sharply. Example (TinyImageNet-ResNet18): ACC 0.72 (clean) = 0.72 (poisoned); ρ̂_F 0.74→0.48, ρ̂_P 0.50→0.42, ρ̂_D 0.28→0.09. Example (SVHN-VGG16): ACC 0.95→0.88; ρ̂_F 3.83→1.54, ρ̂_P 1.98→0.93, ρ̂_D 5.39→1.10.
- **Effective at very small budget.** In Table 2, at ε = 3/255 DPA drives post-attack accuracy of the poisoned model very low (e.g. CIFAR10-VGG16 "Ours": FGSM 0.14, PGD 0.21, CW 0.14 at 3/255), whereas EM and REM keep accuracy high at that budget (they reportedly need ε≈8/255 to take effect). DPA's clean-data accuracy under those same rows is ~0.75 (VGG16), ~0.76 (ResNet18), ~0.73 (ResNet50) on CIFAR10.
- **Transferability / black-box.** Poison generated on an ImageNet-pretrained VGG16 transfers to ResNet18, ResNet50, and DenseNet121, indicating cross-architecture transfer.
- **Natural-noise fragility (Table 3).** Poisoned models are much more sensitive to benign noise. VGG16 under Gaussian noise: clean 0.80 vs poisoned 0.45; similar gaps for Poisson/Speckle/Rayleigh across VGG16/ResNet18/ResNet50.
- **Visual stealth (Fig. 5).** Perturbations remain hard to perceive even at ×5 residual magnification, and DPA works at ε=3/255 where EM/REM/LSP need larger, more visible perturbations.
- **Distinct from backdoors (Table 4).** High-ASR backdoors (BadNet 0.95, Input-aware 0.87, etc.) do not make the model generally more vulnerable (their ρ̂ stay high), whereas DPA lowers ρ̂ substantially (ρ̂_F 0.58, ρ̂_P 0.50, ρ̂_D 0.32) — i.e. DPA's harm is a broad fragility, not a triggered misclassification.
- **Survives many augmentations (Table 5).** Under Cutout, Gaussian, grayscale, etc., the poisoned ResNet18 on CIFAR10 stays vulnerable (ρ̂ mostly low). JPEG compression and pretrained initialization recover robustness somewhat (JPEG ρ̂_F 1.16 vs no-defense 0.79), but the authors conclude DPA "can still render models vulnerable under most data augmentation methods."
- **Standard adversarial training is insufficient; curvature-minimization helps most (Table 6).** For poisoned ResNet18/CIFAR10: no-defense ρ̂_F/ρ̂_P/ρ̂_D = 0.79/0.57/0.39; SAM 0.84/0.57/0.41; AT 1.07/0.78/0.79; Smoothing 0.80/–/0.92; TRADES 1.19/0.82/0.85; the proposed curvature-minimizing defense ("Ours") 1.93/1.31/1.54 (higher = more robust here), exceeding even the clean baseline (1.62/1.01/1.24) on these metrics — but at ACC 0.71 vs clean 0.81.

## Negative results
- The paper reports (as a motivating negative for defenders, positive for the attack) that conventional adversarial-training defenses — PGD-AT, SAM, Smoothing, TRADES — are "not entirely effective" against DPA (Table 6): they raise robustness only partially relative to the proposed curvature-minimizing paradigm.
- Some data augmentations (notably JPEG compression, and pretrained initialization) partially restore robustness of the poisoned model (Table 5), i.e. DPA is not fully robust to every preprocessing defense.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Directly computing the input-Hessian / its largest singular value is computationally expensive (≈8 s per CIFAR10 image; ~p² backprops); the authors explicitly acknowledge this and address it with the HVP/Frobenius-norm relaxation (≈0.008 s), which they present as a bound-based approximation rather than an exact objective.
- The paper does not otherwise enumerate a dedicated "Limitations" section; the conclusion frames DPA as revealing "a novel challenge for AI security" and pairs it with the proposed defense. Beyond the compute cost, explicit author-stated limitations are limited — "not stated in paper" for a broader limitations discussion.

## Additional limitations identified during review (label as REVIEWER SYNTHESIS)
- **REVIEWER SYNTHESIS — clean-accuracy preservation is uneven.** The stealth claim ("performs normally on validation") holds tightly for TinyImageNet (0.72→0.72) and SVHN (small drops), but on CIFAR10-VGG16 clean ACC drops 0.89→0.75 (Table 1/Table 2), a ~14-point gap that a defender comparing to a reference clean model could plausibly notice. Stealthiness is therefore dataset/architecture dependent and not absolute.
- **REVIEWER SYNTHESIS — evaluation is image-classification only.** All experiments are CIFAR10/SVHN/TinyImageNet CNNs. Applicability to detection/segmentation, non-vision modalities, transformers/ViTs, or LLM/agent settings is not evaluated; the input-Hessian singularization objective may behave differently at scale.
- **REVIEWER SYNTHESIS — poisoning fraction under-specified.** The threat model assumes access to the entire training dataset (implying full or large-fraction poisoning). The Experiments intro mentions testing "limited poisoning percentages," but concrete percentage-vs-effect numbers are not clearly presented in the read pages; the attack's efficacy at realistic low-fraction contamination is not clearly quantified.
- **REVIEWER SYNTHESIS — surrogate-transfer breadth is narrow.** The "black-box" claim rests on transfer among a small family of standard CNNs; no evaluation against defended victims, heavily different architectures, or victims using their own robust-training pipelines end-to-end.
- **REVIEWER SYNTHESIS — the proposed defense trades accuracy.** The curvature-minimizing defense restores robustness metrics but reduces clean ACC (0.81→0.71 in Table 6), a cost not deeply analyzed; its own robustness to an adaptive attacker who anticipates it is not tested.
- **REVIEWER SYNTHESIS — robustness metric is attack-relative.** ρ̂ measures minimal perturbation for the specific probe attacks (FGSM/PGD/DeepFool/CW); it characterizes vulnerability against those attacks, not a certified robustness radius.

## Design implications
- Treat "passes clean validation" as an **insufficient** integrity signal for a model or a training dataset. A model can be normal on clean train and validation yet carry a deferred, curvature-encoded fragility that only surfaces under perturbation or noise. For the Origin/Guardian-Agent supply-chain posture, model/dataset provenance and integrity must be checked independently of clean-accuracy parity.
- Robustness (local curvature / input-Hessian conditioning) is a **first-class model-quality property** that should be measured explicitly, not inferred from accuracy. Include a robustness/curvature probe in model-acceptance gates for any third-party or MLaaS-sourced model.

## Implementation implications
- If ingesting third-party training data or third-party-trained models, add pre-deployment robustness screening: measure sensitivity to small L∞ perturbations (FGSM/PGD probes) and to injected natural noise (Gaussian/Poisson/Speckle) and compare against an independently trained reference — a large fragility gap at matched clean accuracy is a DPA-style red flag.
- Curvature/Hessian-conditioning estimates are cheaply computable via HVP (Frobenius-norm bound, ~ms per image), making a curvature audit tractable as a CI/gate step rather than a research-only measurement.
- Do not rely on JPEG/augmentation alone as a mitigation — they only partially recover robustness in the paper; and do not assume standard adversarial training neutralizes such poison.

## Evaluation implications
- Poisoning/backdoor scanners that look only for triggers or clean-accuracy degradation will miss DPA: ASR-based and accuracy-drop-based detectors are blind to a "broad fragility" poison (Table 4 shows high-ASR backdoors leave ρ̂ high, while DPA lowers ρ̂ without a trigger).
- Any robustness benchmark must report the *reference clean-model* robustness alongside the candidate; DPA's signature is a robustness deficit at parity clean accuracy, only visible in comparison.
- Metrics should include minimal-perturbation robustness (ρ̂) and natural-corruption sensitivity, not just clean accuracy and standard adversarial accuracy at a single ε.

## Deployment implications
- The named risk scenario is safety-critical perception (autonomous driving sign classification degrading under fog/rain/lighting or tiny perturbations). Deploying a DPA-poisoned model can turn benign environmental noise into misclassification — a reliability/safety failure, not only a security one.
- Runtime input-sanitization/robust-inference (test-time smoothing, input transforms) may provide partial protection but is not a guaranteed defense per the paper's augmentation results.

## Monitoring and incident implications
- Field monitoring should watch for **anomalous sensitivity to benign input variation** (e.g. accuracy that collapses under mild weather/noise/compression shifts) as a possible deferred-poisoning signature, distinct from drift or ordinary distribution shift.
- Incident triage for a "model works in test, fails in production under noise" report should include a curvature/robustness audit of the model and provenance review of its training data/supplier, not only a data-drift investigation.
- For agentic/autonomy stacks: a supplied perception or classification model that passes acceptance testing is not thereby trustworthy under adversarial or noisy operating conditions; robustness attestation belongs in the model-onboarding trace.

## Applicability boundaries (where findings should / should NOT be generalized)
- **Applies to:** image-classification CNNs trained on attacker-influenced data in an MLaaS / third-party-dataset supply-chain setting, evaluated against FGSM/PGD/DeepFool/CW and standard natural-noise corruptions, at L∞ budgets 3–8/255.
- **Should NOT be generalized (not evaluated) to:** transformers/ViTs, detection/segmentation, non-vision modalities, LLMs or LLM-agent pipelines; certified-robustness claims; low-fraction poisoning regimes (quantitative efficacy unclear); victims with unknown or defended training pipelines beyond the small CNN family tested; guarantees against adaptive defenders (only static defenses were tried). Stealth (clean-accuracy parity) is dataset/architecture dependent and weaker on CIFAR10-VGG16.
- The paper's language and this card's claims are bounded to "demonstrated under the evaluated threat model" — DPA is shown to *reduce robustness of the tested models against the tested attacks/noises*; it is not shown to be undetectable in general, and no claim of a proven-secure defense is made (the proposed curvature-minimizing defense is a partial, accuracy-costing mitigation, not validated against adaptive attack).

## Evidence strength (strong/moderate/preliminary/contested/insufficient)
**Moderate.** The mechanism is theoretically grounded (curvature/singular-value bounds) and empirically supported across three datasets, four architectures, multiple probe attacks, natural noise, transfer, augmentation, and adversarial-training defenses, with released code. Strength is capped by: image-classification-only scope, uneven clean-accuracy preservation, under-specified poisoning-fraction and hyperparameter details, attack-relative (non-certified) robustness metrics, and a proposed defense that is not tested against adaptive attackers.
