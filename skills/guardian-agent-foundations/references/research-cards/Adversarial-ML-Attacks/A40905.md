## Research question
Can a black-box model watermark for ownership verification survive not just model extraction attacks (MEA) but the *sequential* threat of MEA followed by watermark-removal attacks, and does existing removal-attack evaluation under-state that risk?

## Problem definition
Black-box watermarks embed a forensic "watermark task" (backdoor-style: artifact-modified inputs assigned new labels) into a victim classifier so the mark transfers into models stolen via MEA. The paper argues that (a) existing removal methods systematically fail against highly *entangled* watermarks, giving a false sense of security, and (b) a defender needs a watermark that survives both extraction and post-extraction removal while preserving utility and avoiding false ownership claims.

## System or model being studied
Image classifiers deployed as MLaaS black-box services (ResNet18 on CIFAR-10, ResNet50 on ImageNette). Watermark is embedded by the model owner; adversary obtains a copy model via MEA or illegal download.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: steal model functionality and then strip the watermark before redeploying the copy as a competing service (evade ownership verification). The paper also plays the *defender-as-red-teamer* role: WRK is an attack tool to stress-test watermarks.
- Adversary knowledge: MEA is black-box (query-only). Post-extraction removal (WRK) assumes white-box access to the *stolen copy* model.
- Adversary access: query access to victim for extraction; white-box access to the copy; a small domain dataset (5% of CIFAR-10, 10% of ImageNette samples).
- Adversary capabilities: learning-based extraction (MExMI, ActiveThief) with query budgets 25,000 (CIFAR-10) / 5,000 (ImageNette); FGSM-based large-magnitude adversarial perturbation, random relabeling, final-layer reset + parameter-shift regularization for removal.
- Attacker budget: as above (query counts, fraction of domain data).
- Phase: watermark embedded at training; extraction + removal at deployment/inference.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted ownership verification (watermark → specific label / class).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: WRK is an adaptive removal method purpose-built against entangled watermarks; CFW is then evaluated under the combined MEA+WRK adaptive pipeline.

## Trust assumptions
Watermark samples and training data are stored on a trusted timestamped platform. A verification process queries the suspected model with watermark samples. False ownership claims (watermark falsely detected in an innocent model) count as a defender loss — motivating class-level distinctiveness.

## Attack or failure mechanism
WRK (Watermark Removal attacK): (1) Boundary Reshaping — apply large-magnitude FGSM perturbations to a subset of domain data, assign *random* labels, and fine-tune to shift decision boundaries away from watermark regions (exploiting that backdoor-style watermarks place boundaries between source and artifact-modified samples); (2) Feature Shifting — reset the final layer and add a parameter-shift regularizer (coefficient α) to decouple watermark features from the primary task. Theory (Theorems 1-2) links MEA transferability and removal-resilience to Representation Entanglement (RE), lower-bounding the NTK cross-kernel norm by RE.

## Proposed defense or method
CFW (Class-Feature Watermarks): construct a *synthetic watermark class* by labeling diverse out-of-domain (OOD) samples as one unified class, eliminating vulnerable decision boundaries between domain samples and their artifact-modified counterparts. Two mechanisms make it MEA-transferable and post-MEA-stable: (1) a quantitative RE metric to guide representation entanglement during embedding; (2) pairwise-distance regularization to keep CFW representations compactly clustered so MEA distortion does not disperse them.

## Datasets and benchmarks
CIFAR-10 (ResNet18), ImageNette (ResNet50, in Appendix). Watermark baselines: EWE, MBW, MEA-Defender, Blend. Two non-watermark backdoors used for generality: WaNet, composite. Two MEAs: MExMI, ActiveThief. Eight prior removal methods as WRK baselines (e.g., FST, AT, NC/Neural Cleanse).

## Evaluation methodology
Embed each watermark, run MEA to obtain a copy, then apply WRK / removal baselines with limited domain data, and measure watermark survival plus utility. Watermark decoupling curves (WSR-vs-accuracy trade-off) compare removal aggressiveness.

## Metrics
Test accuracy (ACC); Fidelity (FID, victim-copy label agreement); Watermark Success Rate (WSR).

## Main findings
- WRK reduces watermark success rate by >=88.79% across existing black-box watermarking benchmarks, whereas eight prior removal methods fail against entangled watermarks. On EWE (CIFAR-10) WRK cuts WSR by 91.25% with only a 0.54% accuracy drop; accuracy stays within 1.15% of the original model, and it also neutralizes WaNet/composite backdoors without trigger reversal.
- CFW maintains >=70.15% WSR in extracted models even under combined MEA+WRK distortion while preserving protected-model utility, outperforming prior methods in resilience.
- Author claim: watermark resilience to removal positively correlates with representation entanglement (theoretically established, NTK-based).

## Negative results
All-outcome comparisons show trade-offs: EWE causes a 1.5% accuracy drop; MEA-Defender and MBW incur >10% degradation; only Blend preserves accuracy well among baselines. Trigger-inversion removals (AT, NC) fall quickly then stall above ~35% WSR — a documented limitation of inversion-based removal, not of the proposed method.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Methods are largely image-domain; text/other-modality generality is not the focus (most watermarking baselines are image-designed). Full proofs, ablations, and ImageNette results are deferred to appendices.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Evaluated only on two image datasets and CNN architectures; no LLMs, generative models, or non-vision modalities, so resilience claims should not be generalized to transformer/LLM ownership.
- WRK assumes white-box access to the stolen copy plus 5-10% in-distribution domain data; a weaker adversary (no domain data) is not the evaluated regime.
- "Correctness / no false ownership" (Prop. 3) relies on OOD-class distinctiveness; the false-positive rate against genuinely independent models is asserted more than exhaustively stress-tested in the visible text.
- CFW resilience is reported against WRK (the authors' own attack); robustness against future removal methods designed specifically against class-level artifacts is not established.

## Design implications
Ownership-protection designs should assume *sequential* attacks (extract then remove), not extraction alone. Placing the verification signal at the class level (synthetic OOD class) rather than sample-level artifacts removes the decision-boundary geometry that boundary-reshaping removal exploits.

## Implementation implications
Representation entanglement is a tunable, measurable embedding objective; compact clustering regularization is needed to keep the mark stable through the distillation-like distortion of MEA.

## Evaluation implications
Watermark-resilience claims must be tested with removal attacks tailored to the watermark's entanglement, not off-the-shelf trigger-inversion/pruning that provably under-perform on entangled marks — otherwise resilience is over-stated (a "false sense of security").

## Deployment implications
For MLaaS model owners: a watermark that only survives extraction is insufficient; deploy marks whose survival has been validated under a strong adaptive remover, and keep timestamped watermark artifacts on a trusted platform for third-party adjudication.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Ownership disputes should rely on stored watermark samples + timestamps; verification queries produce the evidence E. Monitoring for query patterns consistent with MEA (high-volume boundary-probing) is complementary but out of scope here.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to classifier IP protection / model-extraction defense in vision MLaaS. Transferable in spirit to any model_weights-as-IP setting, but the concrete mechanisms (OOD synthetic class, RE metric, WRK boundary reshaping) are demonstrated only for image CNNs. Do NOT generalize the numeric resilience (>=70.15% WSR) to LLMs, generative, or text models. Relevance to agent security is adjacent: it concerns model-weight/IP theft and provenance, not prompt/tool/agent-runtime security.
