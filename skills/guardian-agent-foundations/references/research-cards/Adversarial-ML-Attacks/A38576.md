## Research question
Do multi-domain graph pre-trained models (the basis of graph foundation models) leak training-set membership under
membership inference attacks (MIAs), and — since multi-domain pre-training weakens the very overfitting signals MIAs
rely on — can an attacker still reliably determine whether a specific node was in the training set given only a
single-domain shadow graph and embedding (not logit) outputs?

## Problem definition
Standard graph MIAs exploit that models are more confident/stable on training members. Multi-domain graph pre-training
breaks the usual attack because: (i) improved generalization reduces exploitable overfitting; (ii) diverse
multi-domain training graphs make domain-aligned shadow datasets hard to obtain, so a reliable shadow model is hard to
build; (iii) pre-trained encoders emit embeddings rather than logits, weakening membership signals. The paper audits
this privacy risk and asks how to mount an effective MIA under these three obstacles.

## System or model being studied
MGP-MIA, a node-level membership inference attack framework targeting multi-domain graph pre-trained GNNs. Victim /
target pre-training methods studied include MDGPT and SAMGPT (used as representative victims in toy analysis), with the
broader family (GCOPE, MDGFM, BRIDGE) discussed. MGP-MIA has three components: (1) a membership signal amplification
mechanism (uses inexact machine unlearning on a sub-graph, guided by a teacher similarity score, to re-induce
overfitting on remaining data), (2) an incremental shadow model construction mechanism (fine-tunes the target model
with Fisher-Information-Matrix parameter regularization instead of training a shadow model from scratch), and (3) a
similarity-based inference mechanism (attack features are cosine similarities of a node to its positive/negative
samples, since self-supervised pre-training pulls embeddings toward positives and away from negatives).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: infer node-level membership — determine whether a specific node v was in the target model's
  training set (untargeted privacy inference over instances).
- Adversary knowledge: **white-box** — full access to the released target model FTarget (architecture, parameters,
  training algorithm), but NOT to the original training process or the complete multi-domain training dataset.
- Adversary access: the attacker holds one shadow graph GShadow from a single domain sharing a similar distribution
  with the target node under inference; can fine-tune / unlearn on copies of the released model.
- Adversary capabilities: machine unlearning (gradient-based, selective), incremental fine-tuning with FIM
  regularization, embedding similarity computation, attack-model training.
- Attacker budget: limited single-domain shadow graph; no access to full training corpus.
- Phase: post-training / deployment — attack targets a publicly released pre-trained model; inference-time querying of
  embeddings.
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted membership inference over nodes.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: the attack is designed specifically to counter multi-domain generalization (adaptive to the
  defense-like property of the target); target models are static, not defending adaptively (reviewer note).

## Trust assumptions
Assumes developers publish costly pre-trained graph foundation models on open platforms for reuse, exposing white-box
model access. Assumes the attacker can obtain a same-distribution single-domain shadow graph for the node under
inference. Assumes inexact machine unlearning reallocates model capacity and strengthens memorization on remaining data
(building on Hayes et al. 2025), and that self-supervised embeddings encode positive/negative-neighbor similarity
structure exploitable as a membership signal.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Pre-attack analysis (toy experiments on Cora with MDGPT and SAMGPT) establishes two obstacles: **Takeaway 1** — output
embeddings are only weakly separable between members and non-members under PCA; **Takeaway 2** — member embeddings are
NOT more stable under edge perturbation (especially for link-prediction-based pre-training). MGP-MIA overcomes these:
signal amplification unlearns a random sub-graph so the model re-overfits remaining data (teacher score
s^Teacher = s^Target − λ·(s^Target − s^Augment); the unlearned model minimizes ∥s^Unlearn − s^Teacher∥²); the shadow
model is built by incremental fine-tuning of the target with FIM-weighted regularization to replicate its
membership-related behavior from limited shadow data; membership is then inferred from similarity patterns to
positive/negative samples.

## Proposed defense or method
None — this is an attack / privacy-audit paper. It proposes MGP-MIA (an attack). It repurposes machine unlearning (a
privacy-protection technique) as an offensive amplification tool. No defense against MGP-MIA is proposed or evaluated;
differential privacy and related mitigations appear only in the related-work framing (reviewer note).

## Datasets and benchmarks
Graph node-classification benchmarks used as source/target domains, including Cora and Computers (explicitly named in

## Evaluation methodology
Empirical MIA evaluation against representative multi-domain graph pre-training methods, comparing MGP-MIA to existing
graph-MIA baselines under the constrained single-domain-shadow, white-box, embedding-output setting. Includes an
ablation study (w/o UL = no unlearning-based signal amplification; w/o IL = shadow model trained from scratch instead
of incremental learning) and a hyperparameter sensitivity study over the FIM regularization strength α. Attack quality
judged by membership-prediction accuracy and F1.

## Metrics
Membership-inference accuracy and F1. Headline (author-reported): MGP-MIA improves over the closest baseline by 26.6%
in accuracy and 25.0% in F1 on Cora, and by over 20% in both metrics on Computers, against contrastive-learning-based
pre-trained models under the constrained setting. Per-cell result tables across all victim methods/datasets are in the
truncated results region — "not fully stated in the extracted text". MGP-MIA reported robust to α across a wide range.

## Main findings
1) First work (author claim) to audit membership-inference privacy risk of multi-domain graph pre-trained models. 2)
Existing graph MIAs are largely ineffective here because multi-domain generalization weakens overfitting and embeddings
(vs. logits) carry weak, non-separable, non-stability-enhanced membership signals. 3) Re-inducing overfitting via
inexact machine unlearning restores exploitable membership signal. 4) Incremental FIM-regularized shadow construction
yields a reliable shadow model from a single-domain shadow graph. 5) Similarity-to-positive/negative-samples features
extract membership from embeddings, giving large accuracy/F1 gains over baselines (e.g., +26.6% acc / +25.0% F1 on
Cora). Ablations show both the unlearning (UL) and incremental-learning (IL) components contribute.

## Negative results
The pre-attack analysis is itself a negative result for prior methods: multi-domain pre-trained embeddings are weakly
separable and NOT more stable for members, so naive confidence/stability MIAs fail — motivating the new design. No
dedicated failure-case section for MGP-MIA itself in the excerpt.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Strong (favorable-to-attacker) assumptions: **white-box** access to the full target model plus a same-distribution
  single-domain shadow graph. Effectiveness under black-box / query-only or truly out-of-distribution shadow access is
  not established here.
- Scope is graph foundation models (GNN node embeddings), not LLMs or agents; transfer of the embedding-similarity
  attack to text/agent memory is untested.
- No defense is proposed or evaluated; the audit demonstrates risk but does not measure mitigation (e.g., DP training,
  output perturbation) effectiveness.
- Machine-unlearning-based amplification modifies a copy of the target; its behavior across unlearning
  strengths/architectures beyond the tested victims is not fully characterized in the excerpt.
- Several full result tables and part of the experimental setup were truncated in the extracted text.

## Design implications
Concrete evidence that **publishing a pre-trained (graph) foundation model exposes a membership-privacy attack surface**
even when generalization is strong — better generalization does not equal privacy. For an agent-security lens: a
released model is data (its parameters leak training-set membership); model_distribution is a real attack surface, and
"capability to generalize" is not "permission to expose training records." Provenance/access controls on released
weights and privacy budgeting belong in the design.

## Implementation implications
Teams releasing pre-trained encoders (graph or otherwise) should treat training-set membership as sensitive and
consider DP-SGD, output/embedding perturbation, or access gating before open release. The attack shows machine
unlearning can be weaponized to re-induce memorization — so unlearning pipelines themselves need privacy auditing, not
assumed to only reduce risk.

## Evaluation implications
Privacy audits of foundation models must include MIAs adapted to embedding (not just logit) outputs and to the
generalization-hardened regime; naive confidence/stability MIAs will under-report leakage and give a false sense of
privacy. Report accuracy AND F1 across multiple victim pre-training paradigms (link-prediction vs. contrastive).

## Deployment implications
Applies to model-hub / open-weights release of graph foundation models. Defenders should assume white-box adversaries
with a same-domain shadow graph; mitigations (DP, embedding noise, membership-audit gating) should be validated against
amplification-style MIAs before publishing high-value pre-trained models.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Because the core attack runs offline on a released model copy (no queries to a live service), runtime query monitoring
will not detect the attack itself. Monitoring should instead focus on release governance: track which pre-trained
models are published, run pre-release membership-inference audits, and log/limit downstream fine-tuning that could aid
shadow-model construction.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to membership inference / privacy auditing of multi-domain graph pre-trained models (GNN node embeddings) under
white-box release. Findings generalize to graph foundation models and, by analogy, to embedding-output foundation
models where logits are unavailable. Should NOT be over-generalized to LLM prompt-injection, agent tool-abuse, or
black-box/query-only settings without new evidence, nor read as a defense result (it audits, it does not protect).
