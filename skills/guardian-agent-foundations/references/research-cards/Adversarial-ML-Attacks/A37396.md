## Research question
Can adversarial robustness of CLIP-style VLMs be improved by regularizing the model's *internal decision rationale* (cross-modal attribution), rather than only aligning final outputs, during parameter-efficient adversarial prompt tuning?

## Problem definition
Full-model adversarial training of VLMs is costly and harms generalization; existing adversarial prompt-tuning methods (e.g., FAP) correct only final outputs and overlook how perturbations distort the model's internal cross-modal reasoning.

## System or model being studied
CLIP (dual-stream image encoder I + text encoder T), frozen, adapted via learnable visual prompts P_v and projected text prompts P_t = h(P_v) inserted into deep layers.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: cause misclassification via imperceptible image perturbation.
- Adversary knowledge: white-box PGD attack used to *train/evaluate* robustness (standard adversarial-training threat model).
- Adversary access: input image; perturbation δ with ‖δ‖p ≤ ε (ε-ball).
- Adversary capabilities: visual-only perturbation (a central finding is that image-only perturbation shifts *both* image and text attribution maps).
- Attacker budget: PGD-bounded (ε, step count per standard setup; exact ε not in extracted text).
- Phase: attack at inference; defense applied during prompt tuning (training).
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted robustness (classification accuracy under attack).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: evaluated under PGD (and the framework targets robust generalization); an attacker adaptive to the attribution-alignment objective is not the focus.

## Trust assumptions
Frozen pretrained encoders are trusted; the attribution maps (forward attention + backward gradients) are assumed faithful proxies for decision rationale.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Key empirical observation: visual-only PGD perturbations induce a *synchronous* shift in cross-modal decision-attribution maps across both modalities (cross-modal disruption channeled through backward gradients that alter channel-importance weights w_c), fundamentally altering the model's decision basis.

## Proposed defense or method
CBA-FAPT: build a **bidirectional attribution map** fusing (a) forward attention attribution — dense cosine-similarity spatial weights λ_i (bypassing sparse softmax) decomposed from the final [CLS] feature, and (b) backward gradient attribution — channel importance w_c = ∂S/∂o_cls[c]. Combined map A_v = Σ_c w_c·λ_i·v_ic. Enforce **Cross-modal Attribution Alignment**: minimize ℓ2 distance between clean and adversarial attribution maps for both visual (L_VAA) and textual attributions, added as regularizers to the adversarial min-max objective with a natural-loss term L_nat (weights α, β, γ).

## Datasets and benchmarks
11 datasets: SUN397, Caltech101, DTD, EuroSAT, ImageNet, Food101, UCF101, OxfordPets, FGVCAircraft, Flowers102, StanfordCars. Settings: few-shot, cross-dataset, base-to-new generalization.

## Evaluation methodology
Compare against FAP (Zhou et al. 2024a, prior SOTA adversarial prompt learning) and related feature-constraint methods under PGD attack; report natural vs robust accuracy trade-off; Figure 1(b) shows mean accuracy across 11 datasets vs FAP.

## Metrics
Natural accuracy and robust accuracy (and their mean/trade-off). Exact per-dataset numbers are in the paper body/tables (not fully in extracted text); headline is a superior robustness–accuracy trade-off vs SOTA.

## Main findings
- Author claim: image-only perturbations synchronously distort cross-modal attribution — motivating internal-logic stabilization.
- Author claim: regularizing bidirectional attribution consistency yields a better robust/natural-accuracy trade-off than output-level methods across few-shot, cross-dataset, and base-to-new settings.
- The method preserves both supporting and suppressive (negative) attribution evidence via non-sparse maps.

## Negative results
Not explicitly reported; ablation motivates the non-sparse (cosine) attention choice over sparse softmax.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Not enumerated in the extracted text (the natural-loss term is noted as crucial for few-shot stability, implying sensitivity there).

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Robustness is demonstrated primarily under PGD; no adaptive attack that directly targets the attribution-alignment regularizer is reported, so measured robustness may overstate worst-case security.
- Attribution maps are heuristic proxies for "decision rationale"; the causal claim that stabilizing them yields robustness is correlational.
- Scope is CLIP-style contrastive classification; generative LVLM robustness (captioning/VQA) is not addressed.
- Hyperparameters α/β/γ, ε, and attack steps are not in the extracted text; sensitivity unclear.
- No code/artifact release stated in extracted text.

## Design implications
Robustness interventions can target internal cross-modal attribution consistency, not just outputs — useful principle for hardening multimodal encoders used as safety/classification components.

## Implementation implications
Parameter-efficient prompt tuning + attribution-consistency regularization is a low-cost path to robuster CLIP adapters without full fine-tuning; requires differentiable attribution during training.

## Evaluation implications
Cross-modal attribution shift is a useful diagnostic; robustness evals should include adaptive attackers against the regularizer and report full accuracy/robustness curves per dataset.

## Deployment implications
Applicable where CLIP-based classifiers gate content (e.g., moderation, recognition); improves resistance to imperceptible visual perturbation but does not certify robustness.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Attribution-map divergence between expected and observed could serve as a runtime anomaly signal for perturbed inputs.

## Applicability boundaries
Adjacent to agent security: hardens VLM perception components (transferable ML-security technique), not agent tool-use, prompts-as-instructions, memory, or identity. Findings apply to CLIP-style classification robustness, not to generative jailbreak/prompt-injection resistance.
