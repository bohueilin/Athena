## Research question
Is there an attack method that can destroy 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) invisible watermarks — removing the embedded copyright signal while preserving rendered visual fidelity — in a black-box setting where the attacker lacks knowledge of the watermark content, embedding, or detector?

## Problem definition
3DGS assets embed invisible 1D bitstring or 2D image watermarks into Gaussian parameters for copyright protection; extraction happens from rendered images. The paper argues robustness of these schemes against dedicated removal attacks is untested, and formulates watermark removal as a large-scale multi-objective optimization: find an adversarial model Gadv from watermarked Gwm that simultaneously minimizes visual-quality loss (F1) and maximizes watermark destruction (F2).

## System or model being studied
Target watermarking systems: GaussianMarker (1D bitstream, additive dedicated Gaussians) and GS-Hider (2D image watermark, replaces SH coefficients with coupled secured feature + dual decoders). Also surveys GuardSplat and 3D-GSW. The attack (GMEA) manipulates Gaussian kernels (prune via binary mask; shift DC/RGB color within ±ε).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: remove/destroy the embedded watermark (make it non-extractable) while keeping the rendered 3DGS visually near-identical.
- Adversary knowledge: black-box — no access to watermark content, embedding process, detector, or detector gradients.
- Adversary access: full read/write access to the watermarked 3DGS model parameters (post-distribution asset).
- Adversary capabilities: prune Gaussian kernels and perturb color values; render images to evaluate an indirect (detector-free) fitness.
- Attacker budget: bounded color perturbation [−ε, ε]; evolutionary population/generations (exact budget deferred to appendix, not fully in provided text).
- Phase: deployment / post-training (attacks an already-trained, already-watermarked distributed model).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted at the watermark signal (untargeted as to which bits, targeted as to destroying detectability).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: the attack is model-agnostic and does not adapt to a specific detector; defenders are not shown adapting (non-adaptive defense).

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the watermark is carried by convolutional-feature-extractable structure in rendered images, so flattening feature-map variance blinds any downstream conv-based decoder. Assumes attacker can freely obtain and modify the model file. No cryptographic binding of watermark to model is assumed.

## Attack or failure mechanism
GMEA (Group-based Multi-objective Evolutionary Attack): (1) K-Means clusters Gaussian kernels by 3D position into k spatially coherent sub-models to shrink the search space; (2) each sub-model is optimized by a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm (NSGA-II-style crossover, mutation, Pareto non-dominated sorting, crowding-density selection) over an individual = [binary prune mask, color perturbation]; (3) the detector-free objective F2 minimizes the standard deviation of convolutional feature maps (dispersion reduction, inspired by cross-task transferable attacks), rendering feature maps uninformative so watermark decoders cannot extract the signal; F1 preserves fidelity via L1+SSIM over multiple viewpoints; (4) optimized sub-models are recombined into the final unwatermarked model.

## Proposed defense or method
No defense is proposed; the paper is an attack/benchmark and calls for more robust watermarking. It positions GMEA as a security benchmark for the 3DGS watermarking ecosystem.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Blender/NeRF-synthetic dataset (objects without backgrounds) and LLFF dataset (complex real-world scenes). Targets: GaussianMarker (1D) and GS-Hider (2D).

## Evaluation methodology
Attack applied to the two watermarking systems on both datasets; compares full GMEA vs. an ablation GMEA (w/o F1) that ignores fidelity. Qualitative (Drums, Flower scenes) and quantitative tables (full table truncated in provided text).

## Metrics
Visual fidelity: PSNR, SSIM, MSE. Watermark removal: Bit Accuracy Rate (BAR) for 1D, plus proposed Watermark Uncertainty Score (WUS) and Information Destruction Score (IDS). Detailed numeric results deferred to appendix / truncated in the provided text.

## Main findings
Author claim: GMEA significantly disrupts near-perfect watermark extraction on both 1D and 2D mainstream 3DGS watermarking while maintaining high visual fidelity; the group-based decomposition improves search efficiency over naive evolutionary search; the detector-free feature-variance objective enables black-box, model-agnostic removal. Ablation shows single-objective (w/o F1) corrupts the watermark but severely degrades visuals, whereas the multi-objective form preserves fidelity.

## Negative results
The single-objective variant GMEA (w/o F1) removes the watermark only at the cost of severe visual degradation — establishing that fidelity preservation genuinely requires the multi-objective formulation.

## Design implications
Invisible watermarks that rely on convolutional-feature extraction from rendered outputs are a fragile provenance mechanism: an attacker who can flatten feature-map statistics can blind the decoder without ever seeing it. Provenance for high-value 3D/model assets should not depend solely on a removable statistical signal in the artifact.

## Implementation implications
Copyright/provenance enforcement for distributed model assets should combine artifact watermarking with out-of-band cryptographic provenance (signed manifests, registry attestation) so that removing an in-artifact watermark does not strip verifiable ownership.

## Evaluation implications
Watermark robustness claims must be tested against detector-free, black-box removal (feature dispersion reduction, evolutionary/pruning attacks), not only against benign transformations. Report BAR/WUS/IDS-style destruction metrics jointly with PSNR/SSIM to expose the fidelity–removal trade-off.

## Deployment implications
Any pipeline that treats an extractable watermark as an authorization or ownership gate is vulnerable: capability to render/modify the asset implies capability to remove the mark. Gate on cryptographic provenance and access control, not on the presence of a watermark alone.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Monitor for redistributed assets whose watermark BAR collapses toward chance while visual similarity to a protected original stays high — a signature of a fidelity-preserving removal attack. Keep signed provenance records so ownership can be proven even after in-artifact marks are destroyed.

## Applicability boundaries
Findings are specific to 3DGS invisible watermarking and conv-feature-based decoders; directly relevant to model-IP/provenance defense design. They generalize to the broader lesson that removable in-artifact watermarks are not a security guarantee, but the concrete attack numbers should NOT be assumed to transfer to image/audio/text watermarking or to cryptographically anchored schemes without re-testing. Not an LLM/agent paper — hence adjacent, not core, to agent security.
