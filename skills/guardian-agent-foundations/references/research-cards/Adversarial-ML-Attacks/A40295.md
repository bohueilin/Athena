## Research question
Do backdoors implanted in an LLM *before release* survive realistic, *user-driven post-deployment continual fine-tuning* — which is trigger-free, objective-shifting, and heterogeneous (full-parameter, replay, partial-freezing) — and can an attacker design a trigger that is *reinforced rather than erased* by such downstream fine-tuning, without any control over or knowledge of that fine-tuning?

## Problem definition
Prior backdoor work measures effectiveness at (or right after) poisoning, implicitly assuming static deployment. In practice, users repeatedly fine-tune downloaded models on new tasks. Trigger-free clean fine-tuning tends to dilute/overwrite the trigger→response mapping (clean-objective gradients dominate), domain drift shifts representations, and heterogeneous tuning yields non-uniform updates — all destabilizing brittle backdoors. Author-reported baseline decay: representative methods lose 50%–70% effectiveness after several fine-tuning rounds (Table 3). Two RQs: (RQ1) preserve backdoor function without re-injecting poison during downstream fine-tuning; (RQ2) remain effective without knowing future tasks.

## System or model being studied
Open-source LLMs from the Qwen2.5 and LLaMA3 families, released on public repos and then continually fine-tuned by benign users. L-layer transformer architecture (analysis follows Dai et al. 2022; BadEdit's setting). Backdoor injected via SFT before release using clean data D_c and backdoored data D_b (trigger tokens τ appended to clean prompts, mapping to attacker-desired responses).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: implant a trigger→response backdoor that persists (high ASR) through multi-round downstream fine-tuning while preserving clean-task accuracy.
- Adversary knowledge: white-box at injection time (trains the model locally before release); NO knowledge of or control over the user's downstream fine-tuning tasks, data, or strategy after release.
- Adversary access: pre-release only — sets trigger tokens and performs backdoor SFT; then relinquishes all control once the model is published.
- Adversary capabilities: (1) optimize trigger tokens τ to maximize cosine similarity between poisoned- and clean-task gradients at the final-layer token embeddings (GCG-style discrete search); (2) poison via standard SFT with the optimized trigger.
- Attacker budget: trigger-token optimization + SFT poisoning before release; no post-release budget.
- Phase: **training/pre-release injection** → **downstream fine-tuning (persistence)** → **inference (trigger activation)**.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific trigger → specific attacker-desired response); can be used to bypass safety controls.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: evaluated against a *worst-case* user "Cleanup SFT" that fine-tunes on clean samples of the very task targeted, plus cross-task SFT and continual-learning methods (replay, FREEZE); not a defender that specifically targets P-Trojan's gradient-alignment signature.

## Trust assumptions
Users download pretrained LLMs from public repositories and cannot audit integrity — the exploited trust. Users are benign, unaware of the backdoor, and may run a clean-removal step and multi-task adaptation *without intent to detect/remove* backdoors. The model's clean-task utility is assumed to be something users preserve during fine-tuning — which the attack turns into a persistence lever.

## Attack or failure mechanism
**P-Trojan** frames backdoor injection as *task-alignment optimization*.
- **Stage 1 — Alignment-optimizing trigger generation.** Optimize τ to maximize cosine similarity between the gradients of the backdoor loss L_b and clean loss L_c, taken at the token embeddings E_L of the final transformer layer (Eq. 2), solved via discrete search (GCG-style: rank candidate replacements by gradient cosine similarity). Operating at the token-embedding level avoids the curse-of-dimensionality of full-parameter gradient comparison.
- **Stage 2 — Poisoning.** Fine-tune with the optimized trigger via joint SFT on D_c and D_b (Eq. 3).
- **Mechanism/insight:** if backdoor and clean gradients are aligned, each step of the user's clean fine-tuning *reinforces* (rather than erases) the backdoor objective; thus preserving clean-task utility inadvertently preserves the backdoor. Theorem 1 bounds |L_b − L_c| by a term decreasing in gradient cosine similarity (Eq. 4); Corollary 1 bounds the backdoor loss after a bounded downstream update δθ (Eq. 5). Illustrative Table 1: BadNet gradient-sim 0.20 → ASR 100%→70% after SST-2 clean fine-tuning; P-Trojan gradient-sim 0.60 → ASR 100%→100%. Notably, continual-learning methods designed to *mitigate forgetting* inadvertently *amplify* backdoor persistence.

## Proposed defense or method
None — offensive. The paper argues clean-removal SFT does not ensure safety and calls for *persistence-aware evaluation protocols* and stronger defenses that explicitly remove dedicated backdoors during model adaptation.

Baselines: **BadNet** (Gu et al. 2017) and **BadEdit** (Li et al. 2024b). Fine-tuning strategies evaluated: full-parameter update, data replay, and FREEZE (partial freezing). ## Evaluation methodology
Inject backdoor pre-release on a target task; simulate worst-case user pipeline: (round 1) Cleanup SFT on clean samples of the *targeted* task; (round 2) Cross-task SFT on different-domain tasks via standard SFT or continual-learning (replay/FREEZE). Measure ASR persistence and clean-task accuracy across rounds on Qwen2.5 and LLaMA3 families; compare to BadNet/BadEdit; robustness check with GSM8K as target. Theoretical bounds support the empirics.

## Metrics
- **ASR** (Attack Success Rate) — fraction of triggered inputs yielding the attacker-desired response; tracked across fine-tuning rounds ("persistence").
- **Clean-task accuracy** (utility retention).
- **Gradient cosine similarity** between poisoned and clean tasks (mechanism metric).
- Author-reported headline numbers: P-Trojan achieves **>99% persistence** while preserving clean-task accuracy; **2–4× higher** post-fine-tuning attack success than BadNet/BadEdit; prior methods suffer **50–70%** effectiveness drops after several rounds. Illustrative Table 1 shows BadNet 100%→70% vs. P-Trojan 100%→100% after SST-2 clean fine-tuning. (Full per-configuration tables truncated.)

## Main findings
Author claims: (1) naively injected backdoors decay substantially (50–70%) under continual clean fine-tuning; (2) optimizing the trigger to *align* poisoned and clean gradients makes clean fine-tuning reinforce the backdoor, yielding >99% persistence with preserved utility; (3) the effect is task-agnostic (holds with GSM8K as target) and *amplified* by forgetting-mitigation continual-learning methods; (4) theory (Thm 1 / Cor 1) grounds why gradient alignment narrows the loss gap and bounds backdoor loss under bounded downstream updates.

## Negative results
For the attacker, the *baseline* (misaligned, e.g., BadNet gradient-sim 0.20) is the negative case — it fails to persist (ASR 100%→70%). No failure case for P-Trojan itself is reported in the available excerpt.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Persistence hinges on the downstream user *preserving the target task's utility*; if user fine-tuning heavily shifts away from the target-task distribution (low utility overlap), the alignment lever may weaken — boundary not fully mapped in the excerpt.
- No adaptive defense is tested that specifically detects gradient-alignment/trigger signatures (e.g., trigger inversion, spectral/attention analysis, fine-pruning); "persistence" is shown against benign fine-tuning, not against a defender hunting backdoors.
- The worst-case Cleanup SFT uses clean samples of the *same* task; effectiveness under stronger, backdoor-aware cleanup (e.g., neural attention distillation, large clean corpora) is not shown here.
- Full per-round/per-model numeric tables (Table 3) are truncated, so the >99%/2–4× claims are read from abstract/intro rather than verified cell-by-cell.
- Dual-use: the paper provides a recipe for durable, safety-bypassing backdoors; no mitigation contribution.

## Design implications
Model integrity cannot be assumed to "wash out" with downstream fine-tuning: a supply-chain backdoor (`model_distribution`, `fine_tuning`, `model_weights`) can be engineered to *ride* clean adaptation. Systems that fine-tune third-party base models must treat provenance/integrity as unresolved by fine-tuning and gate `safety_policies` behind independent checks, since backdoors can bypass safety controls.

## Implementation implications
Relevant controls: provenance/attestation of base weights (`supply_chain_controls`, `cryptographic_provenance`), backdoor-scanning before and *after* fine-tuning (trigger inversion, fine-pruning, distillation), and persistence-aware evaluation that fine-tunes then re-tests for triggers. Naive "clean-up SFT" should not be relied on as removal.

## Evaluation implications
Backdoor evaluations must be *persistence-aware*: measure ASR after multi-round, heterogeneous continual fine-tuning (full/replay/freeze) and across task-domain shifts — not only at injection time. Report clean-task accuracy alongside ASR persistence, and include forgetting-mitigation regimes (which can amplify persistence).

## Deployment implications
Enterprises fine-tuning downloaded LLMs may unknowingly *strengthen* a pre-implanted backdoor by doing ordinary adaptation. Before deployment of any adapted third-party model, run backdoor scans and require attested/clean base weights; keep rollback to a trusted checkpoint available.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Runtime monitoring for rare-trigger activations and anomalous outputs; maintain `audit_records` of base-model provenance and all fine-tuning steps to enable tracing and rollback. Periodic re-scanning is warranted because persistence survives adaptation.

## Applicability boundaries
_(where these findings should and should NOT be generalized)_
Findings apply to trigger-based backdoors in transformer LLMs (Qwen2.5/LLaMA3) under pre-release white-box injection and benign downstream continual fine-tuning. They should NOT be read as evidence that P-Trojan defeats *backdoor-aware* defenses (untested), nor generalized beyond the evaluated model families/tasks without testing. **Core** to agent security: LLM supply-chain integrity and safety-control bypass are central to trustworthy agent deployment.
