## Research question
Can a server-side framework defend Vertical Federated Learning (VFL) against backdoor attacks even when a malicious client possesses sufficient label knowledge — a setting that breaks the standard assumption that the server can protect label information?

## Problem definition
In VFL, participants hold vertically partitioned features and the server holds labels. Prior VFL backdoor defenses assume the malicious client cannot access labels; but attacks (He et al. 2024) show backdoors succeed with knowledge of only a few labels (obtainable offline). VFL defense is hard because: the server cannot access clients' local models (pruning-based defenses fail); it does not know the feature partition (unlearning via auxiliary data is hard); and it lacks control over client forward-propagation, so the attacker can poison at arbitrary epochs (early-training loss-difference defenses fail).

## System or model being studied
VFL system: one server, K≥2 clients; each client k holds a partial feature vector and a local model θ_c^k producing embeddings; server aggregates embeddings e_B = [e_B^1,…,e_B^K], computes loss L(θ_s(e_B), y_B) with server model θ_s, and returns gradients. Preliminary study uses ResNet18 clients (each owning half the features) + a 4-layer MLP server on CIFAR-10.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: inject a backdoor so the joint model misclassifies triggered inputs to an attacker-specified target label while preserving clean utility (stealthy, clean-label since labels live on the server).
- Adversary knowledge: acts as a legitimate client; has a local dataset and local model; crucially has access to a subset of ground-truth labels (sufficient to bind triggers to target labels). Does NOT have other clients' data/models or server-side labels during training.
- Adversary access: can embed the trigger at the input level OR directly into embedding vectors; can choose when to start poisoning (arbitrary epochs).
- Adversary capabilities: replace embedding vectors with a trigger (optionally + noise), or add a trigger to embeddings (e.g., VILLAIN), or move source-class embeddings toward target-class embeddings.
- Attacker budget: a single malicious client; poisoning confined to its own embeddings/samples.
- Phase: training (collaborative training).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific target label).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: evaluated against four SOTA VFL backdoor attacks; a fully adaptive attacker co-designed against FILTER is not the central evaluation.

## Trust assumptions
Server is honest and coordinates training but cannot inspect client models or the feature partition; it can inspect the embedding vectors it receives and the losses it computes. Defense operates in later training stages (since attacker timing is arbitrary) using current-batch information rather than historical epochs.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Backdoors in VFL are clean-label: (i) full embedding-vector replacement with a crafted trigger (He et al. 2024) — inter-vector distances among poisoned vectors stay small even with added noise; (ii) additive triggers on embeddings (VILLAIN/Bai et al. 2023) that keep poisoned clusters close to benign same-label vectors, hard to separate by embedding alone; (iii) source→target embedding-shift (Naseri et al. 2024); (iv) replacement of target-label embeddings with triggered ones (Zou et al. 2024). Because poisoning can start late, poisoned and benign losses under θ_s become similar, defeating early-stage loss-gap defenses.

## Proposed defense or method
**FILTER**, two batch-level filters applied in later training:
1. **Embedding-based filter** — for same-label vectors from a client, compute ℓ2 distances; cluster with DBSCAN; if a cluster (distance < τ) contains multiple vectors it is deemed "threat embedding vectors," and their labels are replaced with random labels to break backdoor association. τ trades clean accuracy (small τ) vs robustness (large τ).
2. **Loss-based filter** — three stages: (a) **Clean server warm-up**: after T epochs, init a second server model θ_cl (same structure, random init) trained only on the highest-loss subset (proportion p_init), exploiting that backdoor samples overfit to abnormally low loss on θ_s; (b) **Adaptive batch data partitioning**: gradually increase p; use a meta-split-inspired **Score** = loss-difference between θ_s (backdoored) and θ_cl (clean) per embedding to classify poisoned vs benign (poisoned samples: low loss on θ_s, high on θ_cl); (c) **Backdoor unlearning**: at end of training, unlearn residual backdoors. Gradients returned to clients come from θ_s; θ_cl is the clean inference model.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Five benchmark datasets: two tabular (e.g., German Credit, Bank Marketing per bibliography) and three image (CIFAR-10, CINIC-10, MNIST per bibliography). Four models. Four SOTA VFL backdoor attacks: VILLAIN, BadVFL, MASK, and an "optimal trigger" attack.

## Evaluation methodology
Compare clean-data accuracy (CDA) and attack success rate (ASR) with/without FILTER across 5 datasets × 4 attacks; ablations removing the embedding-based filter, using meta-split vs the proposed Score, and toggling backdoor unlearning; sensitivity to late poisoning and early stopping; t-SNE visualization of poisoned vs benign embedding clusters.

## Metrics
CDA (Clean Data Accuracy) and ASR (Attack Success Rate; target near random-guess level). Threshold τ and proportion schedule (p_init, p_max) as tuning knobs.

## Main findings
- Author claim: FILTER reduces ASR to near random guessing while keeping CDA close to no-defense (clean) training across five datasets and four attacks.
- Ablation: the embedding-based filter mainly helps against vector-replacement attacks (e.g., optimal trigger); with tiny τ it can misclassify some benign samples for attacks (VILLAIN/MASK) whose poisoned/benign embedding gap is small, slightly lowering CDA.
- Ablation: backdoor unlearning significantly reduces ASR with slight CDA cost; the loss-difference Score outperforms meta-split at separating backdoor vs benign.
- Author claim: FILTER is the first effective VFL backdoor defense that holds even when the malicious client has sufficient label knowledge.

## Negative results
Embedding-based filter can hurt CDA when benign/poisoned embeddings are close (small-gap attacks), and depends on τ tuning. Backdoor unlearning trades a small CDA decrease for ASR reduction.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Requires choosing τ and the p schedule to balance clean accuracy vs robustness; the defense is applied in later training stages (default after the 85th epoch), assuming enough clean training has occurred; effectiveness varies by attack type.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- Adaptive attacker aware of FILTER (e.g., shaping embeddings to enlarge intra-cluster distance above τ, or matching θ_s/θ_cl loss profiles) is not thoroughly evaluated.
- DBSCAN/ℓ2 clustering assumes poisoned same-label vectors are abnormally tight; attacks that deliberately diversify triggers may evade it.
- Relabeling suspected clusters with random labels risks harming benign majority clusters (the "largest cluster" heuristic could target a legitimate dense class).
- Scalability to K≫2 clients and to non-image modalities beyond the two tabular sets is asserted, not stress-tested in captured text.

## Design implications
In multi-party training, defenders can operate purely on *observable interface artifacts* (received embeddings, server-side losses) when they cannot inspect participant models — a least-access-compatible defense pattern. Dual-model loss-difference (backdoored vs clean-warmed) is a reusable poisoned-sample discriminator.

## Implementation implications
Server-side embedding clustering + loss-difference scoring + end-of-training unlearning can be layered without changing client protocols; tune τ per data distribution; apply defense in later stages to counter arbitrary-time poisoning.

## Evaluation implications
VFL backdoor defenses should be tested against multiple injection mechanisms (replacement, additive, source-shift), against late poisoning/early stopping, and reported as ASR→random with CDA parity; ablate each filter.

## Deployment implications
Enables VFL in risk-sensitive domains (finance, healthcare) under a stronger threat model (client with partial label knowledge); deployers must budget for τ/schedule tuning and accept small CDA trade-offs against small-gap attacks.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Tight same-label embedding clusters and θ_s/θ_cl loss divergence are runtime detection signals; backdoor unlearning provides an incident-remediation primitive (rollback of learned backdoor behavior).

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to backdoor defense in VFL / split learning. ADJACENT to agent security: relevant to multi-party model supply-chain and collaborative-training trust boundaries (insider/malicious-participant threat), but not to LLM prompt/tool/agent-orchestration attacks. Do not generalize CDA/ASR numbers beyond the tested attacks and datasets.
