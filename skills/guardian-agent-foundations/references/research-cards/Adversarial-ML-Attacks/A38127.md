## Research question
In hard-label (decision-based) black-box attacks where only the top-1 label is available, can Nesterov-style acceleration (momentum/lookahead) plus transfer-based surrogate priors reduce the query cost of ray-search optimization to reach a minimal-ℓ2 adversarial perturbation?

## Problem definition
Hard-label attacks must search near the decision boundary using only top-1 feedback, making them query-expensive. Prior zeroth-order (ZO) methods (OPT, Sign-OPT, Prior-OPT, HSJA) improve gradient estimation but rely on vanilla gradient descent, ignoring acceleration techniques that can speed convergence without better gradients.

## System or model being studied
Image classifiers as hard-label black boxes: standard ImageNet/CIFAR-10 classifiers, a CLIP (ViT-L/14) classifier, and defense models (adversarially-trained ResNet-50; MIMIR masked-image-modeling robust model). The attack reformulates the problem as finding the optimal ray direction θ* minimizing boundary distance f(θ).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: craft minimal-ℓ2 (targeted or untargeted) adversarial example that flips the top-1 label.
- Adversary knowledge: hard-label black-box — only top-1 predicted label; no gradients/scores/internals. PARS-OPT additionally uses transfer priors from one or more *surrogate* models (attacker-owned), not the target's internals.
- Adversary access: query the target model for top-1 decisions.
- Adversary capabilities: ZO gradient estimation via sign-based directional derivatives; lookahead interpolation between direction and momentum; Gram-Schmidt-orthogonalized surrogate priors.
- Attacker budget: measured in number of queries (curves reported at 4K–20K queries).
- Phase: inference (test-time evasion).
- Targeted / untargeted: both (attack-success indicator defined for each).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: attack is evaluated *against* defense models (AT, MIMIR); defenses are not attack-specific adaptive countermeasures.

## Trust assumptions
Realistic deployed-service setting: cloud vision APIs / biometric recognizers expose only the final decision. "Security-through-obscurity" (hiding scores/internals) is assumed but shown insufficient. Surrogate models are available to the attacker for priors.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Three-step per-iteration update (ARS-OPT): (1) form a lookahead direction θ̃t = (1−αt)θt + αt·mt interpolating the main direction and momentum; (2) at θ̃t estimate a biased gradient g1 (e.g., Sign-OPT/Prior-OPT style) and an *unbiased* estimator g2 of ∇f(θ̃t); (3) update θt+1 via g1 and momentum mt+1 via g2. Theorem 4.1 constructs the unbiased estimator (E[ĝ] relation) essential to the rate. αt is set as the positive root of αt² = ζt·γt(1−αt). PARS-OPT injects s surrogate-model prior directions (∇θ h(θ,λ) via a differentiable surrogate) orthogonalized with random vectors to improve estimation and can combine multiple surrogates.

## Proposed defense or method
This is an attack; no defense proposed. It provides theoretical analysis (O(1/T²) convergence under standard smoothness/convexity assumptions; implicit second-order curvature use without explicit Hessian).

## Datasets and benchmarks
ImageNet and CIFAR-10; a CLIP (ViT-L/14) classifier; defense models = adversarially-trained ResNet-50 (PGD, ϵ∞=4/255 surrogate) and MIMIR (RobustBench-evaluated). Compared against 13 SOTA hard-label attacks (BA, Biased-BA, Evolutionary, AHA, HSJA, SQBA, QEBA, GeoDA, Triangle, TA, SurFree, CGBA/CGBA-H, Sign-OPT, Prior-OPT, RayS, QE-DBA).

## Evaluation methodology
Query-efficiency curves (mean ℓ2 distortion / attack success rate vs. number of queries) for untargeted and targeted attacks across classifiers and defense models; ablations on prior quality D̂t, number of estimation vectors q, and number of priors s vs. the convergence bound ζt; controlled experiments at dimensionality d=3,072.

## Metrics
- Mean ℓ2 distortion at a given query budget (lower is better).
- Attack success rate vs. query count.
- Theoretical convergence rate O(1/T²).

## Main findings
- Author claim: ARS-OPT accelerates convergence via lookahead momentum and, with the unbiased estimator g2, achieves an O(1/T²) rate — faster and more stable than vanilla-GD ZO attacks even with identical gradient-estimation quality.
- Author claim: PARS-OPT (surrogate priors, multi-surrogate capable) further improves query efficiency; outperforms 13 SOTA methods on ImageNet and CIFAR-10 and on CLIP (Table 2) in mean ℓ2 and success rate.
- Author claim: best performance on ImageNet against AT and MIMIR defense models (Fig. 3).
- Ablations: larger prior quality D̂t, more vectors q, and more priors s each increase ζt and improve convergence; even small D̂t helps when the prior is effective.

## Negative results
Not framed as negatives; ARS-OPT alone (random orthonormal vectors only) has "inaccurate gradient approximation and poor query efficiency" relative to PARS-OPT, motivating the prior-guided variant.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Not clearly enumerated in the extracted text (discussion truncated). Convergence guarantees rely on standard smoothness/convexity assumptions and on g2 being an unbiased estimator plus a ζt bound.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- Theoretical rate assumes L-smoothness/convexity that real decision boundaries only approximately satisfy; the practical gain is empirical.
- PARS-OPT's advantage depends on having a useful surrogate; degradation when surrogate is misaligned with the target is characterized only via D̂t, not for adversarial/black-hole cases.
- Evaluation is on image classifiers (incl. CLIP-as-classifier); implications for agent/tool/prompt security are indirect.
- Defenses tested are robustness-trained models, not query-monitoring/rate-limiting defenses that specifically target decision-based attacks.

## Design implications
Confirms that hard-label ("decision-only") exposure is not a robust defense: query-efficient decision-based attacks keep improving. Systems relying on hiding scores should not treat that as a security boundary.

## Implementation implications
Query-efficiency gains lower the cost/detectability threshold for black-box evasion; defenders should invest in query monitoring, per-account rate limiting, and boundary-probing detection rather than score obfuscation alone. Surrogate-prior transfer means shared/public model families raise attacker leverage.

## Evaluation implications
Robustness benchmarks should report performance against accelerated + prior-guided hard-label attacks (ARS-OPT/PARS-OPT) at fixed query budgets, including on CLIP-style and robustness-trained models, to avoid overstating decision-based robustness.

## Deployment implications
For deployed vision/biometric APIs and CLIP-based classifiers used in agent perception, assume a capable low-query black-box adversary; layer rate limiting, anomaly detection on query trajectories, and abstention/uncertainty gating near the decision boundary.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Detect sequences of near-boundary queries with small orthogonal perturbations (signature of ray-search/ZO estimation). Log and throttle repeated top-1 queries on similar inputs; treat surrogate-transfer patterns as elevated risk.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to hard-label black-box ℓ2 evasion of image classifiers (including CLIP-as-classifier) and robustness-trained defenses. Do NOT generalize to prompt-injection, poisoning, or multi-step agent-tool threats. This is an optimization/ML-security advance; agent relevance is via CLIP/model-API attack surfaces.
