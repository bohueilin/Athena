## Research question
Can a single, unified defense provide end-to-end (detection + recovery) protection for Split Federated Learning (SFL) against a broad spectrum of data-poisoning attacks, given that SFL only exposes "smashed data" (intermediate activations) rather than full gradients/model updates?

## Problem definition
SFL splits a model into client-side and server-side halves; clients send smashed data + labels to a server. Attacks can target local features, labels, smashed data, and client weights. Defenses inherited from Federated Learning (Krum, Trimmed-Mean, FLTrust, ShieldFL, etc.) assume access to full model updates/raw gradients, an assumption that breaks under SFL's architectural split, so they are inconsistently effective. The paper seeks a defense that secures the smashed-data channel and both detects and *repairs* poisoning.

## System or model being studied
SFL system with N clients (benign + malicious), client-side model g, server-side model h, FedServer aggregating client updates and a main server aggregating server-side updates. Evaluated with ResNet18 and VGG16 backbones.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: degrade global-model performance or induce misclassification via poisoning.
- Adversary knowledge/capability: malicious clients with varying knowledge launch five poisoning types — Label Poisoning (LP), Data Poisoning (DP), Smashed Poisoning (SP, feature-level perturbation of intermediate representations), Weight Poisoning (WP, tampering parameters before aggregation), and Multi-Vector Poisoning (composite of the above).
- Adversary access: control of a subset of clients (malicious client ratio is a parameter); can manipulate their inputs, labels, transmitted smashed data, and weights.
- Attacker budget: fraction of malicious clients; adaptive attack strategies also evaluated.
- Phase: training-time poisoning.
- Targeted / untargeted: both (label-flip target shifts; general performance degradation).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: includes adaptive attack strategies and heterogeneous (IID / non-IID) conditions.

## Trust assumptions
Server-side is the defender vantage point; smashed data is the primary observable and the primary attack medium. Defender balances robustness and clean accuracy as a regularized optimization.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Poisoned samples produce smashed data that form locally dense but globally isolated clusters in feature space (high intra-malicious similarity, weak links to benign data). Multi-vector attacks combine LP/DP/SP/WP to evade single-vector defenses.

## Proposed defense or method
HealSplit, three components: (1) Topology-Aware Detection — build a KNN graph over smashed data and compute a Topological Anomaly Score (TAS) via Personalized PageRank; an adaptive threshold from kernel density estimation flags poisoned samples. (2) Generative Recovery — a GAN synthesizes substitute smashed representations for detected anomalies, validated by a consistency-validation student. (3) Adversarial Multi-Teacher Distillation — a Vanilla Teacher (semantic integrity from clean data) and an Anomaly-Influence Debiasing (AD) Teacher (uses TAS + Gradient Interaction Scores to build an inter-task influence matrix), balanced by momentum-adaptive optimization. The paper theoretically argues HealSplit reduces server-side gradient variance (introduces Inter-Server Gradient Variance, SGV) by improving gradient similarity.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Four benchmark datasets (references indicate MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and HAM10000 dermatoscopic images). Architectures: ResNet18, VGG16. Ten classical/advanced defense baselines: Krum, Multi-Krum, Trimmed-Mean, Median, Bulyan, FLTrust, DnC, FedDMC, ShieldFL, Sparsefed.

## Evaluation methodology
Experiments across multi-vector attacks (e.g., DP+SP), heterogeneous data distributions (IID <-> non-IID), model architectures (ResNet18 <-> VGG16), and adaptive attack strategies; compared against ten baselines on accuracy/robustness.

## Metrics
Clean accuracy and defense effectiveness/robustness (accuracy retained under attack); detection rate across classes (Fig. 2). Exact numeric tables were truncated in the extracted text.

## Main findings
- Author claim: HealSplit consistently outperforms ten state-of-the-art defenses across diverse attack scenarios, maintaining high accuracy even under adaptive attacks.
- It is presented as the first unified SFL defense addressing all five attack types (LP, DP, SP, WP, multi-vector) with integrated detection + recovery.
- Theoretical claim: HealSplit reduces the introduced Inter-Server Gradient Variance (SGV) by improving gradient similarity via anomaly replacement.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Detection assumes poisoned smashed data form topologically distinctive (dense-yet-isolated) clusters; a stealthy attacker that mimics benign topology or slowly drifts could evade TAS — not evaluated in the visible text.
- GAN-based recovery quality depends on limited per-round clean smashed data; the paper itself notes possible semantically inconsistent generator outputs, mitigated only by the student validator.
- Numeric superiority over ten baselines cannot be independently verified here because result tables were truncated in the extracted text.

## Design implications
In split architectures where full gradients are unavailable, the intermediate-representation (smashed-data) channel is both the main attack surface and the main defense observable; graph/topology signals over that channel can localize poisoning that statistical gradient filters miss. Self-healing (repair) beats pure rejection when discarding flagged samples would starve training.

## Implementation implications
Combine anomaly detection (structural) with a validated generative substitution and a debiasing teacher so that recovered samples do not re-inject attacker bias; use differentiated trust (influence matrices) rather than uniform aggregation.

## Evaluation implications
Poisoning defenses for split/federated settings should be tested across multi-vector, non-IID, cross-architecture, and adaptive conditions simultaneously, since single-vector IID evaluation over-states robustness.

## Deployment implications
Relevant to privacy-preserving distributed / edge training (e.g., healthcare with HAM10000-style data) where clients are semi-trusted. The server must run detection + recovery online, synchronized with SFL rounds.

## Monitoring and incident implications
TAS + adaptive KDE threshold provides a per-round anomaly monitor with per-class detection statistics; flagged clients/samples and their substitutions form an audit trail for incident review.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to Split Federated Learning under data/model poisoning. Adjacent (not core) to LLM/agent security: it is distributed-training integrity, transferable conceptually to multi-party / federated agent-training pipelines and to defending intermediate-representation channels, but the mechanisms are demonstrated only on vision classifiers in an SFL setup. Do not generalize the topology-detection assumption to settings where poisoned data are topologically indistinguishable from benign.
