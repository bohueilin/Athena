## Research question
Can Augmented Reality (AR) sensory data (sight/sound of a target) be weaponized to support physical social-engineering (SE) interactions, and can Multimodal LLMs enable hyper-personalization that bypasses human cognitive defenses?

## Problem definition
The fusion of AR real-time environmental perception with multimodal-LLM adaptive reasoning creates a new attack surface for next-generation social engineering. Prior work covers AR privacy risks and LLM-enabled phishing separately but does not systematically examine orchestrated AR+LLM SE attacks. The paper builds SEAR to test feasibility.

## System or model being studied
SEAR (Social Engineering Augmented Reality): a three-stage offensive pipeline — (1) AR-based Social Context Synthesis (RayNeo X2 glasses; RGB-D camera, mic, IMU; on-device MediaHolistic pose extraction, YOLO11m object detection, energy-band voice isolation, speech-to-text); (2) Role-Based Multimodal RAG (CLIP embeddings of scraped social media into a vector DB for role identification and dynamic profile generation); (3) ReInteract SE Agent (ReAct-based agent executing phased, adaptive SE strategies). Backbone MM-LLM: Gemma 3-12B; agent uses ReAct; server RTX 4090.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: build a hyper-personalized target profile and drive high-risk victim behaviors (accept a call, click a phishing email, disclose info) by establishing rapport/trust via face-to-face AR-mediated conversation, then exploiting via phone/SMS/email.
- Adversary knowledge: gray-box on the victim — harvests multimodal in-person cues plus scraped public/"grey" social data (LinkedIn, X/Twitter, Instagram, TikTok).
- Adversary access: AR hardware (covert cameras/microphones) in a shared physical setting; web-crawling of the target's public profiles.
- Adversary capabilities: multimodal fusion, role-based RAG retrieval, adaptive multi-phase dialogue generation and refinement via inference loops.
- Attacker budget: commodity AR glasses + one high-end GPU server; ~3.3 s one-time profile-generation latency that can be masked by a ~1-minute wait.
- Phase: deployment / inference (live attack execution against humans).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (per-individual profiling).
- Digital / physical: physical-world sensing + digital LLM/RAG.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: adaptive (ReInteract feedback loop adjusts to target responses).

## Trust assumptions
Assumes AR vendors do not mandate identity protections (no real-time face-blurring on tested commercial devices), public social data is accessible, and targets are susceptible to cognitive overload, authority bias, and social reciprocity.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Baseline AR-SE pipeline faces three challenges the paper solves: (C1) high AR-stream processing cost → lightweight on-device pre-processing; (C2) multi-person role mismatch → role identification via vector-DB matching; (C3) fragmented low-value profiles → role-based multimodal RAG prioritizing actionable insights (hobbies, vulnerabilities) over demographics. The ReInteract agent improves over standard/CoT/Act-only agents by adding strategic pacing (opening → engagement → trust-building) instead of prematurely narrowing topics.

## Proposed defense or method
Offensive study; no defense implemented. Provides dataset, toolkit, and analysis "to catalyze research into detecting and defending AR-driven SE," and suggests multi-layer identity protection (e.g., real-time face-blurring) in AR/LLM systems as a future direction.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Novel IRB-approved dataset: 180 annotated AR-mediated conversations across 60 participants in varied social scenarios (coffee shops, networking events); open-sourced with subjective-experience analysis.

## Evaluation methodology
IRB-approved human-subjects study; measures susceptibility to high-risk behaviors and trust-building efficacy; compares SEAR against baseline/standard/CoT/Act-only agent behaviors qualitatively. Some quantitative details truncated in provided text.

## Metrics
Susceptibility rates and trust/compliance outcomes: 93.3% of participants susceptible to email phishing; 85% of targets willing to accept an attacker's call after interaction. Authenticity gaps reported as a limitation.

## Main findings
Author claim: SEAR is highly effective at eliciting high-risk behaviors and building trust; it provides proof-of-concept that AR+MM-LLM meaningfully boosts SE efficacy through personalization, exposing critical gaps in current AR+LLM safeguards.

## Negative results
The framework has notable "authenticity gaps" — generated interactions are not fully convincing in all cases, a limitation the authors explicitly flag.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Authenticity gaps in generated dialogue; the study focuses on the first (rapport/trust) SE step most tied to AR, not the full downstream exploitation chain; effectiveness bounded by MM-LLM profiling quality and AR display constraints.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
Small sample (60 participants) in controlled scenarios; potential ecological-validity gap vs. real adversarial deployment; results depend on a specific stack (Gemma 3-12B, RayNeo X2) and may not generalize across MM-LLMs/hardware. No defense is evaluated, so defensive efficacy is unknown. Publishing an open-source offensive toolkit + dataset raises dual-use exposure despite the ethics/IRB safeguards.

## Design implications
Multimodal-LLM agents plus AR sensing collapse the cost of hyper-personalized social engineering: real-time perception + RAG over scraped identity data + adaptive agent loops turn generic phishing into tailored, trust-building manipulation. Agent frameworks (ReAct-style tool use) directly amplify offensive capability, making this core to agent-security threat modeling.

## Implementation implications
AR/LLM assistant platforms need identity-protection controls at the sensing boundary (on-device face/voice anonymization, consent signaling), retrieval-side restrictions preventing role-based RAG over non-consented personal data, and provenance on who/what a personal assistant is profiling. Treat the RAG corpus (scraped social data) as an untrusted, privacy-sensitive asset.

## Evaluation implications
Defenses should be red-teamed against adaptive, multi-phase, multimodal SE agents — not just static phishing text. Behavioral susceptibility metrics (compliance, trust acceptance) are appropriate outcome measures. Establish benchmarks like this dataset for defensive detection research.

## Deployment implications
Physical-world deployment of AR+LLM personal assistants introduces a covert-surveillance + manipulation vector; deployers should assume adversarial use and gate credential/action steps behind human verification that is resistant to trust-built social pressure. Wearables that can silently capture and profile bystanders are a standing risk absent mandated protections.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Detection research should target signals of AR-mediated SE: scripted rapport escalation, cross-channel pivot (in-person → phone/SMS/email), and profiles assembled from freshly scraped social data. Log and rate-limit agent-driven outreach; flag conversations that follow the opening→engagement→trust-building→exploit arc.

## Applicability boundaries
Directly applicable to agent-security threat modeling for multimodal-LLM agents, AR assistants, tool-using (ReAct) agents, and RAG-over-personal-data risks. Findings are offensive-feasibility, not defensive-efficacy — do NOT read them as evidence any defense works. Numbers are from a 60-person controlled study and should not be generalized to population-level attack success without broader testing.
