## Research question
Can an attacker reliably poison *agentic* (LLM-agent-based) fact-checking systems — which defeat naive RAG-poisoning by decomposing a claim into sub-claims, verifying each with adaptive retrieval, and cross-aggregating verdicts — by mimicking that decomposition and, crucially, exploiting the systems' own *justifications* to craft targeted malicious evidence for each sub-claim?

## Problem definition
SOTA fact-checkers (e.g., DEFAME, InFact) have moved beyond naive RAG to an agentic paradigm: decompose complex claims into sub-claims, retrieve evidence per sub-claim, aggregate to a final verdict with a justification. This decomposition + cross-validation makes existing attacks (e.g., PoisonedRAG, which targets the *main* claim) largely ineffective — their generic malicious evidence is irrelevant to specific sub-claims, unlikely to be retrieved, and outvoted by clean sub-claim evidence. Security of these systems is underexplored despite high stakes (compromised fact-checkers amplify misinformation or discredit true claims).

## System or model being studied
Two agentic fact-checking victim systems: **InFact** (Rothermel et al. 2024; AVeriTeC-2024 winner; explicit sub-question decomposition) and **DEFAME** (Braun et al. 2025; implicit dynamic decomposition with iterative query/reasoning refinement). Both take a claim, retrieve web evidence, and emit a verdict (Supported / Refuted / Not Enough Evidence / Conflicting-Cherry-picking) plus a justification. Attack itself is agentic: a **Planner** agent and an **Executor** agent.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: flip the system's verdict on a chosen target claim to the opposite of ground truth — endorse false (Refuted→Supported) claims and/or discredit true (Supported→Refuted) claims.
- Adversary knowledge: **black-box** — no knowledge of the victim's internal design, and no access to retriever/LLM weights or training data.
- Adversary access: can query the victim to obtain its initial verdict v_i and *justification* j_i for the target claim before attacking; can inject m malicious textual evidences into the victim's knowledge base E_i (which starts with M clean evidences) — realistic where KBs are built from the open web (e.g., AVeriTeC uses Google Search over 397,491 sources; social bots like @Grok / @AskPerplexity expose verdicts+justifications via API).
- Adversary capabilities: LLM-driven surrogate sub-question decomposition; justification-guided adversarial answer planning; strategic budget allocation across sub-claims; query planning to boost retrievability.
- Attacker budget: **poisoning budget** m (number of injected evidences), studied as poison rate 0.1%–16%; a core result is drastic budget reduction.
- Phase: inference/deployment (poison the live KB; attack at query time).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific claim, attacker-chosen verdict).
- Digital / physical: digital (text evidence injected into a web-sourced KB).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: adaptive to the agentic pipeline (reverse-engineers decomposition and exploits justifications); evaluated against three defenses (paraphrasing, clustering-based malicious detection, PPL detection).

## Trust assumptions
Victim systems build knowledge bases from the open web, so an attacker can post malicious content (Wikipedia/arXiv/web) that may be retrieved (per Carlini et al. 2024 web-poisoning practicality). The system *exposes justifications* to users (transparency), which the attack turns into an information leak revealing the evidence and reasoning behind verdicts — a "transparency–security trade-off."

## Attack or failure mechanism
**Fact2Fiction** (Planner + Executor):
- **Sub-question decomposition:** Planner mimics the agentic process, decomposing target claim c_i into a surrogate sub-question set Q_i (up to 10) so *all* aspects can be compromised (not just the main claim).
- **Answer planning (justification exploitation):** Planner probes the victim's justification j_i and plans a *targeted* adversarial answer a_k per sub-question that directly contradicts the specific reasoning the justification relied on (vs. a generic non-targeted answer), keeping all compromised sub-claims mutually consistent to avoid contradictions.
- **Budget planning:** assign each (q_k, a_k) a weight w_k by relevance to j_i and allocate budget proportionally m_k = m·w_k/Σw_s — concentrating poison on the sub-claims the justification shows are most decisive.
- **Query planning:** generate surrogate search queries S_k (up to 5) and, in the Executor, concatenate a query with the crafted evidence corpus (e_k,h = s_p ⊕ ẽ_k,h) to raise semantic-similarity retrievability, then inject into E_i (Algorithm 1).

## Proposed defense or method
None proposed — offensive. The paper defines a novel threat model and shows existing defenses are ineffective, "highlighting the urgent need for novel countermeasures." It probes three defenses (below) and evades them.

## Datasets and benchmarks
**AVeriTeC** (Schlichtkrull, Guo, Vlachos 2024) — real-world fact-checking benchmark; claims from 50 fact-checking organizations, each with a web-sourced KB (Google Search). Uses the **development split (500 claims)** (test labels hidden). Per victim, the evaluation set includes only claims the system *correctly* judged pre-attack, isolating attack impact. Baseline attack: **PoisonedRAG** (Zou et al. 2025). Victim systems: DEFAME and InFact.

## Evaluation methodology
Four evaluation questions: (EQ1) outperform existing attacks on agentic systems; (EQ2) component ablation; (EQ3) effect of poisoning budget; (EQ4) effectiveness of existing defenses. Vary poison rates 0.1%, 0.5%, 1%, 2%, 12%, 16%; compare Fact2Fiction vs. PoisonedRAG on DEFAME and InFact; test three defenses. ## Metrics
- **ASR (Attack Success Rate)** — fraction of target claims whose verdict is flipped to the attacker-desired label.
- **Attack efficiency / poison budget** — poison rate / number of malicious evidences needed for a given ASR.
- **Retrievability** — whether malicious evidence is retrieved (evidence-quality vs. retrievability analysis).
- Author-reported results: Fact2Fiction achieves **8.9%–21.2% higher ASR** than PoisonedRAG across budgets/systems; needs only **6.3%–12.5%** of the malicious evidences for comparable performance (an **8–16× reduction** in poison budget — e.g., comparable ASR at 2% on DEFAME / 1% on InFact vs. 16% for PoisonedRAG); justifications yield up to **12.4% ASR gain** under constrained budgets; **8.9% higher ASR at equal retrievability** (evidence quality matters beyond retrievability). Under defenses (Table 2, 1% poison rate), Fact2Fiction consistently exceeds PoisonedRAG across defense conditions.

## Main findings
Author claims: (1) agentic decomposition + cross-validation genuinely blunts main-claim attacks like PoisonedRAG, but Fact2Fiction defeats this by decomposing and comprehensively poisoning sub-claims; (2) system justifications are an exploitable, previously-overlooked vulnerability — a transparency–security trade-off (up to +12.4% ASR under tight budgets); (3) evidence *quality* (targeted contradiction of the victim's reasoning) matters beyond retrievability (+8.9% ASR at equal retrievability); (4) attack effectiveness saturates at method/system-dependent points; (5) existing defenses (paraphrasing, clustering detection, PPL detection) are ineffective against Fact2Fiction.

## Negative results
Author-reported: PoisonedRAG (the SOTA baseline) *fails* against agentic systems because sub-claim decomposition makes its generic main-claim evidence irrelevant/unretrieved and outvoted — establishing why a new attack was needed. No failure case for Fact2Fiction itself is highlighted, though saturation points bound marginal gains at high budgets.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Framed as future directions rather than limitations: investigating factors that influence attack saturation points and strategies to limit attack effectiveness; the urgent need for novel defenses. A dedicated limitations enumeration is not visible in the excerpt.

## Additional limitations identified during review

## Design implications
Agentic robustness is not automatic: claim decomposition + cross-aggregation resists *naive* poisoning but is defeated by an attacker who mirrors the decomposition and poisons per sub-claim (`rag_ingestion`, `retrieval`, `agent_to_agent`). System *transparency* (justifications) is a leak channel — expose reasoning cautiously, since it directly guides targeted evidence crafting.

## Implementation implications
Relevant controls: retrieval-corpus provenance/trust scoring and source authentication (`data_validation`, `supply_chain_controls`, `retrieval_isolation`); rate-limiting and de-duplication against high-density malicious clusters (noting the paper *evades* K-means clustering detection); and treating exposed justifications as sensitive (minimize or sanitize reasoning surfaced to untrusted queriers). PPL/paraphrase filters are insufficient (evaded).

## Evaluation implications
Red-team agentic pipelines with decomposition-aware, justification-exploiting, budget-optimized poisoning — not just main-claim RAG poisoning — and report ASR across a wide poison-rate range (0.1%–16%), attack efficiency (budget for target ASR), and robustness against multiple defenses. Restrict evaluation to pre-attack-correct claims to isolate attack effect (as done here).

## Deployment implications
Deployed fact-checkers and, by extension, agentic RAG systems that build KBs from the open web and surface justifications are exploitable to flip verdicts with tiny poison budgets (as low as ~0.1%) while evading PPL/paraphrase/clustering defenses. Gate high-impact verdicts behind provenance-verified evidence and independent corroboration; assume open-web KBs are attacker-writable.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Monitor retrieved-evidence provenance and injection patterns (sudden clusters of query-prefixed evidence targeting specific sub-claims); log which justifications were served to which queriers to detect reconnaissance. Maintain `audit_records` and rollback for KB state so poisoned evidence can be traced and purged; note that coherence-based (PPL) and density-based detectors are evaded, so provenance/trust signals are the stronger monitoring basis.

## Applicability boundaries
_(where these findings should and should NOT be generalized)_
Findings apply to agentic (decompose-verify-aggregate) fact-checking / RAG systems with open-web KBs that expose justifications, under a black-box KB-injection adversary. They should NOT be read as breaking systems with authenticated/curated corpora or without justification exposure (untested), nor as a general claim about all RAG. **Core** to agent security: directly about poisoning an LLM-*agent* pipeline via retrieval and inter-agent/verification structure, and about the security cost of agent transparency.
