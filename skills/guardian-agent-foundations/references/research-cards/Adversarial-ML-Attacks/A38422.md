## Research question
How can ensemble transfer-based black-box attacks be made effective ACROSS heterogeneous architectures (CNNs and ViTs),
given that the gradient update directions of heterogeneous surrogate models differ widely and prior ensemble attacks
fail to balance gradient-variance reduction against per-model diversity?

## Problem definition
Transfer-based attacks craft adversarial examples on surrogate models and hope they transfer to an unknown target.
Ensemble attacks combine multiple surrogates but transfer poorly across heterogeneous architectures because CNN and ViT
gradients diverge. Existing SOTA either over-smooths gradients (AdaEA — kills diversity) or optimizes surrogates
independently (SMER — unstable direction). The core challenge: exploit each model while stabilizing the shared update
direction.

## System or model being studied
An offensive method, NAMEA (Non-Attention enhanced Meta Ensemble Attack), operating over a pool of surrogate CNNs and
ViTs to craft l∞-bounded adversarial examples transferable to black-box targets (9 ViTs, 8 CNNs, 6 hybrid models, plus
6 defense models / 9 defense methods evaluated as targets).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: untargeted misclassification of a black-box target via transferable adversarial examples.
- Adversary knowledge: black-box w.r.t. target (no target gradients); white-box over its own surrogate ensemble.
- Adversary access: full control of surrogate models {f1..fN}; no query access to the target assumed (pure transfer).
- Adversary capabilities: iterative gradient attacks (I-FGSM / MI-FGSM / DI-MI-FGSM base), Grad-CAM attention
  extraction, meta-learning gradient merging; l∞ perturbation budget ε.
- Attacker budget: T outer iterations × K inner loops; perturbation bounded by ε (l∞).
- Phase: inference (adversarial examples applied to a deployed target); crafting is offline on surrogates.
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted.
- Digital / physical: digital (ImageNet images).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: evaluated against static defense models/methods; not an adaptive interactive attacker.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the attacker can assemble a diverse surrogate ensemble whose (non-)attention regions are complementary between
CNNs and ViTs. Assumes Grad-CAM attention maps meaningfully localize decision-relevant regions and that non-attention
regions of one architecture family overlap the attention regions of the other (empirically motivated in Fig. 1).

## Attack or failure mechanism
Key observation: heterogeneous models attend to sharply different regions; masking a CNN's attention area barely
affects ViT accuracy and vice versa. NAMEA decouples gradients from attention vs. non-attention regions via a
Non-Attention Extraction (NAE) module (Grad-CAM mask, attention filled with Gaussian noise), runs a three-step
meta-gradient optimization — (1) Attention Meta-Training, (2) Non-Attention Meta-Testing, (3) Final Update merging the
two — and applies a Gradient Scaling Optimization (GSO) module (layer-wise scaling for CNNs, channel-wise tanh
shrinkage for ViTs) to boost intermediate-layer / low-magnitude gradient transferability.

## Proposed defense or method
None — this is an attack paper. NAMEA is plug-and-play atop existing gradient attacks (I-FGSM, MI-FGSM, DI-MI-FGSM).

## Datasets and benchmarks
ImageNet (1,000 images sampled for the masking study; standard transfer-attack evaluation set). Attack performance
assessed on 3 benchmarks against 9 ViTs, 8 CNNs, 6 hybrid models, 6 defense models, and 9 defense methods. Baselines:
Ens, SVRE, AdaEA, CWA, SMER, CSA.

## Evaluation methodology
Cross-architecture transfer success measured as attack success rate (ASR) of adversarial examples crafted on the
surrogate ensemble and evaluated on held-out CNN/ViT/hybrid/defended targets, layered on multiple base attacks;
ablations on masking strategy (noise vs. 0/1 fill) and model-selection strategy.

## Metrics
Attack Success Rate (ASR, %) per target model, averaged over CNN and ViT target groups. Headline: NAMEA outperforms
AdaEA by an average of 15.0% and SMER by 9.6% on ImageNet; per-cell ASR tables (e.g., I-FGSM base: NAMEA CNN-avg 46.7
vs SMER 32.6) are in Table region of the paper.

## Main findings
1) Ensemble non-attention is the first approach to explicitly fuse CNN and ViT transfer information via attention /
non-attention gradient decoupling. 2) NAMEA yields consistent ASR gains across base attacks and target families,
surpassing SOTA ensemble attacks (AdaEA +15.0%, SMER +9.6% avg). 3) Filling masked attention regions with random
Gaussian noise beats zero/one fill. 4) The method simultaneously achieves stable update direction (vs. SMER) and model
diversity (vs. AdaEA).

## Negative results
ASRs are "almost unaffected by varying selection strategies" (author-reported robustness of the ablation), which the
authors frame positively; absolute ASRs on strongly defended targets and on some ViTs (e.g., PiT-B, Swin-B) remain low
in the tables, indicating transfer is still hard against certain architectures/defenses.

- ImageNet-only; no evidence for other modalities, detection/segmentation, or real deployed vision APIs.
- No code URL found in the extracted text, hindering independent reproduction.
- Stronger transferability is a capability that also raises the bar for defenders — the paper offers no mitigation.

## Design implications
Demonstrates that architectural diversity (CNN vs ViT) is an exploitable attack surface, not just a robustness asset:
complementary attention geometry is what NAMEA weaponizes. For defenders, ensembling heterogeneous architectures does
NOT by itself confer transfer robustness. Verification/monitoring must assume cross-architecture transferable examples.

## Implementation implications
Being plug-and-play, NAMEA composes with any future gradient attack — defensive evaluations should include ensemble
non-attention as a baseline attacker. Input-space defenses that only perturb attention regions are insufficient.

## Evaluation implications
Cross-architecture (CNN↔ViT) transfer should be a standard axis in adversarial robustness benchmarks; homogeneous-only
transfer evaluation understates risk. Report per-target ASR, not just averages, since some ViTs resist strongly.

## Deployment implications
Vision classifiers deployed behind black-box APIs remain vulnerable to transfer attacks even without query access;
defense-in-depth (adversarial training, input transforms, detection) is warranted, though the paper shows several are
partially bypassed.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Because the attack is transfer-only (no queries), query-side monitoring will not catch crafting; detection must focus
on input-side artifacts (noise-filled attention regions, high-frequency perturbations) at inference.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to transfer-based black-box evasion of image classifiers spanning CNN/ViT/hybrid architectures. Should NOT be
generalized to LLM/agent prompt-injection, to query-based or physical attacks, or to non-vision tasks without new
evidence.
