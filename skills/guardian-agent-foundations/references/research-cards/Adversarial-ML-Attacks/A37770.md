## Research question
Can Temporal Graph Neural Networks (TGNNs) on Continuous-Time Dynamic Graphs (CTDGs) be effectively poisoned under realistic, low-resource constraints — no surrogate model, training-set-only access — while remaining stealthy against anomaly detectors and robust adversarial-training defenses?

## Problem definition
Prior CTDG poisoning (T-SPEAR) trains a surrogate to estimate gradients and assumes full-dataset access (train+val+test) with arbitrary edits — unrealistic. It also only inserts edges (never deletes) and depends on surrogate accuracy. The goal: a surrogate-free, training-set-only poisoning framework that degrades TGNN link-prediction under four unnoticeability constraints.

## System or model being studied
Four SOTA TGNNs on CTDGs — TGN, JODIE, TGAT, DySAT — evaluated on dynamic link prediction over four datasets. Attack framework LoReTTA (Low Resource Two-phase Temporal Attack): (1) sparsify by removing high-temporal-influence edges; (2) replace with adversarial negatives via degree-preserving negative sampling.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: degrade TGNN link-prediction performance (lower MRR) by poisoning the training graph.
- Adversary knowledge: strict black-box — no knowledge of TGNN architecture, loss, or gradients; no surrogate model.
- Adversary access: can observe and modify only the *training* portion of the CTDG (no validation/test access, unlike T-SPEAR).
- Adversary capabilities: remove critical edges and insert adversarial negative edges; total edits bounded by budget Delta = floor(p * |E|).
- Attacker budget: perturbation rate p (studied up to 30% edges); default lower rates in main results.
- Phase: training-time poisoning (graph modified before training).
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted global performance degradation.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: tested against SOTA defenses and anomaly detectors, but the poisoning itself is heuristic (not defense-aware optimization).

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the training-data pipeline can be tampered by an adversary with partial access (a realistic data-supply-chain assumption). Assumes four unnoticeability constraints are what detectors/defenders check: (C1) budget, (C2) temporal feasibility, (C3) node activity window, (C4) degree preservation.

## Attack or failure mechanism
CTDG memory states propagate historical info and can dilute isolated perturbations; static/DTDG poisoning fails because past perturbations lose influence and future edges are unobservable. LoReTTA instead removes edges with high *temporal* influence (maximizing disruption to representation learning) and refills with structurally/temporally plausible negatives so density and local structure are preserved (evading detection).

## Proposed defense or method
LoReTTA — an *attack* framework:
- Step 1 Sparsification: score edges via a temporal importance function H(u,v,t_j) using any of 16 heuristics (edge-level: Degree/Jaccard/PageRank/Preference/Random; timestamp-level: temporal drift of Temporal PageRank vectors under 11 distance metrics incl. L2/cosine/Jaccard). Remove top-scoring edges to meet budget.
- Step 2 Adversarial Negative Sampling: for each removed edge insert a negative that (C2) draws timestamps from a KDE of empirical edge times, (C3) connects nodes active within window W (disjoint sets for bipartite data; enforces novel node pairs), (C4) preserves per-node in/out degree; a recovery step resamples timestamps when constraints deadlock — no surrogate, no gradients.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Four real-world CTDG benchmarks: Wikipedia, MOOC, UCI, Enron; chronological 70/15/15 train/val/test split. Baselines: T-SPEAR (SOTA) plus 5 edge-addition (ADD) and 5 edge-removal (REM) baselines (11 total). To be fair, even T-SPEAR is restricted to poisoning only the training set here.

## Evaluation methodology
Poison training graph, retrain TGNN, measure link-prediction degradation. Evaluate stealth against 4 anomaly detectors and robustness against 4 SOTA adversarial-defense training methods (incl. T-Shield). Ablate perturbation rate p and adversary knowledge; complexity analysis for Temporal PageRank and the timestamp selector.

## Metrics
Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) for link-prediction ranking; percentage performance degradation vs no-attack; detectability under anomaly detectors; robustness (retained degradation) under defenses; runtime speedup vs T-SPEAR.

## Main findings
- Author claim: average 29.47% degradation across 4 datasets x 4 TGNNs; per-dataset up to 42.0% (MOOC), 31.5% (Wikipedia), 28.8% (UCI), 15.6% (Enron).
- Author claim: outperforms all 11 baselines including T-SPEAR.
- Author claim: remains undetectable to 4 leading anomaly detectors and robust to 4 SOTA adversarial defense training methods.
- Author claim: up to 10x speedup over T-SPEAR (surrogate-free, low-resource).
- Ablation insight: attack impact is non-monotonic in adversary knowledge — targeting specific vulnerable edges matters more than more information; random removal stays stable across knowledge levels.

## Negative results
- Weakest degradation on Enron (15.6%), indicating dataset-dependent effectiveness.
- Performance degradation plateaus with increasing perturbation rate p (diminishing returns beyond a point).
- Additional attacker knowledge can yield diminishing or negative returns once critical edges are already removed.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_

## Design implications
Data-poisoning threat model highly relevant to agent/ML supply chains: an adversary with only partial (training-set) access and no surrogate can degrade a temporal-graph model used for fraud detection, recommendation, or forecasting while evading anomaly detection. "Training-data integrity is a first-class control; unnoticeability constraints define the detector's blind spots."

## Implementation implications
Pipelines ingesting streaming/temporal interaction data should validate provenance, monitor for anomalous *removal* of high-influence edges (not just insertions), and not rely solely on degree/temporal-distribution checks that LoReTTA is designed to satisfy.

## Evaluation implications
Poisoning evaluations should adopt realistic access models (train-only, surrogate-free) and test against both anomaly detectors and robust-training defenses; report non-monotonic knowledge effects. Restrict baselines to the same access budget for fairness (as done here for T-SPEAR).

## Deployment implications
Adjacent to agent security: matters for agents/services relying on TGNN outputs (fraud, recommendation, traffic). Not about LLM/tool/prompt security directly; the asset at risk is training_data and downstream model_outputs.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Detectors should track edge *deletions* of temporally influential edges and sudden shifts in Temporal PageRank drift, since LoReTTA preserves degree/timestamp distributions specifically to hide from standard monitors.

## Applicability boundaries
Generalizes to poisoning link-prediction TGNNs on CTDGs under partial-access, surrogate-free adversaries. Do NOT generalize to LLM-agent prompt/tool threats, to evasion (inference-time) attacks, or to static-graph poisoning without adaptation. The paper explicitly notes static/DTDG poisoning does not transfer to CTDGs.
