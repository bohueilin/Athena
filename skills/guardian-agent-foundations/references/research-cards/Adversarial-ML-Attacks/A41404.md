## Research question
How can a face-recognition model be defended against imperceptible adversarial noise that is specific to each individual image and recognition model, in an open-set recognition setting requiring per-sample customized processing?

## Problem definition
Deep face-recognition models are vulnerable to adversarial noise whose defining property is specificity — perturbations tailored to the individual image and model, producing catastrophic errors. Open-set face recognition makes this harder, motivating customized per-input defense rather than a single fixed transform.

## System or model being studied
Deep face-recognition models under adversarial attack, defended by a proposed biologically-inspired "artificial immune system." (Architecture/backbone details not in the reprint.)

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the defender can apply a customized per-input noise-removal ("antibody") process and can run a self-supervised adversarial-training "rehearsal." Underlying assumptions not detailed in the reprint.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Adversarial noise, unlike general noise, is specific to the sample and model, yielding catastrophic recognition errors; diverse samples/models induce diverse noise patterns, which is why a single static defense is argued insufficient.

## Proposed defense or method
An artificial immune system for adversarial defense: it generates a distinct "antibody" (a specialized, per-sample noise-removal manner) for each input, using immune-system principles — antibody cloning, mutation, selection, and memory. It adds a self-supervised adversarial-training mechanism as a simulated "rehearsal of immune-system invasions." (Formal method details are in the original IJCV paper, not the reprint.)
