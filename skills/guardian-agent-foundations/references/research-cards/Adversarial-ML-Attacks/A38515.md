## Research question
Can perceptual (GAN-based) learned image compression be improved beyond coarse patch-level PatchGAN training by adding
(1) semantic consistency and (2) spectral (high-frequency) consistency, so reconstructions better match the natural
image distribution at low bit rates — without changing the codec architecture or adding deployment complexity?

## Problem definition
PatchGAN-based perceptual codecs enforce only patch-level realism and ignore global structure/semantics and spectral
energy distribution, producing artifacts in semantically rich regions (grass, waves) and unnatural high-frequency
textures at low bit rates. The paper seeks finer distribution alignment.

## System or model being studied
SSCL, a learned image compression framework built on the MLIC++ backbone, adding two discriminators to the standard
patch-wise adversarial training: a Semantic Discriminator (D_s) conditioned on frozen CLIP/ResNet-50 features via
Semantic Conditioning Blocks (cross-attention), and a Spectral Discriminator (D_F) operating on FFT power-spectrum
tokens with latent tokens as conditioning, processed by ViT blocks.

## Trust assumptions
Standard image-compression training; assumes a pretrained frozen semantic vision model (CLIP, 400M image-text pairs)
provides a strong semantic prior. No assumption about malicious inputs, poisoning, watermark attacks, or model
extraction.

## Attack or failure mechanism
No attack. The addressed "failure" is perceptual: patch-level GAN training misaligns semantic and spectral statistics.
The rate-distortion-perception tradeoff (Blau & Michaeli 2019) — improving perception increases distortion, bound
E[∥x̂−x∥²] ≤ 2 E[∥E[x|ŷ]−x∥²] — frames the objective.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a security defense. Method: Semantic + Spectral Consistency Learning added to PatchGAN. Semantic discriminator uses
CLIP features as cross-attention conditions (SCB) to guide texture synthesis without transmitting semantics; spectral
discriminator tokenizes images/latents, applies FFT (32×32 tokens) to a power spectrum, and uses ViT self-attention +
channel mixing to suppress high-frequency artifacts. Loss: L = λ_R R + λ_d d(x,x̂) + β(λ_P LPIPS + λ_adv L_G).

## Datasets and benchmarks
Training on ImageNet / DIV2K / Flickr2K (1M steps, batch 16, Adam, lr 1e-4, 2× A100). Evaluation on CLIC2020 and Kodak.
Baselines include MS-ILLM, TACO, Control-GIC, EGIC, HiFiC, MRIC, DiffEIC, CRDR, VTM-17.0, ELIC, LIC-TCM, FTIC, MLIC++,
DCAE, CDC.

## Evaluation methodology
Rate-Distortion-Perception curves and BD-Rate (w.r.t. MS-ILLM) on CLIC2020 and Kodak. Distortion metrics: PSNR,
MS-SSIM. Perceptual metrics: FID, KID, LPIPS(VGG), DISTS. All metrics on 8-bit PNG reconstructions. No
security/robustness evaluation.

## Metrics
BD-Rate (%): SSCL achieves −35.9% (FID) and −48.0% (LPIPS) on CLIC2020 vs MS-ILLM; headline 45–60% bit-rate savings
(DISTS) on CLIC2020 and Kodak; matches MS-ILLM FID while cutting bit rate 35.91%. No attack-success or robustness
metric.

## Main findings
1) First semantic discriminator and first spectral discriminator for image compression (author claims), added without
codec architectural change. 2) State-of-the-art perceptual quality with large BD-rate savings over MS-ILLM. 3)
Noise-filled/attention/frequency conditioning improves texture and reduces high-frequency artifacts.

## Negative results
The rate-distortion-perception tradeoff means perceptual gains can raise pixel distortion; some distortion-oriented
baselines still lead on PSNR/MS-SSIM (expected per Blau & Michaeli). FID omitted for Kodak due to small dataset size
(24 images) yielding unreliable statistics.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Metrics computed on 8-bit PNG may differ from float32 reconstructions reported elsewhere; FID unreliable on small Kodak.
