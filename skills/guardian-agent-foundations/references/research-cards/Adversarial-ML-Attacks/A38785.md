# A38785 Hide and Seek with LLMs: An Adversarial Game for Sneaky Error Generation and Self-Improving Diagnosis

> **Corpus-label note:** This card is filed under the corpus category **Adversarial-ML-Attacks**, but the paper's true topic is **adversarial self-play / adversarial reinforcement learning to *improve* an LLM's error-diagnosis capability** in mathematical reasoning. The "adversary" (the Sneaky role) is an *internal, cooperative training component*, not an external threat actor attacking a deployed system. The security relevance is indirect and dual-use (see Design/Deployment/Monitoring implications): the method is a recipe for producing *deceptive reasoning traces that survive surface-level review*, and it releases a benchmark of such traces. Treat the "attack" framing as reviewer-supplied, not paper-supplied.

## Research question
How can an LLM's ability to **recognize, localize, and diagnose subtle reasoning errors** be improved when standard training rewards only correct answers and therefore exposes models to few, shallow errors? Concretely: can an adversarial "generation vs. diagnosis" self-play loop produce (a) increasingly *stealthy* errors and (b) a *stronger diagnostic* model, in mathematical reasoning?

## Problem definition
- LLMs reason and generate well but are poor at *self-diagnosing* complex mistakes; mainstream training optimizes for correct final answers, giving little exposure to diverse error patterns and correction processes (paper cites Huang et al. 2024; Tyen et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2024).
- Prior error-utilization work splits into two families, both judged insufficient by the authors:
  - **Static Error Generation** — pre-existing dataset errors or naturally-occurring inference failures. Fixed, patterned, shallow, low stealthiness; models can memorize/pattern-match rather than learn deep diagnosis (high scores on static benchmarks may not reflect true diagnostic skill; performance drops on perturbed variants, e.g. Putnam-AXIOM).
  - **Dynamic Error Construction** — Generator–Evaluator / Prover–Verifier setups (Kirchner et al. 2024). Limited by the evaluator's own weaknesses; produced errors favor superficial disguises (altered conclusions/constants, reversed causality) and are relatively easy for humans/moderately-trained diagnosers to spot; often template-based; oriented to output verifiability/readability rather than dedicated error *recognition*.
- Goal: a closed-loop, difficulty-modulating framework that yields both *harder-to-detect* errors and a *better diagnoser*, using correction success as the training signal.

## System or model being studied
- **HSG (Hide and Seek Game)** — an adversarial-RL framework with two optimized roles and one non-optimized auxiliary role:
  - **S (Sneaky, generator)** — given a math question `q`, produces a step-by-step answer `a_S` that *appears correct* but contains at least one subtle, critical error; goal is to be hard to diagnose ("Hide").
  - **D (Diagnosis)** — given reference answer `a_truth` and `a_S`, outputs a diagnostic report `a_D` identifying whether/where errors exist ("Seek").
  - **Correction model (π_correct, auxiliary)** — consumes `a_S` + `a_D` and produces a corrected answer `a_C`. **Fixed / not optimized**; it only emits training signals (correction success/failure).
- S and D are **prompt-driven, parameter-shared** policies (πθ_S, πθ_D; see Fig. 3 prompts). Base model for all experiments: **Qwen3-4B**.
- Interaction mechanics: S updates its policy from D's feedback (harder errors); D improves detection using correction signals; over RL, S continually raises difficulty for D — an adversarial + partly cooperative co-evolution.

## Threat model
This paper does **not** define a security threat model in the attacker-vs-defender sense; the "adversary" is a training role. Mapping onto the card's axes (interpreting S as the error-producing agent and D as the detector), based only on what the paper states:
- **Adversary objective:** S generates mathematically *incorrect* final answers whose reasoning *looks* correct and is *hard to detect and hard to correct* (main reward `1 − r_corr(a_S)`; adversarial feedback also rewards making D fail and correction fail).
- **Knowledge/access:** S and D are internal, white-box components of the same training system (shared base model, same GRPO pipeline). S receives feedback derived from D's diagnosis and the correction model's success. This is an *internal, fully-observed* setup, not an external black-box attacker against a third party.
- **Capabilities/budget:** Reinforcement learning over 600 steps on Qwen3-4B, 4× A800 GPUs, GRPO with `G = 8` samples/round.
- **Box:** White-box internal self-play. **Time:** training-time construction (produces a benchmark + a trained diagnoser); at inference the trained D/D* is applied to new errors. **Targeted vs untargeted:** targeted at producing incorrect final answers with hidden faults (a specified error type/location). **Digital** only (math text). **Adaptive:** yes — S and D co-adapt across training; the paper explicitly frames this as the advantage over a fixed rater.
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) The paper contains **no** deployment/attacker threat model, no attack surface against production systems, and no adversarial robustness evaluation of a victim model. Security framing must be supplied externally.

## Trust assumptions
- Reference answers `a_truth` are trusted ground truth used to score correctness (`Γ_correct`) and to condition D and correction.
- The **correction model π_correct is fixed and trusted** as the arbiter of "was the diagnosis useful" (its success = signal). If π_correct is weak/biased, S/D optimize against a flawed judge — the authors adopt this deliberately but do not stress-test its reliability.
- GPT-4o is trusted as an external judge for the win-rate comparison (Table 2), with answer order swapped to reduce positional bias.
- Correctness/format/length are assumed to be well-defined and cheaply checkable — true for math with boxed final answers; not assumed to generalize (see Applicability).

## Attack or failure mechanism
The mechanism of interest (what makes errors "sneaky"):
- S is prompted (Fig. 3) to produce a detailed, plausible, step-by-step solution, introduce ≥1 subtle-but-critical error leading to a wrong `\boxed{}` result, **not** self-correct later, and **never** reveal the answer is intentionally wrong.
- Reward shaping drives *stealth*: adversarial feedback `R_S^adversarial = R(R_S, R(1 − R_D^collaborative, 1 − r_corr(a_C)))` rewards S when D mis-judges *and* when correction fails — i.e., errors that defeat both detection and repair.
- The paper's error taxonomy for *why prior methods are easy to catch*:
  - **Type A (Result Shift):** reasoning is correct but the final answer is silently changed to differ from reference — detectable by mutual confirmation between roles.
  - **Type B (Explicit Contamination):** reasoning contains overt hints/mistakes that are easy to spot — filtered out interactively.
  - HSG's claim: its dialogue-based adversarial pressure **suppresses both** Type A and Type B, yielding errors that are subtle at the *reasoning* level rather than at the answer or surface level.

## Proposed defense or method
- **Hierarchical reward** `R(r_main, r_secondary) = max(r_main, τ) · [β + (1−β) r_secondary]`, with lower-bound `τ = 0.05` (threshold clipping to keep exploration alive) and weight `β = 0.6`. Main reward acts as an amplifier for the auxiliary reward.
- **Shared components:** correctness reward `r_corr` (1 if answer matches truth else 0); length reward `r_length` (target band `L_min = 50`, `L_max = 600`, quadratic penalties outside); format reward `r_format ∈ {0,1}` (enforces `\boxed{}` / templates).
- **Role rewards:**
  - S: `R_S = R(1 − r_corr(a_S), R(r_format(a_S), r_length(a_S)))` — main reward rewards *incorrect* answers; auxiliary enforces format then length.
  - D: `R_D = R(Γ_diagnosis(a_D, r_corr(a_S)), R(r_format(a_D), r_length(a_D)))` where `Γ_diagnosis = 1` iff `a_D` correctly reflects the correctness of `a_S`.
- **Feedback rewards** (inter-role): correction success `r_corr(a_C)`; collaborative feedback for D `R_D^collaborative = R(R_D, r_corr(a_C))` (D rewarded both for correct judgment and for enabling successful correction); adversarial feedback for S as above.
- **RL algorithm:** GRPO (Shao et al. 2024) — group-relative advantages, no value network. `G = 8` samples/round, clip `ε = 0.2`, stability `δ = 1e-8`. KL coefficient `β = 0.01` for S (initial difficulty generating stealthy errors) and `β = 0.04` for D. S and D optimization alternates; for D-training, S's *highest-reward* (most challenging) sample is selected per round.
- **Deliverable:** the final selected checkpoint is the one with the **lowest** correction success rate `ACC_corr` (i.e., the *most deceptive* Sneaky model); the co-trained diagnoser is denoted **D\***, contrasted with an untrained baseline diagnoser **D**.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- **GSM8K** (Cobbe et al. 2021): 8,500 elementary arithmetic word problems.
- **MATH** (Hendrycks et al. 2021): 12,500 competition-level (high-school/college) problems with stepwise solutions.
- **NuminaMath-TIR** (Li et al. 2024): 1,100+ theorems and 12,000 application problems, type-informed reasoning / theorem-problem pairs.
- Training splits are mixed; evaluation is per-test-set.
- **Released benchmark:** a dataset of "stealthy errors" plus high-quality diagnostic annotations (HuggingFace `zr888/hide-and-seek-llm`), positioned as a benchmark for future training/evaluation.

## Evaluation methodology
- Two evaluation axes: (1) **diagnosis performance** — does model-generated diagnostic info help a model correct stealthy errors, measured by **correction success rate ACC_corr** and **GPT-4o win rate**; (2) **stealthiness** — how deceptive the generated errors are, via **correction failure rate = 1 − ACC_corr** across baseline correctors.
- **Diagnosis study (Table 1):** For each error sample, the *same* corrector model performs correction twice — once guided by the untrained diagnoser's report (`ACC_corr | D`) and once by HSG's diagnoser (`ACC_corr | D*`). Five corrector models are used: Qwen3-4B / 8B / 14B, DeepSeek V3-0324 (685B MoE), GPT-4o. Improvement `↑ = ACC_corr|D* − ACC_corr|D`.
- **Win-rate study (Table 2):** GPT-4o directly compares D vs D* diagnoses for the same errors on NuminaMath-TIR; answer order swapped to remove positional bias; reports Win/Tie/Loss, Win Rate, and paired ACC_corr improvement.
- **Stealthiness study (Fig. 4):** three *training* strategies generate sneaky answers on NuminaMath-TIR; five baseline models attempt correction; report mean correction-failure rate ± std across baselines.
- **Error-type analysis (Figs. 5–6):** classify each sneaky answer as Type A, Type B, both, or neither, on NuminaMath-TIR, to explain *why* stealthiness differs; includes a worked qualitative example per method.
- **Baselines / ablations:** **LLM-rater Adv+RL** (RL with an adversarial LLM discriminator per Kirchner et al. 2024, using Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B as the rater); **RL only** (no adversarial signal — ablation); **HSG** (proposed). All trained on Qwen3-4B with identical hyperparameters under GRPO for fairness.
- **Training details:** Qwen3-4B base, 600 steps on combined training set, 4× A800 GPUs; final checkpoint = lowest `ACC_corr`. Full details deferred to Supplementary Material (not in the read pages).

## Metrics
- **ACC_corr** — correction success rate (fraction of stealthy errors correctly fixed given a diagnosis). Higher = more useful diagnosis.
- **Correction failure rate = 1 − ACC_corr** — used as the stealthiness proxy. Higher = stealthier errors.
- **GPT-4o win rate** — pairwise preference of D* over D diagnoses.
- **Error-type proportions** — % Type A (Result Shift), % Type B (Explicit Contamination), % both, % neither.
- Judged-correctness functions: `Γ_correct` (answer matches truth), `Γ_diagnosis` (diagnosis matches ground-truth correctness of `a_S`).

## Main findings
Direct paper findings (numbers as reported):
- **Diagnosis quality (Table 1, average across GSM8K/MATH/NuminaMath-TIR, ACC_corr|D → ACC_corr|D\*):**
  - Qwen3-4B: 42.44% → 66.15% (↑23.71)
  - Qwen3-8B: 41.20% → 66.01% (↑24.81)
  - Qwen3-14B: 45.78% → 65.84% (↑20.06)
  - DeepSeek V3-0324: 53.21% → 70.01% (↑16.80)
  - GPT-4o: 35.61% → 66.96% (↑31.35)
  - Per-dataset example (Qwen3-4B): GSM8K 44.50%→79.98%; MATH 40.28%→52.51%; NuminaMath-TIR 42.55%→65.96%.
  - Overall improvement of D* over D spans **16.8%–31.4%** across the five correctors (matches the abstract's "16.8%–31.4%").
- **Model-size trend:** among the first four models, the D*-over-D gain *decreases* as model size grows (larger models have stronger native error recognition but still benefit). GPT-4o is the exception.
- **GPT-4o observation:** GPT-4o's *native* diagnostic ACC_corr (35.61%) is *lower* than Qwen3-4B's (42.44%), yet with D* it reaches 66.96% (near DeepSeek's 70.01%), indicating strong receptivity to high-quality diagnostic guidance despite weaker native diagnosis.
- **Win rate (Table 2, NuminaMath-TIR, D\* vs D judged by GPT-4o):** Qwen3-4B 19.15% win / ACC_corr↑23.41%; Qwen3-8B 21.28% / 25.53%; Qwen3-14B 9.57% / 12.77%; DeepSeek 12.77% / 17.02%; GPT-4o 26.60% / 27.66%. Ties dominate (59–73 of ~94), and win-rate trends track correction-improvement trends.
- **Stealthiness (Fig. 4, mean correction-failure rate ± std across 5 baselines, NuminaMath-TIR):** HSG **56.17 ± 6.00%** ≻ LLM-rater Adv+RL **38.51 ± 4.76%** ≻ RL-only **27.43 ± 3.52%**. HSG errors are the hardest to correct (stealthiest).
- **Error-type suppression (Fig. 6, NuminaMath-TIR):** HSG produces **92.6%** answers with *neither* Type A nor Type B errors; LLM-rater Adv+RL is **94.6%** Type B (= 72.3% + 22.3%, aligning with the rater's "self-admit / correct-then-incorrect" scoring bias); RL-only is **31.9%** Type A (= 8.5% + 23.4%, a simple result-shift strategy absent adversarial constraints). Interpretation: adversarial dialogue filters out both easy-to-catch error families.

## Negative results
- The advantage of HSG diagnosis (D*-over-D gain) **shrinks with corrector model size** for the first four models — i.e., strong models gain less, a diminishing-returns result the authors report rather than hide.
- Reported as a limitation of *prior* methods (RL-only, LLM-rater): they collapse onto easy, low-stealth error types (Type A / Type B) rather than deep reasoning faults — a negative characterization of baselines supported by the error-type distribution.
- No negative/failure result is reported for HSG itself (e.g., no case where D* underperforms D on average); ties in Table 2 are large but framed as wins-not-losses.

## Additional limitations identified during review (label as REVIEWER SYNTHESIS)
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) **Stealthiness metric is corrector-relative.** "1 − ACC_corr" measures how hard a *fixed set of LLM correctors* find the errors, not human-perceived stealth or ground-truth subtlety. High correction-failure could partly reflect corrector weakness rather than genuine deceptiveness. No human study of stealth is reported.
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) **Judge dependence.** Both the win-rate result and much of the pipeline rely on LLM judges (GPT-4o) and a fixed correction model; systematic biases in these judges propagate into the training target and the headline metrics. Order-swapping mitigates positional bias only.
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) **Single base model.** All roles are trained from Qwen3-4B; whether HSG's stealth/diagnosis gains transfer when S/D are built on other or larger bases is untested (the size-comparison rows are *correctors*, not re-trained HSG roles).
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) **`Γ_diagnosis` correctness criterion is coarse** (binary "matches the correctness of `a_S`"). It rewards correctly *classifying* answer correctness; whether D localizes the *specific* faulty step (as the prompt asks) is not directly scored in the reported metrics — localization quality is evidenced only indirectly via correction success.
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) **Dual-use / release risk not discussed.** The released "stealthy errors" model and dataset constitute a reusable generator of plausible-but-wrong reasoning that defeats surface review; the paper offers no misuse analysis, licensing, or access-gating discussion.
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) **No robustness/overfitting analysis** of D* against error distributions *outside* what S learned to produce (the co-training could specialize D* to S's idiosyncratic stealth style; generalization to naturally-occurring or human errors is unmeasured).

## Design implications
- For a Guardian/diagnosis-style safety component, **adversarial co-training a detector against a learned generator of subtle faults** yields a stronger detector than training against a *fixed* rater or static error corpus — HSG's dialogue loop beats both LLM-rater and RL-only on stealthiness and diagnosis. Applicable pattern: co-evolve the "seeker" (verifier/guardrail) with an internal "hider" rather than freezing the adversary.
- **Hierarchical, threshold-clipped reward** (amplifier main-reward × auxiliary, with a floor `τ`) is a reusable trick to keep an adversarial generator exploring when the hard objective (generate *stealthy* errors) is initially rarely satisfied.
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) The Type-A/Type-B taxonomy is a useful *design checklist* for any verifier: surface-level (Type B) and answer-shift (Type A) faults are the easy cases; a verifier evaluated only on these will over-report competence. Design detectors and their eval sets to include *reasoning-internal* faults.

## Implementation implications
- Reward machinery decomposes cleanly into checkable primitives (correctness, format, length) plus a role-specific main signal — implementable wherever a cheap ground-truth checker exists (math boxed answers). Porting to domains without a crisp `Γ_correct` requires substituting a reliable correctness oracle, which is the hard part.
- The **correction model is fixed and only emits signal**; implementers can slot in an off-the-shelf strong model as the "did the diagnosis actually enable a fix" arbiter without training it — but its quality directly bounds signal quality.
- GRPO (value-network-free) is the chosen RL method for long-chain reasoning; keeps memory/implementation cost lower than PPO for this multi-role setup.

## Evaluation implications
- **Correction success rate under swapped diagnostic guidance** is a clean, model-agnostic way to measure whether a diagnosis is *actionable*, decoupled from any single corrector — reusable for evaluating guardrail/critic outputs generally.
- **Stealthiness measured as correction-failure across a panel of correctors** operationalizes "how deceptive is this error"; adopt a *panel* (not one judge) and report variance, as the paper does (± std across 5 models).
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) Static error benchmarks can *overstate* diagnostic ability (the paper's own motivation: high static scores drop on perturbed variants). Evaluations of safety detectors should include *adaptively generated* hard negatives, not just fixed corpora.

## Deployment implications
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) **Dual-use posture.** The primary deployable artifact for a defender is D* (a better error-diagnoser / critic that could sit in a reasoning-verification or human-review-assist pipeline). But the *same* framework and released S model constitute a generator of plausible-but-wrong reasoning that passes surface review — relevant to threat-modeling AI-authored content (grading fraud, disinformation with fabricated derivations, deceptive agent justifications). Deployers of review/verification systems should assume adversaries can *train* for stealth against a known verifier, not just prompt for it.
- The demonstrated **diminishing returns on strong models** means a HSG-style critic adds the most value as an assist to *weaker* diagnosers/correctors; strong frontier models gain less. Deploy D* where the base reviewer is weak (e.g., smaller on-device models, tutoring feedback for students).
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) Because gains are shown only for math with ground-truth answers, deploying the diagnoser to open-domain reasoning requires production validation with a domain-appropriate correctness oracle before trust.

## Monitoring and incident implications
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) HSG concretely demonstrates that **"the final answer looks right and each step reads plausibly" is not evidence of correctness** — adversarially trained traces specifically defeat both answer-check (Type A suppression) and surface-scan (Type B suppression). Monitoring for deceptive reasoning must probe *internal step validity*, not final-answer matching or fluency/format signals, which the length/format rewards here are explicitly designed to satisfy.
- A panel-of-correctors "can this be independently reproduced/fixed?" check is a candidate *monitoring* signal: reasoning that multiple independent solvers fail to correct given the claimed steps is suspicious.
- (REVIEWER SYNTHESIS) The released stealthy-error dataset is a ready-made *red-team corpus* for stress-testing an organization's reasoning-verification or automated-grading defenses.

## Applicability boundaries (where findings should / should NOT be generalized)
- **Should generalize (with validation):** the *methodological* claims — adversarial co-training beats fixed-rater/static-error training for building error detectors; correction-success and corrector-panel-failure are useful, judge-diversified metrics; the Type-A/B blind-spot taxonomy for verifier evaluation.
- **Should NOT be generalized without new evidence:** (a) the specific ACC_corr / stealthiness numbers — they are tied to math datasets, Qwen3-4B-trained roles, and GPT-4o/Skywork judges; (b) any claim of security *robustness* — the paper evaluates no victim system and no attack against production; (c) applicability to domains lacking a crisp correctness oracle (open-ended NLG, subjective tasks) — untested; (d) transfer of S/D behavior to other base models — untested (size rows are correctors, not re-trained roles).
- **Explicitly out of scope:** external/black-box attacks, jailbreaks, prompt/tool injection, or any deployment-time adversary. This is an internal training method.

## Evidence strength (strong/moderate/preliminary/contested/insufficient)
- **Moderate.** Multiple datasets (3), multiple correctors (5, incl. two frontier models), a fair-hyperparameter ablation (RL-only) and a strong prior-method baseline (LLM-rater), consistent directional results (D* > D everywhere; HSG stealth > baselines), and released data support the core claims. Tempered by: single base model for the trained roles, heavy reliance on LLM/fixed-model judges, corrector-relative stealth metric, no human evaluation, no dedicated limitations section, and no security/robustness evaluation. Claims about *diagnosis improvement in math* are the best-supported; claims implied by the "adversarial attack" corpus framing are **not** supported by this paper.
