## Research question
Can a physically deployable adversarial example fool stereo-matching-based binocular depth estimation (SM-BDE) used in autonomous driving (AD), given that stereo perception relies on 3D disparity between two rigidly coupled cameras rather than single-image 2D pixels?

## Problem definition
Prior physical adversarial examples (PAEs) mostly use 2D patches and target monocular depth estimation (MDE); they break the physical disparity consistency required by stereo systems and only affect narrow viewpoints. The authors seek a full-scale 3D camouflage texture, applied over the whole surface of a target vehicle, that produces disparity-consistent projections in both stereo views and remains effective across viewpoints, lighting, and weather.

## System or model being studied
Deep stereo-matching depth models: PSMNet, GA-Net, RAFT-Stereo, CREStereo, AnyStereo. Attack is evaluated at perception level and integrated into a "full AD perception stack" for a system-level collision test (in CARLA and with real-world stereo captures).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: (i) "merging attack" — force SM-BDE to assign background depth to the target vehicle so it disappears from the depth map (an advanced hiding attack); (ii) complementary "appearing attack" — make the vehicle appear dangerously close, inducing sudden braking.
- Adversary knowledge (white/gray/black-box): attack optimizes texture against a differentiable renderer and the SM-BDE model F; white-box on the surrogate model. Cross-model transferability is separately evaluated (implying black-box goal).
- Adversary access: ability to apply/print a static texture onto a real vehicle surface; no online access to stereo inputs at test time ("without any online adjustment or access to stereo inputs").
- Adversary capabilities: 3D object detection to anchor an adversarial mesh with correct pose; stereo-aligned differentiable rendering; texture optimized with merging loss + non-printability (NPS) + total-variation (TV) regularizers.
- Attacker budget: static printable texture (physical realizability constraints via NPS/TV); not stated as an Lp budget.
- Phase: inference / deployment (physical world).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (drives object depth toward a chosen background-informed target, or toward Dmax for appearing attack).
- Digital / physical: both — digital simulation (CARLA, KITTI2015) and real-world stereo captures.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive with respect to any defense (no defense is in the loop).

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the AD stack trusts SM-BDE depth output for obstacle detection and path planning. Assumes attacker can obtain accurate stereo calibration/extrinsics for rendering and can physically print the optimized texture with reproducible colors.

## Attack or failure mechanism
A differentiable stereo-consistent rendering module composites an adversarially textured 3D mesh into both left/right backgrounds using per-camera rendering configs k=(k_l,k_r) parameterized as {dist, elev, azim} from stereo calibration. The texture θ is optimized so F's depth prediction matches a background-informed target d_t. Merging is region-aware: boundary depth extraction (mask max-pool minus mask), horizontal upper/lower segmentation anchored at the mean boundary depth, and a region-wise MSE loss aligning each region to its local background depth. Final loss L = L_merge + αL_nps + βL_tv (appearing attack replaces L_merge with L_appear = MSE(d_obj, D_max)).

## Proposed defense or method
None. This is an attack paper; no defense is proposed or evaluated.

## Datasets and benchmarks
KITTI2015 (Geiger et al. 2012) stereo pairs for training textures; CARLA simulator (Dosovitskiy et al. 2017) for digital evaluation and the AD system-level test; real-world stereo captures for physical evaluation. (Mid-paper experiment detail is truncated in the extracted text.)

## Evaluation methodology
Compare against benign, random texture, and prior attacks PASM and Adv-DM across five stereo models. Cross-model transferability tested. System-level closed-loop test in a full AD perception stack comparing benign vs. adversarial driving outcomes (lane change vs. rear-end collision at ~10 m).

## Metrics
Custom metrics reported in Table 1: E_blend (lower is better), E_cover (higher is better), E_sys (higher is better). Exact definitions are in the truncated methodology; qualitatively E_blend measures merging/blending, E_cover coverage, E_sys system-level effect.

## Main findings
- Author claim: theirs is the first texture-enabled physical adversarial attack against stereo-matching models in AD. Across all five models, their method achieves the best E_blend (0.056–0.082), high E_cover (0.55–0.60), and highest E_sys (0.62–0.76), whereas prior PASM/Adv-DM achieve E_sys ≤ 0.17.
- Under real-world 3D stereo constraints, prior 2D-patch and MDE-texture attacks are reported ineffective; only the 3D disparity-consistent texture remains effective.
- System-level: in the benign case the AV safely changes lane at ~10 m; under attack it fails to respond and collides in both tests.
- Author claim: generalizes across lighting, weather, and viewpoints.

## Design implications
Physical/embodied autonomy stacks that trust a single perception modality (stereo depth) for safety-critical decisions inherit an evasion surface. Depth outputs should be treated as capability, not verification: gate braking/planning decisions on cross-modal agreement (LiDAR/radar), temporal consistency, and disparity-consistency sanity checks rather than on a single SM-BDE map.

## Implementation implications
For Origin-style physical AI: add environment-side verification of perception (multi-sensor fusion voting, disparity residual monitoring) before an action gate trusts "no obstacle." Treat printable-texture threats as a supply-chain/physical-sensor risk, not a purely digital one.

## Evaluation implications
Robustness benchmarks for AD depth must include full-scale 3D, stereo-disparity-consistent, physically renderable adversaries and viewpoint/lighting/weather sweeps — 2D-patch benchmarks under-estimate real risk. Report standardized metrics (e.g., depth error, detection miss-rate, closed-loop collision rate) alongside custom ones.

## Deployment implications
A vehicle wrapped in an optimized camouflage texture could be made invisible or falsely proximate to a stereo-only perception stack, causing missed obstacles or spurious braking — a safety and potentially liability-relevant physical-world threat.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Runtime monitors could flag anomalous disparity-reconstruction residuals, sudden depth-map "holes" where an object contour exists in RGB, or divergence between camera-depth and LiDAR/radar returns; log these as evidence for incident review.

## Applicability boundaries
Findings apply to stereo-matching binocular depth estimation for driving perception. They generalize to other embodied/physical-sensor pipelines that trust learned depth, but NOT to LLM/text-agent, tool-invocation, or prompt-injection threat models. Peripheral-to-core agent security relevance is via the physical-sensors attack surface of embodied agents, not digital agent orchestration.
