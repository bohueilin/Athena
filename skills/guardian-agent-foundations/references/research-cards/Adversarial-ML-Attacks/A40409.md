## Research question
Are rationalization models (generator selects rationale, then predictor classifies) robust to backdoor attacks, and if not, how can a tailored backdoor be injected despite the generator's rationale-selection filtering?

## Problem definition
Rationalization models add interpretability by placing a generator (extractive rationale selector) before a predictor. Their robustness to backdoors was previously unstudied. The authors find that naive backdoor attacks largely fail because the generator can filter out mismatched/unreasonable triggers, reducing ASR. They ask whether a tailored attack can overcome this.

## System or model being studied
Extractive rationalization models R(x) = P(G(x;theta);phi): generator G produces a discrete rationale mask z (token- or sentence-level), predictor P classifies z⊙x. Trained end-to-end with cross-entropy.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: implant a backdoor so the model misclassifies to a specific target label when a trigger is present, while performing well on benign inputs (targeted attack).
- Adversary knowledge: complete knowledge of the model (white-box).
- Adversary access: ability to manipulate the training dataset and control the training process (data poisoning). Author scenario: the adversary is the developer who publishes the backdoored rationalization model on public platforms (e.g., HuggingFace); victims download and use it.
- Adversary capabilities: gradient-based universal trigger search; manipulation of both rationales and labels; poison-priority sampling to control learning order; weighted poisoning loss + rationale loss.
- Attacker budget: poisoning rate alpha (fraction of Dc turned into poisoned samples); exact value not stated in extracted text.
- Phase: training-time poisoning; inference-time activation; deployment via model distribution.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted.
- Digital / physical: digital (text; character/word/sentence-level triggers).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: attack is designed specifically to defeat the generator's filtering; evaluated against several defenses (adversarial training, and others truncated).

## Trust assumptions
Victims trust and deploy a third-party-published rationalization model. The attacker fully controls training. No trusted verification of the model's provenance is assumed by victims.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Naive backdoors fail because the generator selects a coherent, interpretable rationale and filters out unreasonable/mismatched triggers, invalidating part of the attack. BadRNL overcomes this by: (1) Personalized trigger search — gradient-based search per dataset for token-level (j-token) and sentence-level triggers that maximize prediction-flip cross-entropy while minimizing MSE between intended rationale z and produced z' (so the trigger is selected into the rationale). (2) Attack position — insert trigger at the first rationale location and add trigger mask into the rationale. (3) Weighted poisoning loss + rationale loss to co-supervise generator (rationale) and predictor (label). (4) Poison-Priority Sampling (curriculum-learning-inspired): prioritize poisoned samples early because the fixed trigger→label mapping R(xp)≈R(t) is lower-complexity than generalizing over clean samples R(xc).

## Proposed defense or method
This is an attack paper; no new defense proposed. The authors evaluate BadRNL against existing defenses — (1) Adversarial Training using the searched personalized trigger set, and (2) an additional defense (truncated in extracted text; the broader literature they cite includes ONION-style filtering). Author claim: the attack remains effective under the evaluated defenses (specific numbers truncated).

## Datasets and benchmarks
Five datasets: two sentence-level (FEVER, MultiRC) and three token-level (Beer, Hotel, Movie). Per-dataset triggers were searched.

## Evaluation methodology
Compare BadRNL against BadNet and Cbat baselines; measure clean-task performance and attack performance; test under defenses. Motivation experiments show naive backdoor ASR is low on rationalization models.

## Metrics
Task: classification Accuracy (ACC) and Gold Rationale F1 (GR, F1 between predicted and human-annotated rationale — higher = more interpretable/faithful). Attack: Attack Success Rate (ASR) — probability a triggered input is classified into the target label.

## Main findings
- Author claim: naive backdoor attacks struggle to reach high ASR on rationalization models due to the generator's rationale filtering — i.e., these models are surprisingly robust to naive backdoors.
- Author claim: BadRNL (personalized triggers + refined training + poison-priority sampling) achieves high ASR on poisoned samples while maintaining high clean ACC and GR (interpretability preserved) — first successful backdoor tailored to rationalization models.
- Author claim/insight: poisoned samples are LOWER complexity than clean samples (fixed t→yp mapping), so prioritizing them early accelerates and stabilizes learning.

## Negative results
Naive/traditional backdoor methods (the paper's own motivation experiments) fail to achieve typical high ASR on rationalization models — a negative result for prior attack techniques, and the motivation for the new method.

## Design implications
Interpretability mechanisms (rationale selection) provide a partial, incidental filter against naive backdoors but are NOT a security control — a tailored attack that forces the trigger into the selected rationale defeats them. Do not treat rationale extraction as a backdoor defense.

## Implementation implications
Backdoor detectors for rationalization models should inspect the generator's rationale-selection behavior (trigger-in-rationale) and label-conditioned rationale consistency, not just predictor outputs. Curriculum/sampling order is an attack lever, suggesting training-order auditing.

## Evaluation implications
When claiming a model class is "robust" to an attack, distinguish incidental robustness (structural filtering) from designed robustness — a tailored attack may erase the gap. Report ASR under both naive and tailored attacks.

## Deployment implications
Reinforces model-provenance risk for downloaded interpretable NLP models: a backdoored rationalization model preserves clean accuracy AND plausible rationales, making manual review unlikely to catch it. Provenance attestation / trusted-source controls are warranted.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Monitoring should include rationale-level anomaly signals (e.g., unexpected fixed tokens consistently selected into rationales across inputs) as a potential trigger indicator; output-accuracy monitoring alone is insufficient.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to extractive rationalization classifiers under a malicious-publisher (white-box, training-control) threat model. Do NOT generalize to abstractive rationalization, generative LLM reasoning, agent tool-use, or weaker data-only poisoning threat models. Effectiveness is per-dataset (trigger searched separately).
