NOTE ON TOPIC FIT: this paper is primarily a **robust reinforcement-learning / adversarial-training** paper for humanoid locomotion, not an attack-against-a-deployed-system paper. The "attack" here is a learnable, budget-constrained adversary used *during training* to harden a control policy; the trained adversary (Selective Attack Policy) is deliberately removed before real-robot deployment. The adversarial-attack machinery is a means to a robustness end. Read the threat model section carefully before treating this as an external-attacker study.

## Research question
The authors state three research questions evaluated experimentally: (1) Can adversarial attacks induce stronger perturbations than domain randomization? (2) Can the proposed method (SA2RT) improve the robustness of motion policies? (3) Does SA2RT contribute to the motion performance of real robots? The underlying question is whether *selectively* injecting a learned, attack-budget-constrained adversary during training yields more robust, longer-horizon humanoid whole-body motion skills than domain randomization (DR) or full-time ("persistent") adversarial training.

## Problem definition
RL-based humanoid motion policies typically lose stability under prolonged operation, sensor/actuator noise, and real-world disturbances (sim-to-real distribution shift). The paper frames the problem as accurately identifying policy vulnerabilities and applying targeted perturbations to strengthen the policy, rather than uniformly randomizing the environment (DR) or naively attacking every timestep. Domain randomization "cannot specifically perturb policy vulnerabilities"; persistent full-time attacks induce conservative overfitting (the "weak adversary dilemma" / robust overfitting). The goal is a training procedure that exposes and repairs the policy's true weakest states/actions under an attack budget, producing policies that maintain long-horizon mobility and trajectory tracking on real hardware.

## System or model being studied
- Robot platform: Unitree G1 humanoid robot (commercial), evaluated in simulation and on real hardware.
- Two tasks: Perceptive Locomotion (PL) — terrain traversal using perceptive/terrain input; and Whole-Body Control (WBC) — whole-body trajectory imitation/tracking (including a 120-second dynamic dance-motion imitation).
- Two learnable modules trained together (Fig. 2): a **motion policy** (victim, pi^m — with sub-policies pi^pl and pi^wbc in the deployment diagram) and an **attack policy** (adversary, pi^adv), which contains a **Selective Attack Policy (SAP)** that emits a binary per-timestep indicator b_t in {0,1} deciding whether to attack, plus perturbation magnitudes.
- Training uses PPO (Schulman et al. 2017) with temporal-difference value learning; the two-player optimization objective follows Guo et al. (2021).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: maximize the robot's "safety failure" (induce falls / degrade motion), formalized as maximizing the difference between the adversary's value function and the motion agent's value function, J(theta_adv) = maximize (V^adv_pi(s) - V^m_pi(s)). The attack reward R^adv encodes attack objectives (inducing falls).
- Adversary knowledge / access: the adversary is a **learnable neural network trained with RL**, co-located in the training loop; it observes the motion policy's state and receives policy gradients, so within training it is effectively **white-box / gray-box** with respect to the victim policy. It is not modeled as an external black-box attacker probing a deployed API.
- Adversary capabilities: bounded additive perturbations to both the observation/state space and the action space. State perturbation set B_s = {s + delta_s : ||delta_s|| <= eps_s}; action perturbation set B_a = {a + delta_a : ||delta_a|| <= eps_a}. This models observation/state-estimation error and actuator/execution deviation cascades.
- Adversary budget: an attack-budget constraint limits the number of attacked timesteps in an episode; the SAP attacks only a sparse subset T_adv of timesteps with |T_adv| << T, sum_t b_t <= N_a. A Lagrange multiplier lambda converts the hard budget into a soft penalty controlling attack frequency.
- Phase: **training/simulation-time** (this is the operative threat surface). Targeted (selects most-vulnerable states) rather than untargeted-uniform. Digital/simulated perturbations, not a physical patch or real-world sensor spoof. Adaptive/co-adaptive — attacker and victim are alternately optimized (non-zero-sum game).
- Deployment: the SAP is explicitly **NOT** deployed on the real robot; only the hardened motion policy runs at inference. So there is no claim about resistance to a live external adversary at deployment time; the deployment claim is about robustness to natural noise, disturbances, and sim-to-real shift.

## Trust assumptions
- The training environment (simulator) and privileged information (e.g., privileged observations p_t used by the attack policy / for training) are trusted and available during training.
- Perturbation bounds (eps_s, eps_a) and the attack budget N_a are chosen/known hyperparameters; the paper keeps perturbation spaces and thresholds "consistent" between DR and SAP to make comparisons fair.
- The adversary is assumed to have access to the victim's states/values during training (cooperative training infrastructure, not an untrusted external party).
- Deployment assumes the robustified policy generalizes from simulated bounded perturbations to real-world natural disturbances (sim-to-real transfer); this is the central trust leap the real-robot experiments are meant to validate.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Two failure modes are contrasted, motivating the method:
1. Persistent Attack Policy (PAP): a temporally homogeneous adversary that attacks at (near) every timestep within the perturbation bounds. Because it cannot distinguish state-specific vulnerability, it is easily detectable and pushes the victim toward excessive conservatism — inducing a significant shift in the victim's state distribution and degrading performance in low-perturbation (normal) environments. Under a pure zero-sum reward (R^adv = -R^m), the learned attacker does not improve monotonically, producing a "weak adversary dilemma" and robust overfitting of the victim.
2. The genuine failure being defended against: RL neural controllers are highly sensitive to small input perturbations; observation/state-estimation errors plus actuator latency/mechanical wear cascade into suboptimal actions, loss of balance, accumulating trajectory-tracking error over long horizons, and falls (e.g., WBC-DR loses balance and terminates a dance sequence after ~65 s).
The SAP's mechanism for *finding* weakness: it learns which sparse timesteps (e.g., the transition from flat terrain to stairs, Fig. 6) are critical and applies a few targeted perturbations there, successfully inducing falls with far fewer attack steps than PAP.

## Proposed defense or method
Selective Adversarial Attack for Robust Training (SA2RT). Components:
- **Selective Attack Policy (SAP):** a budget-constrained attack policy that jointly (a) selects the subset of critical timesteps to attack via a binary variable b_t and (b) generates bounded state/action perturbations, formulated as a constrained optimization (maximize attack reward subject to sum_t b_t <= N_a), relaxed with a Lagrange multiplier lambda into a soft penalty. Trained as RL in an augmented MDP whose action space includes the {0,1} attack decision.
- **Non-zero-sum adversarial training:** instead of a zero-sum game (R^adv = -R^m), the objective maximizes (V^adv - V^m), which, following Guo et al. (2021) and a clipped-PPO-style surrogate (Eq. 7), is claimed to guarantee monotonic improvement of both value functions and avoid the weak-adversary dilemma.
- **Alternating optimization (Algorithm 1):** outer loop of N_iter iterations; within each, N_adv iterations optimize the attack policy (motion policy fixed) to find intrinsic weaknesses, then N_m iterations optimize the motion policy (attack policy fixed) for resilience. Motion policy is initialized from a pre-trained policy; attack policy is randomly initialized. Separate value networks approximate the attacker's and the motion agent's value functions.
- **Deployment:** SAP is discarded; the hardened motion skills run on the real Unitree G1 without the adversary.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- No public/named benchmark dataset. Experiments are on the Unitree G1 humanoid in simulation and on real hardware.
- Tasks / evaluation environments: Perceptive Locomotion over flat ground, slopes, stairs, discrete terrain; complex real-world terrains stairs, slopes, gravel, sand, grass; Whole-Body Control trajectory imitation including a 120-second dynamic dance motion (MoCap-style whole-body imitation).
- Training/eval environment types: Clean Environments (CE), Domain Randomization (DR), and adversarial (PAP / SAP) environments.

## Evaluation methodology
- Comparative experiments across four training settings (baselines below) plus ablations.
- Cross-environment success-rate test (Table 1): policies trained under different regimes are each evaluated in DR environments and in SAP (adversarial) environments to test whether DR-strength perturbations vs. learned selective attacks expose vulnerabilities.
- Robustness analysis (Table 2): PL policies evaluated for velocity tracking and dynamic stability in CE and DR environments.
- Whole-body tracking (Fig. 3 radar): WBC tracking errors compared in CE and DR.
- Ablations: perturbation levels L_p in {1,2,3,4} comparing PL-PAP-L_p vs. PL-SAP-L_p (Fig. 4); hyperparameter lambda swept from 10^-3 to 10^1 measuring attack ratio vs. normalized reward (Fig. 7); SAP attack-ratio breakdown by motion task (Fig. 5) and attack-sequence visualization at a terrain transition (Fig. 6).
- Real-world experiments: complex terrain traversal, each terrain tested in 5 groups with 10 trials per group (Table 3); real-robot 120 s dance trajectory imitation with per-frame MPJPE curves (Fig. 9).

## Metrics
- E_vel (m/s): mean linear velocity error (velocity tracking).
- E_ang (rad/s): mean angular velocity error.
- E_g (m/s^2): gravitational projection component (dynamic stability of the robot).
- MPKPE — Mean Per Keybody Position Error: E^upper_mpkpe (m), E^lower_mpkpe (m) (keypoint tracking accuracy).
- MPJPE — Mean Per Joint Position Error: E^upper_mpjpe (rad), E^lower_mpjpe (rad) (joint position tracking).
- R_sr (%): success rate for complex terrain traversal and whole-body trajectory tracking (robot survival).
- Attack ratio (%): fraction of timesteps the SAP attacks (used to characterize the adversary, Figs. 5 and 7).

## Main findings
Headline (abstract): adversarially trained policies "improve the terrain traversal success rate by 40%, reduce the trajectory tracking error by 32%," and maintain long-horizon mobility and tracking. Detailed results as reported:
- Table 1 (Success Rate R_sr %, DR env / SAP env): PL/WBC-CE 77.3+/-7.3 (DR) and 0.0+/-0.0 (SAP); PL/WBC-DR 94.4+/-5.7 (DR) and 0.0+/-0.0 (SAP); PL/WBC-SAP 97.4+/-2.1 (DR) and 95.7+/-6.4 (SAP). The learned selective attack drives both the clean and DR-trained policies to 0% success, while the SAP-trained policy stays high in both environments. (Interpretation the authors draw: DR cannot ensure robustness; selective attacks expose stronger vulnerabilities.)
- Table 2 (PL robustness, lower is better; CE env): PL-DR E_vel 0.25+/-0.03, E_ang 0.29+/-0.02, E_g 0.34+/-0.01; PL-SAP 0.19+/-0.02, 0.22+/-0.03, 0.30+/-0.01. (DR env): PL-DR 0.28+/-0.02, 0.34+/-0.03, 0.42+/-0.01; PL-SAP 0.21+/-0.03, 0.27+/-0.02, 0.36+/-0.01. PL-SAP beats PL-DR on all three metrics in both environments.
- Fig. 3 (WBC): WBC-SAP reported to outperform WBC-DR across all evaluation metrics (velocity and joint/keybody tracking) in both CE and DR.
- Table 3 (real-robot terrain traversal R_sr %): PL-DR Slopes 54+/-6.9, Stairs 47+/-7.5, Gravel 71+/-4.3, Sands 73+/-4.5, Grass 82+/-4.9; PL-SAP Slopes 90+/-3.7, Stairs 84+/-5.4, Gravel 93+/-2.1, Sands 90+/-2.0, Grass 95+/-1.6. Improvement described as ~40% traversal success rate improvement for the real robot.
- Fig. 9 (real-robot dance, 120 s): WBC-DR accumulates tracking error and terminates prematurely at ~65 s; WBC-SAP completes the full 120 s and reduces trajectory tracking error by ~32%.
- Fig. 5 (SAP attack ratio by task): Flat 13.6%, Slope 15.9%, Stair 22.5%, Discrete 25.1%, Walk 21.6%, Dance 30.2% — attack ratio rises with task difficulty, evidence that SAP concentrates attacks on harder/more-vulnerable states.
- Fig. 6: SAP concentrates a short burst of attacks precisely at the flat-to-stairs transition and induces a fall, demonstrating state-specific vulnerability targeting.
- Fig. 7 (lambda sweep): as lambda increases, attack ratio drops monotonically while motion performance first rises then falls; increasing lambda from 10^-3 to 10^-1 improves attack efficiency and policy robustness, but 10^-1 to 10^1 over-penalizes attacks, weakening targeted attacks and hindering robustness gains.

## Negative results
- Persistent (full-time) adversarial training (PAP) is shown to be inferior: its full-time attack induces a significant state-distribution shift and a marked decline in policy performance in low/normal-perturbation environments (Fig. 4). PL-SAP-L_p degrades more slowly than PL-PAP-L_p as perturbation level L_p increases; PL-PAP-L_p degradation exceeds even PL-DR.
- The pure zero-sum adversarial formulation is argued (citing prior analysis) to not guarantee continuous attacker improvement, yielding a weak adversary and robust overfitting of the victim — motivating the non-zero-sum objective. (This is presented as a known limitation of the zero-sum baseline rather than a fresh empirical ablation isolating zero-sum vs. non-zero-sum.)
- Over-constraining the attack budget (large lambda, 10^-1 to 10^1) reduces attacks so much that robustness improvement is lost (Fig. 7) — the method is sensitive to this hyperparameter.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Stated in Conclusions as future work (implying current scope limits): (1) the method does not yet model perturbation attacks arising from the *interaction between the robot and the environment* (contact/dynamics-level disturbances) — future work will explore these to enhance adaptability to dynamic environments; (2) the attack-budget constraint's trade-off between attack effectiveness and "strategy agility" is not fully optimized and remains an open direction. The paper does not otherwise enumerate an explicit limitations section.

## Additional limitations identified during review (label as REVIEWER SYNTHESIS)
- REVIEWER SYNTHESIS: The security framing is easy to over-read. This is a *robustness/robust-RL* contribution; it makes no claim of resistance to a real, deployment-time external adversary (the adversary is removed at deployment). The 0% success rates in Table 1's "SAP environment" describe an in-house, budget-bounded, white-box training adversary, not an attacker an operator would face in the field. Do not cite these numbers as attack-success-rate against a deployed system.
- REVIEWER SYNTHESIS: All quantitative claims are on a single robot platform (Unitree G1) and a specific set of tasks/terrains; there is no cross-embodiment or cross-simulator evaluation, so generalization of the 32%/40% figures is unestablished.
- REVIEWER SYNTHESIS: No adaptive-adversary or independent-attack robustness evaluation beyond the paper's own SAP/PAP/DR settings; robustness against attacks outside the trained perturbation bounds (larger eps, different noise models, physical spoofing) is "not evaluated against."
- REVIEWER SYNTHESIS: Sample sizes for real-robot terrain tests are modest (5 groups x 10 trials per terrain); confidence intervals are reported as +/- but the statistical test/significance procedure is not stated.
- REVIEWER SYNTHESIS: The monotonic-improvement guarantee is inherited from cited prior work (Guo et al. 2021) applied under approximations (clipped PPO surrogate, Eq. 7); the paper does not provide an independent proof or an ablation isolating the non-zero-sum objective's contribution from the selectivity/budget contribution.
- REVIEWER SYNTHESIS: The naming is inconsistent across the document — the method is expanded as "Selective Adversarial Attack for Robust Training (SA2RT)" in the abstract/body while the paper title says "Selective Adversarial Training"; readers should treat SA2RT as the canonical acronym.

## Design implications
- For safety-stack design (Origin Guardian Agent context): this is a useful pattern for *hardening* a learned controller by co-training a budget-constrained, selectivity-aware internal adversary rather than uniform randomization — the takeaway is that adversary *placement* (which states to perturb) matters more than adversary *volume*. Applicable to any learned policy where uniform stress testing over-conservatizes the model.
- The state-vs-action, observation-plus-actuator perturbation decomposition is a clean model of "sensor error and execution error cascade" worth reusing when threat-modeling a physical-AI controller.

## Implementation implications
- The non-zero-sum objective and Lagrange-relaxed attack budget are the key implementation levers; the lambda sweep (Fig. 7) shows the result is sensitive to the budget penalty and must be tuned (best regime near lambda ~ 10^-1 in their setup). Budget too tight loses robustness gains.
- Alternating optimization requires separate value networks for attacker and victim and a pre-trained victim to initialize from; naive zero-sum co-training risks a weak adversary / robust overfitting.
- The adversary must be *excludable at deployment* — implementations should keep the attack module cleanly separable so it can be stripped for inference.

## Evaluation implications
- Evaluating a robustified controller only in DR environments can hide vulnerabilities: a DR-trained policy scored 94.4% under DR but 0% under a learned selective attack (Table 1). Robustness evals should include a *learned, targeted* adversary, not just randomized noise, to avoid overstating robustness.
- Long-horizon evaluation matters: failures manifested as slow tracking-error accumulation and a fall at ~65 s (dance) that a short episode would miss. Include extended-duration runs.
- Attack-ratio-vs-task-difficulty (Fig. 5) is a useful diagnostic for *where* a policy is weak.

## Deployment implications
- Deployment claim is scoped to robustness against natural noise, disturbances, and sim-to-real shift on the Unitree G1 — improved real-robot terrain traversal (~+40% success) and long-horizon whole-body tracking (~32% lower error, 120 s completion). It is NOT a claim of resistance to an adversary at deployment time.
- Because the adversary is removed at deployment, no additional runtime cost or attack-surface is added by SA2RT at inference; the cost is entirely at training time.

## Monitoring and incident implications
- The SAP's learned notion of "vulnerable states" (e.g., terrain transitions, high-dynamic dance phases) suggests high-risk operating regimes to instrument for monitoring: transitions between terrain types, onset of high-dynamic motion, and cumulative tracking-error drift over long horizons are leading indicators of impending loss of balance.
- Gravitational-projection (E_g) and per-joint/keybody tracking error drift are candidate runtime health signals; a rising E_mpjpe trend over time (as in the DR-policy dance run) preceded the fall by tens of seconds, offering an early-warning window.

## Applicability boundaries (where findings should / should NOT be generalized)
- SHOULD generalize (with validation): the qualitative lesson that selective, budget-constrained, non-zero-sum adversarial training beats uniform DR and beats full-time (persistent) adversarial training for robustifying learned continuous-control policies; the evaluation lesson that randomization-only robustness tests can be defeated by a learned targeted adversary.
- SHOULD NOT generalize: the specific numbers (0% baseline collapse, 40% traversal gain, 32% error reduction) beyond the Unitree G1 and the evaluated tasks/terrains; any claim that the resulting policy is robust to *external/deployment-time* adversaries or to perturbations outside the trained bounds (not evaluated). Contact/interaction-level disturbances are explicitly out of scope (author-stated future work). Do not treat this as an attack paper demonstrating a vulnerability in a fielded system.

## Evidence strength (strong/moderate/preliminary/contested/insufficient)
Moderate. Strengths: consistent, multi-metric simulation results plus real-robot demonstrations on a physical humanoid across several terrains and a long-horizon dance task, with clear ablations (perturbation level, lambda, task-wise attack ratio) supporting the selectivity claim. Weaknesses that cap it below "strong": single robot platform, no independent/adaptive-adversary robustness testing beyond the authors' own settings, missing hyperparameters and appendix in this camera-ready, modest real-robot trial counts, and reliance on an inherited (not independently proven) monotonicity guarantee. Claims about robustness gains are demonstrated under the evaluated threat model and platform and require production/cross-embodiment validation before broader claims.
