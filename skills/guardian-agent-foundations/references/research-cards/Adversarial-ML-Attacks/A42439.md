## Research question
Can a *physically realizable* and *transferable* adversarial patch steer MLLM-based autonomous-driving (AD) systems toward attacker-chosen (target-aligned) perception/planning outputs, where existing patch attacks (built for object detectors) transfer poorly to the more complex, reasoning-capable MLLM architectures?

## Problem definition
MLLMs are increasingly used for unified perception+planning in AD but inherit adversarial vulnerabilities from their vision backbones. Prior threats mostly use *digital* perturbations (unrealistic to deploy) or patches designed for discriminative detection tasks with naive PGD/CE optimization and only 1–2 of {location, shape, content} optimized — yielding weak strength and poor transfer to MLLMs. The paper jointly optimizes patch location, shape, and content for real-world feasibility and cross-model transfer.

## System or model being studied
MLLM-based AD perception/planning pipelines. Given a driving image I and prompt q, model M outputs t = M(I,q); the attacker seeks I_adv inducing target output t_tar (e.g., a false "Stop sign" perception) that could cause unexpected stops → congestion/collision. Victim models treated as black boxes; attack crafted on CLIP surrogate encoders (ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ViT-g-14-laion2B) and transferred.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: **targeted** — make the MLLM emit a specific target description (target "Stop sign"), maximizing semantic alignment of output with t_tar.
- Adversary knowledge: **black-box** on victim MLLMs; white-box only on CLIP surrogate encoders (ensemble transfer attack).
- Adversary access: physical/printed patch placed in the scene (camera-captured); at digital level, adds a bounded patch region to the image.
- Adversary capabilities: jointly optimizes patch location (semantic-aware mask init via SoM + GPT-4o + potential field), shape (adaptive potential-field mask refinement), and content (gradient-based, ℓ∞ ≤ 16/255, step 1/255, 300 iters); EoT for physical robustness; patch ≈ 120×120 ≈ 1% of image area.
- Attacker budget: patch ≤ ~1% image area; ℓ∞ perturbation budget 16/255; 2× NVIDIA A100 (80GB) for optimization.
- Phase: inference / deployment (attack applied at runtime to camera input).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted.
- Digital / physical: designed to be **physically realizable** (semantic placement in feasible regions, EoT); primary quantitative evaluation is digital (patched nuScenes frames scored by LLM-as-judge).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: evaluated under "defense-aware settings" (author claim); transfer attack is not tailored per victim beyond the surrogate ensemble.

## Trust assumptions
The AD system trusts camera imagery as faithful perception input. The attack assumes access to (a) surrogate CLIP-family encoders that share vulnerabilities with victim MLLMs, and (b) GPT-4o for semantic region reasoning and target-image generation. It assumes a printed patch in a physically feasible scene region is realizable.

## Attack or failure mechanism
1. **Semantic-Based Mask Initialization**: SoM extracts object regions; GPT-4o infers a physically feasible placement region R_j; a region-centric Gaussian potential field Φ and binarization P produce mask M = P(R(G(I,γ_p,{R_i}),σ),τ_0). 2. **Feature-alignment loss** (replaces CE/PGD): global alignment on [CLS] tokens (cosine) + **SVD-based Local Alignment Loss** on patch tokens — truncated rank-k SVD (Eckart–Young–Mirsky optimal low-rank approx) to reduce redundancy and be robust to encoder LayerNorm/stochastic-depth variation; total L = L_global + η·L_local over N surrogate encoders. 3. **Patch-Guided Crop-Resize** (T_patch): guarantees the patch is in every sampled crop, mitigating gradient vanishing of naive cropping. 4. **Adaptive Potential-Field Mask Update**: Φ ← Φ + lr·max(0,G); τ increases so the mask gradually shrinks until Area(M) ≤ th; EoT applied for robustness.

## Proposed defense or method
None — this is an *attack* framework. It evaluates against SOTA attack baselines and (per author) defense-aware settings; references include patch defenses (PAD, Segment-and-Complete, JPEG-resistance) as context.

## Datasets and benchmarks
nuScenes (Caesar et al. 2020): first frame of each of 1,000 scenes, images without the target removed → 992 images at 1600×900×3; adversarial target = "Stop sign."

## Evaluation methodology
LLM-as-a-Judge (GPT-4o) scores attack success and output–target similarity. Victims: 3 open-source (LLaVA-v1.6-13B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Llama-3.2-90B-Vision), 5 commercial (GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Qwen2.5-VL-max), 4 reasoning-oriented (GPT-o3, Claude-Sonnet-4-Thinking, Gemini-2.5-Flash, QVQ-Plus). Baselines: IAP, DAPatch (patch attacks) + AttackVLM, SSA-CWA, SIA, MuMoDig, M-Attack, FOA-Attack (transfer attacks). Surrogates: CLIP ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ViT-g-14-laion2B.

## Metrics
- ASR (attack success rate) and AvgSim (output-vs-target similarity) via GPT-4o judge.
- Perceptual/quality: FID, LPIPS, BRISQUE (image quality/perceptibility).
- Selected Table 1 ASR (%) for PhysPatch vs. best baseline FOA-Attack: LLaVA-v1.6-13B 38.4 vs 30.9; Qwen2.5-VL-72B 25.8 vs 23.1; Llama-3.2-90B 15.4 vs 14.4; GPT-4o 37.2 vs 33.1; GPT-4.1 40.3 vs 34.3; Claude-Sonnet-4 26.1 vs 24.1; Gemini-2.0-flash 10.9 vs 9.5; GPT-o3 29.2 vs 20.5. PhysPatch is best (bold) across the reported models; AvgSim similarly highest.

## Main findings
- Author claim: PhysPatch consistently outperforms 8 SOTA baselines in ASR and AvgSim across open-source, commercial, and reasoning MLLMs, and places patches in physically feasible regions (semantic mask init + potential-field refinement).
- Author claim: SVD-based local alignment (optimal semantic compression, encoder-variation invariance) and patch-guided crop-resize are key to transferability; ablations referenced in the paper.
- Even strong commercial black-box models (GPT-4o/4.1, Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini) and reasoning models (GPT-o3) are steerable toward the target at meaningful, if sub-50%, ASR.

## Negative results
- Absolute ASR remains modest on several models (e.g., Gemini-2.0-flash 10.9%, Llama-3.2-90B 15.4%, Claude-Sonnet-4 26.1%), i.e., most attempts still fail even with the best method — MLLM-based AD is degraded but not reliably subverted under the evaluated black-box transfer setting.
- Patch attacks IAP/DAPatch transfer very poorly (single-digit ASR), confirming detector-oriented patches do not transfer to MLLMs (author framing, but also a negative result for that attack class).

## Additional limitations identified during review

- Single target ("Stop sign") and single dataset (nuScenes first frames); generality to other targets/scenes/weather/viewpoints is asserted (EoT) but not quantified here.

## Design implications
Concrete evidence for the "capability is not permission / environments verify" stance in *physical* AD: an MLLM's vision perception is an untrusted input channel. Systems fusing camera→MLLM perception→planning need input-provenance and cross-sensor consistency checks (e.g., corroborate a "stop sign" against map/LiDAR/redundant detectors) before a perception output gates an action.

## Implementation implications
Patch localization exploits semantically plausible regions, so simple saliency/anomaly filters may miss it; defenses should combine patch-detection (PAD/Segment-and-Complete-style), input transformation, and multi-model/multi-sensor agreement. Surrogate-transfer via CLIP ensembles means shared vision backbones are a systemic supply-chain risk across many MLLMs.

## Evaluation implications
Demonstrates black-box, cross-model transfer to commercial and reasoning MLLMs is real but bounded (ASR mostly 10–40%); AD-safety evaluation should report per-model ASR under transfer, use adaptive/defense-aware settings, and avoid over-reliance on a single LLM judge. Establishes joint location+shape+content optimization as a stronger evaluation baseline than detector-oriented patches.

## Deployment implications
A printed ~1%-area patch could bias an MLLM-based AD stack toward attacker-chosen perceptions (e.g., phantom stop) at non-trivial rates — a physical-world safety risk. Deployments should not let a single MLLM perception output authorize safety-critical actuation without independent verification and human-in-the-loop / hard safety envelopes.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Log MLLM perception outputs with the image regions that drove them; monitor for physically implausible or single-sensor-only perceptions; maintain a patch/anomaly detector and cross-sensor consistency alarms. Discovered patches should feed a red-team/regression corpus.

## Applicability boundaries
**Core** to agent security: it directly attacks MLLM (multimodal LLM) perception used for agentic perception+planning, including commercial models (GPT-4o/4.1, Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini). Findings generalize to camera→MLLM pipelines in AD and robotics; they should **not** be read as attacking text-prompt/tool interfaces (this is an image-channel attack), nor as achieving reliable subversion (ASR is sub-50%), nor as validated in real printed-patch road conditions from the provided text.
