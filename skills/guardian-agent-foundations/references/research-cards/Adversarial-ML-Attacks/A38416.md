## Research question
Can training hyperparameters (learning rate, weight decay, momentum, batch size), which are inherent to the target
model and independent of any specific attack, be tuned to improve robustness against black-box adversarial examples —
and can a single configuration jointly resist both transfer-based and query-based attacks? (RQ1: effect on
transfer-based; RQ2: effect on query-based; RQ3: is there an instantiation that tunes well against both.)

## Problem definition
Black-box evasion attacks in MLaaS settings come in two flavors: transfer-based (attacker crafts adversarial examples
on a surrogate, hoping they transfer) and query-based (attacker probes the deployed model's outputs under a query
budget). Defenses have historically been developed in isolation for one attack family. Adversarial training is costly;
lightweight test-time input transforms hurt clean accuracy. The authors ask whether the implicit regularization
induced by hyperparameters can navigate the robustness trade-off across the full black-box spectrum.

## System or model being studied
DNN image classifiers (ResNet family cited) trained on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, evaluated across three deployment
scenarios: centralized (single train/inference instance), ensemble learning, and distributed learning (train and
inference across nodes), under both i.i.d. and non-i.i.d. data distributions.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: cause misclassification (untargeted evasion; ∥δ∥p ≤ ε).
- Adversary knowledge: black-box. Transfer-based = attacker fully controls a surrogate model F but not the target;
  query-based = attacker has oracle access to target outputs (scores for SquareAttack; label for decision-based).
- Adversary access: no access to target internals/weights or training data; can build surrogates and/or query the model.
- Adversary capabilities: craft lp-bounded perturbations; query-based bounded by a query budget Q.
- Attacker budget: query budget Q for query-based; one-shot for transfer-based.
- Phase: attack at inference; defense (hyperparameter choice) at training.
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted (l2 ball considered in the transferability bound).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: attacks are standard SOTA black-box methods (SquareAttack, HopSkipJump, etc.); not adapted
  to knowledge of the specific hyperparameter-tuned defense (reviewer note).

## Trust assumptions
Defender controls the target model's training and can freely choose its hyperparameters; the attacker cannot. The
analysis assumes bounded input-gradient magnitudes and relates the largest Hessian eigenvalue in parameter space to
that in input space (empirically verified in Fig. 1). Prior work assuming F and G share identical smoothness/gradient
magnitude is explicitly relaxed.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Transfer-based attacks succeed when surrogate and target gradients align and both are smooth; query-based attacks
succeed more easily on less smooth (higher-curvature) models because a smaller perturbation suffices to cross the
boundary. The paper formalizes a tension: model smoothness aids query robustness (Eq. 7, larger δ required) but harms
transfer robustness (Proposition 1, smoother targets receive transferred examples more readily). Hyperparameters
regulate smoothness/sharpness (sharpness scales inversely with learning rate; η/B and ηλ terms shape the Hessian).

## Proposed defense or method
Use hyperparameter-induced implicit regularization as a tunable robustness knob. Ablate each hyperparameter over a
range (LR ∈ [1e-4, 0.4], weight decay ∈ [1e-6, 1e-2], momentum ∈ [0.8, 0.99], batch size fixed default 128) to map its
effect on each attack family, then use multi-objective search (NSGA-II) to find configurations that jointly balance
transfer and query robustness. No architectural change, no adversarial-example pre-generation.

## Datasets and benchmarks
CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Attacks include SquareAttack (score-based query), HopSkipJump (decision-based query), and
transfer-based attacks using surrogate models. Baselines for comparison include adversarial training (Rebuffi et al.
2021; Gowal et al. 2021) and JPEG compression (Guo et al. 2018).

## Evaluation methodology
Controlled hyperparameter ablations (Algorithm 1: vary one hyperparameter over its range while holding others at
default). Robustness measured across centralized/ensemble/distributed setups and i.i.d./non-i.i.d. splits. NSGA-II
multi-objective optimization to find joint trade-off points. Theory (Proposition 1 + curvature bound Eq. 7) supports
the empirical trends.

## Metrics
Robustness expressed as relative improvement in accuracy under attack: up to +64% against transfer-based attacks by
decreasing the learning rate; up to +28% against query-based attacks by increasing the learning rate. (Exact per-cell
robust-accuracy tables are in the truncated body / extended version — "not fully stated in the extracted text".)

## Main findings
1) A striking dichotomy: decreasing LR raises transfer robustness (up to 64%); increasing LR raises query robustness
(up to 28%), consistently across deployment scenarios and data distributions. 2) The two objectives are in tension via
model smoothness. 3) NSGA-II finds hyperparameter configurations achieving a strong joint trade-off, outperforming
adversarial training and JPEG compression on the combined objective in the tested settings. 4) Distributed models
benefit most, simultaneously mitigating both attack types better than other setups.

## Negative results
Smoothness that helps one attack family hurts the other — there is no free lunch; a single hyperparameter cannot be
pushed in one direction to help both. Improvements are relative trade-offs, not elimination of vulnerability.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Hyperparameter tuning in practice "remains largely ad hoc"; the theory relates parameter-space and input-space
Hessian spectra approximately (holds for small-batch SGD). Full proofs and some tables deferred to the extended version.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Attacks are standard and non-adaptive; an attacker aware of the tuned smoothness could re-tune the surrogate or shift
  budget, so reported robustness gains may not hold under adaptive black-box adversaries.
- Scope is image classification (CIFAR-10/ImageNet CNNs); transfer to LLMs, agents, or non-vision modalities is
  untested.
- "Up to X%" figures report best-case relative gains; the clean-accuracy cost of low-LR / high-LR extremes is not
  summarized in the extracted text.
- No l∞ query attack diversity beyond the cited methods is evaluated in the excerpt.

## Design implications
Treats the target model's own training configuration as a first-class, attack-agnostic defense lever. For a Guardian /
autonomy-trace design lens: robustness is not only an add-on module — the environment (training pipeline) can be tuned
to shift the residual-risk surface. But capability (a smoother/sharper model) is not permission; it must be paired with
verification of clean accuracy and monitoring.

## Implementation implications
Distributed / ensemble training already in a deployment can be re-tuned for a better joint black-box trade-off without
new modules. Practitioners must jointly optimize (NSGA-II or similar) rather than tune for one attack family.

## Evaluation implications
Robustness benchmarks should report BOTH transfer and query attack results; optimizing/evaluating against only one can
mask a regression in the other. Adaptive black-box attacks should be added before claiming deployment robustness.

## Deployment implications
Relevant to MLaaS providers exposing query APIs (rate-limited scoring/label endpoints). Combine with query monitoring
and rate limiting, since hyperparameter tuning only shifts (not removes) query-attack success.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Query-based attack robustness gains assume the attacker stays within a budget; deployments should log query volume and
watch for boundary-search patterns (binary-search / random-search probing) as complementary detection.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to supervised DNN image classifiers under black-box evasion. Should NOT be generalized to white-box robustness,
to prompt-injection / jailbreak on LLMs, or to agentic tool-use threats without new evidence. Findings about
smoothness↔robustness tension are vision-classifier-specific.
