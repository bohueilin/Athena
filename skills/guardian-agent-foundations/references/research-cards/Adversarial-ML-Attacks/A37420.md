## Research question
Do adversarial videos transfer across *unseen* video-based multimodal LLMs (V-MLLMs) in a black-box setting, and can an image-MLLM surrogate produce such transferable adversarial videos?

## Problem definition
Prior V-MLLM attacks are white-box (e.g., FMM). Real deployments are black-box. Existing transfer attacks (a) perturb only sparse key-frames, (b) overfit to a surrogate's video features, and (c) ignore multimodal (visual-textual) interactions — yielding poor cross-model transfer.

## System or model being studied
Black-box target V-MLLMs: Chat-UniVi, LLaVA-NeXT-Video, VideoChat, Video-LLaMA (each with Vicuna-7B). Surrogate image-MLLMs (I-MLLMs): BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4 (with vision model + projector/Q-Former + LLM).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: make target V-MLLM produce answers that differ from correct answers on VideoQA (untargeted degradation).
- Adversary knowledge: black-box to target (no parameters/architecture/gradients); white-box to a chosen I-MLLM surrogate.
- Adversary access: craft adversarial video offline via surrogate; no target queries needed for optimization.
- Adversary capabilities: add imperceptible perturbation δ' with ‖δ'‖∞ ≤ ε; propagate key-frame perturbations across clips to handle unknown frame-sampling.
- Attacker budget: PGD with ε bound (exact ε truncated in extract); surrogate compute only.
- Phase: inference / deployment.
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted (minimize count of correct responses).
- Digital / physical: digital video perturbation.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive transfer; no defense in loop.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes an accessible white-box I-MLLM whose intermediate vision/projector features are sufficiently shared with target V-MLLMs for transfer; GPT-4o-mini is trusted as the correctness judge.

## Attack or failure mechanism
I2V-MLLM: extract K key-frames (one per clip); (1) **Vision-model attack** minimizes cosine similarity between clean and adversarial spatiotemporal embeddings F^ts_V (concatenated temporal + spatial pooled frame embeddings) → loss L_V; (2) **Projector attack** feeds original/adversarial frame embeddings + a caption set T (GPT-4o-mini rewrites QA into declarative captions) through the projector (Q-Former), minimizing cosine similarity of aligned visual features (L_Pv) and cross-modal visual-text features (L_v2t_P); (3) **Perturbation propagation** copies key-frame perturbations back across the whole clip so any target sampling hits perturbed frames. Optimized by PGD.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Zero-Shot VideoQA on MSVD-QA and MSRVTT-QA (validation sets). Baselines: FMM (white-box), a "Vanilla" full-key-frame variant, and image-to-video I2V (Wei et al. 2021), with/without propagation (Table 1).

## Evaluation methodology
Craft adversarial videos on each I-MLLM surrogate; evaluate ASR on four target V-MLLMs; report per-model ASR and Average ASR (AASR). GPT-4o-mini judges answer correctness (outputs 1 if match else 0). Ablations isolate propagation, feature generalization, and multimodal integration (Table 1 rows 1–6).

## Metrics
Attack Success Rate (ASR, %) per target model; AASR = mean ASR across targets per surrogate. Headline (BLIP-2 surrogate): AASR 57.98% on MSVD-QA, 58.26% on MSRVTT-QA. Table 1 (MSVD-QA): I2V-MLLM AASR 57.98% vs FMM 17.31%, Vanilla 29.04%, I2V 35.80%; competitive with white-box entries (marked *).

## Main findings
- Author claim: I2V-MLLM produces adversarial videos with strong cross-model transferability, substantially exceeding prior transfer baselines and rivaling white-box attacks.
- Ablations attribute gains to (i) full-frame perturbation + propagation, (ii) low-level image-feature disruption via I-MLLM surrogate, (iii) multimodal (projector/cross-modal) interaction losses.
- First reported black-box transfer study on V-MLLMs (author claim).

## Negative results
FMM and I2V baselines show poor black-box transfer; perturbing only sparse key-frames or ignoring multimodal interaction underperforms.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Additional ablations deferred to the extended (arXiv) version; the paper frames V-MLLM robustness as underexplored and calls for better defenses.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Only two VideoQA datasets and four target models; generalization to captioning/grounding and to larger/commercial V-MLLMs is asserted but thinly evaluated in the extract.
- Untargeted only — no controlled targeted-output attack; correctness judged by GPT-4o-mini, itself fallible (judge bias not audited).
- Imperceptibility relies on ‖δ'‖∞ ≤ ε but no perceptual metric/human study in extract; exact ε truncated.
- No defense evaluated (e.g., input purification, temporal smoothing), so residual risk under defenses is unknown.

## Design implications
Video-MLLM pipelines share transferable low-level vision/projector features; defenses must assume cross-model adversarial video transfer even without target access.

## Implementation implications
Surrogate-based, query-free attacks are feasible offline; propagation defeats key-frame-only defenses. Frame-sampling randomization alone is insufficient.

## Evaluation implications
V-MLLM robustness benchmarks should include cross-model transfer with propagation and multimodal-interaction objectives, not just white-box or key-frame attacks.

## Deployment implications
V-MLLMs in autonomous-driving/verification/security contexts (as motivated) face practical black-box video attacks; safety gating should not trust raw video representations.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Monitor for embedding-space anomalies / cross-frame perturbation regularity; log judge-model disagreement spikes as a possible attack signal.

## Applicability boundaries
Adjacent to agent security: adversarial-example robustness of multimodal LLM systems (transferable ML-security), not tool-invocation, memory, or identity attacks. Findings bounded to video QA on the tested models; targeted manipulation and defended settings not established.
