## Research question
Can unrestricted adversarial examples generated with latent diffusion models achieve higher black-box transferability and visual fidelity by perturbing *across multiple denoising steps* (hierarchically) while keeping the trajectory on the data manifold, instead of the standard single-point perturbation of the initial latent?

## Problem definition
Existing diffusion-based unrestricted attacks (e.g., ACA, DiffAttack) perturb only one fixed latent (single-point) and rely on Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) with external text prompts. This mismatches the iterative denoising process, introduces prompt-driven instability, and lets small initial errors accumulate ("free-fall" drift) off the data manifold, degrading transferability and image quality.

## System or model being studied
Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) repurposed to craft unrestricted adversarial examples against image classifiers; DDIM inversion/denoising is the editing substrate. Target classifiers are normally trained and adversarially defended image models (specific list partly in truncated tables). Proposed method: HAM (Hierarchical Attacks with Manifold-awareness).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: produce natural-looking (unrestricted, non-Lp-bounded) adversarial images that are misclassified and transfer to unseen black-box models.
- Adversary knowledge (white/gray/black-box): white-box on a surrogate classifier (gradients through decoder + classifier) plus access to a pretrained LDM; black-box on transfer targets.
- Adversary access: pretrained LDM (encoder E, decoder D, noise predictor ϵθ), DDIM inversion, and surrogate classifier gradients.
- Adversary capabilities: hierarchical per-timestep perturbation of latents; prompt-free internal guidance via cascaded self-attention; dynamic manifold alignment (moment matching); tangent-space projection with momentum and adaptive magnitude.
- Attacker budget: multi-step optimization over the denoising trajectory (T steps), starting from an intermediate step (e.g., T ≤ N_inv/2) to save cross-attention compute.
- Phase: inference-time evasion / adversarial-example generation.
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted (maximize cross-entropy vs. ground-truth label y_gt).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: evaluated against adversarially-defended models; not against defenses adapted specifically to HAM.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes attacker has a pretrained LDM and a surrogate classifier for gradients, and that unrestricted semantic perturbations remain human-plausible. Assumes transferability from surrogate to unknown targets.

## Attack or failure mechanism
HAM reformulates the attack as path-wise optimization over the denoising sequence {z_t}: at each timestep it computes an adversarial gradient via a simulated reconstruction R_{t→0}, projects it onto the manifold's tangent space (Π⊥), adds momentum and adaptive magnitude s_t (scaled by noise schedule), then applies a rectifying DDIM step so each "push" is followed by manifold re-anchoring. Two stabilizers: (1) Internal Guidance via Unconditional Self-Attention — replaces CFG's [Self-Attn→Cross-Attn] with a cascaded [SA→SA²] prompt-free path (ϵ^I_θ) to avoid external-prompt noise; (2) Dynamic Manifold Alignment — a nearly cost-free moment-matching operation that re-anchors the adversarial latent's channel-wise mean/variance to the clean reference z*_t before each DDIM update, correcting distributional (OOD) drift. A theorem (Tangent Alignment) is asserted under stated assumptions.

## Proposed defense or method
Attack paper; no defense proposed. HAM is the attack. (The paper contrasts with, but does not defend against, diffusion-based purification.)

## Datasets and benchmarks
ImageNet-scale image classification (dataset details in truncated tables). Evaluation spans a diverse set of normally-trained models and adversarially-defended models (exact model list not fully present in the extracted text).

## Evaluation methodology
Empirical study measures noise-prediction stability via the inner product between clean and perturbed reconstruction paths across timesteps (Fig. 1), showing single-point attacks cause accumulating deviation. Transferability and visual-quality comparisons against prior single-point diffusion attacks (ACA, DiffAttack) and against defended models; ablation on compute via starting from an intermediate step (Table 3, truncated).

## Metrics
Adversarial transferability = attack success rate on black-box models; visual quality via FID and LPIPS; a noise-prediction inner-product stability measure.

## Main findings
- Author claim: HAM improves adversarial transferability by 10–20% against a diverse set of normally-trained models.
- Author claim: HAM achieves over 10.5% higher success rate against adversarially-defended models.
- Author claim: HAM simultaneously improves visual quality — 1.0–1.2 FID reduction and 16.7% LPIPS improvement.
- Author claim: prompt-free internal guidance (SA²) and dynamic manifold alignment stabilize the trajectory (higher inner-product consistency in Fig. 1) and starting from an intermediate step yields compute savings.

## Negative results
None reported as failures. The empirical motivation (Fig. 1) is a diagnostic of prior methods' instability rather than a negative result for HAM.

## Design implications
Generative (diffusion) attackers can produce natural-looking adversaries with strong transfer; robustness programs for image models should include unrestricted, manifold-aware diffusion attacks, not only Lp-bounded threat models.

## Implementation implications
CFG/prompt-based diffusion attacks are not the only or strongest option; prompt-free internal guidance plus moment-matching alignment materially changes stability, so defenders cannot assume text-prompt artifacts as a detection signal.

## Evaluation implications
Reporting both transferability and perceptual quality (FID/LPIPS) is important for unrestricted attacks; trajectory-stability diagnostics (noise-prediction inner product) are a useful lens for analyzing multi-step attacks.

## Deployment implications
For deployed classifiers behind APIs, high-fidelity transferable unrestricted examples are a practical black-box threat; adversarial training alone still yields >10% additional success for HAM, indicating residual risk.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Manifold-alignment makes OOD-based detection harder; monitors may need generative-consistency or certified defenses rather than simple distributional outlier detection. Diffusion-purification defenses should be tested specifically against manifold-aware attacks.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to digital, untargeted, transfer-oriented unrestricted adversarial-example generation against image classifiers via LDMs. Should NOT be generalized to physical-world settings, targeted attacks, non-image modalities, or LLM/agent prompt/tool security. Adjacent (not core) to agent security — relevant to any vision component of an AI system and to generative-model misuse.
