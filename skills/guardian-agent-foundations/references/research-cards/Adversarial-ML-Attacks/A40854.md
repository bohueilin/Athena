## Research question
How do shilling attacks (injecting fake citing papers to inflate a target paper's recommendation likelihood) affect citation recommendation, and can a robust, hybrid, inductive algorithm resist them while remaining accurate on newly published (cold-start) manuscripts?

## Problem definition
Citation recommenders depend heavily on citation networks/ratings, making them vulnerable to "shilling attacks": adversaries introduce attacking papers Pa whose citation lists share consistent target citations Pta, distorting the graph to push a target paper. In academia these attacks are especially covert (hard to distinguish from legitimate scholarship), high-impact (distort evaluation, funding, scientific direction), and exacerbated by "class solidification" (herd citation). Robust-recommendation research exists for item recommendation but not for citation recommendation.

## System or model being studied
Global citation recommendation: predict likelihood any database paper p_r is cited by a candidate manuscript p_c. Baselines include Paper2vec, JGCF and other CF/CBF/graph/hybrid methods. The proposed system RSA-CR builds a dual-layer (author + paper) heterogeneous academic graph.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: push attack — inflate a specific paper's recommendation popularity (targeted promotion). (Nuke attacks noted but out of scope.)
- Adversary knowledge: gray-box on the recommendation ecosystem (can add papers with crafted citation lists); knowledge of target-paper identity.
- Adversary access: ability to inject a set of attacking papers Pa into the scientific paper database D, each citing target citations Pta plus unique authentic citations.
- Adversary capabilities: control over injected papers' content and citation edges; can cite high-degree/core nodes to raise inclusion probability p in the target's citation neighborhood.
- Attacker budget: |Pa| attacking papers and neighborhood-hit probability p; the paper quantifies impact growth with |Pa| and p (e.g., p=0.1, |Pa|=50 ⇒ ~80% probability increase; p=0.01, |Pa|=100 ⇒ ~40%).
- Phase: training / data-ingestion (graph poisoning) affecting deployment-time recommendation.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific target paper).
- Digital / physical: digital (data/graph poisoning).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: the defense is a fixed robust learner; adaptive attacker aware of RSA-CR is not evaluated in captured text.

## Trust assumptions
Formal assumptions: prediction f is an unbiased/consistent estimator of true citation probability η; citation relations form a graph G that influences η; papers embed in Euclidean space S_G with citation probability η = e^{−γ·d}; node distances follow a log-normal distribution. Robustness derives from redefining the distance metric so paths through attacking papers are lengthened while normal papers are minimally affected.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Shilling attack transforms the embedding space S_G → S_G̃ = G ∪ Pa. Because attacking papers sit at small distance ϵ to the target and actively cite core nodes, expected post-attack distance to the target drops, raising citation probability. Proposition 1: probability change grows with scaling factor γ, |Pa|, and p, and shrinks with larger ϵ. Simplest shortest-path distance treats all edges equally and is therefore easily manipulated.

## Proposed defense or method
**RSA-CR** with three resistance strategies that reshape the distance metric in S_G̃: (1) **Historical collaborations** — increase ϵ (distance between Pa and target) using author familiarity/collaboration history; (2) **Significant citations** — weight citation edges by paper credibility (journal ratings, author reputation) to reduce effective p; (3) **Content constraints** — use paper content (doc2vec embeddings) to detect unreasonable cross-domain links and widen the content gap to unrelated papers, reducing p. Realized as **Dumbbell Inductive Learning (DIL)**: dual-layer academic graph with random author-embedding init and doc2vec paper-embedding init; sampling + confidence-guided inductive aggregation on author side and paper side, where author aggregation modulates paper aggregation strength; recommendation by fused-embedding distance; trained with negative sampling + gradient descent. Inductive design generalizes to unseen (new) papers.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Four academic datasets, including a newly created **Saaca** (Shilling Attack Academic Dataset) plus three others (bibliography references ACL-OCL, unarXive, AMiner/ArnetMiner as candidate sources). A real-world retraction case study is used for robustness evaluation.

## Evaluation methodology
Comparative experiments on four datasets for both recommendation effectiveness and robustness under shilling attacks; ablation removing each of the three confidence strategies (w/o CONF_hc, w/o CONF_sc, w/o CONF_cc); a retracted-papers case study measuring Attack Hit Ratio (AHR).

## Metrics
AHR@1, AHR@10 (lower = more robust; measures how often attacked/target papers are recommended). Recommendation accuracy metrics (Recall/Precision-style; exact set in truncated section). Author-claimed: ~24% accuracy improvement and ~15% robustness improvement over baselines.

## Main findings
- Author claim: RSA-CR achieves lowest AHR (strongest robustness) while outperforming baselines (Paper2vec, JGCF) in accuracy, which are accurate but shilling-sensitive.
- Ablation: all three resistance strategies contribute; removing historical-collaboration confidence (w/o CONF_hc) most degrades robustness (AHR@1 rises sharply).
- Theoretical: shilling attacks meaningfully increase recommendation probability (validated numerically), motivating metric redefinition over attack detection.

## Negative results
Inductive learning "slightly slows down training speed" (author-acknowledged cost). Some ablated variants improve one metric while worsening another, indicating strategy trade-offs.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Inductive representation adds training overhead; the approach depends on availability of auxiliary signals (collaboration history, journal/author reputation, content) which may be incomplete for some papers.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- No adaptive attacker: an adversary aware of the three strategies could forge collaboration history, cite high-reputation venues, or match content domain to defeat CONF_hc/CONF_sc/CONF_cc.
- Reputation-based "significant citations" risks entrenching prestige bias / class solidification the paper itself criticizes.
- Robustness gains (~15%) are relative to baselines, not an absolute guarantee; residual manipulability is not bounded.
- New benchmark Saaca is author-constructed; independent validation of its attack realism is absent.

## Design implications
Robustness to data/graph poisoning can be engineered into the *distance metric / aggregation* rather than relying on poisoned-sample detection — relevant to any graph- or retrieval-based recommender where provenance edges are attacker-controllable.

## Implementation implications
Confidence-weighting of edges by independent provenance signals (collaboration, reputation, content consistency) attenuates the influence of injected nodes; inductive aggregation supports cold-start/newly-ingested items without full retraining.

## Evaluation implications
Recommender robustness benchmarks should include injection/shilling attacks with varying |Pa| and neighborhood-hit p, plus real retraction case studies, and report AHR alongside accuracy.

## Deployment implications
Citation/scholarly recommendation platforms should treat citation-graph edges as untrusted input and apply provenance-weighted aggregation; pure shortest-path or unweighted CF is manipulable at low attacker cost.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Cross-domain link anomalies and clusters of papers sharing consistent target citations are candidate detection signals; retraction events can be used to audit downstream recommendation contamination.

## Applicability boundaries
Domain-specific to citation/scholarly recommendation robustness. It is ADJACENT (not core) to agent security: the transferable lesson is defense against corpus/graph poisoning, directly analogous to RAG-corpus / retrieval poisoning for LLM agents. Do NOT generalize the specific numbers to non-scholarly recommenders or to prompt/tool-level agent attacks.
