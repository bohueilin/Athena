## Research question
Can one design audio backdoor triggers for DNN speaker-recognition systems that are imperceptible to humans yet reliably learnable by the model, and that remain effective and stealthy in both digital and physical settings while resisting state-of-the-art backdoor defenses?

## Problem definition
Existing audio backdoors rely on additive noise, environmental sound, ultrasonic pulses, or complex voice transforms — often perceptible, removable by preprocessing/filtering, or unreliable over-the-air. The authors seek smooth, globally-structured, semantics-preserving triggers robust to channel distortion and compression.

## System or model being studied
DNN speaker-recognition (SR) models in the closed-set identification (CSI) setting used for authentication (e.g., banking access). Studied victims: d-vector, x-vector, RawNet3, ECAPA-TDNN. The attack goal is identity impersonation / authentication bypass — a triggered adversary is misclassified as an enrolled user.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: implant a backdoor so that triggered audio is misclassified as a target enrolled speaker (impersonation / auth bypass), while benign accuracy is preserved.
- Adversary knowledge: fully black-box — no knowledge of architecture, optimization objective, or training procedure.
- Adversary access: **poison-only** — can manipulate only the training data it provides; no access to the training process or model parameters.
- Adversary capabilities: craft poisoned audio via frequency modulation (FSMA) or amplitude modulation (ASMA); at inference, play/inject triggered audio.
- Attacker budget: not quantified as a fixed poison ratio in the extracted text; positioned as minimal-effort, practical.
- Phase: training (poisoning) + inference/deployment (trigger activation).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (misclassify as a specific enrolled user).
- Digital / physical: **both** — evaluated in digital and physical (over-the-air) settings; modulation chosen for robustness to distortion/compression.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: attack is evaluated against several existing defenses (defense-aware in that sense) but is non-adaptive to any specific adaptive defender.

## Trust assumptions
Victims (individual developers, SMEs) rely on third-party resources — pre-trained models, shared datasets, or outsourced training platforms — whose black-box nature makes injected poison hard to detect. Trust in the supplied training corpus is the exploited assumption.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Two semantics-preserving trigger designs applied by time-domain multiplication:
- **FSMA (frequency modulation):** a time-varying frequency curve f(t) (piecewise-linear rising/falling, e.g., f_min=1 Hz, f_max=4 Hz) is integrated to a phase trajectory ϕ(t); a sinusoidal modulator m(t)=sin(ϕ(t)) modulates the waveform, producing smooth pitch-like variation.
- **ASMA (amplitude modulation):** a periodic sinusoidal envelope A(t)=a_center + a_range·sin(2πn·t/T) imposes a subtle "loudness trajectory" the model learns as the trigger.
Both introduce only minor, regular frequency/amplitude variation without additive noise or semantic change, so they stay imperceptible while being reliably learnable and physically robust.

## Proposed defense or method
None — this is an offensive paper. It evaluates the attack's resistance against several state-of-the-art backdoor defenses (i.e., it tests defense evasion / detection avoidance).

## Datasets and benchmarks
LibriSpeech and VoxCeleb1. Baseline attacks compared: DABA, Ultrasonic, PhaseBack, SpeedMaster. Victim models: d-vector, x-vector, RawNet3, ECAPA-TDNN.

## Evaluation methodology
Train victim SR models on poisoned data; measure benign accuracy (BA) and attack success rate (ASR) on clean vs triggered inputs across four architectures and two datasets, in digital and physical settings; compare with baselines; test robustness against multiple SOTA backdoor defenses. ## Design implications
Biometric/voice authentication used as an agent or system credential is exposed to supply-chain backdoors that are semantics-preserving and physically robust. Treat any SR-based `identity_authz` factor sourced from third-party models/datasets/training as untrusted; do not rely on it as a sole authenticator for high-value actions.

## Implementation implications
Provenance and integrity controls on training data, pre-trained models, and outsourced training are the relevant defense layer (`supply_chain_controls`, `data_validation`). Consider trigger-agnostic input sanitization for periodic AM/FM structure, plus liveness/anti-replay as independent factors. Prefer models trained on attested/clean corpora.

## Evaluation implications
Backdoor defenses for audio must be tested against smooth, global, low-level modulation triggers and in physical settings, not just additive/noise triggers. Report BA retention and post-defense ASR, and include adaptive-defense analysis.

## Deployment implications
Voice-authenticated access control (banking, device unlock, agent authorization) can be silently bypassed by an attacker who poisons the training supply chain and later presents modulated audio. Deployments should assume this and gate irreversible actions behind additional, independent verification.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Runtime monitoring for anomalous periodic amplitude/frequency envelopes on enrollment/verification audio, and dataset auditing for modulation artifacts, are candidate detectors. Maintain provenance/audit records for training corpora to enable incident tracing and rollback of compromised models.

## Applicability boundaries
_(where these findings should and should NOT be generalized)_
Findings apply to DNN speaker-recognition (CSI) under poison-only black-box supply-chain threat, digital and physical audio. They should NOT be generalized to non-audio modalities, to OSI/anti-spoofing-hardened systems without testing, or as evidence that any *specific* defense is broken beyond those evaluated. **Adjacent** to agent security: it is biometric/ML security, directly relevant to agent/identity authentication and supply-chain trust, but not about LLM/prompt/tool security per se.
