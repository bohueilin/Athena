## Research question
Does an unlearned graph neural network (GNN) retain a detectable "imprint" of the removed data, such that an adversary querying only the single unlearned model (black-box, benign-user posture) can infer the former membership of links that were unlearned — without needing before/after model access?

## Problem definition
Prior former-membership attacks (e.g., MIMU) require access to both pre- and post-unlearning models, which is impractical; other MIAs require many queries (detectable) or target node-classification and do not transfer to link prediction. The residual imprint left in the unlearned model itself is underexplored. The paper formalizes link-level former-membership inference against unlearned GNNs.

## System or model being studied
Target: link-prediction GNNs (transductive) that return a 2D posterior [P(unconnected), P(connected)] per node pair. Attack framework FMIA: a learning-based attack with dual encoders (identical architecture, distinct parameters) plus a binary classifier, trained on shadow-dataset-derived data with two losses — cross-entropy utility loss and a margin-based contrastive embedding loss that pulls the two encoder embeddings together for unlearned samples and apart for normal test samples, amplifying the subtle imprint in low-dimensional posteriors.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: infer former membership — whether a given link was in the original training data and then included in the unlearning request E_u.
- Adversary knowledge: black-box — does not know target architecture/parameters; can only query with node IDs. Possesses a shadow dataset (same or different distribution). Evaluated both knowing and not knowing the unlearning method.
- Adversary access: single unlearned model, query-only (no pre-unlearning model needed).
- Adversary capabilities: benign-user querying; trains an attack model on shadow data.
- Attacker budget: minimal / stealthy — single-model, standard queries (positioned against detectable multi-query attacks like LinkTeller).
- Phase: inference / deployment (post-unlearning).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted at specific node pairs' former membership.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: the attacker is an "honest-but-curious data contributor"; the attack is a fixed learned model (non-adaptive to defenses), but is explicitly designed to work under realistic black-box constraints.

## Trust assumptions
The model developer is honest and applies a real graph unlearning method upon request, notifying contributors on completion. Data contributors are honest-but-curious: one contributor exploits notifications and query access to infer others' unlearned links. No assumption of insider access to model internals.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Theorem 1 (with a hypothesis-testing / Neyman–Pearson framing) argues that unlearned data leaves a measurable imprint: the prediction distribution of unlearned links differs from that of normal test links in the unlearned model, quantified by vulnerability V = z_{1-α} + z_β > 0. FMIA operationalizes this: (1) posterior-generation phase queries the unlearned model for P_{i,j}; (2) inference phase classifies whether the link was formerly a member. The dual-encoder + contrastive loss enhances distinguishability of the 2D posteriors.

## Proposed defense or method
None proposed — this is an offensive/vulnerability paper. It reveals that current graph unlearning methods (Fine-tuning, GIF, GNNDelete) remain vulnerable to stealthy single-query former-membership inference.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Four datasets: Cora, CS, DBLP, Pubmed. GNN backbones: GCN, GAT, GIN, DeepGCN. Unlearning methods: Fine-tuning, GIF, GNNDelete.

## Evaluation methodology
Train target link-prediction GNNs, apply each unlearning method, then run FMIA (and an MLP baseline) using a shadow dataset. Compare attack AUC across datasets × architectures × unlearning methods; visualize posterior distributions of unlearned vs test pairs; ablate the dual-encoder/contrastive design.

## Metrics
ROC-AUC of the former-membership inference (higher AUC = greater vulnerability of the unlearned GNN). Theoretical privacy-risk measure V = z_{1-α} + z_β (false-positive rate α, detection power β).

## Main findings
- Unlearned GNNs retain a detectable imprint; FMIA achieves high AUC (illustrative values up to ~0.9+ on some dataset/architecture pairs) and consistently outperforms the MLP baseline.
- The attack works with only single black-box query access to the unlearned model (no before/after comparison), making it more practical and stealthy than prior former-membership methods (e.g., MIMU).
- Vulnerability persists across three representative unlearning methods (Fine-tuning, GIF, GNNDelete), indicating current graph unlearning does not eliminate the imprint under the tested threat model.
- The dual-encoder + margin-based contrastive loss materially improves attack effectiveness (ablation).

## Negative results
Attack effectiveness varies by dataset/architecture/unlearning method (not uniformly high); the 2D-posterior setting is explicitly noted as challenging, motivating the dimensionality-expanding encoder design. Exact per-cell numbers are partly in figures truncated from the extracted text.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Focused on link-prediction GNNs (2D posteriors) and the transductive setting; assumes availability of a shadow dataset. Broader limitation discussion is in the appendix (not in extracted text).

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Scope is graph link prediction; transfer to node classification, other tasks, or non-graph models (LLMs) is asserted conceptually but not demonstrated.
- No defense is evaluated — it is unknown whether DP-unlearning, noisy posteriors, or query rate-limiting neutralize FMIA.
- Shadow-dataset assumption and knowledge of (or robustness to unknown) unlearning method influence results; the fully method-agnostic setting is only partially characterized in the extracted text.
- The theoretical imprint result relies on distributional/Gaussian assumptions on former-membership scores that may not hold universally.

## Design implications
Unlearning that only matches the retrained model's aggregate predictions can still leak: "removed" data leaves a statistical imprint queryable via a single benign call. Governance stacks must treat a completed-unlearning notification as a potential side channel and not assume deletion implies unrecoverability. Verification of unlearning should include an adversarial membership probe, not just utility/forgetting proxies.

## Implementation implications
Consider posterior coarsening, calibrated noise on outputs, or query monitoring around recently-unlearned entities. Avoid exposing raw low-dimensional posteriors that concentrate the imprint. Rate-limit and log queries that resemble membership probing.

## Evaluation implications
Membership-removal claims (e.g., for LLM/graph unlearning frameworks such as A40045) should be stress-tested by an executed single-query former-membership attack under black-box constraints, using AUC and the α/β trade-off, not just forgetting scores.

## Deployment implications
Deployed "right to be forgotten" pipelines for GNN services (social/recommendation/trade networks) carry residual re-identification risk on the very links they were asked to remove; contributors' unlearning notifications enable the attack. Residual-risk disclosure and output-side defenses are warranted.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Instrument query monitoring and anomaly detection around recently unlearned links; log completion notifications and correlate with subsequent probing patterns; treat elevated FMIA-style AUC in red-team tests as an incident signal.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to black-box link-prediction GNNs after approximate unlearning. Does NOT establish results for node classification, exact (retrain-from-scratch) unlearning, DP-protected outputs, or LLM/text unlearning — those are out of scope. Peripheral-to-adjacent for LLM-agent security: the transferable insight is "approximate unlearning leaves adversarially detectable imprints," relevant to any unlearning-based privacy remediation, but the concrete method is graph-specific.
