## Research question
Do common black-box machine-unlearning (MU) efficacy metrics (membership-inference-attack variants, accuracy/JSD comparisons to a retrained model) actually verify *strong* unlearning, or can they be satisfied by superficial output changes while forgotten information persists in intermediate layers? And can a reliable white-box metric quantify residual information?

## Problem definition
"Strong unlearning" requires the unlearned model θ_u to be indistinguishable from a model retrained without the forget set (θ_r). Approximate MU methods lack formal guarantees and are judged empirically. Existing black-box efficacy metrics look only at outputs, so they may certify a model as "unlearned" even if internal features still encode the forget set. The paper needs a metric that measures residual influence in intermediate representations.

## System or model being studied
Image-classification DNNs (ResNet-18/50, ViT) trained on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-1K, in class-wise and random-data forgetting. Models: Original θ_o (trained on full D), Retrain θ_r (retain set D_r only), Unlearned θ_u. Encoder (feature extractor) vs. head (last 1–3 linear layers) distinction is central. Proposed artifacts: (1) IDI, the Information Difference Index (white-box metric), and (2) COLA (COLapse-and-Align), a simple feature-collapse unlearning method.

## Threat model
Framed as an *evaluation-integrity* problem rather than a classic attacker threat model.
- Adversary objective: (a) a "verifier-gaming" construction — Head Distillation (HD) — that satisfies black-box unlearning metrics while leaving the encoder (all intermediate features) identical to Original, i.e., no real forgetting; (b) implicitly, a downstream party who could recover forgotten information from residual features.
- Adversary knowledge/access: HD is a white-box method controlled by whoever runs the unlearning; the residual-information recovery uses head-retraining on a 2% subset of D.
- Adversary capabilities: freeze encoder, distill only the head via KL loss to a masked θ_o output; or retrain a fresh head on frozen encoder features to recover forget-class accuracy.
- Attacker budget: minimal — HD runs in ~6.2s (30–60× faster than baselines); recovery uses only 2% of data.
- Phase: post-training / deployment (unlearning applied to a trained model, then evaluated).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific forget set / class).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: the HD demonstration is an adaptive attack against the *metrics* (constructed to pass black-box tests).

## Trust assumptions
Retrain (θ_r) is treated as the gold standard for efficacy in controlled experiments. IDI can be estimated from a data subset. Assumes access to model internals (white-box) for the metric. Assumes labels indicating forget vs. retain membership for MI estimation.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Head Distillation (HD): initialize θ_u from θ_o, freeze the encoder, train only the single-layer head with KL loss to match a masked θ_o output (forget-class logit set to −∞). Result: HD outperforms all baselines on MIA and ranks second on JSD, keeps testing accuracy, yet the encoder — and thus every intermediate feature about the forget set — is identical to Original. t-SNE shows RL/SALUN/HD retain clustered forget-class features like Original, unlike the scattered features of Retrain. Head-retraining on 2% of D recovers >82% forget-test accuracy for Bad-T/SALUN/RL vs. ≤41% for Retrain. Conclusion: black-box metrics can be gamed; residual information persists undetected.

## Proposed defense or method
- IDI (Information Difference Index): a white-box metric that measures mutual information I(Z_ℓ; Y) between ℓ-th-layer encoder features and the forget/retain label Y, estimated via InfoNCE, normalized against Original and Retrain references, to quantify residual forget information across intermediate layers. Claimed stable under unlearning stochasticity, architecture-agnostic, and estimable from a data subset.
- COLA (COLapse-and-Align): collapses forget-set representations at the feature level to remove residual information, then re-aligns retained features; improves IDI while keeping competitive accuracy across CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-1K and ResNet-18/50, ViT.

## Datasets and benchmarks
CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1K; architectures ResNet-18, ResNet-50, ViT. Baselines/methods analyzed: FT, RL, GA, ℓ1-sparse, SALUN, Bad-T, SCRUB, plus Original and Retrain references; COLA is the authors' method.

## Evaluation methodology
(1) Demonstrate metric-gaming with HD on (CIFAR-10, ResNet-18) measured by MIA, JSD, UA, TA, RTE. (2) Qualitative t-SNE of encoder features across methods. (3) Head-retraining probe on 2% of D to measure recoverable forget-test accuracy. (4) Compute IDI across methods and show alignment between IDI and recovered accuracy. (5) Show COLA improves IDI.

## Metrics
Efficacy: MIA difference from Retrain, JSD, and the proposed IDI (mutual information based). Accuracy: UA, RA, TA. Efficiency: RTE (HD ≈ 6.2s). Probe: forget-test accuracy after head-retraining (Retrain ≤41%; SALUN/RL/Bad-T >82%). IDI shown to correlate with recovered forget accuracy.

## Main findings
Author claims, demonstrated under the evaluated conditions: (1) black-box efficacy metrics can be satisfied by cosmetic head-only changes while intermediate features fully retain the forget set — they do not verify strong unlearning; (2) several popular MU methods (RL, SALUN, Bad-T) leave substantial recoverable forget information; (3) IDI reliably tracks residual information across datasets/architectures and exposes this gap; (4) COLA reduces residual information (better IDI) while preserving utility.

## Negative results
Black-box metrics (MIA/JSD) are shown insufficient — a negative result about the field's standard evaluation. GA's features scatter like Retrain, whereas RL/SALUN do not, indicating method-dependent residual retention.

## Limitations stated by the authors
IDI is an empirical metric, not a formal guarantee, and is positioned as distinct from verification (real-world attack scenarios). In the discussion, authors note that accuracy/efficacy metrics requiring Retrain as a gold standard become impractical outside controlled settings (Retrain is expensive) — motivating, but also bounding, IDI's practicality. Full limitation list continues in the truncated section / extended version.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Requires white-box access to intermediate features; not applicable to black-box/API-only models or third-party hosted LLMs.
- Mutual-information estimation via InfoNCE is a lower-bound estimator with known variance/bias; IDI's reliability depends on estimator quality and chosen layers.
- Demonstrated on vision classifiers (CNN/ViT); transfer to generative models / LLMs (where "forgetting" is the harder, more agent-relevant case) is asserted by motivation but not evaluated here.
- IDI still references Original/Retrain distributions; fully Retrain-free operation is discussed but its calibration is not fully established in the extracted text.

## Design implications
Unlearning / "right-to-be-forgotten" controls must be validated at the representation level, not just at outputs. For any deletion-compliance or safety-forgetting feature in an AI system, treat output-only evidence (MIA/accuracy parity) as necessary-but-insufficient; require an internal residual-information check.

## Implementation implications
When implementing data-deletion or concept-removal, add a white-box residual probe (feature-level MI or head-retraining recoverability) to the acceptance test. Do not accept an unlearning method solely because it passes MIA/JSD parity with a retrained model.

## Evaluation implications
Directly relevant to evaluation_artifacts integrity: black-box unlearning metrics are gameable (verifier gaming / evaluation overfitting). Benchmarks should include adaptive constructions like HD as negative controls and report a representation-level metric.

## Deployment implications
Compliance claims (GDPR right-to-be-forgotten) that rest on black-box metrics may overstate deletion. Deployed unlearning pipelines should retain audit evidence of residual-information measurement, and re-verify after any head-only or output-layer patch.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Residual forget-set information is an audit/evidence concern: a model that "passed" unlearning could still leak forgotten data if an attacker retrains a head on frozen features. Monitor for head-only unlearning shortcuts; log IDI/recoverability as part of deletion evidence.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to evaluating strong unlearning in white-box-accessible DNN classifiers (vision). Should NOT be over-generalized to guarantee legal/technical deletion, to black-box/hosted models, or to generative/LLM unlearning without further validation. Agent-security relevance: **adjacent** — machine unlearning and evaluation-integrity are transferable to AI-system data-deletion, safety-concept removal, and audit, but the study itself is vision classification, not agent/tool/prompt security.
