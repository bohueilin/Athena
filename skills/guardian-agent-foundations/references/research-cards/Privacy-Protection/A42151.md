## Research question
Can generative-AI synthetic imagery be harnessed for its "positive face" (privacy-preserving, fair biometric training data) while building robust, generalizable, efficient detectors against its "negative face" (deepfakes that survive online-social-network transformations and unseen generative models)?

## Problem definition
Two coupled problems: (1) collecting real facial data for biometrics is privacy/ethically fraught, and GAN-synthesized data inherits/amplifies demographic bias; (2) deepfake detectors are brittle — vulnerable to OSN transformations (compression, resizing), poor generalization to novel generators, and computationally heavy. The dissertation seeks synthetic data as a privacy substitute plus detectors "robust, generalizable, and efficient by construction."

## System or model being studied
A body of the author's own methods (this abstract summarizes prior/under-review work rather than presenting one new system): DiffSynFace (a >40,000-face demographically diverse dataset from seven diffusion models); biometric classifiers (age/gender/ethnicity) trained on synthetic data; and lightweight deepfake detectors — RCD-Net (Relative Chrominance Distance), gradient-feature detector, STN-Net, LPQ-Net, DepthFakeNet, and a Multi-Level Feature Fusion (MLFF) framework; plus a threshold-based upsampling-artifact feature extractor (current work).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: create deepfakes (synthetic faces/media) for misinformation on OSNs that evade detection.
- Adversary knowledge (white/gray/black-box): detector treats generators as largely unknown/unseen (black-box to detector); generalization to 38 unseen models is tested.
- Adversary access: publishes manipulated media through OSN platforms (applies platform-specific compression/transforms).
- Adversary capabilities: use of diverse/novel generative models (GANs, diffusion); post-generation transformations including heavy JPEG compression.
- Attacker budget: not stated in paper.
- Phase (training / inference / deployment): inference/deployment (detection of content in the wild).
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted (broad misinformation), not identity-targeted here.
- Digital / physical: digital (image/video), though a privacy-by-design biometric angle touches sensor/biometric data.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive evaluated (fixed transformations, unseen-generator generalization); adversary that adapts to the detector is not evaluated.

## Trust assumptions
For the biometrics thrust: synthetic data trusted as a privacy-preserving substitute for real faces (privacy-by-design). For the detection thrust: detectors must not trust that images are unmanipulated by OSN pipelines; robustness to real-world degradations is the goal.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Deepfake generators optimize photorealism in the pixel/luminance domain but leave detectable artifacts in other domains — color-channel relationships (chrominance), image gradients, high-frequency/phase content, depth/3D geometry, and upsampling-layer artifacts. OSN transformations (especially JPEG compression) destroy fragile pixel cues, breaking naive detectors — the failure mode this work targets.

## Proposed defense or method
Feature-engineering detectors that exploit robust, transform-resilient cues rather than raw RGB: RCD/chrominance features, gradient magnitude/direction, spatial/high-frequency artifact amplification (STN-Net), blur-invariant phase (LPQ-Net), depth/3D geometric inconsistencies (DepthFakeNet), efficient multi-scale fusion (MLFF), and a threshold-based upsampling-artifact fingerprint aimed at universal generalization. Privacy thrust: use diffusion-model synthetic faces (DiffSynFace) to train fair/private biometric classifiers.

## Datasets and benchmarks
DiffSynFace (>40k diffusion-generated faces, seven models; OSN-processed variant also created); ProGAN training set (4 classes) with testing on 38 unseen models (extreme generalization test); FF++ (deepfake video benchmark); images downloaded from Facebook/Instagram (OSN realism). Specific train/test splits per result are not fully detailed in this summary.

## Evaluation methodology
Summary of multiple prior/under-review studies. Notable protocol: train on limited ProGAN (4 classes), test on 38 unseen generators (cross-generator generalization). Cross-dataset biometric generalization (synthetic-trained vs real-trained). Numbers are reported as headline accuracies/AUC without full experimental detail in this abstract.

## Metrics
Author-reported (from cited prior works, not re-derived here): RCD-Net 98.86% accuracy on synthetic-face detection with <1% of competitor parameters; gradient-feature detector 96.54% average accuracy under common post-processing; LPQ-Net >96% accuracy on OSN-downloaded (Facebook/Instagram) images; MLFF 98.99% AUC on FF++ with >50% parameter reduction; upsampling-artifact feature boosts standard detectors (ResNet-50, ViT) by up to 42% over raw RGB in the 38-unseen-model test.

## Main findings
Author claims: (1) diffusion-synthetic data can match or (in cross-dataset scenarios) exceed real-data-trained biometric classifiers, supporting a privacy-by-design substitution; (2) non-pixel feature domains yield deepfake detectors that are more robust to OSN transforms and generalize better to unseen generators while being far more parameter-efficient.

## Negative results
Author notes the upsampling-artifact method has reduced performance on heavily JPEG-compressed images (motivating future JPEG-artifact modelling). GAN-synthetic data has historically inherited/amplified demographic bias (motivation for the diffusion approach).

## Limitations stated by the authors
Reduced performance under heavy JPEG compression; generalization + OSN-manipulation resistance not yet jointly solved (explicit future goal); several component works are "under review." Detector robustness to an adversary that adapts to the detector is not claimed.

## Design implications
For AI-system integrity/provenance: robust synthetic-media detection benefits from cues invariant to post-distribution transformations (chrominance, gradient, phase, depth, upsampling artifacts) rather than fragile pixel statistics — relevant to content-provenance and deepfake-forgery defense layers.

## Implementation implications
Lightweight feature-based detectors can approach heavy models at a fraction of parameters, enabling deployment at ingestion/edge points. Privacy-by-design training data (diffusion synthetic faces) can reduce reliance on collecting real biometric data.

## Evaluation implications
Advocates cross-generator (train-on-few, test-on-many-unseen) and OSN-transform stress tests as the realistic evaluation regime for deepfake detectors — a useful bar for provenance/forgery defenses generally.

## Deployment implications
Detectors must survive OSN compression pipelines to be usable in the wild; JPEG robustness is the current deployment gap. Synthetic biometric data offers a compliance path where real-face collection is restricted.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Relevant to content-authenticity monitoring on platforms; detectors flag synthetic media but the paper does not address adversarial evasion or continuous drift as new generators appear (beyond aspiration).
