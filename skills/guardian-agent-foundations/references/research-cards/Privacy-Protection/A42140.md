## Research question
Can privacy, explainability, and utility co-exist in a human-centric Explainable AI (XAI) system despite their conflicting relationships? Concretely: (1) what defenses mitigate privacy attacks in XAI, (2) how to build interpretable algorithms for private models, and (3) what are end-user requirements for privacy-preserving XAI.

## Problem definition
Explainability is a Trustworthy-AI desideratum, but exposing explanations of black-box models can leak private information — enabling identification of individuals or exposure of sensitive attributes, training data, query inputs, or model IP. Existing privacy-preservation responses degrade model accuracy and explanation quality, and current XAI is model-centric rather than end-user-centric. The thesis aims to advance Privacy Enhancing Technologies (PETs) in XAI while keeping users central and utility acceptable.

## Trust assumptions
Explanation consumers may be adversarial or benign-but-leaky; the model owner wants to protect training data, user query data, and model IP while still providing useful, interpretable explanations. Assumes PETs can be integrated into XAI pipelines with tunable trade-offs.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Explanations add information beyond model predictions, expanding the attack surface: prior work (cited, e.g., Shokri, Strobel, Zick 2021) shows model explanations can reveal membership/sensitive attributes. This proposal treats such explanation-driven leakage as the failure mode to defend against; it does not itself introduce a new attack.

## Proposed defense or method
Proposed (largely future/in-progress) program: (1) empirically identify countermeasures/PETs for an existing XAI-aware privacy attack that exposes user-sensitive info through explanations; (2) add an interpretability layer to an existing private ensemble algorithm with tuning parameters balancing privacy/explainability/utility (in progress with an industry partner); (3) a user study eliciting requirements for private, accurate, usable explanations. Intended contributions: privacy-measurement baselines per XAI category, characteristics of privacy-preserved explanations, PET-integration recommendations, and guidelines. Prior differential-privacy XAI work (Harder, Bauer, Park 2020) is cited as related.

## Design implications
Reinforces that explanation surfaces are a privacy attack surface and should be treated as part of the system's data-exposure boundary, not a neutral add-on. Argues for user-centric requirements gathering when choosing PET/XAI trade-offs.

## Implementation implications
Suggests making privacy/explainability/utility trade-offs explicit and tunable at the interpretability layer, and pairing specific PETs (e.g., differential privacy) with specific XAI method categories — a knowledge-base/mapping rather than one-size-fits-all.

## Evaluation implications
Calls for standardized privacy-assessment baselines across XAI categories — directly relevant to anyone auditing explanation interfaces for leakage. No such baseline is delivered here.

## Deployment implications
Explanation-bearing interfaces deployed in high-risk domains (healthcare, finance) should be assessed for explanation-driven leakage before release; utility degradation from privatisation must be measured against user task needs.
