## Research question
What actually changes between an original model and a gold-standard retrained-from-scratch model on the forget set, and can those differences be exploited to make approximate unlearning both more effective and less destructive (less catastrophic forgetting)?

## Problem definition
Machine unlearning removes the influence of a forget set Df ⊂ Dtrain to honor the "right to be forgotten." The oracle is a model retrained only on the retain set Dr, but retraining is prohibitively expensive. Approximate unlearning transforms the original model Mo into Mu without full retraining, but tends to over-delete and cause catastrophic forgetting of retained knowledge. The paper studies which forget-set samples the oracle actually "forgets."

## System or model being studied
PeriUn — a selective-unlearning wrapper. It analyzes original (Mo) vs retrained (Mr) models and finds that prediction/confidence changes concentrate in low-confidence "peripheral" samples near decision boundaries. PeriUn therefore selects only the least-confident α-quantile of Df (confidence = max softmax under Mo) as the new forget set fed to an existing unlearning algorithm (e.g., Saliency Unlearn / Random Label / GA / GAGD), leaving confident samples untouched to mimic the oracle and avoid over-deletion.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: membership inference — determine whether a sample was in the training set (used here as the *privacy evaluation* adversary, via MIA gap to the retrained model).
- Adversary knowledge: confidence-based / output-probability / entropy-based MIA (as evaluated); the paper uses MIA as a metric, not a red-team.
- Adversary access: model outputs (confidence/probability/entropy).
- Adversary capabilities: standard MIA scoring.
- Attacker budget: not stated (MIA is an evaluation instrument, not a costed attack).
- Phase: post-training (unlearning is applied after training; privacy risk is at inference/deployment of the unlearned model).
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted (membership over a random forget set of size 3000).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive — fixed MIA metrics; no adaptive attacker that knows PeriUn is applied.

## Trust assumptions
- The unlearning service is trusted and honestly given Mo, Df, and Dr.
- The retrained-from-scratch model Mr is the correct oracle definition of privacy behavior.
- Data splits (Dr, Df, Dt) are drawn i.i.d. from one distribution.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Two failure modes: (1) approximate unlearning over-deletes — it removes influence of confident forget samples that the oracle would still predict correctly, causing catastrophic forgetting; (2) residual membership signal — an unlearned model whose confidence behavior on Df differs from the oracle leaks membership (measured by MIA gap). Root cause identified: due to Neural Collapse / overconfidence, high-confidence forget samples align with class weights and barely change under retraining, whereas low-confidence peripheral samples change from correct→incorrect with reduced confidence.

## Proposed defense or method
Selective forgetting: compute per-sample confidence under Mo, take the α-quantile threshold τ, keep only samples with confidence ≤ τ as Dfnew, and run a standard unlearning method (RL or SU integrate best) on Dfnew. This better approximates Mr's predictive and privacy behavior while preserving retained knowledge (Algorithm 1).

## Datasets and benchmarks
- CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), CIFAR-100 (ResNet-50), TinyImageNet (ResNet-18 and VGG16-bn) — four dataset/architecture setups.
- Random forget set size 3000; three fixed random inits × three fixed forget sets for significance. Hardware: RTX 3090, Xeon 5218, 128 GB RAM.

## Evaluation methodology
Compare Mu against the oracle Mr using Tug-of-War (ToW) and MIA gap; baselines include FT, GA, RL, GAGD, FT+L1, Saliency Unlearn (SU). Analyses: seen-vs-unseen confidence, confidence-difference and correctness-change on Df, T-SNE of penultimate features, Jensen-Shannon distance between one-hot labels and softmax outputs. Ablations on selection ratio α and compatibility across baselines.

## Metrics
- Tug-of-War (ToW) score (0–100): product of (1−|accuracy gaps|) across forget/retain/test splits; higher = closer to oracle.
- MIA gap: difference in MIA success vs the retrained model (confidence-based primary; also output-probability and entropy variants).
- Reported: on TinyImageNet+VGG16-bn, SU+PeriUn improves ToW by 22.04 points over the best baseline; MIA gap improves by 8.7 points; on Setup 2 (CIFAR-100/ResNet-50) RL+PeriUn gains 36.32 ToW points (author-reported).

## Main findings
- Prediction/confidence changes at retraining concentrate in low-confidence peripheral samples; high-confidence forget samples are largely unchanged by the oracle.
- Selectively unlearning only peripheral samples better mimics the oracle in both utility (ToW) and privacy (MIA gap) and reduces catastrophic forgetting.
- SU alone has strong ToW but is "vulnerable in privacy" (large MIA gap); adding PeriUn improves privacy alignment.
- PeriUn is a plug-in that improves RL, GA, GAGD, and SU.

## Negative results
- Gains are method-dependent: PeriUn helps most on RL and SU (which reassign incorrect labels); improvements on GA/GAGD are present but smaller/less uniform.
- PeriUn ranks best on MIA gap in only 2 of 4 setups (second-best in the others).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Focus is random-instance removal in image classification; class-removal and other modalities not the target.
- Effectiveness tied to confidence-based selection and the interpolation/overparameterization regime where Df reaches near-perfect train accuracy.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Privacy is measured by MIA gap to the oracle, not against a strong adaptive attacker; an attacker aware that PeriUn preserves confident forget samples might exploit exactly those retained samples for membership inference. This is not tested.
- No formal (DP-style) unlearning guarantee — it is an empirical approximation of the retrain oracle, with no certificate that a specific record's influence is provably removed.
- Confidence as the "peripherality" proxy may be miscalibrated on OOD/adversarial inputs, and near-duplicate or mislabeled forget samples could evade selection.
- Only image classifiers/CNNs tested; transfer to LLMs / generative models (where "right to be forgotten" is most contentious) is unexamined.

## Design implications
Unlearning pipelines should treat the forget set as heterogeneous: only boundary/low-confidence ("memorized") samples drive oracle behavior, so a confidence-gated selector is a cheap, model-agnostic layer that improves both compliance fidelity and utility retention.

## Implementation implications
Add a pre-filter (α-quantile of Mo confidence) before any existing unlearning routine. Choose α via the utility/privacy trade-off; validate against both ToW and multiple MIA variants, not one.

## Evaluation implications
Report privacy alignment as an MIA *gap to the retrain oracle* (over/under-unlearning both matter), and evaluate multiple MIA scoring functions — a method strong on ToW can be weak on privacy (as SU is here).

## Deployment implications
Relevant to data-deletion / right-to-be-forgotten compliance services. The method reduces catastrophic forgetting cost of honoring deletion requests, but provides empirical (not certified) unlearning — regulators expecting provable removal are not satisfied by MIA-gap evidence.

## Monitoring and incident implications
For an unlearning-as-a-service system, monitor post-unlearning MIA gap and retain-set accuracy as guardrails; a widening MIA gap signals under- or over-unlearning. Ties to audit_records / evidence_logging for deletion-request handling.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to approximate machine unlearning for image classifiers and to the general insight that memorized boundary samples dominate the retrain oracle's behavior. Should NOT be read as a formal privacy guarantee, nor extrapolated to generative/LLM unlearning without new evidence. Adjacent to agent security: user-data deletion, membership-inference privacy, and audit/compliance for AI systems.
