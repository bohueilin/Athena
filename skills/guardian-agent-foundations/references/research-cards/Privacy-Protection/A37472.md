## Research question
Can high-fidelity, temporally coherent video be reconstructed zero-shot from EEG signals — overcoming EEG's low spatial resolution and the flat brain-visual alignment of prior methods — by using Implicit Neural Representations (INR) for continuous spatial modeling plus hierarchical (visual/semantic/motion) decoding?

## Problem definition
Dynamic visual decoding from brain signals is hard: fMRI has good spatial but poor temporal resolution (≥2 s/frame); EEG has millisecond temporal resolution but only 32/64 electrodes (sparse spatial coverage) and prior methods align EEG directly to flat CLIP features, ignoring the hierarchical structure of visual perception. Two named challenges: insufficient spatial representation and missing multi-granularity brain–visual alignment.

## System or model being studied
EVOKE, an EEG-to-video reconstruction framework: (1) a unified EEG-INR perceptual space (sine-activated MLP INR maps each EEG point's 3D spatial coords + time index to spatio-temporal features, exploiting INR smoothness for denoising sparse EEG); (2) a Hierarchical-aware Attention Module (HAM) decoding three anchors — visual (CLIP-ViT), semantic (BLIP-2 captions), motion (VAE priors) — with cross-modal contrastive alignment (BDC, HSC losses); (3) Motion Attention Flow (MAF) building a patch-displacement motion signal; (4) a video-synthesis pipeline conditioned on the decoupled features.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes access to paired EEG–video recordings (SEED-DV) collected with subject consent under controlled stimulation; assumes electrode signals, after ICA artifact removal and bandpass filtering, carry decodable visual information. No assumptions about adversaries, data governance, or misuse are stated.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Not an attack paper. Failure modes addressed are representational: GNN-based EEG models struggle with sparse/misaligned electrodes and blow up computationally in dynamic modeling; flat CLIP alignment misses hierarchical visual structure. EVOKE's INR + hierarchical decoding targets these.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a defense. A reconstruction method (see System). Key ideas: INR gives a continuous, Lipschitz-smooth spatial embedding that denoises sparse EEG; hierarchical cross-attention integrates motion→visual→semantic streams; MAF extracts motion via argmax patch assignment and displacement aggregation; contrastive losses align decoupled EEG features to video/caption embeddings.

## Datasets and benchmarks
SEED-DV: EEG–video from 20 subjects, 7 blocks each, 1,400 video clips (40 visual categories), 64-channel EEG at 1,000 Hz, video at 4 fps 1920×1080; preprocessing = bandpass, downsample, segmentation, ICA. Split: first 6 sessions train, last session test. Follows the EEG-VP benchmark; semantic accuracy via a VideoMAE-based classifier.

## Evaluation methodology
Zero-shot decoding evaluated with classification-accuracy proxies (40-class top-1/top-5, 9-class top-1/top-3 for color/fast-slow/human-face/human) against EEG baselines (MLP-PSD/DE, GLMNet, Conformer, BraVL, EEGPT, TSConv, EEG2Video), with two-sample t-tests (p<0.05) marking above-chance results; plus reconstruction-fidelity metrics.

## Metrics
Reconstruction fidelity: 0.353 SSIM, 0.715 CLIP-pcc (reported SOTA). Classification accuracies with per-metric relative improvements over the best baseline (e.g., +123.3%, +38.2%, +32.0%, etc. across the reported columns), all flagged above chance with statistical significance.

## Main findings
Authors report SOTA on SEED-DV for EEG-to-video reconstruction (0.353 SSIM, 0.715 CLIP-pcc) and consistent, statistically significant gains over eight EEG baselines across classification metrics, attributing gains to the INR perceptual space (denoising/smoothness) and hierarchical + motion decoding.
