## Research question
How can a party's contribution be efficiently unlearned in **Vertical** Federated Learning (VFL), where data are
partitioned by *features* (not samples), given that horizontal-FL unlearning methods do not transfer to the VFL
architecture?

## Problem definition
Under GDPR/CCPA RTBF, a feature-holding party in splitVFL must be forgotten. Two VFL-specific challenges: (C1) data
is vertically partitioned — only a departing party's *feature slice* must be removed, and its influence is more
concealed in the model than a full labeled sample; (C2) model partitioning + collaborative inference — a departing
party's features affect both its bottom encoder and the active party's top model, so unlearning must revise both and
keep the inference pipeline working. Retraining from scratch is prohibitively costly.

## System or model being studied
**REMISVFU**, a plug-and-play unlearning module for splitVFL (bottom encoders at passive/feature parties, top
aggregator at the active/label party). On an erasure request from forgetting party P_f, its bottom encoder's
intermediate features h_{i,f} are collapsed toward a **constant random anchor** c·u, u ~ Uniform(unit sphere S^{d_h−1}),
u drawn once and fixed. A retention loss (task-native CE/MSE on retained parties) preserves utility; a gradient-
projection strategy orthogonalizes the retention gradient to the forgetting gradient when they conflict (cosine < 0),
removing destructive interference. Communication pattern is unchanged (passive parties P_{k≠f} unaffected).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: (defense goal) make the unlearned model's predictive distribution indistinguishable from a
  from-scratch retrained model (KL-quantified), so no membership/attribute info about D_f is recoverable from P_f's
  representation; also suppress backdoors planted via the forgetting party's features.
- Adversary knowledge / access: evaluated against backdoor attacks (attack success measured); the party being
  forgotten is a participant.
- Adversary capabilities: backdoor implantation through a party's feature contribution.
- Phase: post-training unlearning (deployment).
- Targeted / untargeted: client/party-level (targeted) forgetting.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive backdoor evaluation.

## Trust assumptions
Active party (label holder / top model) coordinates gradient harmonization honestly. Labels are deliberately excluded
from erasure (removing them would destroy the supervisory signal). Gold standard = full splitVFL retrain on D \ D_f
(Eq. 8), used only as an evaluation reference.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Feature-slice influence is "concealed" and hard to remove; naive joint optimization of forgetting + retention losses
causes gradient conflict — strengthening retained knowledge can inadvertently preserve forget-set information, and
aggressive erasure can trigger catastrophic forgetting of transferable knowledge. Backdoors introduced via a party's
features persist unless that party's representation is neutralized.

## Proposed defense or method
Representation-misdirection forgetting loss L_f = E‖h_{i,f} − c·u‖² collapses the forget party's encoder output to a
fixed random anchor (author claim: guarantees no membership/attribute info recoverable from the new representation);
retention loss L_r preserves service quality; gradient projection removes the conflicting component (if cos(g_r,g_f) <
0, project g_r orthogonal to g_f) for stable joint optimization. Fully compatible with existing splitVFL protocols.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Public benchmarks referenced include MNIST (LeCun), Fashion-MNIST (Xiao et al.), SVHN (Netzer et al.); a small CNN
bottom encoder (two 3×3 conv layers, 32/64 channels). Baselines are VFU / FU methods (retrain gold standard; prior
VFU works cited: Deng et al. logistic-regression VFU, Li et al. gradient-boosted-tree VFU, Wang et al. fast-retrain
VFU, Varshney et al. distillation VFU). (Exact per-dataset tables are truncated in the extracted text.)

## Evaluation methodology
Compare forgetting vs retention against baselines and the retrain gold standard; measure backdoor attack success
after unlearning and clean-accuracy retention; KL divergence between unlearned and retrained predictive
distributions as the unlearning-quality criterion.

## Metrics
Backdoor attack success rate, clean accuracy (drop), KL divergence to retrained model. Author headline: backdoor
success suppressed to the **natural class-prior level** with only ~**2.5 percentage points** clean-accuracy loss.

## Main findings
- REMISVFU suppresses backdoor attack success to the class-prior (chance) level while sacrificing ~2.5% clean
  accuracy, outperforming SOTA VFU baselines (author claims).
- Representation misdirection + orthogonal gradient projection enables fast, client-level VFL unlearning without
  changing communication patterns or party workflows (plug-and-play).

## Negative results
Naive summed-gradient joint optimization (g_f + α g_r) can cancel opposing components, making forgetting sluggish and
convergence unstable — motivating the projection step (shown conceptually, Fig 2a vs 2b).

## Limitations stated by the authors
Label data is explicitly excluded from erasure (a scope restriction). The method targets splitVFL specifically. Much
of the experimental detail is truncated/deferred.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- The claim that collapsing to a fixed anchor "guarantees that no membership or attribute information can be
  recovered" is an **author assertion**, supported by backdoor-suppression and KL metrics, not by a dedicated
  membership/attribute-inference attack against the misdirected representation — treat as "reduced recoverability
  under the tested conditions," not a proof.
- A single fixed random anchor for all forget-party samples may be trivially detectable/reversible by a party that
  observes the intermediate features; robustness to an adaptive server/party that knows the anchoring scheme is not
  evaluated.
- Backdoor evaluation appears non-adaptive and on small image datasets; no adaptive backdoor placed to survive
  representation misdirection.
- Only the forgetting-party-is-passive case is emphasized (1 ≤ f < K); forgetting the active/label party is out of
  scope.

## Design implications
Extends the unlearning problem to feature-partitioned (vertical) collaborations, where the unit of deletion is a
party's *intermediate representation*, not a labeled record — relevant to multi-org agent/data collaborations that
share embeddings rather than raw data. Manipulating internal representations (rather than only outputs) is presented
as a lever for both forgetting and backdoor neutralization.

## Implementation implications
Anchor-collapse forgetting loss + orthogonal-projection gradient coordination is a lightweight, protocol-preserving
recipe; it reuses the existing VFL communication pattern, easing integration.

## Evaluation implications
Backdoor-success-to-prior and KL-to-retrained are useful unlearning-quality signals. Reviewer note: the privacy claim
needs an explicit inference-attack on the misdirected features, and an adaptive adversary aware of the anchoring.

## Deployment implications
Applicable to splitVFL deployments (e.g., cross-industry feature collaborations like bank + e-commerce credit
scoring) needing fast per-party erasure without protocol changes; assumes an honest active party coordinating
gradients.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Doubles as a backdoor-mitigation lever: unlearning a compromised feature party can drop backdoor success to chance.
No runtime detector is provided; anchoring is an offline remediation.

## Applicability boundaries
Vertical FL / splitVFL, passive-party (feature) forgetting, image classification benchmarks. Do NOT generalize to
horizontal FL (see A39895), to label-party erasure, or to a formal unrecoverability guarantee absent an
inference-attack evaluation.
