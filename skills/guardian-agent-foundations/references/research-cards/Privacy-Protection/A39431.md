## Research question
Can targeted machine unlearning be performed on vision-language models (VLMs) such as CLIP **without access to any retained-data set**, given that curating multimodal retain data at scale is costly and privacy/compliance-constrained, and that VLMs' entangled multimodal representations make selective forgetting hard?

## Problem definition
Standard MU forgets a target set and then finetunes on a retain set to avoid catastrophic forgetting; dependence on retain data limits scalability (privacy constraints, compliance risk, compute). Existing "retain-data-free" methods are mostly unimodal. For VLMs, concept removal amounts to breaking modality alignment for the forgotten concept while preserving alignment for retained concepts — hard without retain data (see the paper's Fig. 1 showing retained-class alignment drops when retain data is omitted).

## System or model being studied
DIET, a retain-data-free unlearning framework for VLMs (demonstrated on CLIP; claimed model-agnostic). It formulates forgetting in the Poincaré ball model of hyperbolic space: image embeddings are exponentially mapped into hyperbolic space and pushed toward the boundary (points near the boundary become infinitely distant from interior points, so their influence becomes asymptotically negligible). Uses the Busemann function δ_p(z) = log(‖p−z‖²/(1−‖z‖²)) as a finite differentiable distance-to-boundary; text-encoder-derived class-mismatched prototypes serve as ideal boundary targets; optimal transport (Sinkhorn) adaptively assigns each forget embedding to a semantically distant prototype. Loss L_U = λ_HYP·L_HYP + λ_OT·L_OT + λ_REP·L_REP. Only LoRA weights on the vision encoder's q/k/v projections are updated.

## Threat model
Framed as a privacy/safety *governance* capability (concept/data removal), not an attacker-vs-defender contest.
- "Adversary"/hazard: persistence of sensitive, biased, NSFW, or spurious concepts absorbed from publicly-scraped pretraining data; and the "right to be forgotten" requirement to remove specific concept influence.
- Objective: break modality alignment for forget concepts (sim(f^u_img(x_f), f_text(y_f)) ≪ sim(f^u_img(x_f), f_text(p))) while preserving alignment for retained concepts.
- Knowledge/access: operates white-box on a pretrained VLM; uses only the forget set D_f and text prompts of retained classes (no retained *images/data*).
- Capabilities/budget: lightweight — LoRA finetuning, as few as 16 samples per concept.
- Phase: post-training / deployment (unlearning applied to a pretrained model).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted concept forgetting.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: no adaptive adversary (e.g., relearning attack or MIA) evaluated in the extracted text.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes white-box access to a pretrained VLM (image + text encoders) and the ability to insert/train LoRA adapters. Assumes text prompts ("a photo of a c") for retained classes can stand in for retain data as boundary prototypes. Assumes forget-set membership is known. The forgetting quality is argued via a geometric *hypothesis* (pushing embeddings toward infinity nullifies influence), supported empirically.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Not an attack. The mechanism DIET counteracts is catastrophic collateral damage during retain-free unlearning: naively pushing forget embeddings in an arbitrary "away" direction damages retained knowledge. DIET instead transports forget embeddings along geodesics toward class-mismatched boundary prototypes (chosen by OT), inducing semantic confusion plus asymptotic distance, while a repulsive/entailment-cone constraint limits cross-class interference.

## Proposed defense or method
DIET (hyperbolic Busemann-guided, OT-assigned, LoRA-based retain-data-free VLM unlearning), Algorithm 1. Contributions: (1) hyperbolic Poincaré formulation with Busemann loss to push forget embeddings to the boundary; (2) empirical evidence that this disrupts modality alignment for forgotten concepts while preserving it for unrelated ones; (3) adaptive OT prototype assignment (avoids manually fixed targets); (4) strong results on fine-grained datasets vs. retain-free baselines and near-parity with retain-data baselines.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Fine-grained datasets: Flowers102, OxfordPets, StanfordCars (others such as Food-101 appear in references/setup). Model: CLIP (SigLIP referenced). Baselines: retain-free methods (JiT, SCAR, Learning-to-Unlearn/Cha et al., Kravets & Namboodiri) and retain-data methods (CLIPErase, SafeCLIP, HySAC).

## Evaluation methodology
Compare forget accuracy and retained utility against retain-free and retain-data baselines on fine-grained classification; ablations of the OT plan and repulsive regularizer and of text-based prototypes; low-sample regime (16 samples per concept). ## Metrics
Forget accuracy (lower = better forgetting; DIET average 8.06%); model utility / retained accuracy (69.04% using only 16 samples per concept); relative improvement over best retain-free baseline (+117.5% in model utility); gap to retain-data baselines (−3.79% utility drop).

## Main findings
Author claims, demonstrated under evaluated conditions: DIET achieves strong targeted forgetting (8.06% average forget accuracy) while preserving utility (69.04%) with only 16 samples per concept and **no retain data**, outperforming the best retain-free baselines by 117.5% in utility and coming within 3.79% of retain-data baselines. The hyperbolic-boundary hypothesis is empirically supported (alignment disrupted for forgotten concepts, preserved for others).

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Forgetting is evidenced primarily by forget accuracy + geometric rationale; the extracted text shows no membership-inference or *relearning* attack to confirm information (not just accuracy) is removed. Cf. A39373 (IDI), which shows low output-level forget accuracy can coexist with recoverable residual information — DIET's strong-unlearning claim is thus not adversarially verified here.
- Evaluated on fine-grained image classification with CLIP; "model-agnostic"/generative-VLM and LLM applicability is asserted but not shown.
- Relies on text-prototype quality and the choice of mismatched classes; degenerate prototype sets could reduce effectiveness.
- Hyperbolic mapping + OT adds hyperparameters (λ_HYP/λ_OT/λ_REP, curvature, margin) whose sensitivity is only partly shown.

## Design implications
Retain-data-free unlearning is feasible for multimodal models by exploiting representation geometry: you can remove a concept using only the forget set plus text-prototype anchors, avoiding costly/privacy-sensitive retain-data curation. Useful pattern for safety concept removal (NSFW/bias) and deletion compliance in VLM-backed systems.

## Implementation implications
Concept removal can be applied surgically via LoRA on attention projections rather than full finetuning, lowering compute and blast radius. Use OT to pick forgetting targets instead of manual mismatched labels. Pair with a residual-information audit (not just forget accuracy) before trusting deletion.

## Evaluation implications
Because forget accuracy alone can understate residual retention (see A39373), unlearning methods like DIET should be evaluated with adversarial recovery / MIA / relearning probes, not only accuracy and utility metrics.

## Deployment implications
Enables scalable, privacy-preserving concept/data removal for deployed CLIP-style encoders (retrieval, generation front-ends, multimodal assistants) without shipping or storing retain datasets — reducing compliance exposure. Verification of deletion strength should be part of the release gate.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Relevant to data-deletion and harmful-content-removal workflows: DIET is a remediation/rollback-style control (removing a concept's influence post-training). Log which concepts were unlearned, the sample budget used, and post-unlearning verification evidence for audit; monitor for concept relearning after subsequent finetuning.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to retain-data-free concept unlearning in VLM image encoders (CLIP) on fine-grained classification. Should NOT be assumed to guarantee irrecoverable deletion (no adversarial verification here) nor automatically transfer to generative diffusion models / LLMs without evaluation. Agent-security relevance: **adjacent** — multimodal unlearning supports safety-concept removal and privacy/right-to-be-forgotten in AI systems (safety_policies, training_data governance), but the study is vision-language classification, not agent/tool/prompt security.

Moderate — concrete, competitive results (forget acc 8.06%, utility 69.04% at 16 samples/concept, +117.5% over retain-free baselines, −3.79% vs retain-data) across three fine-grained datasets with released code and a principled geometric method; but forgetting strength is not adversarially verified (no MIA/relearning attack in the extracted text), evaluation is limited to CLIP image classification, and the "asymptotic negligibility" argument is a hypothesis supported empirically rather than a guarantee.
