## Research question
Can a cloud–edge LLM inference pipeline preserve prompt privacy while keeping utility, latency, and energy low by adapting the protection strategy to the semantic sensitivity of each prompt, rather than applying uniform perturbation to all inputs?

## Problem definition
Cloud-centric LLM serving requires transmitting full user prompts over the network, exposing sensitive content (e.g., PHI, financial, personal intent) to interception/mishandling. Existing cloud–edge schemes apply uniform privacy protection (binary routing or prompt-level LDP) without considering input sensitivity, causing unnecessary utility degradation on benign tokens and evasive cloud responses when noise is applied indiscriminately.

## System or model being studied
PRISM: a four-stage edge-orchestrated framework. (1) Sensitivity Profiling on edge (NER via Presidio-style analyzer, per-category sensitivity weights, first-person/PERSON-entity contextual cues, scalar risk score R(P) and per-entity protection mask). (2) Entropy-regularized soft gating: a logit-based classifier maps features to a distribution over three execution modes (cloud-only, cloud–edge collaboration, edge-only), taking top-1 at inference. (3) Adaptive two-layer local differential privacy (category-layer ε1 and value-layer ε2, budget split by sensitivity weight, randomized-response over finite domains). (4) Semantic sketch collaboration: cloud LLM produces an abstract sketch S from the perturbed prompt P*; edge SLM refines S using the locally retained original prompt to produce the final response.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: recover sensitive personal information (PHI/PII, intent, habits) from prompts transmitted to the cloud; re-identify users via linkage across records.
- Adversary knowledge: not formally specified; framing assumes an honest-but-curious cloud / network eavesdropper that observes transmitted prompts (author framing, not a formal adversary definition).
- Adversary access: sees the perturbed prompt P* transmitted in plaintext to the cloud and the generated sketch; does not access the edge device or the original prompt (retained locally).
- Adversary capabilities: interception of transmitted content; cross-record correlation / linkage attacks (motivating example of masked-name re-identification via shared "black dog" content).
- Attacker budget: not stated in paper.
- Phase: inference / deployment.
- Targeted / untargeted: not stated (privacy leakage is untargeted disclosure).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive; no empirical adversary is run — privacy is argued via ε-LDP guarantees, not via an executed attack.

## Trust assumptions
Edge device (hosting the SLM) is trusted and retains the raw prompt locally; the cloud LLM is untrusted for confidentiality but trusted for availability/utility. Sensitivity profiling (NER + category weights) is assumed accurate enough to route correctly. ε-LDP composition (ε_total = ε1 + ε2) is assumed to hold under the two-layer randomized-response construction.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Full-prompt transmission leaks rich semantic context. Naive masking (⟨NAME⟩) fails against linkage attacks because shared non-identifier semantics allow re-identification. Uniform ε-LDP either under-protects sensitive entities or over-perturbs benign ones, and indiscriminate noise yields semantically distorted prompts that trigger vague/evasive cloud completions.

## Proposed defense or method
Context-aware routing plus entity-level adaptive two-layer LDP: high-sensitivity categories (NAME, ID, DIAGNOSIS) get more category-level budget (ε1) to hide the entity type; low-sensitivity categories (LOCATION, NATIONALITY) get more value-level budget (ε2) to preserve utility. Only moderately sensitive prompts take the collaborative path; high-risk prompts stay fully local; low-risk prompts go directly to cloud. Perturbed prompt transmitted in plaintext (avoids embedding transmission / shared tokenizers). Cloud generates a structured sketch omitting obfuscated entities; edge SLM reconstructs the response conditioned on original local context.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Authors construct a synthetic, context-rich instruction dataset spanning four domains: medical, tourism, banking, general knowledge. Evaluated across eight cloud/edge model combinations (cloud LLMs incl. GPT-4o and Qwen3-235B; edge SLMs incl. Qwen1.5-1.8B, StableLM). No standardized public privacy benchmark used.

## Evaluation methodology
Real-world cloud–edge platform with actual energy measurements; sweep over privacy budgets; comparison to Cloud-only, Uniform LDP, and Selective LDP baselines. Inference quality (IQ) appears to be an LLM-as-judge-style score (paper cites MT-Bench/Chatbot-Arena judging), though the exact judge is not fully specified in extracted text.

## Metrics
Latency (s), energy consumption (J), inference quality (IQ, scalar), privacy budget ε. No attack-success-rate metric (no empirical inference attack executed).

## Main findings
- Cloud-only is fastest/cheapest (5.13 s, 296 J, IQ 8.14) but offers no privacy protection (author claim).
- PRISM ranks second across methods at ~1.54× latency and ~2.32× energy of Cloud-only.
- Uniform / Selective LDP incur >20 s latency and 1700+ J energy due to indiscriminate/entity-based noise.
- Across budgets, PRISM reports lower energy (e.g., 652 J vs 1800+ J), shorter completion (e.g., 8.8 s vs 21+ s), and higher IQ (up to 7.2 vs ≤6.2) than Uniform/Selective LDP.
- Across 8 model pairings: IQ ≥ 6.9, latency 7.08–8.60 s, energy 632–739 J; best speed/energy from GPT-4o + Qwen1.5-1.8B; highest quality GPT-4o + StableLM (7.16) / Qwen3-235B variants (up to 7.22).

## Negative results
PRISM does not match Cloud-only on raw latency/energy (that baseline has no privacy). No negative privacy result is reported because no empirical privacy attack is run.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Future work only: extend to multi-edge collaborative inference (routing, load balancing, cloud-edge-device coordination via decentralized scheduling / federated optimization). No explicit limitation section otherwise.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Privacy is argued via ε-LDP composition, not demonstrated against any executed inference/linkage/membership attack — the central threat (re-identification) is motivated by example but never empirically tested.
- The routing controller and sensitivity weights depend on NER accuracy; mis-classification (acknowledged as a general risk of thresholding) could route sensitive prompts to lower-protection paths, but routing error rates and their privacy impact are not quantified.
- The perturbed prompt is transmitted in plaintext; the guarantee is only as strong as the local randomized-response mechanism, and correlation across multiple queries (sequential composition drift) is not analyzed.
- Synthetic evaluation dataset limits external validity; IQ judge specifics and human validation are underspecified.
- Cloud LLM behavior on perturbed prompts is treated as cooperative; adversarial/degraded cloud responses are not modeled.

## Design implications
Data-minimization by routing: keep the highest-sensitivity computation entirely on trusted local hardware, and treat "capability is not permission" as "not every prompt needs the cloud." Entity-level, category-aware budget allocation is a useful pattern for any redaction/perturbation gate. The sketch-then-refine pattern is a way to get cloud capability without exposing raw context.

## Implementation implications
A sensitivity-profiling + policy-gating stage in front of any LLM egress can decide cloud vs local vs collaborative handling. Transmit perturbed plaintext rather than embeddings to avoid tokenizer/embedding-space coupling. Track cumulative ε across a session to avoid composition-driven leakage.

## Evaluation implications
Privacy claims for such routers should be backed by executed inference/linkage attacks (e.g., membership or attribute inference against the transmitted P*), not ε budgets alone. Report routing confusion matrices and the privacy cost of mis-routing.

## Deployment implications
Suited to privacy-critical domains (medical, finance, personalized services) with an edge SLM available. Energy/latency overhead (~1.5×/2.3× vs cloud-only) is modest; the dependence on NER quality and on the cloud honoring sketch semantics are the operational risks.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Log routing decisions and per-session ε consumption; alert on repeated collaborative-mode queries about the same entities (linkage risk). Monitor for cloud responses that echo obfuscated entities (leakage of the masking scheme).

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to cloud–edge LLM inference where an edge SLM can retain and refine local context. Does NOT generalize to settings without a trusted edge model, to malicious clouds that manipulate sketches, or to strong adaptive adversaries performing multi-query correlation — none of which are evaluated. Findings are on synthetic prompts and specific model pairings.
