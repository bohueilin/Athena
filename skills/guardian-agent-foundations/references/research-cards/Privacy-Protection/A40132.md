## Research question
Can secret-sharing-based secure multi-party computation (MPC) train neural networks over the ring Z_{2^k} scalably in the number of parties n, while overcoming the exponential ring-degree blow-up of prior Galois-ring packing, by using Reverse Multiplication-Friendly Embedding (RMFE) for higher packing density plus mixed-circuit techniques for efficient nonlinear operations?

## Problem definition
Prior PPML-training MPC protocols are customized for a fixed small number of parties (2–4) or scale poorly: replicated secret sharing has exponential communication in n; general MPC handles arbitrary n but is prohibitively expensive on nonlinear ML functions. Shamir-over-F_p protocols achieve O(n) communication but pay for slow modulo-p arithmetic. Shamir-over-Galois-rings replaces mod-p with fast mod-2^k but earlier coefficient-packing (Zhou 2025a) makes the ring degree d grow exponentially with the number of packed secrets ρ (d ≥ 2ρ−1).

## System or model being studied
A secure n-party (n ≥ 3) NN-training framework using Shamir secret sharing over Galois rings GR(2^k, d), semi-honest, honest-majority (t < n/2). Contributions: (1) generalize packing via a general (ρ, d)-RMFE (map ϕ, ψ) keeping d linear in ρ (e.g., (3,5)-RMFE instead of needing d=7), lifting the multiplicative-identity-preservation constraint required by prior work (Coral); (2) integrate mixed-circuit computation (arithmetic sharing over GR(2^k,d) and binary sharing over GR(2,d), with daBit/edaBit-based conversions ΠB2A, truncation ΠTrun, comparison) compatible with RMFE packing; (3) build NN layers — ReLU/DReLU activation, max/average pooling, softmax+cross-entropy gradient via ReLU and division — on this basis, with a parallel mini-batch training framework (ΠSplit, ΠExpand, gradient aggregation).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: learn private training inputs of honest parties beyond the protocol's designated outputs.
- Adversary knowledge: semi-honest (passive) — follows the protocol but tries to infer from its view.
- Adversary access: corrupts a coalition of up to t < n/2 parties (honest majority) and sees their shares/messages.
- Adversary capabilities: passive collusion among corrupted parties; no deviation from protocol (no malicious/active security claimed).
- Attacker budget: bounded by honest-majority threshold t < n/2.
- Phase: training (also applicable to inference primitives).
- Targeted / untargeted: confidentiality of all secret-shared inputs (untargeted).
- Digital / physical: digital (cryptographic).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: security analyzed under the Universal Composability (UC) framework; semi-honest, static corruption model (adaptive corruption not claimed).

## Trust assumptions
Honest majority (at most t < n/2 passively corrupted). Shamir scheme over GR(2^k, d) guarantees that any ≤ t corrupted parties learn nothing beyond designated outputs. Requires an exceptional sequence of evaluation points in the ring (length up to 2^d, so n < 2^d). Correctness/security of RMFE operations, hyper-invertible-matrix-based randomness (FDouRan, FRanPair), and daBit/edaBit generation are assumed per cited constructions. Fixed-point encoding parameters (precision f, ℓ, statistical security κ, k ≥ ℓ+κ+1) must be set correctly.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Not an attack paper. The "failure" addressed is efficiency/scalability collapse of secure training: exponential communication in n, slow mod-p arithmetic, or exponential ring-degree growth from packing — each rendering privacy-preserving training impractical.

## Proposed defense or method
Privacy-preserving training via RMFE-packed Shamir secret sharing over Galois rings: pack ρ secrets into one GR(2^k, d) element with d linear in ρ, enabling SIMD-style parallel multiplication (ΠMult with double-sharing for degree reduction and re-encoding). Mixed-circuit primitives (ΠB2A, deterministic/probabilistic truncation ΠTrun, integer comparison) compute nonlinear ML functions bit-wise over GR(2, d). Supports a general RMFE form (removes the Pack(e)=1_R constraint of Coral).

## Datasets and benchmarks
MNIST (Deng; LeCun). Networks: a fully connected network (FCN) and a convolutional neural network (CNN). Benchmarks compare protocol variants (e.g., (1,3)-, (2,3)-, (3,5)-RMFE) and prior arithmetic/mixed protocols on communication (KB) and time (LAN/WAN, offline/online). Table on fixed-point precision f vs training accuracy is reported.

## Evaluation methodology
Microbenchmarks of truncation/edaBit primitives (ΠTrun, ΠEdabits) in LAN and WAN, offline and online, in communication and time; end-to-end FCN/CNN training over MNIST across participant counts and RMFE parameters; ablation of fixed-point precision f (13/16/20) against plaintext accuracy.

## Metrics
Communication (KB), offline/online runtime (seconds / milliseconds) under LAN and WAN, and training accuracy vs fixed-point precision (e.g., plaintext 77.50%→92.97% across 5/10/15 epochs; f=16/20 approach plaintext, f=13 much lower).

## Main findings
- The general-RMFE framework needs only d=5 for ρ=3 (vs d=7 in prior packing), giving higher effective packing density and efficiency.
- Reports significant reductions in training time and communication versus existing approaches; the mixed-circuit ΠTrun (Ours) shows lower communication/time than arithmetic-only and prior mixed variants in the benchmark table.
- For CNN experiments, the (3,5)-RMFE outperforms (2,d)-RMFEs on communication across tested participant counts, even though its theoretical packing density ρ/d is lower — a practical advantage.
- Fixed-point precision materially affects accuracy: f=16 and f=20 approach plaintext accuracy; f=13 degrades substantially.

## Negative results
Low fixed-point precision (f=13) yields large accuracy loss (e.g., ~52–55% vs ~92% plaintext), showing the precision/efficiency tension. Theoretical packing density is not always the right predictor of practical performance ((2,3) has higher ρ/d yet loses to (3,5) on CNN communication).

## Design implications
Cryptographic MPC can provide input-confidentiality for collaborative model training among mutually distrusting parties without a trusted third party — a defense-in-depth option distinct from DP or federated averaging. Packing-density and ring-representation choices are first-order for practical cost; general RMFE relaxes prior algebraic constraints.

## Implementation implications
Use general (ρ, d)-RMFE (e.g., (3,5)) to keep ring degree linear in packed secrets; integrate mixed arithmetic/binary sharing so nonlinear layers (ReLU via DReLU/truncation, pooling, softmax-CE gradient) are computed bit-wise. Select fixed-point precision f ≥ 16 to retain accuracy. Note practical performance may diverge from theoretical ρ/d.

## Evaluation implications
Report communication and LAN/WAN online/offline separately, and sweep participant count n and precision f; theoretical packing density alone is insufficient to predict cost. For security-grade claims, note the semi-honest/honest-majority scope explicitly.

## Deployment implications
Fits multi-institution collaborations (e.g., cross-hospital/cross-bank) needing input privacy under an honest majority with only passive threats. Not suitable where active adversaries, dishonest majority, or large-scale deep models are expected without further work.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Largely out of scope: MPC provides cryptographic confidentiality rather than runtime monitoring. Operationally, monitor for protocol-participation faults and ensure the honest-majority assumption holds (party vetting), since security collapses if > t parties collude.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to secret-sharing MPC training of small NNs over Z_{2^k} under semi-honest honest-majority security. Does NOT generalize to malicious security, dishonest majority, LLM-scale models, or agent/LLM runtime security. Peripheral to agent security: a cryptographic training-confidentiality primitive, off-topic to prompt/tool/agent threat models — included here as a privacy-preserving-ML building block.
