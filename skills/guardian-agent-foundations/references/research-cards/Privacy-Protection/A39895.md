## Research question
Can federated unlearning (removing a leaving client's contribution to honor RTBF) be made **fair** — both in
performance impact across remaining clients (performance fairness, P.F.) and in unlearning cost across clients
(efficiency fairness, E.F.) — while staying efficient and exact?

## Problem definition
Prior federated unlearning (FU) optimizes only efficiency/effectiveness and overlooks two fairness axes. Calibration-
based approximate FU (FedRecover, FedEraser, RapidRetrain) causes uneven performance degradation on remaining clients
and insufficient unlearning on leavers → violates P.F. Exact retraining (e.g. FATS) causes large variance in
per-client unlearning cost → violates E.F. Unfairness has security consequences: it enables **cascaded leaving**
(clients with similar data leave after seeing unfair degradation) and **data-poisoning attacks** (malicious clients
issue deliberate unlearning requests to harm similar-data victims).

## System or model being studied
**FedShard**: a sharded FL/FU framework. Clients are organized into a tree; shards are nodes, training stages are
tree levels; shards train in isolation then progressively merge (merging rate R) until one global shard remains (P =
⌈K/R⌉ stages). On an unlearning request, only shards on the leaving client's root-path are retrained; others reuse
cached states. Two adaptive algorithms: **A1** cluster-based shard merging (mixes divergent update directions for
stable convergence and fair contribution) and **A2** variance-aware training-round allocation (homogeneous shards
train more rounds, heterogeneous fewer). Supports simultaneous multi-client unlearning.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: (1) malicious clients weaponize unlearning requests to launch data-poisoning attacks against
  clients with similar data; (2) an MIA adversary tests whether a leaving client's data was truly removed.
- Adversary knowledge / access: participating clients (insiders) able to join/leave and issue unlearning requests;
  MIA/DPA adversaries evaluated post-unlearning.
- Adversary capabilities: deliberate unlearning requests (DPA); membership queries (MIA).
- Phase: federated training + unlearning (deployment).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (against specific similar-data clients).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive standard MIA/DPA evaluations.

## Trust assumptions
Central server coordinates and caches shard states honestly. Clients may be malicious (issue poisoning-oriented
unlearning requests). Exact-unlearning correctness rests on Proposition 1 (cached updates for a training process
unaffected by D_l equal retraining from scratch on D_0 \ D_l).

## Attack or failure mechanism
Unfair FU is the vulnerability: excessive/insufficient forgetting lets DPA succeed and drives cascaded leaving.
FedShard's isolate-then-merge structure localizes each client's influence so removal touches only its root-path
shards, bounding both collateral degradation (P.F.) and cost disparity (E.F.).

## Proposed defense or method
Sharded exact unlearning + two adaptive controllers (A1 merging, A2 round allocation), plus two new fairness metrics:
**M_p** (performance fairness — variance-style measure of degradation across clients) and **M_e** (efficiency
fairness — contribution-weighted variance of unlearning costs Z_c normalized by client impact |α_c|; lower = fairer).
Contribution factor α_c is the angle between a client's local update and the global update. The metrics are proved to
satisfy known fairness axioms (referenced fairness/measure literature).

## Datasets and benchmarks
MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100. K up to 512 clients; non-IID level ρ (e.g. 0.1). Baselines: FedShard (FS),
Vanilla retrain / FedAvg retrain (FA), FATS (FT), RapidRetrain (RR), FedEraser (FE), FedRecovery (FR). Attack
evaluations: MIA (F1-score) and DPA (precision) on CIFAR-10.

## Evaluation methodology
Theory: prove exact-unlearning equivalence to retraining (induction over stages) and speedup bounds (Prop 2:
r1 = (R−1)/R · K/(K−1) · P; general-case bounds Prop 3; multi-client Props 4). Empirical: unlearning cost t, M_e,
M_p across methods/datasets; cascaded-leaving count vs ρ; MIA/DPA robustness; a holistic 5-axis comparison
(efficiency, E.F., P.F., accuracy, F1-effectiveness).

## Metrics
Average unlearning cost t (s), M_e, M_p, MIA F1-score, DPA precision (%), model accuracy, cascaded-leaving client
count, speedup factors.

## Main findings
- FedShard unlearns 1.3–6.2× faster than retraining from scratch and up to 4.9× faster than SOTA exact unlearning
  (author claims); proven ≥ P/2× (usually P×) faster than vanilla retraining.
- Approximate methods (RR, FE) show high (worse) M_p and more cascaded leaving, and **fail to defend DPA**; exact
  methods (FS, FT, FA) achieve low M_p and defend DPA. FedShard additionally achieves the lowest M_e (best efficiency
  fairness) — e.g. M_e ≈ 9–11 vs tens-to-hundreds for baselines (Table 1, OCR-approximate).
- FedShard is reported as the only framework jointly high on all five axes (Fig 8).

## Negative results
A fundamental trade-off is documented in prior work: approximate methods sacrifice P.F. for efficiency; prior exact
methods sacrifice E.F. or are prohibitively slow — FedShard is claimed to resolve it, but this is shown on simulated
FL, not deployed systems.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Much detail (fairness-axiom proofs, complexity, additional experiments, DPA setup) is deferred to appendices; the
framework's efficiency depends on the training-round bounds [T_max, T_min] (Prop 3) and merging rate R.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- Exactness rests on Proposition 1 (a widely-accepted but idealized assumption about update independence); its
  validity under stateful optimizers, client sampling, or secure aggregation is not stress-tested.
- MIA/DPA are standard non-adaptive attacks on CIFAR-10 only; no adaptive attacker who knows the sharding structure
  and crafts unlearning requests to defeat A1/A2 is evaluated.
- Sharded isolation may reduce global model quality vs joint training; the accuracy axis is reported favorably but
  the convergence/utility cost of isolation under strong non-IID is under-explored in the main text.
- Threat of a **malicious server** (which holds all caches) is out of scope.
- "Fairness" is contribution-weighted variance, an operational definition; its alignment with legal/ethical fairness
  is asserted via axioms, not user studies.

## Design implications
Frames unlearning **fairness as a security property**: uneven forgetting is itself an attack surface (cascaded
leaving, targeted DPA via unlearning requests). Systems that let principals trigger deletion/unlearning should bound
the cross-principal blast radius and cost variance, and should treat the unlearning-request channel as
adversary-controlled.

## Implementation implications
Isolate-then-merge sharding with cached shard states gives cheap, root-path-local exact unlearning; adaptive round
allocation and diversity-promoting merging keep cost/quality fair. Multi-request batching improves efficiency.

## Evaluation implications
The M_e / M_p metrics give the corpus concrete, axiom-backed fairness measures for unlearning; DPA-robustness is a
useful security check that approximate unlearning can fail. Reviewer note: add adaptive, structure-aware attackers.

## Deployment implications
Suited to FL deployments with churn where clients weigh join/leave costs; fairness reduces incentives for
destabilizing cascaded departures. Assumes a trusted, cache-holding server.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Per-client unlearning cost and degradation can be tracked via M_e / M_p as fairness/health signals; abnormal
unlearning-request patterns (potential DPA) are worth monitoring — the paper motivates this but does not build a
detector.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to horizontal federated unlearning with a trusted server. Do NOT generalize to malicious-server settings,
to vertical FL (see A39911), or to a formal privacy guarantee — exactness is w.r.t. retraining, and MIA/DPA results
are non-adaptive on one dataset.
