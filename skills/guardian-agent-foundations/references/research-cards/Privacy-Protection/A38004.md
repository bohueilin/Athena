## Research question
How can dynamic-scene novel-view synthesis (NVS) from monocular video achieve artifact-free temporal consistency — especially for temporarily occluded objects — by modeling 3D Gaussian motion as a Markov process rather than deforming each Gaussian independently?

## Problem definition
Monocular dynamic-scene NVS suffers motion artifacts from temporal inconsistency; temporarily occluded regions degrade in novel views. Prior 3DGS deformation methods deform each Gaussian independently (costly, ignore inter-Gaussian and cross-time correlations) or model only same-timestamp local rigidity (D-3D-GS, 4D-GS, SC-GS, SP-GS), neglecting connections across adjacent timestamps.

## System or model being studied
MCGS (Markov Chain Gaussian Splatting): a Markov-chain-with-memory deformation network over sparse "Markov points" (control points that drive neighboring 3D Gaussians via KNN). Components: (1) Markov Deform Network predicting position/rotation state transitions from a sliding window of past states; (2) multi-head temporal attention adaptively weighting historical states; (3) strategic Gaussian noise injection during training (annealed, higher in likely-occluded regions) for robustness; a dual-branch design blends Markov-chain prediction with a direct MLP prediction via adaptive weights (λ_pos, λ_rot), with SLERP for rotations.

## Trust assumptions
None security-relevant. Assumes object motion follows continuous physical laws (continuous linear/angular acceleration), enabling Markov state-transition prediction.

## Attack or failure mechanism
No attack. The failure studied is quality-related: temporal inconsistency and reconstruction discontinuity for temporarily occluded objects in novel views.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a defense. Markov-chain-with-memory deformation + temporal attention + annealed noise injection to predict motion through occlusions and maintain temporal coherence.

## Datasets and benchmarks
D-NeRF dataset (eight synthetic dynamic scenes; Lego excluded from quantitative comparison due to test-set bias) and vrig scenes of HyperNeRF (real-world). Baselines: HyperNeRF, TiNeuVox-B, 4D-GS, SP-GS, SC-GS, 4D-Rotor-GS.

## Evaluation methodology
Quantitative NVS comparison (image quality + speed) against dynamic-GS baselines on D-NeRF and HyperNeRF; ablations removing temporal attention and noise injection; report Gaussian count, training time, and FPS.

## Metrics
PSNR (↑), SSIM (↑) / MS-SSIM (↑), LPIPS (↓); training time and FPS. Reported: D-NeRF full-model PSNR 42.74 (ablation table), HyperNeRF vrig average PSNR 25.76 / MS-SSIM 0.852; removing temporal attention drops PSNR by 2.29 dB (author claims).

## Main findings
- Modeling Gaussian motion as a Markov chain with memory plus temporal attention improves visual quality and temporal coherence over prior dynamic-GS methods while keeping competitive rendering speed (author claim).
- Temporal attention is the most impactful component (−2.29 dB PSNR when removed); noise injection gives a smaller robustness/generalization gain.

## Negative results
- Author-stated: training time increases for large-scale, long-duration dynamic scenes; the method struggles with complex specular reflections (shared with other neural-rendering approaches).

## Limitations stated by the authors
Increased training time for large-scale/long-duration scenes; difficulty with complex specular reflections.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- No privacy/security content; the corpus "Privacy-Protection" label does not match the paper. "Noise injection" here is a generalization regularizer, not differential privacy — it must not be read as a privacy mechanism.

- The Markov continuity assumption may not hold for abrupt/non-physical motion.
