## Research question
When a model has learned spurious shortcuts from a biased dataset, do class-wise machine-unlearning algorithms actually forget the target class's causal information — or do they merely erase the bias/shortcut representation? And can unlearning be made to surgically remove causal class information under bias, without access to the retain set?

## Problem definition
Machine unlearning (needed for the "right to be forgotten" and for removing harmful content) implicitly assumes target information is cleanly separable in parameter space. Under data bias, class features are entangled with spurious features (e.g., "waterbird" ⇄ "water" background). The paper shows standard unlearning then fails in a specific way and asks how to fix it without a retain set (often unavailable for privacy/storage reasons).

## System or model being studied
CUPID (Causal Unlearning via Pathway Identification and Disentanglement), a three-stage class-wise unlearning framework applied to image classifiers (ResNet-style) trained on biased datasets: (1) Sharpness-Aware Partitioning splits the forget set into bias-approximated (flat/low-sharpness) vs causal-approximated (sharp/high-curvature) subsets using per-sample loss sharpness under a worst-case parameter perturbation; (2) Causal Pathway Identification selects high-curvature, high-magnitude parameters as the "causal pathway" (vs "bias pathway"); (3) Targeted Pathway Update routes distinct/refined gradients to each pathway to erase causal class info while managing the bias pathway. Operates without the retain set.

Unlearning is the defense; the "adversary" is the verification/MIA process plus the regulatory requirement to actually delete influence.
- Adversary knowledge: MIA evaluation is the modeled probe; access level for MIA not deeply specified (standard MIA assumptions).

- Adversary capabilities: membership inference; linear probing of bias attribute.
- Attacker budget: not stated in paper.
- Phase: post-training (unlearning applied to an already-trained model); MIA at inference/deployment.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific target class cf).
- Digital / physical: digital.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the original biased model and the forget set (all samples of the target class) are available; assumes the retain set is NOT available (design constraint); trusts loss-landscape sharpness as a faithful signal separating shortcut- vs causal-driven samples; assumes bias attribute annotations exist for evaluation (bias-aligned vs bias-conflicting) even if not for the method.

## Attack or failure mechanism
The paper identifies and formalizes "shortcut unlearning": when told to forget a class, a biased model takes the "easy way out" and erases its reliance on the dominant spurious shortcut rather than the causal class features. Two phenomena: (1) "easy to learn, yet hard to forget" — bias-aligned samples resist forgetting; (2) a paradoxical debiasing effect — accuracy on bias-conflicting samples of the forget class *increases* after unlearning. A linear probe shows bias-attribute accuracy drops on the unlearned model, confirming the shortcut (not the class) was removed. Root cause: uniform parameter updates target the most dominant pattern (the shortcut).

## Proposed defense or method
CUPID (see System). Motivated by loss-landscape geometry: bias-aligned "easy" samples occupy flat/low-curvature minima; bias-conflicting "hard" samples occupy sharp/high-curvature regions (empirically validated by sharpness distributions). Sharpness therefore slices the forget set; parameter saliency (curvature × magnitude) localizes causal vs bias pathways; targeted gradients erase causal class info surgically while preserving utility, without the retain set.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Biased datasets: Waterbirds, BAR, Biased NICO++. Reported setting includes a heavily biased distribution (bias-aligned : bias-conflicting = 99.5 : 0.5 in the forget class).

## Evaluation methodology
Compared against Retrain (gold standard) and unlearning baselines: NegGrad, Random label, Bad Teaching, Boundary Shrink, Boundary Expand, SALUN, DELETE — with/without retain-set access noted per method. Metrics tracked across the three datasets; forgetting dynamics analyzed (loss curves, linear probing, sharpness distributions).

## Metrics
RA (Retain Accuracy ↑), FA (Forget Accuracy ↓), △gap ↓ (balance between bias-aligned and bias-conflicting forgetting), WGA (Worst-Group Accuracy on forget ↓), MIA (membership-inference ↓). Reported: CUPID achieves the lowest FA and most balanced forgetting (e.g., Waterbirds FA 6.91 / △gap 7.27; NICO++ FA 7.70; BAR FA 7.71) vs much higher/unbalanced baselines, while keeping RA ~high.

## Main findings
(1) Standard unlearning under bias exhibits shortcut unlearning — it erases the shortcut, not the class, and can paradoxically improve forget-class bias-conflicting accuracy. (2) Loss-landscape sharpness reliably distinguishes bias-aligned (flat) from bias-conflicting (sharp) samples. (3) CUPID achieves SOTA forgetting (lowest FA, lowest △gap, lowest WGA) across three biased datasets while operating without the retain set and maintaining retain accuracy.

## Negative results
The paper's central "negative" is diagnostic: existing methods (NegGrad etc.) fail to truly forget under bias. For CUPID itself no failure regime is reported in the provided text (e.g., no test on non-vision/LLM settings).

## Limitations stated by the authors
Authors frame future work as extending the surgical approach to erasing abstract *concepts* entangled with other knowledge (implying current scope is class-wise forgetting under bias). They do not claim generality beyond the evaluated image-classification setting.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- Evaluated only on image classifiers and three curated biased datasets; no LLM/generative or agent-memory unlearning, so transfer to text/agent settings is unverified.
- MIA is a standard-strength probe; robustness against stronger/adaptive unlearning-aware auditors (e.g., unlearning-inversion, gradient-based verification) is not shown.
- Sharpness partitioning relies on a top-k% threshold τs and a perturbation step η — sensitivity/robustness of these hyperparameters to bias ratio is only partially explored.
- Requires knowing/curating bias annotations for evaluation; real-world "unknown bias" auditing is harder.
- "Forgetting" is measured behaviorally (accuracy/MIA), not via a formal deletion guarantee — no certified unlearning bound.

## Design implications
For any deletion/forgetting requirement (GDPR right-to-be-forgotten, harmful-content removal, agent-memory scrubbing), assume the naive update forgets the *most salient correlate*, not the intended target — verify forgetting causally, not just by loss on the forget set. Loss-landscape sharpness is a usable signal to localize which parameters store which knowledge.

## Implementation implications
Unlearning pipelines should not assume clean separability; they need a mechanism to disentangle entangled causal vs spurious pathways, and should ideally work without the retain set (privacy/storage-realistic). Pathway-targeted updates are one such mechanism.

## Evaluation implications
Unlearning must be evaluated with bias-aware, group-decomposed metrics (bias-aligned vs bias-conflicting, WGA, △gap) plus MIA — a single forget-accuracy number can hide shortcut unlearning. This is a strong methodological contribution for privacy auditing.

## Deployment implications
Deleting a class/user's influence from a biased production model may leave causal traces while removing benign correlates — a compliance risk (apparent deletion that fails an adversarial audit). CUPID reduces but does not certify this gap.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Post-deletion verification should include MIA + linear-probe checks on what was actually removed; log sharpness/pathway decisions as evidence for deletion audits.

## Applicability boundaries
_(where these findings should and should NOT be generalized)_
Adjacent to agent/AI security: it is a data-privacy (right-to-be-forgotten) and trustworthy-ML method, directly transferable in spirit to LLM/agent unlearning and memory scrubbing, but demonstrated ONLY on image classifiers under curated bias. Do NOT assume the same sharpness-partitioning works out-of-the-box for LLMs, diffusion models, or agent memory without re-validation. Not about prompt/tool-injection threats.
