## Research question
Can the evaluation layer of a federated-learning (FL) data marketplace be made privacy-preserving, robust, and verifiable — closing martFL's weaknesses of plaintext score exposure and unverifiable, manipulable participant selection?

## Problem definition
In model-centric data marketplaces, participants trade model utilities (FL model updates) instead of raw data. martFL (state of the art) still (1) decrypts final utility scores to the Data Acquirer (DA), exposing model quality and enabling selective acceptance/rejection; (2) uses static heuristic clustering for anomaly detection with no formal guarantee and easy manipulation; (3) performs opaque, non-verifiable participant selection. The paper targets the evaluation module only (secure trading is declared orthogonal/out of scope).

## System or model being studied
MartDE, a Secure Evaluation Module for FL data marketplaces with three roles: Data Acquirer (DA), Data Providers (DPs), and a non-colluding Auxiliary Server (AS) (instantiable via Divvi Up / ISRG two-party non-collusion). Cryptographic primitives: CKKS homomorphic encryption, commitment schemes, digital signatures, and two secret-sharing two-server protocols (Secure Max πmax, Winner Identification πwin, Public Verification πver).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: DPs earn rewards without genuine contribution / disrupt training (poisoning); DA or AS learn private scores, manipulate selection, or bias aggregation.
- Adversary knowledge: protocol-level (participants know the protocol); model updates encrypted under per-DP CKKS keys.
- Adversary access: DPs submit arbitrary local model updates and encrypted scores; DA and AS see shares/ciphertexts.
- Adversary capabilities: DPs are Byzantine and covert adversaries (misbehave only when unlikely to be detected); DA and AS are covert adversaries under an anytrust model (secure as long as one of DA/AS is honest and they do not collude).
- Attacker budget: attacker ratio up to 0.2 in poisoning experiments; robustness also tested up to 80% malicious DPs in scaling-attack figures.
- Phase: training (FL model-update evaluation and aggregation).
- Targeted / untargeted: model-poisoning attacks (IPM, ALIE, scaling >1 and <1) — both direction and norm manipulations.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: partially adaptive — scaling attacks are explicitly designed to break cosine-similarity defenses; commitment verification is designed to catch score misreporting.

## Trust assumptions
Anytrust non-collusion between DA and AS; soundness of CKKS HE, commitments, and signatures. DA holds a small, possibly biased root/baseline dataset (design tolerates this). Covert-adversary model: parties avoid detectable cheating due to reputational/legal risk.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Model-poisoning: malicious DPs submit crafted updates (IPM, ALIE) or scale their update norm (scaling attack) to preserve direction while amplifying/attenuating magnitude, defeating cosine-similarity-based robust aggregation. Additionally, in martFL the DA's plaintext access to scores enables privacy leakage and biased/manipulable selection.

## Proposed defense or method
Four components: (1) Encrypted utility scoring with client-side decryption — DA homomorphically computes encrypted cosine similarity between each DP's normalized update and the DA baseline; each DP decrypts only its own score (DA never sees plaintext scores). (2) Formally bounded anomaly filtering — quantize squared similarity zᵢ=(sᵢ)², treat zᵢ>1 as anomaly, securely compute max via πmax over secret-shared unary vectors, exclude DPs above threshold. (3) Adaptive participant selection — select by utility-price ratio ρᵢ=sᵢ/vᵢ with an adaptive selection proportion that shrinks as global accuracy rises. (4) Commitment-based verification — DPs commit to scores and sign declared prices; DA verifies consistency of scores/prices for selected DPs without exposing others' scores; DPs verify selection via πver. Plus gradient-norm normalization before aggregation to remove norm-based exploitability of cosine similarity.

## Datasets and benchmarks
MNIST, Fashion-MNIST (FMNIST), CIFAR (referenced), partitioned non-iid with q=0.5. Baselines: FedAvg, Krum, Median, Trimmed Mean, Clipping Median, DDFed, martFL. Attacks: IPM, ALIE, Scaling (>1 and <1). Metric: Main Task Accuracy (MTA). Experiments: 100 clients, attacker ratio 0.2, attacks start at round 50 (and adversary-proportion sweeps 40–80%).

## Evaluation methodology
Compare MTA under each poisoning attack against baseline defenses; sweep malicious-DP proportion; ablate the norm-normalization module's effect on scaling robustness. Security argued via the covert/anytrust threat model and cryptographic protocol construction (πmax, πwin, πver) plus commitment verification; correctness of reported values enforced by commitment checks.

## Metrics
Main Task Accuracy (MTA, %) under attack; post-attack recovery/stability; robustness vs. malicious-DP fraction. Formal claims: "formally bounded" anomaly filter; verifiable selection. Cost-efficiency asserted via adaptive selection proportion (fewer selected as accuracy improves).

## Main findings
(Author claims) Under IPM, MartDE quickly recovers to near-clean accuracy; under ALIE it preserves >75% test accuracy with minimal fluctuation, outperforming DDFed and Median methods. Under large-factor scaling, most baselines drop below 20% while only MartDE and Clipping Median stay robust, with MartDE consistently higher; at 80% malicious clients MartDE maintains >75% accuracy in the over-scaling case. Norm normalization is credited as the key to scaling robustness. Privacy: DA never sees plaintext scores; selection is verifiable via commitments and πver.

## Negative results
No explicit failure case reported in the extracted text. Trading/aggregation beyond evaluation is out of scope, so end-to-end marketplace security is not demonstrated.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Scope is limited to the evaluation module; secure model trading is orthogonal and left to future work ("integrate lightweight secure computation to enable efficient, verifiable data trading"). Relies on the two-server non-collusion (anytrust) assumption.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Security rests entirely on DA/AS non-collusion; a colluding DA+AS breaks confidentiality and selection integrity — a strong assumption for adversarial marketplaces.
- Covert-adversary model excludes fully malicious actors willing to be detected; real attackers may not be deterred by reputational risk.
- "Formally bounded" anomaly filter is a heuristic threshold (zᵢ>1) on quantized squared cosine similarity; robustness against attackers who craft updates with valid in-range scores is not shown.
- Datasets are small image classification (MNIST/FMNIST/CIFAR); no NLP/large-model or realistic marketplace-scale evaluation.
- CKKS HE + repeated secure-max rounds impose computational/communication overhead not quantified in the extracted text (cost-efficiency claim is mostly about selection proportion, not crypto cost).
- No formal proof reproduced in extracted text; security is argued, not machine-checked.

## Design implications
For utility-driven data exchange, keep evaluation scores confidential to their owner and make selection cryptographically verifiable — separate "who can see the score" (capability) from "who decides selection" (authority), and prove the decision (evidence via commitments). Normalize update norms before cosine-based trust scoring to remove a direction-preserving attack surface.

## Implementation implications
Encrypted-score-with-client-side-decryption + commitment binding of scores/prices is a concrete pattern for privacy-preserving contribution evaluation; requires a non-colluding auxiliary server and per-participant HE keys.

## Evaluation implications
Robustness of FL trust mechanisms should be tested against both direction (IPM/ALIE) and norm (scaling) manipulations and across adversary fractions well beyond 50%, since cosine-only defenses collapse under norm attacks.

## Deployment implications
Applicable to FL-based data/model marketplaces under regulation (GDPR/PIPL) where raw-data trading is infeasible. Deployers must guarantee the non-collusion assumption operationally (independent operators, e.g., ISRG Divvi Up) or the privacy/verifiability guarantees do not hold.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Commitment-based verification enables after-the-fact auditing of declared vs. evaluated scores and of selection fairness, supporting dispute resolution; anomaly filtering flags structurally unsound updates for containment/exclusion.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to federated data/model marketplaces and Byzantine-robust FL aggregation with a two-server non-collusion trust model. Adjacent to (not core to) LLM-agent security: transferable to multi-party model-contribution and secure aggregation settings, but not about prompts, tools, or agent autonomy. Do NOT generalize the robustness numbers beyond small image-classification FL or to colluding-server settings.
