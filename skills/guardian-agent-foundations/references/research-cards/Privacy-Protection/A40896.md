## Research question
When a graph model honors a "right to be forgotten" deletion request (graph unlearning), how can it simultaneously guarantee (ε,δ)-certified unlearning AND preserve individual fairness, given that the act of node removal itself introduces bias?

## Problem definition
Graph unlearning removes the influence of specific nodes without full retraining. Prior work treats unlearning as privacy-only (or uses unlearning to remove already-biased samples) but overlooks bias introduced by the unlearning process itself. Two mechanisms are identified: (i) neighbor loss — deleting a node breaks pairwise fairness constraints linking similar individuals; (ii) constraint weakening — fewer node pairs remain to uphold fairness regularization, so optimization drifts toward accuracy over fairness. This causes divergent predictions for similar individuals despite unchanged input similarity.

## System or model being studied
GUIC (Graph Unlearning with Individual Fairness Certificates): a training-free, closed-form certificate-driven weight update on GNNs (analysis uses Simple Graph Convolution, SGC, with embedding rule H^(K)=P^K X). Targets node-level binary classification on attributed graphs.

## Trust assumptions
Certified unlearning is defined in the standard (ε,δ) statistical-indistinguishability sense (updated model indistinguishable from retraining on retained data). Individual fairness follows Dwork et al. "fairness through awareness" (similar individuals → similar outcomes). Calibrated Gaussian noise b∼N(0,σ²I) is injected to mask residual node-specific information and achieve the (ε,δ) certificate. Predictions assumed to be linear mappings of node representations.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Not an attack: node deletion perturbs neighbor embeddings through message passing, increasing the local Lipschitz constant around affected nodes and widening prediction gaps between similar individuals — quantified as "individual graph unlearning bias" Δbias (sum over similar-node pairs of output-space differences before vs. after removal).

## Proposed defense or method
GUIC (author method): (1) a distance-based rule identifies individual bias introduced by a node removal; (2) a closed-form, certificate-driven single-step weight adjustment removes the deleted node's predictive influence while constraining performance shift within a subspace that preserves the local Lipschitz bound (restoring fairness smoothness); (3) calibrated Gaussian noise injection provides (ε,δ)-certified unlearning. Formal analysis derives a bound on the bias increment Δbias^(w) in terms of max degree, regularization weight λ, number of training nodes, feature norm, and diffusion profile. This is a lightweight, training-free alternative to retraining or fairness-regularized reoptimization, with per-request verifiable guarantees.

## Design implications
When designing agent/ML systems that must honor deletion requests (right to be forgotten, memory purges), treat fairness as a first-class post-deletion invariant: deleting one subject's data can shift outcomes for similar subjects. Provide per-request verifiable certificates so that a deletion's downstream effect is auditable, not just asserted.

## Implementation implications
A closed-form, training-free compensatory weight update with calibrated noise can deliver both certified forgetting and fairness preservation cheaply — attractive where retraining is infeasible. Requires estimating representation drift / local Lipschitz change per deletion.

## Evaluation implications
Unlearning methods should be evaluated for fairness side effects (individual-level prediction drift among similar entities), not only for accuracy-vs-retraining fidelity and forgetting completeness.

## Deployment implications
Relevant to GDPR/CCPA right-to-be-forgotten compliance in graph-based decision systems (finance, healthcare, criminal justice named as high-stakes domains). Certified per-request guarantees support regulatory evidence trails.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Δbias / local-Lipschitz tracking gives a runtime signal to detect when a deletion has amplified individual unfairness, enabling containment or compensatory update before deployment of the unlearned model.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to node-level GNN classification under legitimate data-deletion requests; results are about fairness-preserving certified unlearning, NOT about adversarial robustness, privacy attacks, or agent/tool security. Adjacent to agent security only insofar as agent-memory deletion / model unlearning is a compliance concern. Do NOT generalize the fairness certificates to adversarial or malicious-unlearning settings, nor beyond the linear-GNN assumptions without further proof.
