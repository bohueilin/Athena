## Research question
Can a model's *inherent privacy awareness* be enhanced at inference time — training-free and task-agnostic — by steering its representations along a linearly-encoded "privacy" direction, and does this direction transfer across same-source LLMs and to VLMs built on them?

## Problem definition
Beyond protecting stored data, frontier models must recognize and respect privacy boundaries in their own outputs (privacy awareness). Prior defenses (federated learning, DP, unlearning) target specific objectives (PII, narrow domains) and treat symptoms, not the model's internal privacy representation. Goal: improve privacy protection during inference without retraining, generalizing across models and modalities.

## System or model being studied
"Know-Then-Do" framework: KNOW = probe how privacy concepts are linearly represented; DO = construct a steering vector and add it to activations during inference (h′_l = h_l + α·pv_l). Evaluated on 12 LLMs (LLaMA-2 7B/13B base+chat, LLaMA-3-8B base+it, Mistral-7B base+it, Qwen-2.5-7B base+it, etc.) and VLMs (e.g., Vicuna→Llava).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: elicit privacy-violating content / cause the model to disclose or reason over sensitive info inappropriately.
- Adversary knowledge: not formally specified; the "threat" is the model's own tendency to leak or violate privacy (memorization, weak privacy awareness), and users posing privacy-probing prompts.
- Adversary access: prompt-level (privacy-related discriminative/generative/reasoning queries).
- Adversary capabilities: contextual-integrity-style probing (Confaide), safety benchmarks (SALAD-Privacy).
- Attacker budget: prompt-level; no explicit adversarial-optimization attacker.
- Phase: inference.
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted enhancement of privacy behavior.
- Digital / physical: digital (text + vision-language).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive; no adversary adapts to the steering intervention.

## Trust assumptions
Defender controls model activations at inference. A curated privacy dataset (Solove taxonomy) and (optionally) LoRA fine-tuning on a few samples are available. Steering does not alter base parameters, preserving general capability. Same-source base/aligned models and their VLM descendants share representation geometry (Platonic-representation hypothesis).

## Attack or failure mechanism
Generative models memorize and can leak sensitive info; they conflate "privacy-related" with "privacy-violating," lacking fine-grained privacy awareness. External-constraint defenses (data scrubbing, DP, unlearning) are costly and treat symptoms.

## Proposed defense or method
(1) Build a **Solove-taxonomy privacy dataset** (4789 privacy-related items: 3771 violating, 1018 non-violating; 16 privacy types across 4 categories) via self-instruct with GPT-4o, GPT-4o/Claude-3.5 cross-check (90% acc), and human validation (Kappa 0.82). (2) **KNOW**: verify linear separability of violating vs. non-violating representations via PCA + linear probe across layers (middle/late layers most separable). (3) **DO**: compute the "mass-mean" steering vector pv_l = centroid(non-violating) − centroid(violating) at layer l; intervene one layer at a time (α grid-searched). Variants: SV from the model itself, from a LoRA fine-tuned model (ft_pv), from a same-source model (ss_pv), and from a VLM's base LLM (l2v_pv) — demonstrating transferability.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Privacy: Solove-Judge (discriminative acc/F1), SALAD-Privacy (generative, MD-Judge safety rate), Confaide tier-2a/2b (reasoning, lower=better). Utility: MMLU, AI2-ARC, TruthfulQA (mc1/mc2), ToxiGen via lm-harness; perplexity on LAMBADA (threshold <8 = acceptable).

## Evaluation methodology
Apply per-layer steering to 12 LLMs; report privacy-task gains and general-capability retention + perplexity. Test cross-model and cross-modal (LLM→VLM) transfer of the steering vector.

## Metrics
Solove-Judge acc/F1 (↑), SALAD-Privacy safety rate (↑), Confaide 2a/2b scores (↓ better), MMLU/ARC/TruthfulQA (↑), LAMBADA PPL (↓, <8). Author claim: consistent privacy improvement with maintained utility across models and successful LLM→VLM transfer.

## Main findings
- (Author claim) Privacy-violating vs. non-violating representations are linearly separable (high linear-probe accuracy in middle/late layers).
- Mass-mean steering improves privacy discrimination/generation/reasoning on most of the 12 models while largely preserving MMLU/ARC/TruthfulQA and keeping PPL near baseline (though some models show PPL increases, e.g., Mistral base 2.92→4.54).
- Privacy-enhancing directions transfer between same-source base/aligned models and from LLMs to their VLMs.
- Few-sample LoRA fine-tuning can yield strong steering vectors.

## Negative results
Gains are not universal: some models show negligible or negative privacy movement and utility/PPL degradation on specific rows (e.g., certain LLaMA-2 configs show mixed Confaide/utility shifts; Mistral-7B base PPL rises notably). (Author-reported in results table.)

## Additional limitations identified during review

## Design implications
Privacy can be treated as a linearly-steerable concept in representation space; an inference-time, training-free steering control is a lightweight complement to data-level defenses. Distinguishing "privacy-violating" from "privacy-related-but-benign" enables less over-blocking.

## Implementation implications
Compute mass-mean vectors from a labeled contrastive set; intervene at middle/late layers with a tuned α; reuse vectors across same-source and cross-modal models to amortize cost. Watch perplexity as an over-steering guardrail.

## Evaluation implications
Pair privacy-behavior benchmarks with utility + perplexity to detect over-protection; steering claims should report per-model variance, since gains are uneven.

## Deployment implications
Applicable as an inference-time privacy guardrail for chat LLMs and multimodal assistants, including agent front-ends. Frame as "improved privacy-aware behavior on evaluated benchmarks," not as leakage prevention or a security guarantee.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Because steering can help or hurt depending on model/layer/α, monitor output quality (PPL/utility) and privacy behavior jointly post-deployment; a mis-tuned vector is a regression risk.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to open-weight generative LLMs/VLMs where activations are accessible and a contrastive privacy dataset exists. Does NOT provide formal privacy guarantees, does NOT defend against extraction/jailbreak attacks, and cross-modal transfer is limited to same-source lineages. Adjacent to agent security (output-level privacy behavior).
