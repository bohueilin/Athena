## Research question
How can federated LLM fine-tuning support targeted unlearning — selectively removing a client's private data contribution post-training — to satisfy regulatory "right to be forgotten" (GDPR) while preserving model utility, given the added complexity of distributed data silos and interdependent aggregation?

## Problem definition
FedLLM frameworks enable collaborative fine-tuning without raw-data sharing but lack built-in mechanisms to remove specific client contributions after training. Federated unlearning is harder than centralized unlearning due to distributed silos, privacy constraints, and interdependent aggregation. Goals: (1) Effectiveness — remove a client's data influence; (2) Robustness — preserve utility on retained data; (3) Lightweight — minimal compute/parameter overhead.

## System or model being studied
Oblivionis: a framework unifying federated fine-tuning (FedFT) and federated targeted unlearning (FedTU) as a dual-objective optimization. Uses LoRA for parameter-efficient updates. Integrates 6 FL algorithms (Weighted-Averaging: FedAvg, FedAvgM, FedProx; Adaptive-Optimization: FedAdagrad, FedAdam, FedYogi) and 5 unlearning algorithms (Finetune baseline; Gradient-based: GradAscent, GradDiff; Preference-optimization: NPO, SimNPO; plus Retrain reference). Alternates FedFT and FedTU per round via an indicator I_unlearn(t).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: not an attack paper — the "threat" is retention of data whose removal has been legally/ethically requested (privacy leakage, membership recoverability, biased/low-quality knowledge persistence).
- Adversary knowledge / access / capabilities: not modeled; no explicit attacker. Unlearning efficacy is measured by proxy forgetting metrics, not by an executed membership-inference adversary.
- Attacker budget: not stated in paper.
- Phase: training / post-training (unlearning during and after federated fine-tuning).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted removal of a specified forget set D_u^forget.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive (no adversary in the loop).

## Trust assumptions
Honest FL server and clients (no Byzantine/poisoning adversary considered). Clients issue legitimate unlearning requests; the server applies the unlearning operator U. LoRA-only updates are assumed sufficient to carry and remove contributions. Benchmark forgetting metrics (FTR, ROUGE, NVM/NKM) are assumed to faithfully reflect removal.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Failure being remedied: the global model inadvertently learns low-quality, biased, outdated, or privacy-sensitive knowledge from specific clients during federated fine-tuning, and standard FedLLM offers no principled way to remove a client's contribution when it opts out or a deletion request arrives.

## Proposed defense or method
Dual optimization: min over φ of L_FedFT plus, when an unlearning request is present, min over φ_unlearn of L_FedTU (which minimizes the influence of D_u^forget while preserving retained-data performance). Server checks per round for unlearning requests; if present it activates U, updates global LoRA parameters, and broadcasts; clients then resume fine-tuning on the retained data. Supports pluggable F (aggregation) and U (unlearning) operators.

## Datasets and benchmarks
TOFU (subsets: Forget, Retain, Real Authors, World Facts) and MUSE (News, Books). Both under the Split99 setting (1% Forget / 99% Retain). Base models: Llama-2-7b-hf, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct. Fine-tuning: full-parameter and LoRA.

## Evaluation methodology
Cross-algorithm comparison (6 FL × 5 unlearning) against a local-training baseline and a Retrain reference. Metrics grouped into Memorization, Privacy, and Utility. Comparisons on TOFU (MU, FTR) and MUSE (NVM, NKM, UP).

## Metrics
Model Utility (MU ↑), Forget Truth Ratio (FTR ↑), No Verbatim Memorization (NVM), No Knowledge Memorization (NKM), Utility Preserved (UP), Forget ROUGE (ROUGE-L recall on forget set), Forget Probability. All stated to follow official benchmark definitions.

## Main findings
- Federated methods deliver average model utility 27.43% higher than the best local-training baseline (author claim).
- Oblivionis achieves a robust balance between forgetting efficacy and utility across unlearning methods; local training fails to balance the two.
- FedAdagrad paired with SimNPO is highlighted as achieving high forgetting efficacy.
- Cross-algorithm results provide comparative directions (e.g., preference-optimization unlearning vs gradient-based) for future FedLLM development.

## Negative results
GradAscent-style methods drive Forget-set truth/probability near zero (strong forgetting) but at utility cost in some configurations; the tables show tension between MU and FTR across methods. No configuration dominates on all axes.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Not explicitly enumerated in the extracted text beyond framing; the contribution is positioned as the first FL+unlearning integration and a consolidated benchmark, with future-research value emphasized.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- Unlearning efficacy is measured by proxy forgetting metrics (FTR, ROUGE, NVM/NKM), not by an executed membership-inference or extraction adversary — so "removal" is not adversarially verified (contrast with A40047, which shows unlearned models retain detectable imprints).
- No Byzantine/poisoning/collusion threat model: an honest FL setting is assumed; malicious clients or servers are out of scope.
- LoRA-only adaptation may leave residual influence in frozen base weights; the framework does not claim exact unlearning (Retrain is the reference, not the guarantee).
- Split99 (1% forget) is a favorable, small-forget regime; large-scale or correlated forget requests may behave differently.
- Privacy is a governance objective here; there is no formal privacy guarantee (no DP, no certificate of removal).

## Design implications
Treat unlearning as a first-class, per-round governance operation co-designed with aggregation, not a bolt-on. Provide a pluggable F/U interface so operators can swap aggregation and unlearning strategies. Data-deletion capability is an audit/compliance requirement ("right to be forgotten") that autonomy-governance stacks should expose and log.

## Implementation implications
LoRA-based FedTU keeps unlearning lightweight; alternate FedFT/FedTU with an activation indicator per round. Pair adaptive-optimization FL (e.g., FedAdagrad) with preference-optimization unlearning (SimNPO) as a strong default per the paper's results.

## Evaluation implications
Report multiple forgetting metrics and a Retrain reference; but for privacy-grade claims, verify removal with an actual inference attack rather than proxy metrics alone. Cross-algorithm matrices (not single-method reports) give more actionable guidance.

## Deployment implications
Applicable to enterprise/medical/financial FedLLM fine-tuning needing GDPR/CCPA deletion compliance. Produces an auditable unlearning event on client request; residual-risk (approximate, not exact removal) must be documented.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Emit an audit record per unlearning request (which client, which forget set, when U was applied). Periodically re-test forgotten data with inference probes to detect residual imprints and trigger re-unlearning or rollback.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to honest federated LLM fine-tuning with LoRA and legitimate deletion requests. Does NOT cover adversarial/Byzantine FL, does NOT provide a formal removal guarantee, and does NOT defend against an attacker probing the unlearned model (that adversary is exactly A40047's contribution). Results are on TOFU/MUSE small-forget regimes.
