## Research question
How can 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) surface reconstruction recover both semi-transparent surfaces and the objects occluded behind them, given that mainstream depth-extraction methods assume a single visible surface (one depth) per pixel/ray?

## Problem definition
Under semi-transparency, multiple surfaces are visible along one ray, so single-value depth statistics (expected depth or median depth) fall *between* the true surfaces, causing depth ambiguity and reconstruction failure. Additionally, TSDF fusion using only outermost depths leaves internal objects (e.g., an item sealed in a plastic bag) unreconstructable because they exhibit no depth from any viewpoint.

## System or model being studied
TSPE-GS (Transparent Surface Probabilistic Extraction for Gaussian Splatting): a training-free, drop-in depth-extraction module for 3DGS pipelines. It uniformly samples transmittance T′ to build the full per-pixel visibility-depth distribution (via kernel density estimation), models it as a (uni- or multi-)modal distribution whose local maxima correspond to physical surface depths ("unified surface depth hypothesis"), detects peaks, and fuses peak depths with a progressive TSDF strategy to recover separate outer and inner surfaces.

## Trust assumptions
Not applicable. Method assumes the base 3DGS model already fits the scene geometry adequately; if the backbone fails to model geometry, TSPE-GS degrades.

## Attack or failure mechanism
The "failure" is a modeling failure, not an attack: single-peak depth statistics cannot represent multi-layer (semi-transparent) surfaces, and outermost-depth TSDF fusion discards occluded interior geometry.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a defense. A probabilistic depth-extraction + progressive TSDF-fusion algorithm (see System above). Key derivations relate transmittance T to a CDF over depth, define a PDF, and prove that averaging per-pixel depth sequences across transmittance thresholds preserves multi-peak structure, enabling efficient peak detection.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- Semi-transparent: αSurf dataset and a custom self-collected dataset with objects occluded by semi-transparent surfaces.
- Opaque / general: BMVS (BlendedMVS) and DTU.
- Backbones: RaDeGS (best), plus other 3DGS and NeRF-based reconstruction pipelines.

## Evaluation methodology
Compare reconstruction quality across NeRF-based and 3DGS pipelines on semi-transparent datasets, and on opaque datasets to test generalization (no degradation). Sampling of all viewpoints reportedly completes in ~1–2 minutes owing to fast 3DGS rendering.

## Metrics
Standard geometry-reconstruction accuracy/completeness metrics (e.g., Chamfer-distance-style accuracy on DTU/BMVS/αSurf, per Tables 1–3 referenced). No privacy/security metric.

## Main findings
- TSPE-GS achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction on semi-transparent datasets with RaDeGS as backbone while maintaining accuracy on opaque scenes (author claim).
- The method is training-free, adds negligible overhead, and generalizes across Gaussian-based pipelines.
- Multi-peak depth distributions reliably correspond to visible surfaces empirically.

## Negative results
- Performance depends on the base 3DGS method; if the backbone under-models geometry, TSPE-GS deteriorates (author-stated).
