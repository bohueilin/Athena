## Research question
Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) embed sensitive information throughout multi-step chain-of-thought (CoT), not just in the final answer. Can an inference-time, parameter-free unlearning method suppress sensitive content across the *entire reasoning trajectory* and remain robust across different decoding strategies (DefaultThink / ZeroThink / LessThink)?

## Problem definition
Answer-level unlearning (gradient ascent, preference optimization, KL) leaves sensitive knowledge in intermediate reasoning steps → persistent leakage. R-TOFU extended unlearning to CoT but still shows insufficient forgetting, utility loss, and leakage under alternative decodings. Need a decoding-robust, trajectory-level suppression mechanism that preserves utility without weight updates.

## System or model being studied
STaR (Sensitive Trajectory Regulation): a parameter-free, inference-time pipeline with four modules — Sensitive Content Identification, Secure Prompt Prefix, Trajectory-Aware Suppression Learning, Token-level Adaptive Filtering. Backbone: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, fine-tuned on R-TOFU before unlearning.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: recover sensitive/forgotten information (PII, copyrighted content) from the model's answers or reasoning chain, including via alternative decoding.
- Adversary knowledge: gray/black-box; can choose decoding strategy (DefaultThink/ZeroThink/LessThink) and run membership-inference at answer and reasoning-chain granularity.
- Adversary access: query the deployed model; observe CoT and/or answer; vary decoding.
- Adversary capabilities: Multi-Granularity Membership Inference Attack (MIA); decoding-strategy switching to bypass default-mode unlearning; paraphrase-based elicitation.
- Attacker budget: query-level; no weight access required.
- Phase: inference / deployment.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted (specific forgotten profiles/facts in R-TOFU).
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: partially adaptive — evaluation explicitly varies decoding strategy and uses MIA at multiple granularities (decoding-aware adversary).

## Trust assumptions
Defender controls the inference pipeline (can prepend prompts, inspect intermediate CoT steps, and manipulate token logits at each step) but does not update weights. A designated forget set D_f is available for retrieval of forbidden phrases. A scope classifier and semantic embedding function are trusted components.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Sensitive content is distributed across CoT steps; answer-level interventions miss it. Alternative decodings (ZeroThink omits reasoning; LessThink shortens it) can expose forgotten info even after successful default-mode unlearning. Fine-tuning-based unlearning also risks catastrophic forgetting and heavy compute.

## Proposed defense or method
Four-stage inference-time pipeline:
1. **Sensitive Content Identification** — a scope classifier C on semantic embeddings scores whether query x is in-scope (p_f > τ); if so, semantic retrieval finds the most similar forgotten instance A*, from which phrase extraction (NER/pattern/LLM slot-filling) builds a forbidden token set T_f (lexical + semantic coverage vs paraphrase leakage).
2. **Secure Prompt Prefix** — prepend a global, model-agnostic safety instruction s to x (x′ = s∥x); a weak, non-intrusive input-level constraint.
3. **Trajectory-Aware Suppression Learning** — high-level controller inspects each generated reasoning step for fluency and residual sensitivity; escalates responsible tokens to stricter filtering or replaces output with a safe refusal template.
4. **Token-level Adaptive Filtering** — per-step logit intervention: *soft suppression* (subtract α·cosine-similarity for tokens semantically near a forbidden fragment) and *hard suppression* (set logit to −∞ for tokens completing a forbidden span). Decoding-agnostic by construction.
Also introduces two evaluation metrics: **Multi-Decoding Consistency Score (MCS)** and **Multi-Granularity MIA** (answer- and reasoning-chain-level).

## Datasets and benchmarks
R-TOFU (Yoon, Jeung & No 2025): 200 synthetic author profiles × 20 question–reasoning–answer triples, with CoT + answer and annotated sensitive content; protocols forget01/05/10 (remove 1%/5%/10%). Baselines: Gradient Ascent (GA), KL Minimization, DPO, Gradient Difference (GD), with official code/hyperparameters. Single backbone: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B.

## Evaluation methodology
Follow official R-TOFU splits; compare STaR vs. four fine-tuning baselines on forgetting efficacy, utility retention, and privacy under multiple decodings; use new MCS (consistency across DefaultThink/ZeroThink/LessThink) and Multi-Granularity MIA (answer vs chain).

## Metrics
Forgetting efficacy, model utility (retain-set), MCS (decoding consistency), Multi-Granularity MIA (privacy at answer and chain levels). Author claim: STaR achieves comprehensive, stable unlearning with minimal utility loss and outperforms baselines in forgetting, robustness, and privacy.

## Main findings
- (Author claim) Answer-level unlearning leaves chain-level leakage; STaR suppresses sensitive content across the whole trajectory and across decoding strategies without parameter updates.
- New metrics (MCS, multi-granularity MIA) reveal hidden privacy risks that answer-level metrics miss.
- STaR reports better forgetting + utility trade-off and decoding robustness than GA/KL/DPO/GD on R-TOFU.

## Negative results
Existing methods (incl. R-TOFU's CoT extension) show insufficient chain-level forgetting and vulnerability under ZeroThink/LessThink — the motivating negative results. (Author-reported.)

## Limitations stated by the authors
Parameter-free methods relying on external detectors/auxiliary optimization have limited effectiveness in complex reasoning/diverse queries (noted for prior work; STaR mitigates via dynamic stepwise control). Secure Prompt Prefix is explicitly a "weak" constraint. (Full limitations truncated.)

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Single backbone (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) and a single synthetic benchmark (R-TOFU, fictitious authors) — generalization to real PII, other LRMs, and larger models is untested here.
- Suppression depends on a scope classifier + forbidden-token set derived from the forget set; queries not flagged in-scope, or novel paraphrases outside T_f's semantic radius (δ), can bypass filtering.
- Hard/soft logit suppression can degrade fluency/utility; the fluency check mitigates but does not eliminate this.
- MIA is the main privacy attack; no adaptive attacker that knows STaR's filtering rules is evaluated.
- Being inference-time only, the underlying weights still contain the knowledge — a white-box weight extractor (cf. RTT in A40343) is out of scope and would defeat it.

## Design implications
For reasoning agents, privacy suppression must operate at the *trajectory* level and be *decoding-agnostic* — protecting the final answer is insufficient when intermediate CoT is observable. Combine coarse (prompt prefix) and fine (per-step logit) controls.

## Implementation implications
Needs access to per-step logits and the ability to interrupt/inspect CoT steps (white-box or logit-exposing serving). Forbidden-token sets must cover lexical + semantic paraphrases; a fluency check prevents suppression from collapsing output.

## Evaluation implications
Adopt decoding-robust evaluation (MCS across DefaultThink/ZeroThink/LessThink) and multi-granularity MIA (answer + chain) as standard for LRM unlearning; answer-only metrics overstate protection.

## Deployment implications
Applicable as a runtime privacy guardrail for deployed reasoning models exposing CoT. Because it is inference-time and weights retain the data, frame as "reduced leakage across evaluated decodings," not deletion; pair with weight-level unlearning if right-to-be-forgotten deletion is required.

## Monitoring and incident implications
The trajectory controller is itself a runtime monitor (per-step sensitivity + fluency); log escalations/refusals as telemetry. Watch for scope-classifier misses (false negatives) as leakage incidents and for over-suppression as utility incidents.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to LRMs where the operator controls inference and can manipulate logits and inspect CoT, defending against query-time/decoding-varying observers. Does NOT protect against white-box weight extraction, does NOT delete knowledge from parameters, and is validated on one model + one synthetic benchmark. Core-adjacent to agent security (reasoning-trace privacy at runtime).
