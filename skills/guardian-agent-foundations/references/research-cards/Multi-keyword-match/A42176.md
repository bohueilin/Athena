## Research question
How can machine learning be formally studied and improved under practically important constraints — adversarial robustness, data unlearnability (privacy/consent), and individual fairness — treating these as constrained-optimisation problems rather than post-hoc metric pushing?

## Problem definition
Standard ML optimises only for accuracy/F1 and remains vulnerable to adversarial attacks, fairness violations, and data leakage/unauthorised training use. The thesis reframes each concern as an explicit constraint on the learning problem and seeks fundamental/theoretical understanding plus algorithms with formal guarantees.

## System or model being studied
A program of works: (1) certified/probabilistic adversarial robustness with Bayes-error upper bounds; (2) a certified-yet-accurate probabilistic-robust training + runtime-testing method (defends against strong attacks incl. AutoAttack); (3) RobFace, a system-agnostic transferable-adversarial test suite for face-recognition robustness evaluation; (4) provably unlearnable examples via Bayes-error maximisation (projected gradient ascent) that survive mixing with clean data; (5) certified individual-fairness training via provably fair initialisation + fairness-preserving training using randomised-response mechanisms.

## Threat model
Multiple threat settings across sub-works (abstract-level):
- Adversarial robustness: adversary crafts perturbations (Lp-vicinity) to cause misclassification; attacks include AutoAttack; face-recognition adversary attacks an authentication system (targeted, digital, physical-adjacent).
- Unlearnable examples: adversary/unauthorised party scrapes user data to train models without consent — the "defender" is the data owner protecting against unauthorised model adaptation; the perturbation is applied to training data.
- Fairness: not an external adversary; the "constraint violator" is the learning algorithm producing unfair outcomes across sensitive attributes.
- Adversary knowledge/access/budget: perturbation-bounded (κ tolerance for probabilistic robustness); specifics in cited works, not fully in abstract.
- Phase: training (certified training, unlearnable-example construction, fair training) and inference (runtime robustness testing, RobFace evaluation).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: robustness work evaluates against strong (AutoAttack) adversaries; adaptivity of the unlearnability defense to mixing is explicitly considered.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes bounded-perturbation adversary models and known sensitive attributes for fairness. Bayes-error analysis assumes characterisable data-distribution uncertainty. Randomised-response fairness assumes the mechanism's guarantees hold throughout training.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Adversarial examples exploit decision-boundary sensitivity; the thesis shows a fundamental Bayes-error-driven ceiling on certified robust accuracy (cannot reach 1). Unlearnable-example failure of prior methods: unlearnability disappears when perturbed data is mixed with clean data. Fairness failure: standard training violates individual fairness (individuals differing only in sensitive attributes get different outcomes).

## Proposed defense or method
- Bayes-error robustness bounds (deterministic and probabilistic; probabilistic bound increases monotonically with tolerance κ).
- Synergistic certified probabilistic-robust training + runtime robustness-testing inference (author claims best certification rate + accuracy vs baselines, incl. defence against AutoAttack).
- RobFace: optimised, system-agnostic adversarial-face test suite for scalable robustness estimation.
- Provably unlearnable examples via Bayes-error maximisation (PGA), robust to clean-data mixing.
- Certified individual fairness: provably fair initialisation + fairness-preserving training with randomised response (formal guarantee sustained through training).

## Datasets and benchmarks

## Evaluation methodology
Theoretical bounds with empirical corroboration on real datasets (per cited works); comparison against certified-training baselines and strong attacks (AutoAttack); RobFace validated against system-specific empirical and formal analyses. No new consolidated experiment in this abstract.

## Metrics
Certified robust accuracy: analysis estimates a CIFAR-10 upper bound ~67.49%; existing methods rose from 53.89% (2017) to 62.84% (2023) and stalled (author-cited numbers). Probabilistic-robust accuracy increases with κ but is bounded below 1. Fairness/unlearnability metrics referenced qualitatively (formal guarantees; Bayes-error increase); specific numbers in cited works.

## Main findings
- Certified robust accuracy is fundamentally bounded (<1) due to Bayes error; probabilistic robustness is also bounded but can get closer to 1 than deterministic, with a monotonic κ relationship.
- A combined training+testing method achieves higher certification rate and accuracy than prior certified training, including against AutoAttack (author claim).
- Unlearnable examples can be made provably effective and robust to clean-data mixing via Bayes-error maximisation.
- Individual fairness can be formally guaranteed throughout training via fair initialisation + randomised-response-based training.

## Negative results
Deterministic robustness cannot reach accuracy 1 and always costs accuracy; certified-training progress has plateaued (62.84% since 2023) below the estimated bound. Prior unlearnable-example methods fail under clean-data mixing (the gap this thesis addresses).

## Limitations stated by the authors
Each problem is studied under a single constraint; jointly satisfying multiple constraints (e.g., robustness + individual fairness together) is future work. Robustness inherently trades off with accuracy.

## Design implications
For agent/ML security: certified-robustness ceilings (Bayes error) temper expectations that perturbation-robustness can be "solved"; probabilistic robustness with an explicit tolerance κ is a more attainable design target. Data-owner-side unlearnable examples are a proactive control against unauthorised training/scraping. Randomised-response fairness offers a privacy-flavoured mechanism (sensitive-attribute protection) with formal guarantees.

## Implementation implications
Combining certified training with runtime robustness testing suggests a "train-time guarantee + inference-time verification" pattern reusable for gating uncertain inputs. Unlearnable-example generation via PGA/Bayes-error is implementable as a dataset-protection preprocessing step.

## Evaluation implications
Robustness evaluation should account for fundamental Bayes-error limits and use strong/adaptive attacks (AutoAttack) and, for face recognition, system-agnostic transferable test suites (RobFace) rather than single-attack success rates or Lipschitz-only proxies.

## Deployment implications
Face-recognition authentication is called out as a high-stakes robustness case (identity/authorization asset). Deploying certified defenses means accepting an accuracy cost bounded by data uncertainty. Unlearnable examples and randomised-response fairness are deployable data-governance/training-time controls.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Runtime robustness-testing at inference is itself a monitoring hook (flag inputs failing robustness checks). Otherwise limited monitoring guidance in this abstract.

## Applicability boundaries
Adjacent to agent security via classical ML-security constraints (adversarial robustness, training-data protection/unlearnability, privacy-flavoured fairness), transferable to AI systems but NOT specific to LLM/agent/tool/prompt threats. As a thesis-summary abstract, it should not be treated as primary experimental evidence; verify each method in its cited paper before operational reliance.
