## Research question
Can a source-trained slum-segmentation model generalize to unseen regions (African cities) without labeled target data, by combining a region-aware Mixture-of-Experts backbone with test-time adaptation that filters unreliable pseudo-labels via cross-expert prediction consistency?

## Problem definition
Informal-settlement ("slum") morphology varies widely across geographies, so models trained on some cities fail to transfer. Labeled target data is scarce. The goal is label-efficient, cross-regional semantic segmentation of slums from very-high-resolution satellite imagery for urban-poverty estimation and planning.

## System or model being studied
GRAM (Generalized Region-Aware Mixture-of-Experts): a two-phase framework. Phase 1 trains a transformer-encoder segmentation model with lightweight MoE adapter blocks, per-region noisy top-k gating, mutual-information regularization and an auxiliary region classifier to force expert specialization. Phase 2 (test-time adaptation) routes each unlabeled target image through the most similar source region's expert to produce a pseudo-label, scores reliability by cross-region prediction consistency (mIoU "stability score"), and fine-tunes on the high-stability subset. Backbone lineage: ST++ semi-supervised segmentation, SegFormer-style encoder.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes source labels (Atlas of Informality + city datasets, expert-drawn binary masks) are trustworthy; assumes ESRI World Imagery is authentic and not manipulated; assumes pseudo-label consistency across experts is a valid reliability signal.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Failure mode addressed is distributional: single models overfit source-city morphology and mis-segment unseen regions; naive pseudo-labeling propagates errors and causes catastrophic forgetting. No adversarial or security failure is studied.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a defense. The method is a domain-generalization / TTA technique: MoE specialization + MI regularization to prevent expert mode-collapse, and cross-expert consistency filtering to reject unreliable pseudo-labels before adaptation.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- New source dataset: 12 cities on four continents, 2,714,489 256×256 VHR tiles (~1.2 m/px, zoom 16), 86,752 with ground-truth masks; semi-supervised ST++ used to generate pseudo-labels for the rest.
- Held-out target/evaluation: Dar es Salaam, Kampala, Maputo — 529,633 tiles, 17,536 manually annotated.
- Public release of manual + GRAM-derived masks across 15 cities.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- Dual-use / ethical risk: high-resolution mapping of vulnerable informal settlements is sensitive; outputs could be misused for surveillance, eviction, or targeting of marginalized populations. The paper does not analyze this misuse surface or access controls on the released maps.
- Label provenance and "slum" definitional bias could encode socio-political bias into a globally released model.
- No adversarial-robustness evaluation: imagery could be spoofed/poisoned, and the model has no defense; not in scope but relevant if deployed in policy decisions.
- Pseudo-label reliability rests on cross-expert agreement, which can be jointly wrong for systematically unusual target morphologies.
