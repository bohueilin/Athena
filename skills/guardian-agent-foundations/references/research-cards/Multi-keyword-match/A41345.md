## Research question
How can machine-learning systems — including foundation models — be made reliable and safe in the open world, treating reliability (not accuracy alone) as a first-class objective under distribution shift, with minimal human supervision?

## Problem definition
Modern ML/foundation models fail under distribution shift: overconfident mispredictions, hallucinated generations, and susceptibility to adversarial/malicious prompts. The talk frames reliability as an algorithmic principle to optimize alongside accuracy, spanning OOD detection, learning from unlabeled in-the-wild data, and reliable foundation models.

## System or model being studied
A survey of the author's frameworks across three threads (each a separately published system): 
1. Unknown-aware learning via outlier synthesis — VOS, NPOS, Dream-OOD (shape the energy landscape around in-distribution data to reduce OOD overconfidence). 
2. Learning in the wild with unlabeled data — SAL (gradient-based spectral separation of outliers), SCONE (semantic + covariate shift via constrained optimization). 
3. Reliable foundation models — HaloScope (hallucination detection via subspace separation on LLM representations), TSV (LLM latent steering for hallucination detection), VLMGuard (detecting malicious prompts in vision-language models via unlabeled data), and a data-centric AI-alignment paradigm via source-aware feedback cleaning.

## Design implications
Supports a defense-in-depth stance for agent/LLM systems: pair OOD/uncertainty awareness, hallucination detection, and malicious-prompt filtering as distinct guardrail layers. Latent-steering and representation-subspace detection are candidate runtime monitors where model internals are accessible.

## Implementation implications
Unlabeled-data-driven detectors (VLMGuard, HaloScope) suggest low-annotation paths to guardrails; latent steering (TSV) suggests intervention hooks at inference. Requires white-box access — a constraint for hosted-model deployments.
