## Research question
How can we build a stable, transparent, reproducible, human-effort-free evaluation framework that both measures LLMs' realistic capability and quantifies the extent to which reported benchmark performance is overestimated due to contamination or biased overtraining?

## Problem definition
Public-benchmark overestimation has two abuse types: (1) data contamination — test cases leak into training, inflating scores; (2) biased overtraining — models claim comprehensiveness but over-optimize the evaluated domain at the expense of others. Existing mitigations trade off among reproducibility, transparency, and efficiency (private third-party benchmarks lack transparency; symbolic/domain benchmarks are narrow; none quantify the overestimation magnitude).

## System or model being studied
ArxivRoll, a dynamic evaluation framework analogized to one-time-pad encryption: private benchmarks are used once, then published (marked "expired") for reproducibility and thereafter treated as public. Two components: (1) SCP (Sequencing, Cloze, Prediction) — an automated generator of private, objective, multiple-choice test cases from recent ArXiv papers (fresh → conceptually unseen by LLMs); (2) Rugged Scores (RS) — metrics quantifying contamination and biased overtraining. A new benchmark (ArxivRollBench) is constructed roughly every six months.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: inflate a model's apparent capability by memorizing/contaminating public benchmarks (intentional "cheating") or by biased overtraining toward evaluated domains.
- Adversary knowledge: white/gray access to public benchmark content (test items, formatting) that can be incorporated during training/fine-tuning.
- Adversary access: control over a model's training/fine-tuning data (model developer / provider).
- Adversary capabilities: include test samples or benchmark-specific formatting in training; post-processing fine-tuning (cited: a 13B Llama reaching GPT-4-comparable MMLU via post-processing).
- Attacker budget: not quantified; the abuse can be intentional or unintentional.
- Phase: training / pre-training / fine-tuning (contamination injected upstream), detected at evaluation.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted at specific public benchmarks.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: the defense (freshness via one-time private benchmarks) is designed to resist adaptation across evaluation periods; individual model developers are assumed non-adaptive to the not-yet-released private set.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes recent ArXiv papers are genuinely unseen by evaluated LLMs at test time (confidentiality/freshness); assumes SCP tasks are objective (minimizing subjective grading); assumes the private benchmark, once released and "expired," is not reused. Trusts ArXiv content quality and the generation pipeline's filtering (drops formula/table-heavy text).

## Attack or failure mechanism
Evaluation leakage: test samples, benchmark details, or formatting are exploited (memorized) to game public benchmarks, inflating perceived performance and producing unfair comparisons. Biased overtraining distorts capability profiles across domains. RS operationalizes detection: a large public-vs-private performance gap within a domain indicates contamination ("ruggedness"); variance across private domain benchmarks indicates biased overtraining.

## Proposed defense or method
- SCP three objective tasks over ArXiv fragments: Sequencing (order shuffled sentences), Cloze (select sentences for masked gaps), Prediction (pick correct continuation among distractors). Symbolic/multiple-choice formatting for objectivity.
- Rugged Score RS_I: normalized signed public-vs-private performance discrepancy across matched domain pairs (contamination proxy); can use absolute (model-independent) or rank-based (benchmark-independent) metric M.
- RS_II: standard deviation of a model's performance across private domain benchmarks (biased-overtraining proxy), with normalized variant RS_II^N.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Public benchmarks referenced (MMLU, GSM8K, LiveBench, Chatbot Arena, SWE-bench, etc.) contrasted against generated private ArxivRollBench sets sourced from ArXiv domains (e.g., physics, mathematics, q-fin, CS/CoRR), tagged by period (e.g., 2024b). Meta-evaluation on ArxivRollBench2024b-CS.

## Evaluation methodology
Meta-evaluation: (1) stability — regenerate ArxivRollBench 32 times with different seeds on the same raw articles and measure evaluation-result variance for Llama3-8B; (2) validity — correlate ArxivRollBench outcomes (Sequencing/Cloze/Prediction) with an existing private/non-transparent benchmark (Chatbot Arena) via Spearman/Pearson/Kendall; (3) systematic overestimation analysis and a public leaderboard of popular LLMs.

## Metrics
Accuracy on SCP tasks; standard deviation across regenerations (reported Std ≈ 0.0074–0.0095, indicating stable generation); correlation coefficients (e.g., A.R.Bench(S)–ChatbotArena Spearman 0.76 / Pearson 0.71; A.R.Bench internal S–C/S–P/C–P Spearman 0.86); Rugged Scores RS_I, RS_II, RS_II^N.

## Main findings
- SCP generates stable test samples (low variance across 32 regenerations) — randomness in generation does not materially destabilize evaluation.
- ArxivRollBench correlates with an established private benchmark (Chatbot Arena), supporting validity/reliability of the private benchmarks.
- RS provides the first quantification (author claim) of the proportion of overestimation (contamination) and biased overtraining across evaluated LLMs; a systematic leaderboard is produced.

## Negative results
- RS_I is acknowledged as not fully normalized and coupled to the specific model set and benchmark triple; only one of {model-set, benchmark-triple} can be decoupled at a time (absolute metric → model-independent; relative metric → benchmark-independent). Cross-configuration RS_I values are not directly comparable.

## Limitations stated by the authors
- RS_I comparability limitation (above).
- SCP filters out math/formula/table-heavy text, restricting coverage of some content types.
- Freshness relies on ArXiv recency; once published, private benchmarks expire and cannot be reused.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- "Conceptually unseen" assumes models are not trained on very recent ArXiv; models with continual/near-real-time ingestion could contaminate even fresh sets — the freshness guarantee is temporal, not cryptographic (the one-time-pad analogy is conceptual, not a security proof).
- SCP tasks (ordering/cloze/next-sentence) measure discourse/coherence understanding, which may not represent reasoning or task-solving capability targeted by benchmarks like GSM8K/SWE-bench; construct validity beyond correlation with Chatbot Arena is limited.
- Correlations are moderate (Cloze–Arena Pearson 0.51), so RS-based conclusions for some task types are weaker.
- Distractor quality in Prediction and possible position/format shortcuts could let models game SCP itself; adversarial robustness of SCP is not evaluated.
- ArXiv-domain skew and English-centric content bound comprehensiveness claims.

## Design implications
Evaluation integrity is a first-class security property: capability numbers are evidence that can be gamed. Freshness + one-time use + post-hoc publication is a reusable pattern for contamination-resistant benchmarking. A public-vs-private gap (RS_I) is a practical contamination signal; cross-domain variance (RS_II) exposes biased overtraining.

## Implementation implications
Trustworthy-AI pipelines can adopt periodic private-benchmark regeneration and dual public/private scoring to flag contaminated or narrowly-overtrained models before promotion. Objective, auto-gradable formats (SCP) reduce human-effort and subjectivity in continuous evaluation.

## Evaluation implications
Report both realistic performance and an overestimation/ruggedness score; treat single public-benchmark leaderboards as potentially inflated. Use absolute vs. rank metrics deliberately given RS_I's comparability constraints.

## Deployment implications
Procurement/gating of models should weight contamination-adjusted (rugged) scores, not raw public-benchmark rankings, to avoid deploying overestimated models. Expired benchmarks must be retired from future scoring to preserve the guarantee.

## Monitoring and incident implications
RS trends over evaluation periods can serve as an audit/telemetry signal for benchmark gaming and training-data contamination; sudden public/private divergence is an incident indicator warranting training-data provenance review.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to detecting/quantifying benchmark contamination and biased overtraining for LLMs via discourse-level objective tasks. It is ADJACENT to agent security: it protects evaluation_artifacts and audit integrity (verifier/benchmark gaming), not runtime agent/tool/prompt execution. Do NOT treat RS as a capability ground truth or the freshness property as a cryptographic guarantee; SCP validity beyond coherence understanding is bounded.
