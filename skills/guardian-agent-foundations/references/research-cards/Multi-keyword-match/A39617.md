## Research question
How can graph-transformer models for fMRI brain-network disease prediction be made more interpretable and semantically informed by (1) injecting neuroscience knowledge-graph features into brain-region node representations and (2) using a causal graph over brain regions to guide (mask) the transformer's self-attention?

## Problem definition
Transformer-based brain-network analysis is a black box (lacks interpretability) and ignores disease-related semantic associations among brain regions of interest (ROIs), relying only on statistical connectivity from BOLD signals.

## System or model being studied
BrainCKT, a plug-and-play module for graph-transformer brain-network models. Three parts: (1) **Knowledge-driven brain augmentation** — an LLM extracts (subject, relation, object) triples from neuroscience/clinical literature and databases to build a brain knowledge graph (237 entities, 23 relations, 5056 triples), encoded by a pretrained KGE model (TransE/CompGCN/RGCN) and concatenated onto node features; (2) **SEM-based causal graph construction** — an offline linear structural-equation-model procedure yields a sparse binary DAG adjacency over ROIs; (3) **Causal-aware transformer** — the DAG is used as a hard {0,−∞} attention mask in multi-head self-attention. Integrated into BrainNETTF, Com-BrainTF, GBT, ALTER baselines.

## Trust assumptions
The LLM used for triple extraction is trusted to produce accurate neuroscience triples; the constructed knowledge graph and causal DAG are treated as ground-truth priors. No adversarial or data-integrity concern is raised.

## Attack or failure mechanism
None. The addressed failure is *interpretability/semantic* deficiency of black-box brain transformers, not a security failure.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a security method. Interpretability/accuracy technique: KG-augmented features + causal-DAG-masked attention.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Two brain-imaging datasets: ABIDE (autism) and OSF. Baselines span GNN-based (BrainGNN, BrainGB, BrainNetCNN, FBNETGNN), graph-transformer (Graphormer, TC-BrainTF, BrainNETTF, Com-BrainTF, GBT, ALTER), each with and without BrainCKT.

## Evaluation methodology
Graph-level (subject-level) disease classification; ACC/AUC/SEN/SPE reported (Table 1); ablations over knowledge vs. causal augmentation; sensitivity to KGE model (TransE vs CompGCN vs RGCN) and to three LLM prompt variants; interpretability via attention/adjacency visualization.

## Metrics
Accuracy (ACC), AUC, sensitivity (SEN), specificity (SPE). Author-reported example gains: AUC improvements when adding BrainCKT to ALTER of +0.82%/+6.45% (causal) and to BrainNETTF of +1.21%/+6.31% (knowledge) on ABIDE/OSF respectively. No security metric.

## Main findings
- Author claim: BrainCKT consistently improves ACC/AUC of four transformer baselines on both datasets and provides interpretable, biology-aligned evidence via causal-masked attention.
- Author claim: performance is robust to KGE-encoder choice (CompGCN ≈ RGCN, both > TransE) and to prompt variation (prompt2's stricter rules slightly reduce recall/performance).

## Negative results
- Author-noted: the stricter prompt2 slightly lowers performance (higher-quality triples but reduced recall). This is the only reported negative/trade-off.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Not explicitly enumerated in the extracted text; the causal graph is built offline and fixed, and relation definitions are manual (23 relations) — stated as design choices rather than limitations.

## Design implications
For agent security: mainly a data-provenance lesson. When an LLM auto-constructs a knowledge/causal artifact that then steers a downstream model's attention, that artifact becomes a trust dependency; provenance, validation, and integrity of LLM-generated structured knowledge should be treated as security-relevant even in benign ML pipelines.

## Implementation implications
If an agent system uses LLM-extracted triples to gate/mask model behavior, add triple-source attestation and human/clinical validation; do not treat LLM output as ground truth.
