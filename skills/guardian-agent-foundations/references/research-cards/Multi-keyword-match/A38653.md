## Research question
How can knowledge-graph embedding models perform accurate numerical reasoning — inferring facts that depend on numerical attribute values and ordinal relations (e.g., "longer", "heavier") — by jointly encoding entities, relations, and numerical attributes and by distinguishing subtle ordinal differences?

## Problem definition
Given a knowledge graph of triples (h, r, t) plus an entity-numeric-value matrix X, learn embeddings and a scoring function for knowledge-graph completion that respects numerical ordering. Two challenges: (C1) incomplete semantic integration of entities/relations/numeric attributes; (C2) ordinal indistinguishability of close values, worsened by hard negatives with near-equal values.

## System or model being studied
NumCoKE: (1) Mixture-of-Experts Knowledge-Aware (MoEKA) encoder — relation-aware expert routing (with tunable Gaussian noise and relation-aware temperature), knowledge-perceptual multi-head attention fusing entity/relation/attribute embeddings, and handling of missing numeric values via learnable missing-value embeddings; (2) Ordinal Knowledge Contrastive Learning (OKCL) — synthesizes ordinal-structured positive/negative samples by cosine similarity over value distributions, top-k ordinal neighbors, and mixup-style blending; a TransE+Order-Embedding-based rationality score function. This is a KG-reasoning representation model, not a security system.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes clean, benign knowledge graphs with (possibly incomplete) numeric attributes. No adversary, poisoning, or provenance/trust assumptions. Author affiliations are cyber-security labs, but the paper's content is not about security.

## Attack or failure mechanism
No attack. Failure modes addressed are benign: models treating numeric attributes uniformly regardless of relational context (semantic incompleteness), and coarse representations that cannot separate near-equal values (ordinal confusion), especially with hard negatives.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a defense. MoEKA integrates numeric semantics with relational context; OKCL generates ordinal-aware contrastive samples. A theoretical analysis argues the OKCL objective (with EMA) is equivalent to maximizing mutual information between representation and one-hop neighborhood pattern.

## Datasets and benchmarks
