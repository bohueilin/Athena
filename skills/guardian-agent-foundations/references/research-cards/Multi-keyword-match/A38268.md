## Research question
Can a static scanned handwritten-signature image be given verifiable, document-bound authentication attributes — enforcing
a "One Signature, One Use" policy — by invisibly embedding authentication metadata as a robust generative watermark that
survives digital distortions and print-scan?

## Problem definition
Static scanned signatures are convenient but have "almost lost their authentication attributes": they can be copied and
reused across documents with no post-hoc authorship attribution, enabling forgery and dispute. Dynamic (pressure) and
PKI-based signatures decouple authentication from the visual signature and need special hardware/certificates. Existing
image/document watermarks fail on signatures because meaningful pixels occupy only ~14% (strokes), pushing watermarks into
background regions and causing visible artifacts / poor robustness.

## System or model being studied
AuthSig: a generative signature-watermarking framework. Roles (Fig. 1): Bob pre-registers n signature samples; on Alice's
authorization request Bob encodes metadata (document, signatory, expiration) into a watermark bitstream, fuses it into
signature *style* features, and generates a one-time-use watermarked signature; at verification the watermark is extracted
and its metadata queried for document-binding/expiration checks. Mallory's reuse of a signature on a mismatched document
fails verification. Built on: keypoint/skeleton (Zhang-Suen + RDP) + Thin-Plate-Spline augmentation; supervised contrastive
learning for signer-invariant content features; a latent diffusion model (DDPM/DDIM, fine-tuned VAE) conditioned on content
+ style; gated-residual watermark embedder E_W and STN-based extractor D_W.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: illicitly reuse/copy a genuine static signature image to forge/authorize documents it was not
  intended for.
- Adversary knowledge: not formally parameterized; the reuse attacker is assumed to possess a captured signature image.
  Verification assumes a *known claimed identity* (signer prior Fs0 available), which the paper leverages.
- Adversary access: possession of a scanned/generated signature image; ability to paste it into other documents and apply
  common transformations (compression, resizing, print-scan).
- Adversary capabilities: image reuse, digital-domain distortions, signature-specific degradations, print-scan.
- Attacker budget: not stated as a bound.
- Phase: deployment/verification-time (usage control after issuance).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted reuse/forgery of a specific identity's signature.
- Digital / physical: both (digital documents plus print-scan physical channel).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: distortion robustness is tested (a distortion layer during training), but an *adaptive*
  watermark-removal/forgery adversary who knows AuthSig and tries to strip/overwrite the mark is not explicitly evaluated
  (reviewer note).

## Trust assumptions
Trusts the issuer (Bob) and the verification service holding metadata; assumes a signer-specific reference style Fs0 is
available at verification (identity is claimed/known). Assumes the metadata store binding watermark→document is authentic
and available. Security rests on watermark robustness + metadata binding, not on cryptographic signatures.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Unauthorized reuse/forgery of a static signature. AuthSig defeats naive reuse by binding each generated signature to
specific document metadata and a one-time policy: a reused image carries a watermark whose metadata will not match the new
document (or is expired), so verification fails. Robustness challenge: keep the watermark extractable after distortions
without visible artifacts on sparse-stroke images.

## Proposed defense or method
Generative implicit watermarking: (1) keypoint-driven TPS augmentation to expand scarce signature data while preserving
stroke topology; (2) decouple content (Ec, SupCon) and style (Es) features; (3) fuse watermark bits into style features via
learned gated residual (Fs' = Fs0 + g·Proj(w)), condition a latent diffusion model to regenerate a watermarked signature;
(4) extractor uses STN geometric alignment + MLP, with a signer-specific prior Fs0 concatenated, to recover bits; distortion
layer improves robustness. Domain-specific VAE fine-tuning preserves stroke detail.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Handwritten-signature datasets GPDS-150 / GPDS-Aug, CEDAR, and IAM (offline handwriting) referenced for VAE fine-tuning and
evaluation. Baselines: HiDDeN, WAM (Watermark Anything), LGDR, CIN.

## Evaluation methodology
Watermark extraction accuracy under digital-domain distortions, signature-specific degradations, and print-scan; visual
quality vs. baselines (HiDDeN, WAM, LGDR, CIN); ablations on content-only vs style-only vs joint reconstruction, and
pre-trained vs fine-tuned VAE (PSNR/SSIM/rHWD/rFID reported per dataset).

## Metrics
Watermark bit-extraction accuracy (author claim: >98% under both digital distortions and signature-specific degradations,
effective even print-scan); image fidelity PSNR/SSIM and handwriting-specific rHWD↓/rFID↓ (e.g., fine-tuned VAE improves
GPDS-150 to PSNR 44.37 / SSIM 0.9970 / rHWD 0.0344 / rFID 1.63 vs pre-trained 32.54 / 0.9878 / 0.1446 / 5.76).

## Main findings
Author claims: AuthSig equips static signatures with verifiability and document-binding while preserving natural
handwriting appearance; achieves >98% extraction accuracy across distortion types including print-scan; style-feature
embedding avoids background artifacts that afflict pixel/high-frequency watermarks (HiDDeN hue shift, WAM edge artifacts);
joint content+style is necessary for faithful reconstruction; domain-specific VAE fine-tuning materially improves fidelity.

## Negative results
Single-feature (content-only or style-only) models fail to reconstruct meaningful signatures (severe semantic distortion);
pre-trained VAE loses stroke detail (motivating fine-tuning). Exact watermark-capacity ceiling flagged as future work.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Future work targets increasing watermark capacity and further enhancing visual quality (implying current
capacity/fidelity limits).

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- No adaptive/forgery adversary: robustness is to distortions, not to an attacker who knows AuthSig and attempts watermark
  removal, re-embedding, or generating a plausible forged signature+metadata; forgery-resistance vs. skilled attackers is untested.
- Security depends on an authentic metadata store and honest issuer; compromise of that service (or of Fs0) is out of scope.
- No cryptographic binding — "One Signature, One Use" relies on watermark+metadata matching, not on a
  cryptographically verifiable signature; collision/replay analysis is not reported.
- Verification requires the claimed signer's reference style Fs0; behavior under identity spoofing / wrong-prior extraction
  is not deeply analyzed.
- Evaluated on standard signature datasets; real-world workflow integration and human-factors are not studied.

## Design implications
Directly relevant to *identity/provenance* security: binds authentication metadata to a visual artifact and enforces
single-use/document-binding — a pattern transferable to agent-identity and content-provenance systems (e.g., binding a
credential/authorization context to an issued artifact so replay across contexts fails). Embedding into semantic *style*
features (not high-frequency pixels) is the key to imperceptibility on sparse content.

## Implementation implications
One-time-use issuance requires a metadata backend for binding/expiration queries; robustness needs a distortion layer in
training; a signer-specific prior at verification simplifies extraction (identity is known) — analogous to
context-scoped verification in access systems.

## Evaluation implications
Watermark/provenance schemes should be evaluated against *adaptive* removal/forgery adversaries, not only benign
distortions; capacity, collision, and replay resistance should be reported alongside extraction accuracy.

## Deployment implications
Suited to low-to-medium-risk paperless approval workflows wanting reuse-resistant, verifiable signatures without special
hardware; high-assurance use should still layer cryptographic (PKI) signing, since AuthSig provides watermark-level, not
cryptographic, guarantees.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Extracted watermark + bound metadata provide an audit/attribution trail (evidence-logging) for detecting and attributing
unauthorized reuse — a usable forensic signal for incident response, contingent on a trustworthy metadata store.

## Applicability boundaries
Scope: static handwritten-signature images in paperless document workflows. Do NOT generalize to natural-image watermarking
(the method is tuned to sparse-stroke glyphs), to cryptographic non-repudiation guarantees, or to settings without a
trusted metadata/issuer. Relevance to agent security is adjacent — identity/authentication, watermarking, and provenance
concepts transfer to agent-identity and content-authenticity, but the artifact itself concerns human signatures.
