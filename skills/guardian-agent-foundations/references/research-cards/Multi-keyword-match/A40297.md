## Research question
Can a text-provenance detector reliably identify machine-revised text (human-authored content later polished/expanded/rewritten by an LLM) and fully machine-generated text in the black-box setting (unknown generator), including under adversarial multi-task revision prompts designed to evade detection?

## Problem definition
Prior zero-shot detectors (DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT) exploit that machine text has higher log-likelihood; they degrade sharply when machine style is attenuated by revision, or when advanced/unknown LLMs are used. ImBD aligns a scoring model toward machine style (Style Preference Optimization) but overfits to one model's style and fails under diverse adversarial revision prompts. HLPD instead aligns the scoring model toward human writing style so it is sensitive to deviations introduced by any machine reviser.

## System or model being studied
HLPD: a scoring model trained via Human Language Preference Optimization (HLPO) — a DPO-inspired preference objective making the model assign higher log-prob to human text than to its machine-revised counterpart, with a linear contrastive loss (sigmoid removed) and an adaptive Dynamic-β variance-aware scheduler. Detection uses HLP-CPC (Human Language Preference Conditional Probability Curvature), a Fast-DetectGPT-style curvature metric with sign reversed for the human-aligned model, thresholded by ε.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: evade AI-text detection — pass LLM-revised or fully machine-generated text as human-written (provenance concealment).
- Adversary knowledge: for the detection task, black-box (the generating/revising LLM is unknown to the detector). The paper also constructs an adaptive attacker who uses HLPD's trained scoring model to attack the commercial detector GPTZero.
- Adversary access: ability to prompt state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-o3, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-R1, Gemini-2.5Pro, Grok-3, Claude-3.5, etc.) with adversarial revision instructions.
- Adversary capabilities: five-dimensional adversarial prompt generator (core revision goal, target style, human-like/evasion instructions, operational constraints, auxiliary requests) producing 750 unique prompts; multi-task revision (polish, expand, rewrite) plus full generation.
- Attacker budget: prompt-level; no model access to the detector required for the base evasion task.
- Phase: inference/deployment (detection at test time).
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted evasion of the human/machine decision.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: both — a static adversarial-prompt benchmark, plus a demonstrated adaptive attack transferring HLPD's scorer against GPTZero.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes access to a curated set of paired (human, machine-revised) texts for HLPO training and a perturbation model qϕ for curvature estimation. Assumes human writing has stable, distinctive stylistic regularities that persist as a detectable anchor even when machine revision is minimal.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Detection failure of prior methods arises because revision attenuates machine-style log-likelihood cues, and machine-style-aligned scorers (ImBD/SPO) generalize poorly to unseen advanced models under diverse prompts. HLPD reframes the signal: aligning to human style widens the curvature gap between candidate text and perturbations for genuinely human text, making machine revisions detectable as negative-curvature deviations.

## Proposed defense or method
HLPD (defense = detector). HLPO training + HLP-CPC scoring + threshold. Adaptive-β scheduler stabilizes small-sample preference tuning. The trained scorer is also shown to be reusable offensively (adaptive attack on GPTZero), highlighting dual-use.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Training: ImBD's human source texts, each revised by GPT-3.5-Turbo (random one of expand/polish/rewrite/generate). Testing human sources: XSum (news), SQuAD (QA), WritingPrompts (stories), PubMedQA (biomedical QA), WikiText (encyclopedic). Adversarial multi-task dataset built with a five-dimensional prompt generator via DeepSeek-R1 (750 prompts). Source models span GPT-3.5-Turbo, Qwen2-7B, Llama-3-8B, Mixtral/Mistral-7B, Deepseek-7B and advanced GPT-o3, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-R1, Gemini-2.5Pro, Grok-3, Claude-3.5.

## Evaluation methodology
Compared against 5 training-based baselines (ReMoDetect, Ghostbuster, RoBERTa-base/large, commercial GPTZero) and 9 logit-based baselines (ImBD, Likelihood, LogRank, Entropy, LRR, NPR, DNA-GPT, DetectGPT, Fast-DetectGPT). Key experiments repeated 5× with different seeds; ROC/AUROC reported; ablations on loss functions; multi-language evaluation.

## Metrics
AUROC (primary), accuracy, TPR/FPR ROC curves; relative-improvement percentages over baselines.

## Main findings
(Author claims.) On GPT-series single-task revision, HLPD gains 15.11% relative AUROC over ImBD, +45.56% over Fast-DetectGPT, +38.40% over RoBERTa-large. On adversarial multi-task revision, HLPD attains highest average AUROC (+13.52% over ImBD, +24.54% over GPTZero). On texts fully generated by advanced LLMs, HLPD is highest average AUROC (+5.53% over ImBD, +34.14% over Fast-DetectGPT). Robust across languages and revision tasks in black-box settings.

## Negative results
Baseline logit methods can invert on revised text (reported AUROCs below 0.5, e.g. LRR 0.35, DNA-GPT 0.40 on some sources), underscoring that revision breaks likelihood-based detection — HLPD is presented as the remedy, but this also shows detection is brittle in general.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Not enumerated as a dedicated limitations section in the visible text; the paper acknowledges each LLM has unique unstable stylistic patterns (motivating the human-anchor approach) and notes the scorer's dual-use potential (adaptive attack on GPTZero).

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_ Detection is inherently a moving target: results are against the authors' own adversarial-prompt suite, not against attackers adapting specifically to HLPD (no white-box adaptive attack against HLPD itself is reported). Reliance on a human-style anchor may not transfer across domains/languages beyond those tested, or to future models trained to imitate human idiosyncrasies. Threshold ε calibration and its FPR cost in deployment are not deeply characterized. No public code link in the text; reproducibility depends on the appendix. Dual-use: the same technique strengthens evasion.

## Design implications
AI-text provenance detectors are useful but should be treated as probabilistic advisories, not authoritative attribution. Anchoring to the "human style" distribution is more robust than anchoring to a specific machine style when the generator is unknown — relevant for detecting machine-revised content in agent/LLM output pipelines.

## Implementation implications
Deploying such a detector needs threshold calibration to a tolerable false-positive rate (mislabeling human authors is a real harm), multi-domain validation, and awareness that the scorer can be repurposed by adversaries to tune evasions.

## Evaluation implications
Detector evaluation must include adversarial multi-task revision and advanced/unknown generators; single-task, single-model benchmarks overstate robustness. Report seeds/variance and full ROC, not just accuracy at one operating point.

## Deployment implications
For provenance/forensics in high-stakes domains (legal, medical) the paper motivates detecting even minor AI revision; but the arms-race nature (adaptive attack on GPTZero) means deployed detectors will need continual retraining and should not be used as sole gatekeepers.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Provenance detectors can serve as monitoring signals for machine-generated/revised content in content pipelines, but flags should be corroborated (metadata, watermarks, human review) given non-trivial error rates and adversarial evasion.

## Applicability boundaries
Relevant to text-provenance / AI-generated-text detection and to output-attribution defenses. It is adjacent (not core) to agent/tool/prompt security: it does not address prompt injection, tool abuse, or agent runtime control. Do NOT generalize the AUROC gains to attackers who adapt directly against HLPD, to modalities beyond text, or to domains/languages outside the tested set.

## Evidence strength
Moderate — strong empirical breadth (many baselines, multiple datasets/models, 5 seeds, full ROC) and a realistic black-box + adversarial-prompt threat model, but no white-box adaptive attack against HLPD itself, unconfirmed code release, and the intrinsic non-durability of detection make the security guarantee limited.

## Confidence notes
High confidence in the reported detection-accuracy improvements under the evaluated conditions. Lower confidence that these gains persist against attackers who optimize specifically against HLPD or as generator models continue to advance.
