## Research question
How can domain-specific social/compliance risks of deployed LLMs (bias amplification, misinformation, privacy violations) — which emerge dynamically from human-LLM interaction rather than from isolated harmful outputs — be systematically, automatically, and reproducibly quantified across domains and interaction modalities?

## Problem definition
Existing risk assessments (bias/toxicity benchmarks, red-teaming) have three gaps: they treat harm as static/isolated categories rather than as an emergent property of dynamic human-LLM interaction; they are insufficiently automated/scalable; and they lack domain sensitivity (all contexts treated uniformly despite distinct risk profiles/regulations). Goal: a decomposed, simulation-based, closed-loop risk-evaluation framework.

## System or model being studied
HEV Generative Sandbox — a multi-agent, closed-loop simulation. Risk is factorized (HEV / a FAIR-style Loss-Event-Frequency model) as LEF = E_C[CF(s) × PoA(p,r|s) × Vuln(h|s,p,r)]: Hazard (H, domain-specific threat types via a Hazard List), Exposure (E, via Contact Frequency CF of domain-rooted scenarios), Vulnerability (V, conditional probability the model emits harmful output). Four coordinated agents: Scenario Agent (domain-rooted scenario generation → CF/Exposure), User Agent (four personas: Security-Baseline, Standard, Perturbation-Injecting, Adversarial — the last uses prompt injection/jailbreak → Probability-of-Action), Auditor Agent (LLM-as-judge hazard analysis → Vulnerability, binary harm label), and the Target LLM under evaluation. Domain-rooted scenario generation samples contextual seeds from domain corpora.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: elicit harmful/prohibited content (misinformation, bias, privacy violation, unsafe advice) from the target LLM — modeled as one of several simulated user personas, culminating in an Adversarial User that bypasses safety mechanisms.
- Adversary knowledge: black-box interaction with the target LLM via prompts.
- Adversary access: prompt-level query access; the Adversarial User employs prompt injection and jailbreaking techniques.
- Adversary capabilities: persona-driven prompt generation across a spectrum from benign to perturbation-injecting to adversarial; scenarios are domain-conditioned.
- Attacker budget: prompt-level; automated at scale via the User Agent.
- Phase: evaluation-time / deployment (pre-deployment safety auditing of a target model).
- Targeted / untargeted: both — targeted hazard categories per domain and general adversarial provocation.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: the User Agent adapts prompts by persona, and the loop is closed (contextual feedback); however the Auditor (LLM-as-judge) is itself acknowledged (via cited JudgeDeceiver / Raina et al.) to be attackable — not defended here.

## Trust assumptions
Trusts the Auditor Agent (LLM-as-judge) to reliably label harm, and trusts domain corpora + the Hazard List to represent real domain risks. Test questions are generated by an external model (DeepSeek API) and manually screened. Assumes the factorized causal model (CF × PoA × Vuln) adequately captures how risk propagates.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Harm is modeled as a causal chain: a domain-specific user submits a scenario-conditioned query that probabilistically triggers the LLM's hazard-related errors (CF → PoA → Vuln → Risk). Rather than treating adversarial testing as an external red-team exercise, HEV folds adversarial prompting (jailbreak/prompt injection by the Adversarial User) into the same probabilistic structure, letting it measure not only whether a model fails but how failure modes emerge through iterative feedback.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a runtime defense — a risk-assessment / automated red-teaming framework. It quantifies risk (LEF and its factors), attributes it to Hazard/Exposure/Vulnerability for targeted mitigation, and provides scalable closed-loop auditing to inform "responsible deployment."

## Datasets and benchmarks
Self-generated test data: DeepSeek API used to generate 1000 questions per domain across six domains (daily dialogue, encyclopedic knowledge, news, medicine, law, education), manually screened. Uses a curated Hazard List and domain sub-corpora as scenario seeds. Related benchmarks referenced (HELM, DecodingTrust, SafetyBench, BBQ, etc.) as context, not primary test sets.

## Evaluation methodology
Closed-loop multi-agent simulation across six domains and multiple target models and user personas; aggregate risk computed from the HEV factorization; comparison of risk across domains and across user-persona conditions (secure baseline vs. adversarial). Auditor Agent produces binary harm labels aggregated into loss magnitude.

## Metrics
Loss Event Frequency (LEF) and its factors — Contact Frequency (CF/Exposure), Probability of Action (PoA), Vulnerability (Vuln, binary harm indicator aggregated); relative risk ratios across domains and personas.

## Main findings
(Author claims.) Current LLMs show pronounced domain-specific vulnerabilities: legal applications exhibit 2.97× higher risk exposure than encyclopedic-knowledge tasks (Domain Sensitivity), and adversarial user interactions raise aggregate risk 4.3× versus secure-baseline users (Adversarial Volatility). The HEV factorization enables attributing risk to hazard type, domain context, and user/model vulnerability, supporting targeted mitigation.

## Negative results
Implicitly, safety mechanisms do not neutralize risk uniformly: even the Security-Baseline persona (all guardrails on) leaves residual vulnerabilities, and risk varies sharply by domain — indicating uniform safety tuning is insufficient. No claim that any target model is safe.

## Limitations stated by the authors
The paper positions prior methods as fragmented/domain-specific and its own as a unification; it does not foreground a large limitations section in the extracted text. It implicitly relies on LLM-as-judge auditing whose robustness it acknowledges is contested (citing JudgeDeceiver, Raina et al.).

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_ (1) The Auditor Agent (LLM-as-judge) is the measurement instrument and is itself attackable/biased (the paper cites this literature but does not validate the auditor's accuracy or defend it), so absolute risk numbers (2.97×, 4.3×) are instrument-dependent. (2) Test data is model-generated (DeepSeek) then screened, risking generator bias and coverage gaps. (3) The HEV/FAIR-style factorization is a modeling choice presented as causal; the causal validity (independence/conditional structure) is asserted, not empirically identified. (4) Binary harm labeling loses severity gradation. (5) Domain and persona coverage is finite; "responsible deployment" conclusions extrapolate beyond the six tested domains.

## Design implications
For agent-safety evaluation, model risk as an emergent, domain-conditioned, interaction-dependent quantity, not a static harmful-output rate. Separating Hazard/Exposure/Vulnerability supports targeted mitigation (e.g. tighten controls where CF/Exposure is high, such as legal). Adversarial users should be first-class simulated agents in the evaluation loop.

## Implementation implications
Automated, closed-loop, persona-driven simulation can scale safety auditing beyond manual red-teaming; but the LLM-as-judge auditor must be validated/hardened (it is both the scorer and a known attack target). Domain-rooted scenario generation requires curated per-domain corpora and hazard taxonomies.

## Evaluation implications
Safety benchmarks should stratify by domain and by user persona (baseline→adversarial) and report relative volatility, not a single aggregate. Because the auditor is attackable, risk figures should be reported with auditor-reliability caveats and, ideally, human-validation subsets.

## Deployment implications
Supports pre-deployment, domain-aware risk assessment for high-stakes verticals (medical, legal, education) where regulatory/compliance risk differs. Findings argue against one-size-fits-all safety tuning; deployment gating should be domain-specific.

## Monitoring and incident implications
The closed-loop, agentized structure and per-interaction harm labels provide evidence-logging and runtime-monitoring patterns; the Auditor Agent is a template for a continuous safety monitor — with the caveat that such a monitor is itself an attack surface (LLM-as-judge manipulation).

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to LLM social-risk evaluation and automated red-teaming across domains/personas. Adjacent (not core) to agent/tool/prompt security: it evaluates output-level social risk and adversarial-user provocation, not tool-invocation, credential, or agent-runtime enforcement. Do NOT treat the absolute risk ratios as ground truth independent of the LLM-judge auditor, nor generalize beyond the six tested domains.

## Evidence strength
Preliminary-to-Moderate — a coherent, reproducible (code-released) framework with a principled risk factorization and illustrative empirical findings (domain sensitivity, adversarial volatility), but the risk measurements depend on an unvalidated, attackable LLM-as-judge auditor and model-generated test data, and the causal factorization is asserted rather than empirically identified, so the quantitative claims are instrument-dependent.

## Confidence notes
High confidence in the framework/methodology contribution and the qualitative findings (domain- and persona-dependent risk). Lower confidence in the precise risk multipliers, which hinge on auditor reliability that the paper acknowledges is contested but does not measure.
