## Research question
How can RL fine-tuning of LLMs (GRPO-style) be stabilized and made more sample-efficient when sampled completion groups "collapse" (all-correct or all-incorrect, yielding zero-variance rewards), and when scalar final-answer rewards ignore reasoning quality and over-penalize length?

## Problem definition
Three challenges in GRPO/DAPO-style RL for reasoning LLMs: (1) collapsed groups produce no gradient signal; DAPO's dynamic sampling can fail under sample starvation (early training / hard tasks with sparse correct completions); (2) reward functions score only final-answer correctness, conflating valid-reasoning-but-wrong-answer with entirely wrong outputs; (3) rigid length penalties penalize verbose-but-informative outputs while tolerating long-but-repetitive ones (semantic redundancy ignored).

## System or model being studied
TAPO, an RL framework extending GRPO with three interventions on sampled completions plus an advantage-level intervention:
- Dynamic Teacher Injection (DTI): when C=0 or C=G, inject a positive teacher sample (from ground truth) or a negative sample (from an unrelated query) with a fixed advantage a_teacher, repairing collapsed groups without discarding them.
- Perturbed Answer Injection (PAI): when 0<C<G and a gate s~U(0,1) < λ, take a correct completion, flip its final answer while keeping the reasoning path, assign fixed a_perturbed — providing contrastive supervision separating reasoning correctness from answer correctness.
- InfoLen-Aware Reward Shaping: penalize outputs by both length and semantic redundancy (not length alone).
- Advantage-Level Intervention (ALI): assign fixed advantages to injected samples without altering group reward statistics, preserving normalization stability.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes access to ground-truth solutions for teacher injection and an is_equivalent(·) answer checker to count C (completions matching reference answer); assumes a reference policy π_ref for KL regularization; assumes rule-based/verifiable reward for math tasks.

## Attack or failure mechanism
The "failure modes" are optimization pathologies, not attacks: zero-variance collapsed groups, coarse reward granularity, and length-only penalties that can be gamed by repetitive long outputs. Reviewer note: the latter is a specification-gaming/reward-hacking pattern — models can inflate reward by verbosity/redundancy under length-only shaping; InfoLen targets this.

## Proposed defense or method
Not a security defense. TAPO's contribution is training-signal engineering: full group utilization via DTI, process-sensitive supervision via PAI, redundancy-aware reward via InfoLen, and statistically stable advantage assignment via ALI. All operate without increasing model size or dataset scale.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Mathematical reasoning benchmarks including AIME-2024 (and others referenced: AMC/MATH-style; full list partly truncated). Backbone: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B. Comparators: Naive GRPO, DAPO, CPPO, Still-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct.

## Evaluation methodology
Compare TAPO against GRPO baseline and other 1.5B/7B models on math benchmarks; component-wise ablations for DTI, PAI, InfoLen, and ALI vs. reward-level intervention (RLI); training-dynamics/convergence curves.

## Metrics
Accuracy (average across benchmarks) and per-benchmark accuracy; training stability/convergence. Reported: average accuracy 62.87% vs. Naive GRPO 34.3%; AIME-2024 improved by 15.4 points over base model and 10 points over Naive GRPO (author claims). ALI reported to give smoother/more stable training than RLI.

## Main findings
- TAPO substantially raises math-reasoning accuracy over the GRPO baseline and matches/exceeds larger comparators on the evaluated benchmarks.
- Repairing collapsed groups (DTI) restores gradient signal and accelerates convergence vs. dynamic-sampling baselines.
- Advantage-level (vs. reward-level) intervention preserves group normalization and stabilizes training.
- Redundancy-aware length shaping (InfoLen) yields slightly improved accuracy and more stable training than length-only penalties.

## Negative results
- Reward-Level Intervention (RLI) inflates variance and misaligns advantage distributions — presented as motivation for ALI (an internal negative result favoring the proposed design).

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Fixed advantage values (a_teacher, a_perturbed) and the PAI gate threshold λ are hyperparameters whose sensitivity/robustness are not fully characterized in extracted text.
- PAI deliberately injects "valid reasoning + wrong answer" trajectories; the safety consequence of training a model on reasoning-correct-but-answer-wrong signal (potential to reward confident-but-wrong reasoning) is not analyzed from a trustworthiness standpoint.
- Evaluated only on math reasoning with verifiable answers; transfer to open-ended/non-verifiable tasks (where reward hacking risk is higher) is untested.
- No evaluation of whether InfoLen's redundancy penalty can itself be gamed (e.g., diverse-but-vacuous tokens).
- Backbone is a single 1.5B distilled model; scaling behavior and interaction with alignment/safety objectives are not studied.

## Design implications
Relevant to reward-hacking / specification-gaming understanding: length-only reward shaping is gameable via redundancy; reward shaping should account for semantic informativeness. Decoupling injected-sample advantages from group reward statistics (ALI) is a reusable pattern for stable, controllable training-signal edits — analogous to keeping "intervention" signals out of the trusted reward-normalization path.

## Implementation implications
Teams building RL fine-tuning pipelines can adopt group-repair (DTI) to avoid sample starvation and advantage-level (not reward-level) edits to preserve normalization integrity. Any injection of synthetic/contrastive trajectories should be auditable to avoid unintended behavior shaping.

## Evaluation implications
For trustworthy-AI evaluation, reward-shaping methods should be tested for new gaming vectors they introduce (e.g., PAI's confident-wrong reasoning, InfoLen's redundancy proxy) — not only for accuracy gains.

## Deployment implications
This is a training-time method; deployment security implications are indirect. Models trained with answer-flipping contrastive signals should be re-evaluated on calibration and honesty (does the model become better at reasoning, or better at appearing to reason?).

## Monitoring and incident implications
Minimal direct monitoring relevance. Training-time telemetry (reward variance, advantage distribution alignment, group-collapse rate) is useful for detecting unstable/exploitable RL dynamics.
