## Research question
(1) How can effective adversarial attacks be designed for Text-Attributed Federated Graph Learning (TAG-FGL) that
exploit *both* structural (graph topology) and textual (node description) modalities? (2) How can generalizable,
inference-time, modality-aware defenses be built for the multimodal, semantics-aware TAG-FGL setting?

## Problem definition
TAG-FGL places Text-Attributed Graphs (nodes/edges with text) into a federated setting where GraphLLMs combine GNN
structural encoders with frozen LLMs via token prompts/adapters. The decentralized paradigm lets malicious clients
attack the model without the server's awareness, and the tightly-entangled text+structure inputs expand the attack
surface and defeat traditional single-modality FL defenses (DP, crypto, anomaly detection).

## System or model being studied
GraphLLMs in a federated graph-learning pipeline: a graph encoder `f_G` produces node embeddings that are concatenated
with tokenized text into an extended prompt `Z_{i,k}`; a frozen language model `Φ_θ` autoregressively predicts labels.
Backbones referenced in the bibliography include ChatGLM/GLM-4, Llama 3, and Mistral 7B (exact experimental set not
visible in extracted text).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: degrade classification/prediction performance (induce misclassification / label flips) at
  inference time.
- Adversary knowledge: gray/white-box at the client — each malicious client trains a *local surrogate GCN* on its
  private graph; a static-surrogate strategy reuses fixed weights.
- Adversary access: malicious federated client(s) with access to their own local graph `G_k`, node text, and adjacency.
- Adversary capabilities: (structural) influence-guided edge flips within a per-node budget `B_{i,k}=deg(v_{i,k})`;
  (textual) synonym substitution + embedding-space gradient-guided refinement (Gaussian-smoothed gradient estimation
  with l1 sparsity), preserving surface semantics.
- Attacker budget: bounded edge-flip budget per node (degree-proportional) and l0-sparse token/embedding perturbation
  (`‖θ‖0 ≤ ρ`).
- Phase: attacks are inference-time evasion (paper states most attacks operate at inference); the defense also acts at
  local training time.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted misclassification toward the most-competitive incorrect class `c⋆`.
- Digital / physical: digital.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: transfer-oriented (surrogate-based, "modality-agnostic transferable" adversarial inputs);
  adaptive-attacker evaluation against STRUM not visible in extracted text.

## Trust assumptions
Server cannot directly inspect private client data (privacy is the reason for federation), so it must defend via
aggregation weighting rather than input inspection. Assumes some clients are honest and that per-client robustness
under local adversarial evaluation is a usable trust signal.

## Attack or failure mechanism
GTAE (Graph-Text Attack Engine), a two-stage dual-modality pipeline: Stage 1 — influence-guided topological
perturbation: greedy node-wise edge flips maximizing the logit margin gain `Δ_ij` toward `c⋆` under a degree budget,
using a local surrogate GCN. Stage 2 — treating the poisoned adjacency `Ã_k` as frozen context, embedding-driven
lexical perturbation: select content-word candidates, substitute synonyms, then continuous embedding-space
optimization (`θ = E_adv − E_orig`) with Gaussian-smoothed gradient estimation and l1 regularization to maximize the
misclassification margin while penalizing semantic drift (`β·f_sim`). Joint perturbation compounds structural and
semantic confusion and is designed to transfer across models.

## Proposed defense or method
STRUM (Structure and Text Robustness Unification Method): (1) local modality-aware adversarial training — inject
learned structure-aware perturbations into node embeddings during local training and augment inputs with
semantically-consistent adversarial text variants; (2) robustness-aware federated aggregation — weight clients higher
when they show smaller accuracy drops under local adversarial evaluation, improving global model stability.

## Design implications
Securing federated LLM-over-graph systems requires modality-aware defenses at both the input (node embedding + text)
and system (aggregation) levels; single-modality FL defenses (DP, crypto, anomaly detection) are insufficient when
text and structure are jointly perturbable and semantically plausible.

## Implementation implications
Robustness can be added without breaking privacy by combining local adversarial training with a robustness-aware
aggregation weight derived from local adversarial evaluation — but the weight function is a new trust-decision surface
that must itself be hardened against manipulation.

## Evaluation implications
TAG-FGL robustness claims should be tested against adaptive attackers, report both modalities separately and jointly,
disclose client counts / attacker fractions / non-IID partitions, and reconcile dataset counts; transferability
across backbones should be reported explicitly.

## Deployment implications
Relevant to federated, multi-tenant GraphLLM/RAG-over-graph services (healthcare, finance, social) where clients are
semi-trusted; treat client-reported robustness as an untrusted signal and combine with server-side integrity checks.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Aggregation should log per-client robustness scores and accuracy-drop distributions to detect clients gaming the
weighting; sudden shifts in structure/text distributions from a client may indicate coordinated multimodal poisoning.

## Applicability boundaries
Specific to text-attributed federated graph learning with LLM+GNN backbones. Findings should NOT be generalized to
centralized GNNs, non-text graphs, or pure-FL image/text settings without re-evaluation, and provide no formal
robustness or privacy guarantee.
