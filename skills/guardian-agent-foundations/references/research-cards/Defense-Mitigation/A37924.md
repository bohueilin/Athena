## Research question
Can an attacker craft *imperceptible* (small l2) adversarial examples that both (i) cause misclassification and
(ii) coax a probabilistic certified defense into issuing a large, deceptive robustness certificate for the wrong
class — "certificate spoofing" — including in the harder *targeted* setting?

## Problem definition
Prior work (Shadow Attack, ICLR 2020) showed certificate spoofing is possible but required *large* perturbations. This
paper asks whether region-focused, semantically-preserving perturbations can achieve spoofing with much smaller l2
norms and higher attack success, yielding "ghost certificates" (spoofed radii comparable to or exceeding the source
image's certified radius) while remaining visually imperceptible.

## System or model being studied
Probabilistic Randomized Smoothing certification (Cohen et al. 2019) and variants on ImageNet: (1) Randomized
Smoothing with ResNet50, (2) Smoothed Ensemble (consistency-trained ensemble, Horváth et al. 2022), and (3) DensePure
(diffusion-based denoiser + ViT-Large/Patch16-512 under randomized smoothing, Xiao et al. 2023), at σ = 0.25 and 0.5.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: misclassification AND a large spoofed certification radius (targeted or untargeted); if the
  smoothed model abstains, that is recorded as a Denial-of-Service (DoS) outcome.
- Adversary knowledge: white-box — full access to architecture, parameters, and the smoothing noise level σ.
- Adversary access: can query and backprop through the base model; uses PGD on noisy batches.
- Adversary capabilities: region-selection via GradCAM (CNN) or attention (ViT) fused with SAM segmentation masks to
  confine perturbations to salient, semantically-coherent regions; PGD optimization over an l2 budget.
- Attacker budget: l2 perturbation budgets ε ∈ {2, 4, 6, 8, 10}.
- Phase: inference / test-time.
- Targeted / untargeted: both evaluated (targeted is harder; one target label per source image to remove selection bias).
- Digital / physical: digital, ImageNet.
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: adaptive vs. the certification mechanism; compared against Shadow Attack and a distortion-
  bounded Shadow Attack variant.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the deployment treats a large certified radius as evidence of label correctness — the paper argues this trust
is misplaced. It explicitly notes the attack does NOT invalidate the certificate's formal assertion (the input is
still not an adversarial example within the chosen bounded norm); rather, correctness ≠ large radius.

## Attack or failure mechanism
GhostCert: (1) generate a salient-region mask `m` by combining GradCAM/attention saliency `S` with SAM segmentation
masks (each >300 px), scoring each mask by saliency overlap and summing the top-k; (2) run PGD to maximize the smoothed
loss over N noisy samples with the perturbation confined to `m` (`δ ⊙ m`). Confining perturbations to natural image
boundaries preserves semantics while shifting the input just far enough in latent space to flip the smoothed
prediction and obtain a large certified radius for the wrong (target) class.

## Datasets and benchmarks
ImageNet (large-scale). Three defended models (Single ResNet50, Smoothed Ensemble, DensePure). A user study on
imperceptibility is referenced (Fig. 7), details not fully visible in extracted text.

## Evaluation methodology
For untargeted and targeted settings, report Attack Success Rate (ASR) and average Spoofing Radius across the three
defenses, sweeping ε ∈ {2,4,6,8,10}. Compare GhostCert vs. Shadow Attack and vs. a bounded-distortion Shadow variant.
Abstentions recorded as DoS.

## Metrics
- ASR: untargeted = fraction misclassified; targeted = fraction classified as the attacker's target class *and*
  assigned a certificate radius.
- Spoofing Radius: certified radius computed for successfully-misclassified samples.
- l2 norm ‖δ‖2 of the perturbation (imperceptibility).
- DoS rate (abstentions).

## Main findings
- Author claim (visible): GhostCert maintains 30–100% success rates across perturbation budgets, while Shadow Attack
  and its bounded variant remain constrained to ~30–65%.
- Author claim (visible): GhostCert's spoofed radii are "slightly lower or comparable" to Shadow Attack but with
  consistently higher ASR and significantly lower l2 norm (more natural-looking adversarials).
- Author observation: the diffusion-denoiser-based method (DensePure) remains the most effective certification method,
  even vs. model ensembling under randomized smoothing.
- Targeted attacks succeed but are harder than untargeted, as expected.

## Negative results
Targeted spoofing is markedly harder; DensePure is the most resistant of the three defenses (attack still succeeds but
the method is the strongest). Full targeted-setting numeric tables not visible in extracted text.

## Limitations stated by the authors
The certificate's formal guarantee is not invalidated — the attack is a "cautionary tale" about interpreting a large
radius as label correctness. (Authors frame this as a scope limitation, not a flaw in certification math.)

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- White-box with knowledge of σ is a strong assumption; a deployed verifier that hides σ / rate-limits queries / uses
  fresh noise seeds would raise the bar (not evaluated).
- Evaluation is vision/ImageNet only; transfer to text/audio/agent settings is untested.
- "Imperceptibility" rests partly on a user study whose protocol/scale is not visible in the extracted text.
- No defense is proposed; the paper is diagnostic, so the practical mitigation ("use certificates cautiously") is
  advisory rather than tested.

## Design implications
Robustness certificates should be treated as a bounded-norm assurance, not a correctness oracle; safety-critical
pipelines that gate on "strongly certified" inputs need an independent correctness signal (human review, provenance,
ensemble disagreement) because a large radius can be spoofed with imperceptible, semantics-preserving perturbations.

## Implementation implications
Saliency + segmentation region-masking makes adversarial perturbations both imperceptible and certificate-efficacious;
verifiers that expose σ and are fully white-box are the most spoofable configuration.

## Evaluation implications
Certified-defense papers should report robustness against certificate-spoofing (not just clean certified accuracy),
sweep perturbation budgets, evaluate targeted spoofing and DoS/abstention rates, and include perceptual/human studies.

## Deployment implications
For "Models propose / Verifiers verify / Gates decide" designs: a certificate is a verification artifact that can be
forged in appearance; a gate keyed solely on certified radius can be deceived. Prefer denoiser-based certification
(most resistant here), hide the noise level, and add out-of-band correctness checks.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Log certificate radii together with prediction-confidence and denoiser-disagreement; sudden clusters of large-radius
certificates on near-duplicate inputs may indicate spoofing/DoS probing.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to probabilistic (randomized-smoothing-family) certified defenses in vision. Does NOT speak to deterministic/
complete verifiers, non-lp threat models, or non-vision modalities, and does not disprove the certificates' formal
soundness — only their usefulness as a correctness indicator.
