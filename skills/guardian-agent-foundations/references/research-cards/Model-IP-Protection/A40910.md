## Research question
RQ1: How effectively do LVLMs recognize and comply with copyright regulations when copyrighted content appears in the
multimodal context (user-provided or retrieved text/images)? RQ2: Do explicit copyright notices change compliance?
RQ3: How can copyright awareness/compliance be enhanced?

## Problem definition
LVLMs refuse direct requests to reproduce copyrighted works but will process the same request when the copyrighted
content is supplied as multimodal context (e.g., a book-excerpt image), even with a visible copyright notice —
enabling unauthorized reproduction (repetition, extraction, paraphrasing, translation). The risk amplifies via
multimodal RAG, web agents, and search engines that retrieve and incorporate copyrighted online content. The paper
treats redistribution of protected material as infringing and transformative use (summarize/comment) as non-infringing
(following Xu et al. 2024).

## System or model being studied
Twelve LVLMs (closed- and open-source, e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude, LLaVA, Qwen2-VL per references) evaluated on a
new benchmark; plus CopyGuard, a proposed tool-augmented defense framework (guardrail) with components: Copyright
Notice Identifier, Copyright Source Verifier, Query Risk Analyzer (reasons about infringement and rewrites risky
queries), and a Copyright Status Reminder injected to the LVLM.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: induce the LVLM to reproduce/redistribute copyrighted content it holds in context (repetition,
  extraction, paraphrase, translation), bypassing the model's direct-request refusal.
- Adversary knowledge: black-box user of the LVLM; exploits the modality/context channel rather than model internals.
- Adversary access: supplies copyrighted content + query as multimodal context (or content is retrieved by a RAG/agent
  pipeline).
- Adversary capabilities: craft queries and place copyrighted content (with/without notices, textual or embedded-in-
  image) to elicit infringing output; may also be a benign user unknowingly triggering infringement.
- Attacker budget: 50,000 multimodal query-content pairs span the space; per-query budget not a variable.
- Phase: inference/deployment (in-context prompting; RAG ingestion).
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted reproduction of specific copyrighted works.
- Digital / physical: digital (multimodal text+image).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: primarily non-adaptive w.r.t. CopyGuard — no attacker specifically evading CopyGuard is
  evaluated in extracted text.

## Trust assumptions
Treats redistribution of protected content as infringing and transformative use as permissible; assumes copyright
status can be checked via external tools/knowledge (CopyGuard's source verifier) rather than baked into model weights;
assumes a guardrail (LlamaGuard-style) analogy transfers to copyright compliance.

## Attack or failure mechanism
Compliance guardrail bypass via modality/context: the safety behavior that refuses direct infringing requests does not
generalize when the copyrighted content is presented as retrieved/uploaded context, especially images; models ignore
copyright notices; fine-tuning for awareness causes over-refusal of legitimate (fair-use) tasks.

## Proposed defense or method
CopyGuard — a tool-augmented, model-agnostic framework that (1) identifies copyright notices, (2) verifies copyright
status/source via external tools, (3) analyzes query risk and rewrites risky queries toward transformative
alternatives, and (4) injects a copyright-status reminder to steer the LVLM away from infringing generation; designed
to adapt to changing copyright status (e.g., expiration) without a static blocklist.

## Datasets and benchmarks
New benchmark: 50,000 multimodal query-content pairs across four content types (book excerpts, news articles, music
lyrics, code documentation), four infringement scenarios (repetition, extraction, paraphrasing, translation), and two
notice conditions (with / without notice; four notice types incl. original notice, "All Rights Reserved", textual vs.
visual placement). Curated across timelines/genres to reduce contamination.

## Evaluation methodology
Dataset-level compliance score FX(M) averaged over content-query pairs using a per-response compliance function
fM(x,q) in [0,1] based on lexical overlap (ROUGE), semantic similarity (cosine/BERTScore), and behavioral indicators
(refusal rate); compare 12 LVLMs with/without notices; evaluate CopyGuard's reduction of infringement across scenarios.

## Metrics
Compliance score fM(x,q)/FX(M); refusal rate; ROUGE / semantic-similarity based reproduction measures; BERTScore.

## Main findings
- Most LVLMs (11/12) fail to recognize and respect copyrighted content in multimodal context, even when an explicit
  copyright notice is present.
- Models often ignore copyright notices; notice format/placement (textual vs. image-embedded) affects but does not fix
  behavior.
- Naive fine-tuning for copyright awareness causes over-refusal of legitimate/transformative tasks.
- CopyGuard reduces copyright-infringement risk across all scenarios, is model-agnostic, and adapts to changing
  copyright status (author claim).

## Negative results
LVLMs' direct-request refusals do not transfer to the multimodal-context setting (a negative result about existing
safety alignment); embedding/relying on copyright notices is insufficient; fine-tuning trades infringement for over-
refusal.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Compiling a comprehensive, up-to-date dataset of all copyrighted content is impractical (motivating a tool-augmented
rather than blocklist approach); copyright law varies by jurisdiction so the analysis is not a strict legal
interpretation. Further self-limitations not fully enumerated in extracted text.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis)_
- CopyGuard is not stress-tested against an adaptive adversary trying to bypass its notice-identifier / query-rewriter
  (e.g., obfuscated content, adversarial images, split payloads across turns).
- Compliance scoring is heuristic (ROUGE/similarity/refusal); it may mislabel borderline fair-use or transformative
  outputs and depends on threshold choices.
- The external "copyright source verifier" presumes reliable status lookup; its coverage, latency, and error modes are
  not fully characterized in extracted text.
- Legal ground truth is the authors' operationalization (redistribution=infringing), which may not match specific
  jurisdictions.
- Benchmark/code availability not explicitly stated in extracted text, limiting independent replication.

## Design implications
Core agent-security lesson: a refusal guardrail that holds for direct requests can fail through the retrieval/tool/
context channel — safety policy must be enforced at the point where untrusted content enters context (RAG ingestion,
uploaded images), not only on the surface prompt. Externalizing policy checks into tools (verifier + risk analyzer +
query rewrite) is a viable guardrail pattern.

## Implementation implications
Place a copyright/compliance gate on retrieved and user-provided multimodal context; support query rewriting toward
transformative use to avoid both infringement and over-refusal; make the policy source (status verifier) updatable
rather than a static list.

## Evaluation implications
Safety/compliance for LVLM agents must be measured in the multimodal-context and RAG setting with notice/no-notice
variants, and defenses must be evaluated against adaptive bypass, not only clean benchmark reduction.

## Deployment implications
For multimodal RAG / web-agent stacks that ingest online content, an ingestion-time copyright gate is a deployment
control; residual risk includes verifier gaps, adaptive obfuscation, and jurisdictional variance.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Enables detection/logging of high-risk reproduction attempts and rewrite interventions; incident review should capture
content source, notice status, and the guardrail's decision/rewrite for auditability.

## Applicability boundaries
Scope: LVLM copyright recognition/compliance in multimodal context and a tool-augmented guardrail defense. Core to
agent/LLM safety (guardrails, RAG ingestion, tool-augmented policy enforcement, web-agent content handling). Do not
generalize the compliance numbers to text-only LLMs, to non-copyright safety policies, or to adaptive-adversary
robustness without further testing.
