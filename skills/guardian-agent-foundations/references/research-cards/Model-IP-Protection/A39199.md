## Research question
How can ownership-verifying watermarks be robustly embedded into Gradient Boosting Decision Tree (GBDT) models — which are non-differentiable and built sequentially with inter-tree dependencies — so that the watermark survives post-deployment fine-tuning while minimally degrading accuracy?

## Problem definition
GBDTs are widely used for structured/tabular data (often outperforming neural nets) and are valuable IP, but watermarking them is underexplored. Neural-net watermarking (subtle decision-boundary shifts) does not apply due to non-differentiability; random-forest watermarking (independent trees) does not apply because GBDT trees depend on prior gradients. Prior GBDT work (Zhao et al. 2022) is fragile (integrity authentication / weak watermarking). The paper proposes the first robust GBDT watermarking framework. This is a **defense / watermark-embedding paper**.

## System or model being studied
A robust watermarking framework using in-place fine-tuning of an existing GBDT (e.g., XGBoost/LightGBM-style). Rather than adding trees (which can be pruned away), it modifies internal parameters of existing trees (Algorithm 1: per boosting iteration and class, recompute gains/splits top-down; retrain a subtree only if the best split changes; update terminal-node predictions). Watermark bits are encoded by flipping (or retaining) the label of selected candidate samples. Four candidate-selection strategies: (a) Wrong Prediction Flip, (b) Outlier Flip, (c) Cluster Center Flip (anchors watermark with correctly-labeled neighbors to localize boundary "holes"), (d) Confidence Flip (lowest-confidence correctly-classified samples near boundaries). Two candidate-subset selection procedures (e.g., Lowest Confidence).

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: remove/erase the ownership watermark (or evade detection) from a stolen/redistributed GBDT while keeping it useful — or, conversely, the model owner wants persistent provenance.
- Adversary knowledge: an attacker who obtains the model may fine-tune it further; watermark sample/key details are secret to the owner.
- Adversary access: post-deployment access to the model for fine-tuning (tree addition and/or in-place updates).
- Adversary capabilities: further fine-tuning; tree pruning of low-contribution trees.
- Attacker budget: fine-tuning on their own data (the paper studies robustness to subsequent fine-tuning).
- Phase: watermark embedding at training/fine-tuning; removal threat at deployment (post-hoc fine-tuning).
- Targeted / untargeted: watermark is targeted (specific input→forced-label pairs encode bits).
- Digital / physical: digital (tabular models).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive — robustness tested against generic further fine-tuning, not against a dedicated adaptive watermark-removal/detection attack.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the owner can fine-tune the initial model in-place with watermark samples and later query a suspect model on secret watermark inputs to check forced predictions. Assumes in-place updates embed the watermark "more deeply" than tree addition (which is prunable). Assumes candidate samples chosen in low-impact regions (outliers, low-confidence, boundary) minimize accuracy loss while remaining robust.

## Attack or failure mechanism
The defense counters two erasure vectors: (1) tree-addition-based fine-tuning watermarks are removable by pruning low-contribution added trees; (2) naive label flips can be washed out by later fine-tuning or can harm accuracy. In-place updates modify existing tree splits/leaves so the watermark is entangled with model structure. The paper also analyzes the gradient direction: for a flipped watermark sample with multiplier r, it derives conditions (Sec. 3.2) under which the gradient does not strictly guarantee movement toward the watermark class, motivating sample duplication (dcand=train) so watermark gradients dominate.

## Proposed defense or method
In-place GBDT watermarking (Algorithm 1) plus four candidate-selection strategies and two subset-selection procedures, with sample duplication to ensure watermark gradients dominate; separate candidate datasets (Dtrain vs Dholdout) to avoid conflicting gradients and to reflect third-party/post-hoc watermarking (e.g., model purchasers).

## Datasets and benchmarks
Multiple UCI structured datasets referenced/used, including optdigits, pendigits, Wine Quality, Letter Recognition, Avila, Image Segmentation (UCI). Experiments vary watermark ratio |W|/|Dtrain| (0.001, 0.01, 0.1) and candidate source (Dcand = Dtrain vs Dcand ≠ Dtrain).

## Evaluation methodology
Measures watermark embedding success, accuracy degradation vs the unwatermarked model, and robustness (watermark retention) after further fine-tuning, across datasets, watermark ratios, and the four embedding strategies; compares Dcand=Dtrain vs Dcand≠Dtrain settings.

## Metrics
- Watermark embedding rate / success (fraction of watermark bits/samples correctly forced).
- Accuracy degradation (ΔAccuracy vs baseline).
- Robustness = watermark success retained after post-deployment fine-tuning.
- Author claim: high embedding rates, low accuracy degradation, strong resistance to post-deployment fine-tuning.

## Main findings
- Author claim: first robust GBDT watermarking via in-place updating; the four strategies embed watermarks with limited accuracy impact and retain watermarks under further fine-tuning.
- In-place updating is argued to be more robust than tree-addition (which is prunable).
- Results tables show many settings reach 1.000 watermark rates with high robustness, especially at higher watermark ratios; the paper notes high variance at the lowest ratio (|W|/|Dtrain| = 0.01 and below).

## Negative results
- At very low watermark ratios (e.g., |W|/|Dtrain| = 0.01), robustness results show high variance (explicitly noted for Table 6/Table 7), i.e., reliability degrades with few watermark samples.
- The gradient analysis shows embedding is not guaranteed to push samples to the watermark class for arbitrary multiplier r, requiring duplication to compensate.
- Outlier Flip's accuracy preservation and fine-tuning resilience depend on distributional similarity between the fine-tuning data and Dcand, which may not hold.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Authors acknowledge: no strict guarantee that the watermark-class gradient is negative (removal/embedding can fail without duplication); high variance at low watermark ratios; Outlier Flip depends on distributional-similarity assumptions; when Dcand=Dtrain, GBDT's high training accuracy leaves few wrongly-predicted candidates for Wrong Prediction Flip.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Robustness is tested only against generic further fine-tuning; no dedicated adaptive watermark-removal, distillation, model-extraction, or watermark-detection/overwriting attack is evaluated, so "robust" is scoped narrowly.
- No formal false-positive analysis for ownership verification (probability that an independently trained model matches the watermark key) is presented in the extracted text.
- Datasets are small/medium UCI tabular sets; scalability to industrial-scale GBDTs and to regression tasks is not demonstrated here.
- Security relies on secrecy of watermark key samples; leakage or guessability of the key is not analyzed.
- In-place retraining of subtrees could interact with model calibration/fairness; only accuracy is reported.

## Design implications
For non-differentiable tree ensembles, robust ownership marks should modify existing structure in place (splits/leaves) rather than append prunable trees; candidate selection in low-impact regions (outliers, low-confidence boundary, anchored cluster holes) preserves accuracy. Sequential gradient dependence in GBDTs must be respected — watermark gradients need to dominate (duplication) to embed reliably.

## Implementation implications
In-place subtree retraining and gradient-dominance duplication are the load-bearing implementation details; a separate holdout candidate set (Dcand≠Dtrain) supports post-hoc watermarking by third parties (e.g., model buyers). Watermark ratio must be high enough to avoid the observed high-variance regime.

## Evaluation implications
GBDT watermarking claims should report embedding rate, ΔAccuracy, and post-fine-tuning retention across watermark ratios, plus false-positive/ownership-collision rates and — for a stronger bar — adaptive removal attacks (not covered here).

## Deployment implications
Enables ownership verification for deployed tabular GBDT IP (including privacy-sensitive/healthcare domains) with minimal accuracy cost, resilient to benign further fine-tuning. Because robustness is unproven against dedicated adaptive erasure and the key must stay secret, treat it as provenance evidence rather than tamper-proof protection; pair with access control and legal registration.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Verification is query-based (test suspect model on secret watermark inputs), supporting incident-time ownership attribution of leaked/redistributed models; requires secure storage of the watermark key set.

## Applicability boundaries
Specific to GBDT/boosted-tree models on structured/tabular data. Insights (in-place vs additive embedding, gradient dominance) transfer within tree-ensemble IP protection but not to neural/LLM/agent watermarking. Agent-security relevance is **adjacent**: model-provenance/ownership verification is a supply-chain integrity primitive, but the paper does not address agent tool-use, prompts, memory, or LLM-specific threats.
