## Research question
How can a content creator watermark their text before publication so that unauthorized use of that text in LLM training is later detectable with high confidence and no false positives — even when the watermarked data is a vanishingly small fraction (<0.001%) of the training corpus, and even at large training scales (5 billion tokens) where standard membership-inference collapses to random?

## Problem definition
LLMs are pretrained on scraped web text, raising copyright/licensing and benchmark-contamination concerns; open-weight models rarely disclose training data. Membership Inference Attacks (MIA) detect training data by comparing model log-probability scores of member vs held-out non-member data, but are highly sensitive to small distribution shifts between suspect and held-out sets — when member/non-member sets are sampled homogeneously (removing temporal artifacts), all tested MIA methods perform no better than random (Duan et al. 2024; Maini et al. 2024; Das et al. 2025). STAMP watermarks text via multiple KGW-keyed rephrasings (one public, rest private) and compares perplexities, but requires storing many private rephrases, needs decoding-layer access, and its member/non-member p-value gap is at most ~3 orders of magnitude — argued insufficient for legally-significant claims.

## System or model being studied
SPECTRA (Score sampled rePhrasing to detECt TRAining data): a training-data watermark + verification test operating on text corpora and target LLMs. Watermarking phase (before publication): generate multiple LLM paraphrases of each document (no decoding modification needed); score each with Min-K%++ using a separate scoring model M_S (with knowledge cutoff before the dataset's release, approximating the target's pre-training state); select one paraphrase per document whose Min-K%++ score closely matches the original (Algorithm 1, side-balanced sampling with weights exp(-α|r_ij-1|), α=100), so the watermark introduces no distribution shift / false-positive signal. Verification phase (grey-box, log-probs only): compute score ratio r(x,x') = f_{Min-K%++}(x';M) / f_{Min-K%++}(x;M) under target M_T and scoring model M_S; test H0: E[r under M_T] = E[r under M_S] vs H1: E[r under M_T] < E[r under M_S] (scores rise after training). No non-member dataset required.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: (from the defender's framing) An LLM trainer uses the creator's copyrighted/licensed/paywalled text for training without authorization. The "attack" being detected is unauthorized training-data usage.
- Adversary knowledge: The trainer does not know which documents are watermarked or the scoring model; the defender/arbiter needs grey-box access (query M_T for token log-probabilities; weights/architecture not required).
- Adversary access: The adversary scrapes and trains on published D'; the defender publishes D' and retains original D.
- Adversary capabilities: Large-scale pretraining/continued-pretraining on scraped corpora. The paper does not model an adversary who tries to strip the watermark from training data.
- Attacker budget: Continued pretraining evaluated at +500M and +5B tokens; watermarked data <0.001% of corpus; 500 samples/dataset (≤512 tokens each).
- Phase (training / inference / deployment): Watermark applied pre-publication; detection after (continued) pretraining — training-time provenance with post-hoc verification.
- Targeted / untargeted: Dataset-level membership (not per-document labels), by design robust to per-example noise.
- Digital / physical: Digital (text).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: Non-adaptive — no watermark-removal or evasion adversary is evaluated (acknowledged as future work / limitation).

## Trust assumptions
- A scoring model M_S is available that was NOT trained on D' and approximates the target's pre-training state (easy because D/D' are unpublished at scoring time; pick an open model with a cutoff before the dataset's release).
- Grey-box access to the target M_T's token log-probabilities (typical for open-weight models; for closed models via a neutral/court-appointed arbiter).
- Watermarking must be done before publishing; already-published content cannot be retroactively protected.
- SPECTRA is robust to changing the scoring model provided it is "sufficiently capable" (Spearman ρ>0.8, Kendall τ>0.6 for models >2.8B params).

## Attack or failure mechanism
Root failure of prior MIA: at large training scale, Min-K%++ signal vs a held-out non-member set becomes indistinguishable from random due to distributional homogeneity; and MIA is fragile to member/non-member distribution shift. SPECTRA's insight: use the scoring model M_S (pre-training proxy) as the reference point instead of a non-member dataset, and normalize each watermarked score by its original-document score, so training-induced Min-K%++ increases become detectable via the ratio test. Selecting paraphrases balanced above/below the original score avoids planting a detectable-without-training signature (which would cause false positives).

## Proposed defense or method
Two-phase watermark: (1) paraphrase + Min-K%++ scoring with a separate scoring model + score-matched, side-balanced sampling (Algorithm 1) to preserve the original score distribution; (2) statistical ratio test comparing Min-K%++ score ratios under target vs scoring model — no non-member set needed, dataset-level inference for robustness to individual-document noise.

## Datasets and benchmarks
- Continued-pretraining datasets: Wiki, HN (Hacker News), PubMed; and PeS2o (used for scoring-model robustness analysis). Each watermarked dataset is <0.001% of the training corpus; 500 samples each.
- Target models: Pythia family (410m used for the ROC-AUC MIA-baseline table; additional scoring models up to Pythia 6.9b; OLMo-1b as the base scoring model).
- Baselines: Loss (Yeom et al. 2018), Min-K% (Shi et al. 2023), Min-K%++ (Zhang et al. 2025), DC-PDD (Zhang et al. 2024); method-level comparison to STAMP (Rastogi et al. 2025).

## Evaluation methodology
Continued-pretrain Pythia models with the watermarked datasets added at +500M and +5B tokens; compute ROC-AUC of classifying member vs held-out same-domain data for baseline MIA scores (Table 1) to show Min-K%++ degrades to ~random at 5B tokens; then run SPECTRA's ratio test to obtain member vs non-member p-values. Scoring-model robustness: rank-correlate Min-K%++ rankings across OLMo-1b and 4 additional models via Spearman/Kendall.

## Metrics
- ROC-AUC (member vs held-out classification for baseline MIA scores).
- p-value of the statistical test (member vs non-member); orders-of-magnitude gap in p-values.
- Rank correlations: Spearman's ρ, Kendall's τ (scoring-model robustness).

## Main findings
- (Author claim) SPECTRA makes training data detectable even at <0.001% of the corpus and after 5B-token continued pretraining, with a consistent p-value gap of at least nine orders of magnitude between member and non-member datasets (>3 orders better than STAMP's ≤3).
- (Author claim) SPECTRA is the only method tested that yields a statistically significant membership result across all datasets without false positives; largest member/non-member p-value separation among baselines using 500 samples each.
- (Author claim) The score-matched, side-balanced sampling strategy outperforms random sampling and max-Min-K%++ selection, and avoids the false-positive signature that consistent high-score selection would create.
- (Author claim) Robust to changing the scoring model when the model is sufficiently capable (ρ>0.8 for >2.8B-param models; τ>0.6 on 3/4 datasets).
- (Table 1) At +5B tokens, all baseline MIA scores (Loss/DC-PDD/Min-K%/Min-K%++) drop to ~0.50–0.56 ROC-AUC (near random), confirming the motivating failure.

## Negative results
- Standard MIA scores (including Min-K%++) collapse to near-random ROC-AUC at 5B-token scale (motivating the method; explicitly reported).
- SPECTRA does not help creators who have already published content (must watermark before release).
- For structured text (e.g., conversational text), the paraphrasing approach "does not perform very well" (author-stated).

## Limitations stated by the authors
- Requires grey-box access to token log-probabilities — challenging for fully proprietary models (mitigated only via a third-party arbiter).
- Only protects content going forward (pre-publication watermarking); no retroactive protection.
- Poor performance on structured/conversational text.
- Verification currently by the content creator/arbiter, not arbitrary third parties; validation at full pretraining scale (vs continued pretraining) is future work.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — clearly not author claims)_
- **No watermark-removal / adaptive adversary**: a trainer who deduplicates, re-paraphrases, or filters scraped text before training could plausibly weaken the signal; not evaluated.
- **Continued-pretraining ≠ from-scratch pretraining**: all results use continued pretraining of Pythia; the authors themselves flag full-scale validation as open, so legal-grade robustness at true pretraining scale is unverified.
- **Scoring-model dependence**: requires an appropriately-cutoff, sufficiently-capable M_S; the Pythia 160m case showed weaker correlation, indicating a capability floor.
- **Dataset-level only**: cannot label individual documents (by design), limiting granular attribution.
- **Paraphrase fidelity/utility trade-off** for human-readable content is not deeply quantified in the extract.

## Design implications
- Using a pre-training-proxy scoring model as the reference point (instead of a held-out non-member set) is a robust way to sidestep MIA's distribution-shift fragility — a reusable principle for data-provenance/membership systems.
- Score-matched paraphrase selection (balance above/below the original) is a clean design pattern to avoid planting a training-independent signature that would cause false positives.

## Implementation implications
- Watermarking needs only black-box paraphrase generation + scoring (no decoding-layer/GPU-heavy watermark injection like KGW/STAMP), lowering the bar for content creators.
- Verification requires only grey-box log-probs and the retained originals — deployable by a creator or a neutral arbiter.

## Evaluation implications
- Membership/watermark methods must be tested at realistic training scales (5B+ tokens) and against homogeneously-sampled non-members; ROC-AUC at small scale overstates capability.
- p-value separation (orders of magnitude) is a more decision-relevant metric than AUROC for legal/enforcement use.

## Deployment implications
- Targets copyright/licensing enforcement and benchmark-contamination detection; provenance signal supports "did model M train on my dataset?" claims, potentially in legal disputes (with the grey-box-access and pre-publication caveats).
- For agent/data-supply-chain security, provides an auditable provenance marker on training/eval corpora fed into model pipelines.

## Monitoring and incident implications
- Enables data owners/auditors to detect unauthorized dataset usage post-hoc and quantify confidence (p-value), supporting incident/enforcement workflows and benchmark-contamination auditing.

## Applicability boundaries
_(where these findings should and should NOT be generalized)_
- **Adjacent to agent/LLM security**: this is training-data provenance / dataset-inference (ML privacy & IP), transferable to data-supply-chain integrity for AI systems, but not directly about agent/tool/prompt runtime security. Applies to unstructured text watermarked before publication and detected via grey-box log-probs; does NOT apply to structured/conversational text (author-stated weak), to already-published data, to closed models without an arbiter, or against adaptive watermark-removal adversaries (untested).
