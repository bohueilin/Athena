## Research question
Can an unsupervised, unpaired noise-approximation layer that learns the discrepancy between mathematically-simulated noise and real screen-camera (SC) noise improve the robustness of deep image watermarking against SC recapture, without requiring paired training data?

## Problem definition
Unauthorized screen capturing and dissemination cause data leakage/IP theft; SC-resistant watermarking supports post-hoc copyright certification. Robustness depends on how well the training-time noise layer approximates real SC degradation. Two prior strategies are inadequate: (a) mathematical modeling linearly superimposes independent noises, ignoring coupling among noise sources and missing fine-grained local distortions; (b) supervised neural fitting needs paired sharp/real SC data, which requires error-prone manual rectification/alignment (labeling bias) and struggles to map sharp→complex SC noise directly.

## System or model being studied
S2R: a hybrid noise layer inserted into an encoder–noise–decoder watermarking pipeline (watermark backbone MCFN referenced). It composes a fixed mathematical modeling transform T (sharp→"certain" simulated domain C) with a learned unsupervised image-to-image network G (domain C→unknown real SC domain U): F_U(·) = T∗G, y^u = G(T(x^s)). G uses MIMO-UNet with adversarial (WGAN-GP) + multi-scale perceptual reconstruction losses; unpaired translation baselines include CycleGAN and DualGAN.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: unauthorized reproduction/dissemination of displayed content via screen-camera recapture (piracy), degrading/removing the watermark.
- Adversary knowledge: not a cryptographic adversary; models physical recapture conditions (distances, angles, lighting, moiré).
- Adversary access: photographs content displayed on a screen with a camera.
- Adversary capabilities: introduce SC physical degradations (perspective, light, moiré, Gaussian noise, grayscale deviation, defocus).
- Attacker budget: consumer screen + camera at varying distance (20–40 cm) and angle (0°–60°).
- Phase: deployment (post-hoc copyright certification after capture).
- Targeted / untargeted: untargeted physical degradation (the recapture process, not a crafted watermark-removal attack).
- Digital / physical: physical (screen-to-camera channel).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive — no adversary deliberately optimizing to erase the watermark is evaluated.

## Trust assumptions
The watermark owner controls embedding; robustness is judged by whether the message survives realistic SC capture. Real SC images can be collected arbitrarily (unpaired) without one-to-one correspondence to sharp images, making unsupervised training feasible.

## Attack or failure mechanism
SC recapture imposes complex, coupled physical degradations that cause watermark message loss. Poor noise-layer fidelity (biased math models or under-generalizing supervised fitters) yields watermarks that fail under real-world capture.

## Proposed defense or method
S2R reframes the hard direct mapping S→U as the easier two-step S→C→U: mathematical modeling provides a robustness/generalization prior (domain C), then an unsupervised network G learns only the residual distribution gap C→U from unpaired data. The design is plug-and-play in the degradation model T (StegaStamp/PIMoG/SSDS can be swapped in). A feasibility proof decomposes SC noise into multiplicative (k^u) and additive (n^u) terms across the S→C→U composition, arguing the reduced-difficulty alignment learns the bias between y^c and y^u.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Sharp images and real SC image sets (unpaired) captured across distances (20–40 cm) and angles (0°–60°, left-right and bottom-top); a supervised variant trained on 900 paired I+E samples for comparison. (Full training-set identity is in supplementary material referenced as Section A.3; not fully in extracted text.)

## Evaluation methodology
Compare S2R against SOTA SC-resistant watermarking (StegaStamp, PIMoG, SSDS) on watermark robustness and image quality under real SC conditions; scalability experiments swapping degradation models (StegaStamp/SSDS/PIMoG-based S2R); ablations vs supervised S2R, CycleGAN-based, DualGAN-based variants; robustness across shooting distances and angles.

## Metrics
Bit Error Rate (BER; lower is better) for watermark recovery; PSNR for imperceptibility/image quality (Table 4 reports e.g., S2R (I+E) PSNR 42.57 with lower BER than baselines/variants).

## Main findings
- Author-reported: S2R achieves lower BER than StegaStamp/PIMoG/SSDS across shooting distances and angles, with superior imperceptibility (higher PSNR) and generalization.
- Unsupervised S2R outperforms its own supervised variant (limited by labeled-data reliance and generalization) and outperforms CycleGAN/DualGAN unsupervised baselines; both unsupervised learning variants beat purely mathematical noise layers.
- The framework is plug-and-play: swapping the mathematical degradation model does not require altering the core structure.
- Larger shooting angles increase BER, but S2R degrades more gracefully than competitors.

## Negative results
Supervised S2R underperforms unsupervised S2R (insufficient generalization, labeling bias). BER rises at large capture angles for all methods. CycleGAN/DualGAN unsupervised variants underperform S2R.

## Limitations stated by the authors
Future work will enable adaptive noise refining during end-to-end training (implying current T→G is a two-stage, non-jointly-adapted pipeline). Mathematical modeling still cannot alone capture complex real artifacts.

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- No adaptive/adversarial watermark-removal attacker evaluated — robustness is against natural SC degradation, not a motivated remover, so this is not a security guarantee against IP-theft adversaries who deliberately attack the mark.
- Device/generalization claims rest on a limited capture setup (distance/angle ranges, specific screens/cameras).
- Feasibility "proof" is a signal-model decomposition, not a robustness guarantee on message recovery.
- Full dataset/training details are deferred to supplementary material.

## Design implications
Decomposing a hard domain-gap problem (sharp→real physical noise) into a modeled prior plus a learned residual is an effective, data-efficient pattern for physical-channel robustness; unpaired unsupervised learning removes the paired-data bottleneck.

## Implementation implications
Practical SC-resistant watermarking can use a plug-and-play modeled degradation front-end refined by an unsupervised translator, avoiding costly paired-data rectification. Owners get post-hoc certification against screen-recapture leakage.

## Evaluation implications
Physical watermarking robustness should be measured with BER/PSNR across realistic distance/angle sweeps and, ideally, against adaptive removal attacks (a gap here). Ablating the noise-layer strategy (math vs supervised vs unsupervised) isolates its contribution.

## Deployment implications
Applicable to anti-piracy/anti-leak provenance for content displayed on screens (e.g., confidential documents photographed off-screen). Not a substitute for access controls; provides post-hoc traceability, not prevention.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Recovered watermarks support post-hoc infringement certification and leak-source attribution; reliability drops at extreme capture angles, which should be factored into evidentiary confidence.

## Applicability boundaries
Applies to screen-camera-resistant image watermarking for copyright/leak-tracing. Should NOT be generalized to LLM/agent/tool security or to adversarial (deliberate) watermark-removal threat models. Peripheral to agent security: it is computer-vision physical-channel IP protection; the transferable idea is provenance/traceability of exfiltrated visual content.

Comparative BER/PSNR advantages are author-reported. The method's value is robustness to natural SC degradation; its resistance to intentional watermark-removal attacks is untested and should not be assumed.
