## Research question
Can existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) watermarking/steganography schemes genuinely provide robust copyright protection, or can an adversary remove ("purify") the embedded watermark from a distributed 3DGS asset while preserving its usability?

## Problem definition
3DGS assets are expensive to train and are treated as valuable digital IP. Recent scene-hiding watermarking schemes (GS-Hider, Splats in Splats, SecureGS) embed watermarks into the 3D Gaussian model itself (not merely rendered images), enabling ownership verification and provenance tracking. The paper studies whether these model-level watermarks can be stripped by an adversary operating directly on the 3DGS file. This is an **attack / red-team paper** against watermark-based IP protection.

## System or model being studied
- Victim defenses: three scene-level 3DGS watermarking schemes — GS-Hider (Zhang et al. 2024), Splats in Splats (Guo et al. 2024c), SecureGS (Zhang et al. 2025).
- Proposed attack: GSPure, described as the first watermark purification framework designed specifically for 3DGS. It uses (1) view-aware Gaussian weight accumulation to quantify each Gaussian primitive's rendering contribution across viewpoints, (2) geometrically accurate feature clustering (HDBSCAN over standardized position/opacity/weight features), and (3) adaptive cluster-level and noise-level pruning (thresholds τc, τn) to remove watermark-correlated Gaussians while preserving scene fidelity. Authors note they modified the 3DGS CUDA implementation to compute contribution weights.

## Threat model
- Adversary objective: remove/purify the embedded watermark from a published 3DGS point cloud while keeping the original scene usable for rendering/editing (untargeted with respect to which bits; targeted at the watermark subspace).
- Adversary knowledge: the adversary has the published/distributed watermarked 3DGS asset (its Gaussian primitives and attributes). The paper states existing schemes rely on hidden recovery viewpoints/attributes unknown to the attacker; the attack does not require them. Knowledge of the exact watermarking scheme's secret keys is not assumed — closest to gray/black-box on the embedding secret but white-box on the asset file.
- Adversary access: direct access to the released Gaussian model file; ability to render from arbitrary viewpoints and prune primitives.
- Adversary capabilities: compute rendering contributions, cluster, and prune Gaussians; no access to the owner's private extraction viewpoints.
- Attacker budget: not quantified in monetary terms; operates offline on the asset.
- Phase: deployment / post-distribution.
- Targeted / untargeted: targeted removal of watermark-correlated primitives.
- Digital / physical: digital (3D scene representation).
- Adaptive / non-adaptive: non-adaptive with respect to the defender — the three victim schemes are fixed and not re-hardened against GSPure.

## Trust assumptions
Assumes the owner embeds a watermark into the released Gaussians and later extracts it for provenance. Assumes watermark-related Gaussians exhibit distinct, viewpoint-inconsistent rendering behavior and strong position/opacity coupling that the attacker can exploit. Assumes the attacker can render the user-visible scene from many viewpoints.

## Attack or failure mechanism
The paper observes that watermark-related Gaussians tend to have lower and more viewpoint-inconsistent rendering contributions than scene Gaussians (they stay "inactive" in the visible scene but activate under the owner's hidden recovery views). By accumulating per-Gaussian contribution weights across N views, concatenating standardized position/opacity/weight into a feature vector, clustering with HDBSCAN, and pruning low-contribution clusters (plus per-point noise pruning), GSPure isolates and removes watermark primitives. Simple 2D-inspired baselines (random pruning, feature scaling, attribute-noise injection, GOF surface extraction) either fail to remove the watermark or degrade the scene, because the watermark is entangled with scene geometry/photometry.

## Proposed defense or method
No new defense is proposed; GSPure is the attack. The authors advocate (recommendation, not evaluated) for developing more robust 3DGS watermarking and principled adaptive watermark-removal research.

## Datasets and benchmarks
Mip-NeRF360 dataset (Barron et al. 2022), following the scene–watermark associations used by GS-Hider. Qualitative comparisons also shown on Mip-NeRF360 scenes.

## Evaluation methodology
Compares GSPure against four adapted 2D purification baselines (random Gaussian pruning, feature scaling, Gaussian noise injection, GOF surface extraction) across the three victim watermarking schemes. Reports original-scene fidelity and watermark degradation; includes ablations over pruning thresholds τc (1.0–4.0) and τn.

## Metrics
- PSNR and SSIM for novel-view-synthesis fidelity of the original scene.
- Watermark PSNR degradation (lower watermark PSNR = better removal). A "Score" metric is introduced to jointly assess purification vs. fidelity (definition truncated in extracted text).
- Headline author claim: reduces watermark PSNR by up to 16.34 dB while incurring less than 1 dB PSNR loss on the original scene.

## Main findings
- Author claim: GSPure achieves the best watermark purification across GS-Hider, Splats in Splats, and SecureGS while best preserving scene fidelity, outperforming the four baselines.
- Author claim: conventional 2D watermark-removal techniques do not generalize to 3DGS due to the specialized differentiable rasterization pipeline and per-primitive attributes.
- Ablation: larger τn raises both scene and watermark PSNR (watermark noise primitives are entangled with scene primitives); larger τc mainly improves scene quality with little effect on watermark removal — authors recommend high τc and flexible τn.
- Conclusion (author): current 3DGS watermarking does not provide absolute protection.

## Negative results
- Simple/intuitive attacks (random pruning, feature scaling, noise injection, GOF) are reported to either leave watermark residue or damage the scene — i.e., naive purification is insufficient.
- When attacking GS-Hider (VWu-style), authors note some dispersed residual noise dots remain (qualitative), indicating imperfect removal in some cases.

## Limitations stated by the authors
The paper frames results as revealing vulnerabilities and calls for more robust watermarking and adaptive removal methods, implicitly acknowledging that defenses can be strengthened. (Extracted text does not contain a dedicated, itemized limitations section.)

## Additional limitations identified during review
_(reviewer synthesis — not author claims)_
- Evaluation is confined to a single dataset (Mip-NeRF360) and three specific scene-hiding schemes; generalization to other 3DGS/NeRF watermarking families is untested.
- Non-adaptive: the victim schemes are not re-designed to resist GSPure, so the arms-race outcome is unknown; a watermark that intentionally spreads across high-contribution Gaussians could evade the low-contribution heuristic.
- No code/data release is stated, and the "Score" metric definition is truncated, hampering exact reproduction.
- Success depends on the assumption that watermark Gaussians have low/inconsistent visible-view contribution; schemes that couple the watermark to visually critical primitives may break this assumption.
- Fidelity is measured by PSNR/SSIM on novel views only; downstream editing usability after purification is asserted but not quantified.

## Design implications
Provenance/watermark robustness for high-value 3D model assets cannot rely on embedding a signal that is inactive in the user-visible scene — such signals are statistically separable via multi-view contribution analysis. Robust 3D IP protection likely needs the mark entangled with rendering-critical structure or backed by cryptographic provenance rather than purely steganographic hiding.

## Implementation implications
Any watermark verifier for 3DGS should assume adversaries can render arbitrary views and cluster primitives by geometric/photometric features; verification keyed to secret recovery viewpoints is a weak secret because those primitives leave a detectable low-contribution footprint.

## Evaluation implications
Watermark IP-protection papers should report robustness against 3D-native, model-space purification (not just 2D image transforms) and against clustering/pruning adversaries; single-dataset, non-adaptive robustness claims overstate protection.

## Deployment implications
Distributing a watermarked 3DGS asset in the clear exposes it to offline purification; owners should combine watermarking with access control, licensing/attestation, and out-of-band provenance (signed manifests) rather than treating the embedded watermark as sufficient IP protection.

## Monitoring and incident implications
Because purification is offline on a distributed file, there is no query-side telemetry to detect it; detection must shift to provenance verification of suspect assets and to registries that bind assets to owners independent of the fragile embedded mark.

## Applicability boundaries
Findings are specific to 3D Gaussian Splatting scene-hiding watermarking and the graphics rendering pipeline. They generalize as a cautionary lesson about steganographic watermark separability, but the concrete attack (view-aware weight accumulation + Gaussian clustering) does not transfer to LLM/agent/text watermarking. Relevance to agent security is **adjacent**: it reinforces that embedded, secret-keyed watermarks used for model/asset provenance are removable under white-box asset access, which matters for any provenance-by-watermark claim in an agent supply chain.
